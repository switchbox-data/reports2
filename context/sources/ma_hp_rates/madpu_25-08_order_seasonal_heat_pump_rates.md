## SUMMARY

In this Order, the Department of Public Utilities directs National Grid and Unitil to file proposed winter transmission charges for their heat pump rates for the 2026-2027 heating season no later than June 15, 2026, consistent with the design of winter transmission charges approved for NSTAR Electric's heat pump rates in _NSTAR Electric Company_, D.P.U. 25-55 (2025). Our Order is a continuation of an investigation of near-term rate design and related implementation proposals developed by the Department of Energy Resources and the Interagency Rates Working Group regarding the heat pump rates offered by the Commonwealth's three electric distribution companies. This change to National Grid's and Unitil's current heat pump rates will lower costs for those companies' heat pump customers and promote consistent heat pump rate offerings across the state.

In addition, the Department sets forth specific directives to National Grid, Unitil, and, where applicable, NSTAR Electric regarding the implementation of their heat pump rates. Specifically, the Department directs the Companies to leverage their existing energy efficiency marketing, education, and outreach ("MEO") initiatives to promote their respective heat pump rates; to coordinate with local and statewide organizations to increase awareness; and to clearly communicate through MEO materials and at in-person events that the heat pump rates are intended to be temporary until more advanced rate designs become available following the deployment of advanced metering infrastructure ("AMI") meters.

To help customers make informed decisions about their rate options, the Department further directs the Companies to clearly present opt-out information on their respective heat pump rate webpages and to provide customer service opportunities to assist customers with the opt-out process, including translation and interpretation requests. To improve transparency regarding participation in the Companies' seasonal heat pump rates, the Department directs the Companies, moving forward, to report on: (1) the number of customers enrolled in the seasonal heat pump rates who reside in designated equity communities within their service territory; (2) the total number of customers within their service territory enrolled in the seasonal heat pump rates; (3) a narrative description of lessons learned, successes, and challenges associated with their MEO efforts, with particular attention to outreach directed toward income-eligible households, equity communities, and environmental justice populations, including any resulting adjustments to their MEO strategies; and (4) a narrative description of their collaborative efforts with stakeholders, including community-based organizations, to increase customer awareness of their seasonal heat pump rates as well as lessons learned, successes, and challenges, including any resulting adjustments to their MEO strategies.

Moreover, consistent with the Department's directives to NSTAR Electric in D.P.U. 25-55, the Department directs National Grid and Unitil to coordinate with the Mass Save Program Administrators to compile all relevant customer data across their respective service territories to ensure complete reporting. The Department also directs the Companies to report on the billed revenues recorded from heat pump customers in their annual revenue decoupling filings and to compare those revenues to the amounts that would have been billed if those customers had not enrolled in the heat pump rates.

Additionally, the Department directs the Companies to include links on their dedicated heat pump webpages to the Mass Save heat pump webpage and the Mass Save heat pump guide webpage to help customers better understand their eligibility. The Department also directs the Companies to integrate budget billing information into their customer communication and outreach materials to help customers manage and avoid unexpected increases in summer cooling costs. Finally, the Department directs the Companies to apply lessons learned from ongoing market evaluations to continuously refine and optimize their MEO strategies.

The Department's ultimate priority remains the development of rate structures that will take advantage of the deployment of AMI meters. Time-varying rates, for example, have the potential to help lower electric bills for many customers, including heat pump customers, which is the subject of our review of a petition that DOER filed on April 8, 2026, requesting that the Department open an investigation into time-varying rates (See D.P.U. 26-62).

## I. INTRODUCTION

On March 21, 2025, the Department of Public Utilities ("Department") opened this inquiry into seasonal heat pump rates for the Commonwealth's three investor-owned electric distribution companies: (1) NSTAR Electric Company d/b/a Eversource Energy ("NSTAR Electric"); (2) Massachusetts Electric Company and Nantucket Electric Company, each d/b/a National Grid ("National Grid"); and (3) Fitchburg Gas and Electric Light Company d/b/a Unitil ("Unitil") (individually, "Company," collectively, "Companies"). _Inquiry by the Department of Public Utilities on its own Motion into a Seasonal Heat Pump Rate_, D.P.U. 25-08, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 1 (2025). The Department specifically sought to investigate whether the Companies should modify the current heat pump rate design before the start of the 2026-2027 heating season, as well as issues related to the implementation of heat pump rate offerings, marketing, education, and outreach ("MEO") measures, reporting, and enrollment. D.P.U. 25-08, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 9. The Department docketed this proceeding as D.P.U. 25-08.

Through this investigation, the Department has gathered a substantial body of information regarding seasonal heat pump rates and related implementation strategies from the Companies and a wide range of stakeholders. In this Order, the Department directs National Grid and Unitil, by June 15, 2026, to file proposed winter transmission rates for incorporation into their existing heat pump rates before the 2026-2027 heating season (November 1, 2026 through April 30, 2027) for the Department's approval in separate proceedings. The proposed winter transmission rates shall be developed in a manner consistent with the rates approved for NSTAR Electric in D.P.U. 25-55, at 21-22. The Department also directs the Companies to implement certain new MEO strategies and reporting requirements that align with the Commonwealth's equity and environmental justice ("EJ") objectives.

## II. PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND

In 2024, the Department approved seasonal heat pump rates for National Grid and Unitil as part of their respective base distribution rate proceedings. _Massachusetts Electric Company and Nantucket Electric Company_, D.P.U. 23-150, at 509-513 (2024); _Fitchburg Gas and Electric Light Company_, D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 406-409 (2024). The Department directed National Grid and Unitil to implement the approved seasonal heat pump rates in time for the 2025-2026 winter heating season (i.e., November 1 through April 30) and required them to undertake certain outreach and education efforts to increase customer awareness and participation in the new rate offerings. D.P.U. 23-150, at 512-513; D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 406-409. The Department also established annual reporting requirements for each Company related to the implementation of their respective heat pump rates. D.P.U. 23-150, at 513; D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 408.

On January 31, 2025, DOER filed a petition requesting that the Department initiate this investigation into seasonal heat pump rates. Specifically, DOER requested that the Department direct the Companies to: (1) establish or modify an optional seasonal heat pump rate for residential customers with seasonally differentiated volumetric delivery rates, inclusive of each Company's base distribution charge and all reconciling mechanisms; (2) prepare or revise a seasonal heat pump rate tariff for residential customers; (3) begin preparations for the implementation of the seasonal heat pump rates through tariff filings to ensure that the rate offerings are available to customers by fall 2025; (4) develop MEO plans for the Department's approval; and (5) report on customer enrollment, outcomes, energy usage, and bill impacts (Petition at 1-2). DOER's petition incorporated findings and recommendations from the Interagency Rates Working Group ("IRWG"), a DOER-led partnership with the Massachusetts Clean Energy Center, the Attorney General of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts ("Attorney General"), and the Executive Office of Energy and Environmental Affairs ("EEA"), established to advance near- and long-term rate solutions and innovations (Petition at 2). In support of its petition, DOER provided: (1) the IRWG's Near-Term Strategy Report (December 20, 2024); (2) the IRWG's Near-Term Rate Strategy Recommendations (December 20, 2024); (3) illustrative seasonal heat pump rate designs; and (4) a seasonal heat pump report template.

Pursuant to notice duly issued, the Department accepted comments through June 2, 2025, on: (1) the merits of the IRWG's recommendations, including seasonally differentiated volumetric delivery rates, inclusive of base distribution charges and all reconciling mechanisms; and (2) the merits of the IRWG's recommendations regarding MEO. D.P.U. 25-08, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 10.

In the Vote and Order opening this inquiry, the Department commended DOER and the IRWG for their analytical work evaluating alternative heat pump rate designs intended to encourage heat pump uptake while mitigating the financial impacts on customers transitioning from fossil fuel heating. D.P.U. 25-08, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 7. We also acknowledged, however, that the various heat pump rate design issues presented by the IRWG required a full examination that would take more time than initially contemplated by DOER and the IRWG. Accordingly, the Department determined that National Grid's and Unitil's respective heat pump rates would remain in effect for the 2025-2026 winter heating season while the Department completed its investigation. D.P.U. 25-08, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 7-8. The Department emphasized that it was not rejecting DOER's proposal on the merits and stated its intent to complete its investigation of DOER's petition in time for the start of the 2026-2027 winter heating season. D.P.U. 25-08, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 9. Further, the Department stated that it would continue to study appropriate heat pump rate structures in the broader context of time-varying rates ("TVR"). D.P.U. 25-08, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 10.

## III. SEASONAL HEAT PUMP RATES

### A. DOER and IRWG Proposals

In support of its petition, DOER explains that the IRWG's examination of barriers and opportunities related to electric rates found that households transitioning from fossil fuel-based heating systems, including natural gas and delivered fuels, to electric air-source heat pumps may face higher overall heating costs due to existing electricity rates and rate designs (Petition at 3). DOER asserts that, absent an alternative rate structure that addresses this barrier, households are unlikely to switch to heat pumps at the pace and scale required to meet the Commonwealth's electrification and decarbonization goals, as set forth in Massachusetts' Clean Energy and Climate Plan for 2050 (Petition at 3; see also DOER Comments at 3-4).[^2] DOER further notes that, through a stakeholder engagement process conducted throughout 2024, the IRWG identified broad support for its recommended seasonal heat pump rates (Petition at 4).

[^2]: EEA, [Massachusetts Clean Energy and Climate Plan for 2050](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massachusetts-clean-energy-and-climate-plan-for-2050) (2022) (last visited June 1, 2026).

DOER proposes that each Company implement an optional seasonal heat pump rate for residential customers with seasonally differentiated volumetric delivery charges that incorporate each Company's base distribution charge and applicable reconciling mechanisms (Petition at 1-2; see also DOER Comments at 3-4). DOER asserts that its proposed rate design would provide greater savings for customers who use heat pumps to heat their homes than the heat pump rates previously approved by the Department, thereby addressing a significant economic barrier to electrification and creating a stronger price signal in favor of heat pump adoption (Petition at 1; Exhs. A at 63; B at 17-18, 20; see also DOER Comments at 3-4). To illustrate its proposal, DOER submitted a rate design worksheet demonstrating how its proposed rate design could be incorporated into Unitil's existing heat pump rates (Petition, Exh. C; see also Exh. B at 19).

DOER emphasizes that the magnitude of winter volumetric rate reductions is critical to the effectiveness of any heat pump-specific rate in encouraging heat pump uptake and that substantial reductions in winter delivery rates are needed to produce meaningful bill savings that are competitive with natural gas heating costs (Petition at 3, 6; Exhs. A at 63; B at 16, 19; see also DOER Comments at 5-6). DOER maintains that seasonal differentiation of base distribution charges alone is unlikely to influence customer behavior or significantly improve heat pump affordability because such charges comprise only a small fraction of total winter delivery costs (Petition at 5-6; Exhs. A at 63; B at 19).

Additionally, DOER asserts that a seasonal rate with lower winter prices would be cost-reflective in the near term given existing winter capacity headroom on the electric distribution system and would enable the Companies to make more efficient use of their existing infrastructure during periods when system capacity is underutilized (Petition at 4; Exhs. A at 80; B at 16, 19-20). According to DOER, its proposed seasonal heat pump rate supports the Commonwealth's decarbonization and clean energy objectives without imposing near-term system costs and lays a foundation for more dynamic, data-driven rate designs as load profiles, technology, and system conditions evolve over time (Petition at 1, 4, 6; Exhs. A at 80; B at 20, 23-24).

### B. Summary of Comments

#### 1. Support for DOER and IRWG Proposal

Most commenters broadly support DOER's proposal for the Companies to incorporate seasonal reconciling mechanism charges into their respective heat pump rates, asserting that greater seasonal differentiation of heat pump rate components could result in increased savings for heat pump customers during the heating season (Building Electrification Accelerator Comments at 2-3; Compact Comments at 5; Elephant Energy Comments at 1; Environmental and Climate Justice Advocates Joint Comments at 2-3; GECA Comments at 23; HeatSmart Alliance Comments at 1; Laminar Collective Comments at 2; Mothers Out Front Comments at 2; NEEP Comments at 1-2; Rewiring America Comments at 5; Sierra Club Comments at 3). Many of those commenters highlight the importance of growth in heat pump installations to the Commonwealth's decarbonization goals (see, e.g., Attorney General Comments at 4; Building Electrification Accelerator at 1; City of Boston Comments at 2; Mothers Out Front at 2). Several commenters specifically argue that it is necessary to implement heat pump rate designs that will make operating a heat pump less expensive than fossil fuel heating equipment to promote heat pump adoption (see, e.g., GECA Comments at 2-3, 6-7; HeatSmart Alliance Comments at 1; NEEP Comments at 2; Switchbox Comments at 2). Elephant Energy and HeatSmart add that, based on their experience, homeowners are hesitant to install heat pumps due to concerns about operating costs (Elephant Energy Comments at 2; HeatSmart Alliance Comments at 1).

The Attorney General is generally supportive of DOER's proposal with some refinements. She maintains that the Companies should each offer an optional heat pump rate with seasonally differentiated base distribution and transmission charges to reduce energy burdens for customers transitioning from gas heating to electric heat pumps—a rate design that the Sierra Club also supports as an alternative to DOER's proposal (Attorney General Comments at 2-3; Sierra Club Comments at 3). The Attorney General expresses concern, however, about DOER's proposal to seasonally differentiate reconciling mechanism charges, claiming that it could introduce additional administrative complexity and costs, create customer confusion, and raise equity concerns if the rate structure is not carefully designed (Attorney General Comments at 3-4). Regarding heat pump rate eligibility, the Attorney General recommends limiting enrollment in the heat pump rates to customers whose heat pump systems are sized to meet the full heating needs of their homes due to concerns with the impact of heat pump air conditioning load on system peaks and the associated distribution system costs (Attorney General Comments at 2).

Additionally, the Laminar Collective supports DOER's proposal and argues that concerns raised by other commenters about potential rate shock for customers transitioning from heat pump rates to future rates enabled by AMI are overstated, contending that customers are generally aware that incentives are temporary and would likely accept a gradual reduction to heat pump rate discounts during the shift to AMI-enabled rates (Laminar Collective Comments at 3-4). The Laminar Collective also points out that there is no concrete timeline for the rollout of AMI-enabled rates, which could take many years to implement (Laminar Collective Comments at 2).

#### 2. Low-Income and Income-Eligible Customers

The City of Boston asserts that residential heat pump rates must incorporate discounted rates for low-income customers, make electrification affordable for all customers, and lower heating costs for families facing financial hardship (City of Boston Comments at 3). Similarly, the Environmental and Climate Justice Advocates contend that incorporating discounted rates for low-income customers into heat pump rates would ensure broad customer inclusion and equitable participation (Environmental and Climate Justice Advocates Joint Comments at 3-4). The Building Electrification Accelerator urges the Department to ensure heat pump rates are affordable for low-income households (Building Electrification Accelerator Comments at 3).

#### 3. Support for Implementing Heat Pump Rates on an Interim Basis

The Attorney General maintains that the Companies should offer heat pump rates only on an interim basis, i.e., until AMI-enabled rate designs are implemented (Attorney General Comments at 2). Following AMI deployment, the Attorney General argues that the Companies should implement AMI-enabled rate designs, such as whole-house time-of-use ("TOU") rates and critical peak pricing, and phase out technology-specific heat pump rates (Attorney General Comments at 2). Similarly, the Environmental and Climate Justice Advocates emphasize that heat pump rates should be temporary and urge policymakers and regulators to proactively plan for a winter-peaking system and develop long-term ratemaking frameworks (Environmental and Climate Justice Advocates at 3).

#### 4. Opposition to DOER and IRWG Proposals

The Companies and United/ACT urge the Department to reject DOER's proposal altogether and instead prioritize the timely development of AMI-enabled rates, which they contend are better suited to support the long-term evolution of the electric distribution system (Companies' Joint Comments at 2-6; United/ACT Comments at 3-6). The Companies and United/ACT contend that implementing a near-term rate design could complicate the transition to a long-term, AMI-enabled rate and lead to customer confusion and education fatigue, potentially delaying or preventing the implementation of AMI-enabled rates (Companies' Joint Comments at 2; United/ACT Comments at 4).

The Companies also emphasize that, as the Massachusetts electric system evolves through the energy transition, rate designs need to be evaluated with a long-term perspective, especially given that the system conditions driving current rates may change significantly within the next five to ten years (Companies' Joint Comments at 2-3). Specifically, the Companies note that the electric system is expected to become winter peaking within the next decade and caution that DOER's proposed seasonal heat pump rates could increase winter electricity usage, potentially accelerating the shift to a winter-peaking system and complicating system planning (Companies' Joint Comments at 3). Additionally, the Companies argue that DOER's proposed rates send price signals based on the seasonal consumption patterns of a specific end-use technology rather than on system marginal costs, which are driven by peak demand regardless of season (Companies' Joint Comments at 4). As a result, the Companies contend that implementing DOER's proposed seasonal heat pump rates, even temporarily, would provide misleading price signals to customers about current and future system peaks, particularly as the seasonal discount increases (Companies' Joint Comments at 4). In turn, the Companies argue that DOER's proposed rate design could ultimately increase costs for all ratepayers (Companies' Joint Comments at 4).

Moreover, the Companies argue that the implementation of a non-cost-reflective seasonal rate, such as the one proposed by DOER, increases the likelihood that the rate would remain in place even after the system becomes winter peaking (Companies' Joint Comments at 4). Specifically, the Companies argue that artificially low winter rates would be difficult to sunset because eliminating them could cause rate shock for customers transitioning to the otherwise applicable rates (Companies' Joint Comments at 4).

Finally, the Companies assert that DOER's proposal to revise National Grid's and Unitil's approved heat pump rates would effectively overturn the Department's prior approval of those rates (Companies' Joint Comments at 6). The Companies stress the importance of finality in Department Orders, which they claim provides them with the certainty needed to conduct their business effectively (Companies' Joint Comments at 6). For these reasons, the Companies urge the Department to deny DOER's proposal to modify their approved heat pump rates (Companies' Joint Comments at 6).

### C. Analysis and Findings on Seasonal Heat Pump Rates

Customer conversion to electrified and decarbonized heating technologies, including heat pumps, is an important component of the Commonwealth's transition to clean energy. D.P.U. 25-08, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 1. The acceptance of heat pumps is a critical step toward the electrification of building thermal loads; however, all else equal, heat pump usage billed on the residential electric rates can increase customer electric bills beyond the actual cost to serve these customers due to the increased energy use required to operate a heat pump during the heating season. Last year, the Department addressed this issue by approving heat pump rates for the Companies' customers. As a result of the Department's decisions, each Company had heat pump rates in place during the 2025-2026 heating season, enabling heat pump customers to save money on their electric bills. D.P.U. 25-55, Exh. ES-HP-2 Heat Pump Rate Compliance, Sch. 3; D.P.U. 23-150, Exh. HP-3-Revised at 2. As noted above, the Department initiated this investigation to determine whether the Companies should implement DOER's proposed modifications to the approved heat pump rate design for the start of the 2026-2027 heating season.

Since DOER filed its petition, the Department has issued two significant orders that impact this investigation—_Energy Burden with a Focus on Energy Affordability for Residential Ratepayers_, D.P.U. 24-15-B, Phase I Order on Tiered Discount Rates (February 17, 2026) and _Investigation by the Department of Public Utilities on Its Own Motion into Gas and Electric Delivery Charges and Bill Redesign_, D.P.U. 25-200, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry (December 15, 2025). Together, these Orders address many of the commenters' concerns and chart a course for the development of long-term rate design solutions that promote affordability, equity, and reductions in greenhouse gas ("GHG") emissions, consistent with the consensus of information and arguments submitted by the wide range of stakeholders that participated in this inquiry.

The affordability of utility rates for all customers is of paramount importance. D.P.U. 24-15-B at 20. In furtherance of that priority, the Department recently directed the gas and electric distribution companies to implement tiered discount rates by November 1, 2026, consisting of six tiers, each of which is designed to achieve a target energy burden. D.P.U. 24-15-B at 21. Pursuant to our Energy Burden Order, discounts for the lowest-income tier (households earning less than or equal to 100 percent of the federal poverty limit ("FPL")) must result in a total energy burden of four percent (i.e., a two percent energy burden each for electric customers and gas heating customers and a 0.5 percent energy burden for gas non-heating customers). D.P.U. 24-15-B at 23-24. Further, the discounts for the remaining five income tiers (households earning more than 100 percent of FPL and less than or equal to 60 percent of state median income) must result in a total energy burden of six percent (i.e., a three percent target energy burden each for electric customers and gas heating customers and a 0.75 percent target energy burden for gas non-heating customers), with a discount floor of 15 percent for gas companies and 25 percent for electric companies. D.P.U. 24-15-B at 24. Consequently, all tiered discount rate customers, including customers eligible for heat pump rates, will have these enhanced discounts applied to their total bills starting November 1, 2026, which addresses the concerns about low-income and income-eligible customers raised by the commenters in this proceeding.

Complementing the Department's investigation into energy burden, the Department opened an inquiry to conduct a comprehensive review of gas and electric delivery rates and charges, with the goals of containing customer costs, reducing bill volatility, and improving bill transparency and accessibility. D.P.U. 25-200, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 1. As part of that proceeding, the Department is examining several significant changes to the reconciling mechanisms that DOER proposes to include in the modified heat pump rates. For example, we are investigating whether to eliminate, modify, or restructure existing electric reconciling mechanism charges to contain costs and reduce bill volatility for all customers, promote efficiency and transparency, better align charges with cost incurrence, and reduce administrative burden. D.P.U. 25-200, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 12. The Department is also examining whether and to what extent reconciling mechanism charges should be recovered through fixed charges rather than volumetric charges, as well as the feasibility of establishing a cap on the month-to-month variation in charges assessed to customers. D.P.U. 25-200, Vote and Order Opening Inquiry at 13-14.

Furthermore, the Department is currently reviewing a petition DOER filed on April 8, 2026, requesting that the Department open an investigation into TVR to ensure that TVR is available to customers in advance of full AMI deployment. The Department has docketed that proceeding as D.P.U. 26-62.[^3] At this time, the exact timing of a decision in D.P.U. 25-200 and the establishment of AMI-enabled rates is uncertain. Nevertheless, the Department agrees with many commenters about the critical importance of implementing innovative, long-term rate design solutions to help lower energy bills for customers across the Commonwealth and to better utilize existing distribution infrastructure. To that end, the Department intends to expeditiously examine and prioritize pathways to lower energy bills for all customers, including heat pump customers, through redesigned delivery rates and charges in D.P.U. 25-200 and through AMI-enabled rate structures. The Department expects to complete its review of delivery rates and charges in D.P.U. 25-200 in time to implement such changes before the 2027-2028 heating season. In addition, the Department expects to establish initial AMI-enabled rates before the Companies' anticipated completion of AMI deployment in 2027, which will become effective after the 2027-2028 heating season.

[^3]: DOER's petition in D.P.U. 26-62 states that DOER agrees with the Department that the Companies' heat pump rates should be offered on an interim basis and will no longer be necessary once TOU rates are implemented. D.P.U. 26-62, Exhibit A at 54-55. TOU rates are a type of TVR.

Consistent with the positions of the Attorney General, the Companies, the Environmental and Climate Justice Advocates, and United/ACT, the Department continues to regard the Companies' heat pump rates as interim rate offerings intended to bridge the period until more advanced, AMI-enabled rate designs, such as TVR, become available. D.P.U. 25-55, at 23, 27; D.P.U. 23-150, at 512; D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 409. In light of the developments described above, including the possibility that initial AMI-enabled rate designs may be approved prior to the 2027-2028 heating season, we must consider the likelihood that this interim transition period will conclude within the next few years. We share the concerns raised by the Attorney General, Companies, and United/ACT that incorporating all reconciling mechanisms into the Companies' heat pump rates will increase administrative complexity and costs, create customer confusion, and result in significant rate volatility if heat pump rates transition to AMI-enabled rates within a few years. Therefore, in evaluating DOER's proposal to modify the Companies' seasonal heat pump rates, we must balance the potential for modified seasonal heat pump rates to further incentivize acceptance of heat pump technology with our responsibility to prudently and efficiently manage this transition. The Department must also consider DOER's proposal in accordance with our statutory obligation under G.L. c. 25, § 1A to prioritize safety, security, reliability of service, affordability, equity, and reductions in GHG emissions, as well as traditional ratemaking principles grounded in cost-causation. See also G.L. c. 164, § 141 (requiring the Department in all of its decisions and actions regarding rate designs to consider the impacts of such actions on, among other things, the reduction of GHG emissions as mandated by Chapter 21N to reduce energy use).

The Department acknowledges the broad support for DOER's proposal, which is grounded in the objective of making heat pump operating costs less expensive than fossil-fuel heating system operating costs, thereby incentivizing heat pump conversion to reduce GHG emissions. Establishing electric pricing based on the cost of alternative fuels, however, would be a potentially harmful divergence from cost-causation principles and longstanding Department ratemaking precedent. In the absence of a truly competitive market in which supply and demand for a given commodity naturally reach least-cost pricing, the regulator is responsible for ensuring that prices charged to customers for monopoly services are fair and reasonable. Traditionally, this is accomplished through cost-based ratemaking.

Base distribution and transmission costs are largely capital-driven and highly correlated with the cost to serve demand, making them well-suited to seasonal heat pump rate designs that reflect the timing and manner in which demand-related costs are incurred in a system that is currently summer peaking. D.P.U. 25-55, Exh. ES-HP-1, at 14. In 2025, the Department approved heat pump rates for NSTAR Electric that included a revenue-neutral redesign of transmission charges in addition to winter base distribution rate charges. D.P.U. 25-55, at 23. By contrast, National Grid's and Unitil's seasonal heat pump rates, which the Department approved prior to our approval of NSTAR Electric's heat pump rates, do not include rate design changes to winter transmission charges. D.P.U. 23-150, at 509-513; D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 406-409. The Department found that NSTAR Electric's proposal to implement a revenue-neutral redesign of transmission charges for winter heat pump rates was reasonable, cost-based,[^4] and effective in mitigating the bill impacts associated with increased electric usage due to heat pump adoption. D.P.U. 25-55, at 21.

[^4]: In this context, "cost-based" means that the winter transmission charges were structured to be revenue neutral, recovering the capital costs embedded in the transmission service revenue requirement through adjusted winter sales. D.P.U. 25-55, at 21.

Based on the comments and information provided in this proceeding and building on the Department's prior approval of NSTAR Electric's heat pump rates, the Department determines that including redesigned winter transmission charges in National Grid's and Unitil's respective heat pump rates has the potential to produce greater bill savings for customers during the heating season (Petition, Exhs. A, at 63; B, at 17-18, 20). The Department also finds that reducing the transmission charge during the heating season has the potential to accelerate heat pump adoption by lowering winter operating costs (Sierra Club Comments at 3). This, in turn, would support the Commonwealth's climate goals by incentivizing the uptake of lower-GHG emitting heating systems, delivering tangible customer savings for participating customers, and advancing energy equity by reducing ongoing heat pump operation costs for low-income and income-eligible households.

The Department also concludes that incorporating redesigned winter transmission charges into National Grid's and Unitil's respective heat pump rates would promote greater consistency across the Commonwealth and improve uniformity between the Companies' heat pump rate offerings. From a policy perspective, this approach aligns with the Department's objective of supporting beneficial electrification in a manner that is cost-based, transparent, and equitable. D.P.U. 25-55, at 18-24; D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, 406-407; D.P.U. 23-150, at 509-513; see also _2025-2027 Three-Year Energy Efficiency Plans_, D.P.U. 24-140 through D.P.U. 24-149, at 136-144 (2025) ("2025-2027 Three-Year Plan Order") (discussing statewide efforts to encourage heat pumps). The heat pump rates are designed to be revenue neutral and will not increase costs to other customers beyond the cost to serve those customers.

Accordingly, the Department concludes that incorporating redesigned winter transmission charges into National Grid's and Unitil's respective heat pump rates is appropriate based on cost-causation principles and the need to advance the Commonwealth's climate and equity objectives.

Unlike base distribution and transmission costs, reconciling mechanism charges generally recover operations and maintenance costs or costs associated with policy programs and are less directly tied to customer demand. In this proceeding, there is not clear and convincing information to support a finding that integrating seasonal reconciling mechanism charges other than transmission charges into the Companies' heat pump rates is consistent with cost-causation principles. Considering this, alongside the potential adverse impacts on customers discussed above, the Department declines to direct the Companies to incorporate seasonal reconciling mechanism charges other than transmission charges into their respective heat pump rates at this time. Consistent with our determinations above, the Department directs National Grid and Unitil to file proposed winter transmission charges for their respective heat pump rates for the 2026-2027 heating season on or before June 15, 2026, consistent with the design of winter transmission charges approved for NSTAR Electric's heat pump rates in D.P.U. 25-55. National Grid and Unitil shall calculate the winter transmission charges using the same method used to derive their winter base distribution charges, including the use of the same forecasted summer and winter annual usage and seasonal adjustment factors applied to calculate the winter base distribution rates, and apply these factors to transmission charges to recover the winter transmission revenue requirement over the adjusted winter sales. D.P.U. 25-55, at 7-8. With their filings, National Grid and Unitil shall each submit revised heat pump rate tariffs, bill impact analyses, and supporting exhibits, including rate design worksheets. The Department will review National Grid's and Unitil's proposed rates and tariffs in separate adjudicatory proceedings, to be docketed as D.P.U. 26-71 and D.P.U. 26-72, respectively.

As noted by several stakeholders, the Companies' forecasts indicate that each is expected to experience a shift to a winter-peaking system within the next decade. The Department finds that the directives issued herein provide sufficient flexibility to modify or discontinue the Companies' respective heat pump rate offerings prior to such a shift (Companies' Joint Comments at 3). This approach allows the Department to continue encouraging beneficial electrification while preserving the ability to reassess rate designs as system conditions evolve and as AMI deployment enables more granular data collection and more sophisticated rate designs. See _Electric Sector Modernization Plans_, D.P.U. 24-10/D.P.U. 24-11/D.P.U. 24-12, at 21, 25, 89. Consistent with this approach, the Department will review the heat pump rates in the Companies' next base distribution rate proceedings unless an alternative rate design is approved prior to that time. D.P.U. 25-55, at 23; D.P.U. 23-150, at 512-513; D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 409-410.

Finally, in response to the Attorney General's recommendation to limit eligibility for the heat pump rates to customers whose systems are sized to meet the full heating requirements of their homes, the Department notes that we previously considered this recommendation in D.P.U. 23-150 and D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81. In those proceedings, the Department declined to approve such a restriction, emphasizing the importance of encouraging any form of beneficial electrification at this early stage and that imposing a sizing requirement could discourage potential participants from installing heat pumps, thereby slowing the proliferation of energy-efficient technologies. D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 401, 407; D.P.U. 23-150, at 500, 512. Accordingly, the Department again declines to implement the Attorney General's recommendation to restrict heat pump rate eligibility to customers whose systems are sized to fully meet their home heating requirements.

## IV. IMPLEMENTATION

### A. IRWG Proposals

The IRWG recommends that the Companies streamline enrollment in the heat pump rates for customers who receive heat pump rebates through the Mass Save program and leverage the Mass Save program to promote the availability and benefits of the heat pump rates (Petition, Exh. B at 25, 27-28). The IRWG also emphasizes that MEO efforts should be customer-centric, informed by best practices, and responsive to the needs of diverse customer populations (Petition, Exh. B at 25-26). Specifically, the IRWG recommends that the Companies:

1. simplify verification requirements and documentation using plain, non-technical language that resonates with audiences from diverse cultural backgrounds;
2. establish a single point of contact for all heat pump-related programs and rates;
3. ensure the availability of trained customer service representatives to respond to customer inquiries;
4. provide language access services for customers with limited English proficiency;
5. tailor MEO efforts to reflect customer and community preferences;
6. use a variety of communication channels;
7. collaborate with community-based organizations, particularly those serving underrepresented communities; and
8. target outreach to at-risk households using energy usage and income data, including through email, text, or in-app communications (Petition, Exh. B at 25-26).

In addition, the IRWG stresses that MEO efforts should be regularly evaluated and refined based on observed outcomes (Petition, Exh. B at 26). To that end, the IRWG recommends testing communications prior to their distribution and tracking key performance indicators, including: (1) participation rates; (2) enrollment rates at the census tract or block group level; (3) bill savings; (4) usage data capturing energy-limiting behaviors (i.e., households that under-consume energy during the summer and winter); (5) customer satisfaction; and (6) customer engagement levels (Petition, Exh. B at 26-27).

With respect to reporting, the IRWG recommends additional data collection and reporting by rate class and geography, including whether enrolled households are located within EJ populations (Petition, Exh. B at 29). These recommended additional reporting requirements include: (1) tracking heat pump installations relative to baseline levels and on a year-over-year basis; (2) estimates of the total number of households with heat pumps enrolled in the seasonal heat pump rates; and (3) analyses of available interval data to compare usage patterns between households enrolled in the heat pump rates and those on default electricity rates (Petition, Exh. B at 29). Finally, the IRWG recommends that the Companies compare customers' monthly energy usage before and after enrollment for customers who had a heat pump installed for at least one heating season prior to enrollment; analyze and compare available seasonal energy usage data for households enrolled and those not enrolled in the heat pump rates over the first several years of their implementation; and report shadow billing for each enrolled customer showing what the customer would have paid each month without enrolling in the heat pump rates (Petition, Exh. B at 29).

Below, the Department examines whether, and to what extent, modifications to the existing MEO and reporting requirements established in D.P.U. 25-55, at 24-27, D.P.U. 23-150, at 513, and D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 408-409 are appropriate at this time. The Department then addresses the recommendations raised in the comments below.

### B. Summary of Comments

#### 1. Enrollment

Most of the commenters support automatic enrollment in heat pump rates for customers who receive heat pump incentives through the Mass Save program (Building Electrification Accelerator at 3; City of Boston Comments at 3, 5; GECA Comments at 8; NEEP Comments at 4; Rewiring America Comments at 8). While agreeing with the benefits of automatic enrollment, Building Electrification Accelerator cautions that appropriate safeguards should be in place to ensure that customers are not adversely affected by their automatic enrollment in the Companies' heat pump rates (Building Electrification Accelerator at 3).

#### 2. Marketing, Education, and Outreach

Several commenters presented recommendations to improve MEO related to heat pump rates and incentives. Commenters expressed support for a customer-centric approach to MEO, including providing materials in relevant languages, simplifying processes and reducing administrative burdens, offering multilingual support and personalized assistance, and using multiple communication channels (Attorney General Comments at 5; City of Boston Comments at 4; GECA Comments at 9; Rewiring America Comments at 8-9). Commenters also encourage the Companies to collaborate with stakeholders and community-based organizations to increase awareness about their approved heat pump rates and their benefits (City of Boston Comments at 3; GECA Comments at 8; Rewiring America Comments at 8). Commenters emphasize the importance of targeted outreach to low-income and multifamily households to ensure that underserved communities are informed about and able to access these rates (City of Boston Comments at 3-4; Environmental and Climate Justice Advocates Joint Comments at 5; Rewiring America Comments at 8). Further, commenters recommend coordinating MEO for heat pump rates with the Mass Save program (Environmental and Climate Justice Advocates Joint Comments at 4-5; Rewiring America Comments at 7-9; Sierra Club Comments at 4).

NEEP, Sierra Club, and United/ACT support the development of online portals that enable customers to enroll in heat pump rates, compare their current rates with other available rate options, and estimate their expected monthly and annual bills under the heat pump rates, as well as the implementation of exit surveys to better understand why customers opt out of the heat pump rates (NEEP Comments at 4; Sierra Club Comments at 4; United/ACT Comments at 7). United/ACT stress the need to design customer education in a clear and practical manner, providing guidance on how to lower costs under new rates without overwhelming customers with too much information (United/ACT Comments at 7). United/ACT also support the development of personalized tools that use customer-specific data to model bill impacts under different rate options (United/ACT Comments at 7).

The Companies maintain that they are already implementing some of the IRWG's MEO-related recommendations, such as minimizing technical terms and using plain language in their MEO materials (Companies' Joint Comments at 4-5). The Companies note that they typically do not make robocalls or send text messages to customers without their consent, except for issues related to power outages or safety, and emphasize that the Telephone Consumers Protection Act, 47 U.S.C. § 227(b), prohibits calls and texts unrelated to emergencies unless explicit customer consent is obtained (Companies' Joint Comments at 5). Regarding the IRWG's recommendation for targeted MEO based on customers' energy usage, income, and other data, the Companies stress that they are unable to conduct this type of data analysis and marketing internally and would need to evaluate the feasibility and costs of hiring a third-party vendor to perform this work (Companies' Joint Comments at 5).

#### 3. Reporting

The Attorney General asserts that the Companies' approved heat pump rates should be monitored and evaluated to ensure that they are achieving desired outcomes (Attorney General Comments at 4). The Attorney General contends that this evaluation should include an analysis of enrollment levels, customer outcomes, changes in energy usage, and bill impacts (Attorney General Comments at 4). The Attorney General emphasizes the growing importance of tracking shifts in customer usage and the corresponding impacts on the grid, especially as heat pump and electric vehicle adoption continue to increase (Attorney General Comments at 4). In particular, the Attorney General expresses concern that seasonal heat pump rates could lead to significant shifts in energy consumption, including increased summer cooling load, which may contribute to higher distribution system costs by increasing system peak demand (Attorney General Comments at 4). The Attorney General supports the reporting requirements approved by the Department for National Grid's and Unitil's respective heat pump rates and recommends that the Department direct the Companies to submit a compliance filing after the rates have been in effect for a sufficient period (e.g., 18 months) to determine whether the rates should be modified (Attorney General Comments at 5).

Rewiring America recommends that the Companies file annual reports on the implementation of their heat pump rates, including data on the number of residential and low-income discount customers enrolled in the rates and the number of monthly opt outs, as well as customer survey metrics assessing participants' and non-participants' understanding of their current rate structures and available alternatives (Rewiring America Comments at 7, 10-11).

#### 2. Enrollment

Automatic enrollment in the Companies' heat pump rates benefits heat pump customers by providing timely access to rates designed to lower their energy costs during the heating season and by reducing administrative burdens that could otherwise delay or discourage their participation in the rates (Building Electrification Accelerator Comments at 3; City of Boston Comments at 3; GECA Comments at 8; NEEP Comments at 4). Through stakeholder collaboration and the Department's oversight, the Companies implemented automatic enrollment for Mass Save heat pump rebate participants and self-enrollment processes for other heat pump customers before the 2025-2026 heating season, resulting in the enrollment of approximately 140,000 customers across the Companies (Exhs. DPU-Common 3-5; DPU 1-3; DPU 2-4). The Department directs the Companies to continue these automatic enrollment practices and to apply the seasonal heat pump rates to the bills of customers who enroll through the respective Company's intake process by the next billing cycle following their enrollment unless otherwise directed in a future proceeding.

Automatic enrollment must not preclude customer choice or impede a customer's ability to select the rate that best aligns with their preferences and usage patterns. To ensure that customers can make informed decisions regarding their rate options, the Department directs the Companies to clearly provide opt-out information on their respective heat pump rate webpages and to ensure that customer service representatives are trained to assist customers with the opt-out process. To provide equivalent access for individuals with limited English proficiency, each Company's heat pump rate webpage must either include a tool to translate the information into other languages or prominently display contact information for an employee who can respond to translation requests. In addition, each Company must make interpreters available upon request to support customer service representatives in discussing the opt-out process with these customers. _Procedures for Enhancing Public Awareness and Participation in Department Proceedings_, D.P.U. 21-50-A at 30, 34 (2024); Department Language Access Plan at 5, 7-8, Appendix.[^6]

#### 3. Marketing, Education, and Outreach

Effective MEO strategies will be foundational to the continued customer uptake of the Companies' heat pump rates. Based on the information gathered in this inquiry, the Department determines that approving certain additional MEO requirements will enable efficient Department oversight and increase customer awareness of, and participation in, the Companies' heat pump rates.

The Department agrees with the IRWG and commenters that MEO efforts should be customer-focused and informed by best practices. In addition, MEO actions should direct special attention towards accessibility for individuals with limited English proficiency and responsiveness to the needs of low-income and income-eligible customers (Petition, Exh. B at 25-26; Attorney General Comments at 5; City of Boston Comments at 4; Environmental and Climate Justice Advocates Joint Comments at 5; GECA Comments at 9; NEEP Comments at 4; Rewiring America Comments at 8). The Department is encouraged that the Companies have already incorporated many of the IRWG's recommendations into their MEO strategies, including communicating with customers about their heat pump rates through multiple channels and using plain language in customer communications (Companies' Joint Comments at 5; Exhs. DPU-Common 2-1; DPU-Common 2-2; DPU 1-1; DPU 1-2; DPU 2-2).

The Department is committed to ensuring that individuals with limited English proficiency can access and benefit from the Companies' respective heat pump rates. To that end, the Department notes that Unitil currently translates certain MEO materials into Spanish and Portuguese; however, it is unclear whether NSTAR Electric and National Grid similarly translate their heat pump-related MEO materials (Exhs. DPU-Common 1-2; DPU-Common 3-2; DPU-Common 3-2). To help ensure that individuals with limited English proficiency have comparable opportunities to participate in the Companies' respective heat pump rates, the Department directs the Companies to include in a prominent location in their MEO materials contact information for an employee who can respond to translation and interpretation requests. In addition, the Companies must make appropriate arrangements to provide language services to customers upon request.

The Department also agrees with the IRWG and commenters that collaboration with stakeholders, including community-based organizations such as the Low-Income Energy Affordability Network and Community Action Agencies, and targeted outreach are important to increase customer awareness of the Companies' heat pump rates and effectively reach underserved populations, including low-income and income-eligible customers (Petition, Exh. B at 26; City of Boston Comments at 3; GECA Comments at 8). NSTAR Electric and Unitil have already partnered with local and statewide organizations to increase customer awareness of their seasonal heat pump rates, and we direct National Grid to do the same to the extent it has not already done so (Exhs. DPU Common 3-3; DPU 2-2). The Department also directs the Companies to use their existing energy efficiency MEO initiatives to further promote their heat pump rates to the extent they are not already doing so.

The Department is encouraged that NSTAR Electric and Unitil promote the heat pump rates, as well as available heat pump technologies and incentives, at in-person customer assistance events (Exhs DPU Common 3-3; DPU 2-2). The Department expects both companies to continue these efforts and directs National Grid to undertake similar efforts to the extent it has not already done so.

As previously stated in Section IV.C., above, the Department continues to regard the Companies' heat pump rates as interim rate offerings intended to bridge the period until more advanced, AMI-enabled rate designs become available. D.P.U. 25-55, at 23; D.P.U. 23-150, at 512; D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 409. To ensure customers are aware of the temporary nature of these rates, the Department directs the Companies to include clear, plain language in their heat pump rate marketing materials, webpages, and enrollment forms explaining that the rates are a first step towards improved rate designs that reflect when customers use electricity, not just how much they use, which will give customers more control over their electric bills. The Companies must also communicate the interim nature of the heat pump rates at in-person customer education events.

The Department also approves the Companies' proposal to apply lessons learned from ongoing market evaluations to continuously refine and optimize their MEO strategies (Exhs. DPU Common 3-3; DPU 2-3). In addition, as best practices for MEO continue to be developed in D.P.U. 24-15, the Department expects the Companies to incorporate those practices into their heat pump MEO strategies. As discussed above, the Companies oppose two of the IRWG's MEO recommendations. Regarding the recommendation for the Companies to add robocalls and text messages to their customer communication strategies, the Companies note that they are legally prohibited from sending robocalls or text messages to customers without their consent, except for communications related to power outages or safety (Companies' Joint Comments at 5). The Department acknowledges the legal limitations cited by the Companies and affirm that we do not expect the Companies to implement any MEO strategies contrary to law.

In response to the IRWG's recommendation for the Companies to target outreach using energy usage and income data, the Companies claim that they currently lack the internal resources needed to use customer energy usage, income, and other data to target their MEO efforts directly to households based on their needs (Companies' Joint Comments at 5). The Companies assert that they would need to evaluate the feasibility and costs of engaging a third-party vendor to perform this type of targeted MEO (Companies' Joint Comments at 5). Cognizant of the high cost of energy already borne by the Companies' customers and the temporary nature of the heat pump rates, the Department does not find it necessary at this time for the Companies to evaluate the feasibility and cost of engaging a third-party vendor to perform this type of targeted MEO. The Department may revisit the adequacy of the Companies' MEO strategies and determine whether modifications or additional requirements are appropriate in the future. The Department will continue to monitor the Companies' heat pump rate implementation through their annual reconciliation filings and will determine at a later time whether, and under what circumstances, the Companies should be required to submit a proposal to use customer usage data in the Companies' possession to improve their MEO strategies.

The Department notes that the Companies are already required to track and report on the number of customers who opt out of their heat pump rates, which provides a baseline indicator of customer attrition and participation trends. D.P.U. 25-55, at 27; D.P.U. 23-150, at 513; D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-81, at 408. Additionally, the Companies have no mechanism to compel customer participation in such surveys, and voluntary responses may be limited or biased, thereby limiting their usefulness.

#### 4. Reporting

Absent additional reporting, it would be difficult for the Department and interested stakeholders to assess whether the Companies' MEO efforts are effectively reaching all customers in their respective service territories or if the Companies need to modify these strategies to ensure eligible customers receive the benefits of these rates. After considering the information and recommendations provided in this inquiry, the Department determines that additional reporting requirements are necessary to ensure broad customer inclusion and equitable participation in heat pump rates, inform potential future adjustments to the Companies' MEO strategies, and ensure alignment with the Commonwealth's equity and EJ objectives. Ideally, the Companies would report on heat pump rate enrollment for EJ populations in their service territory compared to heat pump rate enrollment for their service territories as a whole. Nevertheless, the Department acknowledges the potential administrative and financial burden of tracking enrollment for EJ populations.[^12] While Unitil explains that it can track the number of customers enrolled in its heat pump rates who reside in EJ populations in its service territory, NSTAR Electric and National Grid state that they are not currently able to provide such reporting (Exhs. DPU Common 3-6; DPU 2-5). Therefore, the Department will not require the Companies to track the number of customers enrolled in their heat pump rates who reside in EJ populations in their respective service territories at this time.

[^12]: EJ populations are defined in G.L. c. 30, § 62 and EEA's 2021 EJ Policy. Available at [Environmental Justice Policy](https://www.mass.gov/doc/environmental-justice-policy6242021-update/download) (last visited June 1, 2026). A census block group is designated as an EJ population if it meets one or more of the following criteria: (1) the annual median household income is not more than 65 percent of the statewide annual median household income; (2) minorities comprise 40 percent or more of the population; (3) 25 percent or more of households lack English language proficiency; or (4) minorities comprise 25 percent or more of the population and the annual median household income of the municipality in which the neighborhood is located does not exceed 150 percent of the statewide annual median household income.

Nonetheless, given that the Companies already coordinate with the Program Administrators to enroll Mass Save heat pump incentive recipients into their respective heat pump rates (Exh. DPU-Common 2-3; D.P.U. 25-55, at 25) and that the Program Administrators report biannually on the number of heat pumps incentivized in designated equity communities[^13] (D.P.U. 24-140 through D.P.U. 24-149, Exh. 1 App. I, Tab "Wxn & HPs by Zip"), the Department directs the Companies to report annually on: (1) the number of customers enrolled in the seasonal heat pump rates who reside in designated equity communities within their service territory; and (2) the total number of customers within their service territory enrolled in the seasonal heat pump rates. 2025-2027 Three-Year Plan Order at 32-33, 71-72. The Companies shall also report the following qualitative information, in addition to the previously established qualitative reporting requirements: (1) a narrative description of lessons learned, successes, and challenges associated with their MEO efforts, with particular attention to outreach directed toward low-income and income-eligible households, equity communities, and EJ populations, including any resulting adjustments to their MEO strategies; and (2) a narrative description of their collaborative efforts with stakeholders, including community-based organizations, to increase customer awareness of their seasonal heat pump rates as well as lessons learned, successes, and challenges, including any resulting adjustments to their MEO strategies. Further, the Department directs the Companies to report on the billed revenues recorded from heat pump customers in their annual revenue decoupling filings and to compare those revenues to the amounts that would have been billed if those customers had not enrolled in the heat pump rates.

[^13]: A designated equity community, as determined through the Program Administrators' 2025-2027 Three-Year Energy Efficiency Plan, must meet the following criteria: (1) greater than 35 percent of the community's population are renters; (2) more than 8,000 renters reside in the community; and (3) more than 50 percent of the community's population are low- or moderate-income. The Compact is a municipal aggregator that has received Department approval to administer electric energy efficiency to member municipalities. Because none of its member municipalities meet the mentioned criteria, a designated equity community in the Compact's program administrative area must meet the following criteria: (1) greater than 28 percent of the community's population are renters; and (2) more than 40 percent of the community's population are low- or moderate-income. The Program Administrators identified the following 21 designated equity communities based on the criteria above: Boston, Brockton, Chelsea, Everett, Fall River, Framingham, Fitchburg, Lawrence, Lowell, Lynn, Malden, New Bedford, Pittsfield, Quincy, Revere, Salem, Springfield, Woburn, Worcester, Oak Bluffs, and Tisbury. 2025-2027 Three-Year Plan Order at 32-33, 71-72, 232.

The Department finds that these additional reporting requirements strike an appropriate balance between providing actionable and meaningful insight into equitable access to the Companies' heat pump rates and avoiding undue administrative and financial burden on the Companies (See Exhs. DPU-Common 3-6; DPU-Common 3-7; DPU 2-5; DPU 2-6). These reporting requirements are sufficient at this stage to allow the Department to assess whether the Companies' MEO strategies are effectively reaching diverse customer populations and supporting broad participation in their respective heat pump rates.
