## THE CONNECTICUT LIGHT AND POWER COMPANY, DBA EVERSOURCE ENERGY — PERFORMANCE-BASED MULTI-YEAR RATE PLAN (PBMRP) RIDER (REDLINED COPY, PDF pp. 1–7)

### PURPOSE:

The Performance-Based Multi-year Rate Plan (“PBMRP”) adjustment enables the Company, subject to the jurisdiction of the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (“PURA”), to adjust, on an annual basis, its base distribution rates (“Base Rates”). The PBR includes a revenue cap-formula with an annual I-X adjustment, a capital cost recovery adjustment, or F Factor, a consumer dividend, an earnings sharing adjustment if the earned return on equity is higher than established thresholds, and recovery or return of exogenous costs.

### EFFECTIVE DATE:

The initial rates established in Docket No. 26-05-10 shall remain in effect until the Company’s next base-rate proceeding subject to adjustments described in this tariff. The PBMRP rider is authorized for a four-year term starting July 1, 2027. The first annual adjustment pursuant to the PBMRP rider shall be effective July 1, ~~2028~~ **2027**. Subsequent annual adjustments shall occur within the four-year term, with the last adjustment taking effect on July 1, 2030.

Prior to the expiration of the four-year term, the Company shall be eligible to file a request to continue the PBMRP for a subsequent term not to exceed four years with the last adjustment taking effect on July 1, 2034. Such a request must be filed with PURA by August 1, 2029, so that PURA may issue its decision by January 31, 2030.

Unless the PBMRP is extended as described above, the last earnings-sharing adjustment would take effect on July 1, 2031 because the earnings-sharing adjustment lags the PBMRP adjustment by one year.

### APPLICABILITY:

This rider applies to all Customers taking Delivery Service under the Company’s rates.

3. Basis Point shall be one one-hundredth of a percentage point.

### ANNUAL RATE ADJUSTMENT:

The PBMRP adjustment shall be used to derive the incremental revenue adjustment. The incremental revenue adjustment will be allocated among Customer Classes using the Class Allocation Factors and applied to the Base Rate Components for each Customer Class based on the billing determinants established by PURA in setting Initial Base Rates. These adjustments to Base Rate Components will not be subject to true up or reconciliation.

### F ~~FACTORADJUSTMENT~~ **FACTOR ADJUSTMENT**:

The F Factor Adjustment is intended to provide predictable and adequate funding for capital investments. There is a limit to the amount of capital improvements that may be included in the annual F Factor Adjustment, imposing an annual capital expenditure constraint up to the amount forecasted in Docket No.** 26-05-10. The first annual PBMRP adjustment will take effect July 1, 2027 to reflect the prospective revenue requirement on the Forecasted Budget (referred to as the F Factor allowance) for capital expenditures which are not reflected in base rates in Docket No.** 26-05-10. Beginning with the annual PBMRP adjustment effective July 1, 2028, the Company’s actual capital additions for the Prior Year are allowed for inclusion in the calculation of the F Factor to the extent that the actual capital additions do not exceed the Forecasted Budget (referred to as the F Factor allowance). To the extent that the cumulative actual capital costs in-service through the Prior Year, in aggregate, exceeds the Forecasted Budget through the Prior Year, then the F Factor allowance is capped at the cumulative Forecasted Budget. The F Factor allowance will be calculated to determine the revenue requirement (inclusive of depreciation expense, property tax expense, return on rate base, and income tax) on actual plant additions (including plant additions, cost of removal, retirements, and accumulated depreciation) up to the Forecasted Budget amount, and to reflect the revenue requirement for the Prior Year and through the Rate Year on in service capital additions placed in service in the Prior Year, provided that those in service capital additions are not reflected in base rates or in another rate mechanism.

### EXOGENOUS COSTS:

Exogenous Events are positive or negative cost changes that (1) are beyond the Company’s control, (2) arise from a change in tax laws or accounting change, or regulatory, judicial or legislative changes, (3) are unique to the gas distribution industry or are not reflected in the calculation of GDPPI, and (4) meet a threshold of significance of 0.5% of allowed total base distribution revenue requirement, as approved in this rate case. The Company would include any such request for exogenous event cost recovery in its annual PBR adjustment filing. The following are examples that would qualify for cost recovery as an Exogenous Event (whether positive or negative): (1) State Initiated Cost Change; (2) Federally Initiated Cost Change; (3) Regulatory Cost Reassignment; or (4) Externally Imposed Accounting Rule Change.

Exogenous event costs can be ongoing or discreet [sic; possibly intended "discrete"] and non-recurring. The Company proposes to reflect ongoing exogenous event costs as a change in base distribution rates and that a non-

[PDF p. 6 | printed p. 6]

recurring exogenous cost would be collected through a separate reconciling factor or as an amortized expense.

The significance threshold for Exogenous Costs is set at 0.5% for each individual Exogenous Event occurring on or after July 1, 2027, adjusted annually thereafter based on changes in the composite I Factor and PFP X factor ($\boxed{I_t - X}$) used in calculating the PBRM. The significance threshold is noncumulative and is subject to a finding by PURA that the exogenous costs arise from a single exogenous event (i.e., exogenous costs from separate exogenous events cannot be aggregated into a single total for purposes of determining whether the threshold of significance is met). Exogenous Event cost recovery requires that the Company present supporting documentation and rationale to PURA for a determination as to the appropriateness of the proposed recovery or refund.

Once approved for recovery by PURA, the amount of the cost change occurring in the Prior Year and deferred for recovery or refund, shall be recovered, or returned to customers either as an adjustment to base rates (if permanent) or as an adjustment to a separate, reconciling rate, as applicable.

### EARNINGS SHARING

Earnings Sharing provides an important protection for Customers in the event that expenses increase at a rate much different than the revenue increases generated by the PBMRP adjustment. In the event that the Company’s actual Distribution Return on Equity (ROE) for any calendar period ending December 31st of the years 2027 through 2030, unless extended by PURA, exceeds the Earnings Sharing Threshold, the difference between actual earnings and earnings calculated at the authorized Return on Equity shall be shared with customers if the Company’s actual ROE exceeds the authorized ROE. Such earnings above the Earnings Sharing Threshold will be shared 50 percent to the Company and 50 percent to Customers.

Earnings Sharing, when applicable, shall result in a single, uniform per kWh credit to all distribution service customers taking service under retail tariffs. The Earnings Sharing credit will be included with base distribution rates for each class. Any Earnings Sharing credit or charge shall be in effect for a period of one year and shall be subject to a full adjudicatory hearing before PURA.

### INFORMATION REQUIRED TO BE FILED WITH PURA

The Company shall make a PBR adjustment filing no later than April 1st of each year for rates effective July 1st of such year, which commences a Rate Year under this mechanism. As part of such annual filing, the Company shall file information and supporting schedules with PURA necessary for PURA to review and approve the PBMRP Adjustment for such Rate Year. Such information shall include the results and supporting calculations of the PBMRP Adjustment Formula, descriptions and accounting of any Exogenous Costs and an earning-sharing calculation for the year prior to the rate adjustment. **The initial filing establishing the F Factor allowance for the rate year shall incorporate information necessary to calculate the F Factor allowance, informed by data reviewed in Docket No. 26-05-10, including the capital expenditure forecast, depreciation rates, property tax expense rates, the cost of capital, among other factors. In each**

### F FACTOR ADJUSTMENT:

The F Factor Adjustment is intended to provide predictable and adequate funding for capital investments. There is a limit to the amount of capital improvements that may be included in the annual F Factor Adjustment, imposing an annual capital expenditure constraint up to the amount forecasted in Docket No. 26-05-10. The first annual PBMRP adjustment will take effect July 1, 2027 to reflect the prospective revenue requirement on the Forecasted Budget (referred to as the F Factor allowance) for capital expenditures which are not reflected in base rates in Docket No. 26-05-10. Beginning with the annual PBMRP adjustment effective July 1, 2028, the Company’s actual capital additions for the Prior Year are allowed for inclusion in the calculation of the F Factor to the extent that the actual capital additions do not exceed the Forecasted Budget (referred to as the F Factor allowance). To the extent that the cumulative actual capital costs in-service through the Prior Year, in aggregate, exceeds the Forecasted Budget through the Prior Year, then the F Factor allowance is capped at the cumulative Forecasted Budget. The F Factor allowance will be calculated to determine the revenue requirement (inclusive of depreciation expense, property tax expense, return on rate base, and income tax) on actual plant additions (including plant additions, cost of removal, retirements, and accumulated depreciation) up to the Forecasted Budget amount, and to reflect the revenue requirement for the Prior Year and through the Rate Year on in service capital additions placed in service in the Prior Year, provided that those in service capital additions are not reflected in base rates or in another rate mechanism.

### EXOGENOUS COSTS:

Exogenous Events are positive or negative cost changes that (1) are beyond the Company’s control, (2) arise from a change in tax laws or accounting change, or regulatory, judicial or legislative changes, (3) are unique to the gas distribution industry or are not reflected in the calculation of GDPPI, and (4) meet a threshold of significance of 0.5% of allowed total base distribution revenue requirement, as approved in this rate case. The Company would include any such request for exogenous event cost recovery in its annual PBR adjustment filing. The following are examples that would qualify for cost recovery as an Exogenous Event (whether positive or negative): (1) State Initiated Cost Change; (2) Federally Initiated Cost Change; (3) Regulatory Cost Reassignment; or (4) Externally Imposed Accounting Rule Change.

Exogenous event costs can be ongoing or discreet [sic; possibly intended "discrete"] and non-recurring. The Company proposes to reflect ongoing exogenous event costs as a change in base distribution rates and that a non-recurring exogenous cost would be collected through a separate reconciling factor or as an amortized expense.

[PDF p. 13 | printed p. 13]

The significance threshold for Exogenous Costs is set at 0.5% for each individual Exogenous Event occurring on or after July 1, 2027, adjusted annually thereafter based on changes in the composite I Factor and PFP X factor ($\boxed{I_t - X}$) used in calculating the PBRM. The significance threshold is noncumulative and is subject to a finding by PURA that the exogenous costs arise from a single exogenous event (i.e., exogenous costs from separate exogenous events cannot be aggregated into a single total for purposes of determining whether the threshold of significance is met). Exogenous Event cost recovery requires that the Company present supporting documentation and rationale to PURA for a determination as to the appropriateness of the proposed recovery or refund.

Once approved for recovery by PURA, the amount of the cost change occurring in the Prior Year and deferred for recovery or refund, shall be recovered, or returned to customers either as an adjustment to base rates (if permanent) or as an adjustment to a separate, reconciling rate, as applicable.

### EARNINGS SHARING

Earnings Sharing provides an important protection for Customers in the event that expenses increase at a rate much different than the revenue increases generated by the PBMRP adjustment. In the event that the Company’s actual Distribution Return on Equity (ROE) for any calendar period ending December 31st of the years 2027 through 2030, unless extended by PURA, exceeds the Earnings Sharing Threshold, the difference between actual earnings and earnings calculated at the authorized Return on Equity shall be shared with customers if the Company’s actual ROE exceeds the authorized ROE. Such earnings above the Earnings Sharing Threshold will be shared 50 percent to the Company and 50 percent to Customers.

Earnings Sharing, when applicable, shall result in a single, uniform per kWh credit to all distribution service customers taking service under retail tariffs. The Earnings Sharing credit will be included with base distribution rates for each class. Any Earnings Sharing credit or charge shall be in effect for a period of one year and shall be subject to a full adjudicatory hearing before PURA.
