### B. Purpose of Testimony

**Q.** What is the purpose of your rebuttal testimony?

**A.** The purpose of my rebuttal testimony is to address Ameren’s compliance with the Commission’s directive to provide a regression analysis as an additional tool for analyzing equity and reliability for its investments and assessing Ameren’s compliance with Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i), (ii), and (iii) of the Public Utilities Act which was modified by the Clean and Reliable Grid Affordability Act (“CRGA”). Specifically, I respond to rebuttal testimony from Ameren Illinois Company d/b/a Ameren Illinois’ (“Ameren” or the “Company”) and direct testimony filed by witnesses from Illinois Environmental Protection Agency (“IEPA”), the Joint Non-Governmental Organizations (“JNGO”), and Illinois Industrial Energy Consumers and Citizens Utility Board (“IIEC-CUB”) related to regression analysis, make-ready program, time of use rate, commercial tariff, rate payer impact, cost effectiveness analysis, portfolio program budget and other issues.

## III. Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i) – Make-Ready Investment Programs

### A. Direct Testimony Recommendation

**Q.** What did Ameren say about its utility side and customer side make-ready program?

**A.** Ameren defined its utility side make-ready infrastructure as “the distribution system facilities, service extensions, and related equipment to serve electric vehicle charging load up to the point of interconnection with the customer's facilities.” (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 12.) Ameren states it provides utility side make-ready through its standard line extension policy to comply with CRGA. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 12.) Ameren states its standard line extension policy reduces or fully offsets upfront utility side make-ready costs, “through the application of a revenue-based allowance tied to the newly connected electric vehicle load.” (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 12.)

In its BE plan 2, Ameren included a supplemental line extension program for the customer side, in addition to the utility side. (Staff Ex. 1.02, 62.) Ameren explained the customer side supplemental line extension program as an allowance that covers customer-owned electric infrastructure necessary to connect an EV charger. This infrastructure is downstream from the Point of Delivery and can include conductors, metering equipment, trenching for service lines, and labor costs. This excludes costs for the charging equipment and electric vehicle. Id. Ameren also explained the distinction between the customer side and the utility side supplemental line extension program through a visual presentation which demonstrates where the utility side ends and where the customer side starts. Id. That representation is reproduced below.

[p. 9, lines 186–199] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

Id.

In BE Plan 2, Ameren allowed customers to apply any leftover allowance from the utility side make-ready to the customer side make-ready infrastructure as an incentive. Id. at 63. However, in this grid plan Ameren states “it is no longer cost beneficial to do so, the Company does not feel it is appropriate to allow customers to apply left over standard line extension policy funding to support the customer owned customer make-ready.” (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 7.)

**Q.** Please summarize the concerns with Ameren’s EV infrastructure make-ready proposal.

[p. 10, lines 200–220]

**A.** Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i) requires Ameren to include in the grid plan “make-ready investments and other programs to facilitate the rapid deployment of charging equipment throughout this State, especially deployment that targets medium-duty and heavy-duty vehicle electrification and multi-unit buildings.” 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(M)(i). Under Ameren’s previous two beneficial electrification (“BE”) plans, the EV Act required the Company to include make-ready investments as well.[^1] While Ameren initially proposed utility-side only make-ready investments in its BE Plan 1, the Commission did not agree that was sufficient and instead ordered Ameren to expand the program to also cover some of the customer-side make ready investments. Ameren Ill. Co., ICC Final Order, Docket Nos. 22-0431/22-0443, 58 (March 23, 2023). In Ameren’s testimony in this case, Ameren again proposes only to cover the utility-side make-ready and exclude customer-side make-ready from the grid plan. Ameren witness Sackett explained that make-ready investments are treated as Category 2 investments and addressed through the Company’s existing line extension policy, which is outside the scope of the BE program budgets. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 16.) Category 2 investments do not require a BCA because they are “required” investments. Because Ameren consider its utility-side make ready program required, the Company provided no information in direct regarding the estimated costs, expected level of participation or projected benefits associated with its proposed make-ready approach for the 2029-2031 plan years. While Ameren argued that the utility side make-ready investments should be treated as required and thus classified as a Category 2 project that does not require a BCA, Ameren employed a different standard for customer-side make-ready. Ameren suggests the customer-side investments required a BCA and subsequently excluded customer-side programs because they had a negative BCA. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 13.)

[p. 11, lines 221–243] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

[^1]: For comparison, the EV Act language is as follows and is slightly different to the CRGA language: “(i) make-ready investments to facilitate the rapid deployment of charging equipment throughout the State, facilitate the electrification of public transit and other vehicle fleets in the light-duty, medium-duty, and heavy-duty sectors, and align with Agency-issued rebates for charging equipment.” 20 ILCS 627/45(d)(i).

**Q.** Please summarize the recommendations from Direct Testimony with respect to make-ready investment programs.

**A.** Staff recommended Ameren develop a plan specifically for make-ready investments which demonstrates how Ameren’s existing line extension policy will support facilitation of EV infrastructure deployment post-2028 BE period to comply with CRGA. (Staff Ex. 1.0, 20.) This should include, at a minimum, reasonable estimates of the scale of EV deployment, such as the number of customers expected to utilize make-ready infrastructure and level of investment. Id. Staff also requested Ameren develop and clearly describe its education and outreach strategy for its new make-ready investments program. (Staff Ex. 1.0, 20.)

**Q.** How did Ameren respond to Staff’s request for standalone make-ready program in this grid plan?

**A.** Ameren stated that it does not agree with Staff that the Company should have a separate standalone make-ready program. Ameren stated that while I am not an attorney, my understanding is that CRGA does not require the Company to provide a standalone make-ready program. Rather, CRGA requires that Company provide, in the 2028 Grid Plan, a description of efforts the utility is taking to support transportation electrification through make-ready investments and other programs. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 5.)

[p. 12, lines 244–265] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

Ameren stated that the reason it is not moving forward with the make-ready program like it proposed in the BE Plan 2 is because the program failed the cost effectiveness test. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 6.) Ameren stated that it lacked quantifiable benefits associated with providing a customer-side make-ready program. Ameren stated it concluded that it will not have quantifiable benefits that exceeds the cost of expanding the program in the net benefit analysis. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 6.) With regard to Staff’s request for Ameren to develop and clearly describe its education and outreach strategy for its new make-ready investments program, Ameren stated that it is not able to provide the information Staff requested because this information will be available only if the Company has a standalone make-ready program, which it does not have. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 6.)

**Q.** How do you respond to Ameren?

**A.** As discussed further below, Staff recommends that Ameren provide a customer side make ready program. With the addition of a customer side make ready program, Ameren will have a standalone make ready program and should be able to provide the requested information in surrebuttal, which I request Ameren do. However, if Ameren does not provide this information in surrebuttal, I recommend that the Commission direct Ameren to include a customer side make ready program and Staff’s requested information in a compliance grid plan filing. Ameren states it does not want to proceed with customer side make ready because it will not be cost effective. If Ameren argues it need not show a Benefit Cost Analysis (“BCA”) for its existing utility side make-ready investments because it classifies those investments as required, it is unclear why a different standard applies for customer-side investments, leading Ameren to say it will not cover the customer side because those investments fail a BCA. Ameren did not provide the distinction between the customer side make ready and the utility side make ready that it relied on to classify utility side make ready as required and rather expects a BCA for customer side make ready before including it in this grid plan. I believe the customer side make ready falls into Ameren’s Category 2 cost-effectiveness categorization and a formal BCA is not required.

[p. 13, lines 266–288] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

### B. Response to JNGO

**Q.** Please summarize the recommendations JNGO EPA made in their Direct Testimony with respect to Make-Ready Investment Programs.

**A.** In Direct testimony, JNGO shared similar opinions as Staff about the Company’s failure to support customer-side make-ready investments and disagreed with Ameren’s claim of compliance with the make-ready provision in CRGA. JNGO recommended that Ameren cover both customer-side and utility-side infrastructure costs in compliance with CRGA’s definition of make-ready infrastructure. (JNGO Ex. 6.0, 8.)

**Q.** How did the Company respond to JNGO?

**A.** Ameren opined that CRGA does not require the Company to cover 100% of both customer and utility-side costs. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 14.) To be clear, Staff is not advocating for Ameren to cover 100% of the customer and utility-side make-ready cost. Rather, Ameren should provide incentives to cover some portion of the customer-side make-ready cost. Ameren stated it made this decision considering the requirement from CRGA which requires BE programs and activities to be cost effective, noting that providing a make-ready program that covers customer side infrastructure will not be cost effective. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 14.) Ameren states that it focused on the utility side of the meter infrastructure to comply with CRGA’s requirement under Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i) while also satisfying CRGA’s requirement that BE programs be cost effective. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 14.)

[p. 14, lines 289–313] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

**Q.** Do you have any concerns about Ameren’s current utility side make-ready program?

**A.** Yes. I support reinstating customer side make-ready program as offered by Ameren in its BE plan 1 and 2 rather than only covering utility-side make-ready. Ameren provided both utility-side and customer-side make-ready investments to comply with the EV Act in BE plan 2. Ameren Ill. Co. d/b/a Ameren Ill., ICC Final Order, Docket Nos. 24-0494/24-0578 (Consol.) 47 (March 27, 2025) (“BE Plan 2 Final Order”). Ameren stated that The BE Plan 2 includes supplemental line extension credits and rebates for charging equipment installation for various customer types that deploy light, medium, and/or heavy-duty fleets. The BE Plan 2 offers a Fleet Assessment Program to educate, provide an economic assessment, and provide technical support to our customers looking to electrify their fleets. The Trade Ally Program will also provide a vetted list of qualified electrification experts to support fleet electrification. Ameren Illinois Company d/b/a Ameren Illinois, Docket No. 24-0494/24-0578 (Consol.), Ameren Ex. 2.0, 13 (July 1, 2024).

[p. 15, lines 314–335] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

In this grid plan, Ameren is proposing only utility side make-ready, compared to both utility and customer side make-ready provided in its BE Plan to comply with requirements for make-ready investments. CRGA asked for “make-ready investments and other programs to facilitate the rapid deployment of charging equipment throughout this State.” 220 ILCS 6/16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i). The provision did not put a limit on which side of the meter make-ready infrastructure should be incentivized by the Company and it is not clear to me why the Company decided to limit its definition of make-ready to utility side other than its citing of cost-effectiveness concerns. The statute only states “other programs”. While I am not a lawyer, I understand “other programs” as used in this context to be expansive rather than restrictive. I do not think the provision requires Ameren to limit make-ready programs to only utility side and remove customer side make-ready programs. Ameren explained that, There is no separately allocated specific budget for utility-side make-ready infrastructure related to EV charging projects within the 2029-2031 Grid Plan period. Instead, these projects are treated as standard new business investments and therefore utilize Ameren’s standard line extension policy.

(Staff Ex. 1.01, 1.) Ameren is proposing to comply with CRGA with only utility side make-ready investments.

[p. 16, lines 336–358]

### C. Response to IEPA

**Q.** Please summarize the recommendations IEPA made in its Direct Testimony with respect to Make-Ready Investment Programs.

IEPA similarly raises concerns with Ameren’s lack of a customer-side make-ready program. IEPA recommended that Ameren dedicate at least $20 million a year for commercial make-ready programs and $5 million a year to residential make-ready programs which should include both multi-unit and single-family homes. (IEPA Ex. 1.0, 15.) IEPA stated that its budget is prudent because of the high cost of enabling make ready infrastructure and a minimum of $20 million budget for customer side make ready will support a limited number of projects and be inadequate to meet anticipated demand. Id. at 16.

**Q.** How do you respond to IEPA?

**A.** With regard to IEPA’s request that Ameren allocate $20 million a year for commercial make-ready programs and $5 million a year to residential make-ready programs, for a total of $25 million per year for make-ready investments, I have a couple concerns. First, to the extent the Commission interprets the 1% rate impact statutory cap on EV infrastructure to still be in effect (see 20 ILCS 627/45(g)), then the IEPA proposed amount may be above the cap. While I do not have estimates of what the cap is forecasted to be for the years covered by the grid plan (though I request Ameren to provide this detail to the Commission in its surrebuttal), I note that the 1% rate impact cap was $23.7 million in 2025, and the IEPA proposal is above that amount. (ICC Docket Nos. 26-0214/26-0362, Staff Ex. 4.0, 13 (June 16, 2026)). Second, $25 million far exceeds the historical demand for EV infrastructure spend in Ameren’s programs. The actual EV infrastructure spend in Ameren’s territory was only $6.37 million in 2025, which is far below the amount being proposed by IEPA. Id. at 12. Thus, I do not feel that the $25 million has adequate support in the record.

[p. 17, lines 359–381] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

**Q.** How does Ameren respond to IEPA?

**A.** Ameren states that IEPA made this recommendation without considering cost effectiveness and that IEPA’s recommendation needs to be cost effective in order to be included in Ameren’s grid plan. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 16.) Ameren also states that IEPA’s recommendation will likely increase non-incentive costs to support customer side make-ready, which IEPA’s recommendation does not consider. Id. Ameren states it performed a sensitivity analysis for a customer side make ready total budget of $22 million in response to IEPA’s proposed $25 million a year budget, which would total $75 million in three years (2029-2031.) Ameren notes its proposed total budget of $22 million from its sensitivity analysis over 3 years (far less than the IEPA proposes), is expected to produce a net loss of about $7.4 million with a benefit cost ratio (“BCR”) of 0.68. Id. at 17. The proposed budget of $22 million from Ameren’s sensitivity analysis would include incentives for both utility and customer side make-ready, similar to what Ameren has in its BE Plan 2 ChargeReady program. Id. at 17. According to Ameren, the additional budget will cover incentives for Residential, Multifamily, Education Facility, Transit Facility, Public Charging, Facility, and Fleet Facility customers. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 17; Ameren Ex. 19.0, 27.) Ameren added that, if the Commission were to direct them to include the customer side incentives in its grid plan, Ameren reserves the right to recover the cost of this investment. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 18.) Specifically, Ameren states that, [i]f, however, the Commission were to direct the Company to include such investments in the 2028 Grid Plan, the Company reserves its right to seek cost-recovery of these investments in the appropriate forum, and the Company would request the Commission to approve cost-recovery consistent with the Public Utilities Act.

[p. 18, lines 382–405] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

(Ameren Ex. 23.0, 18.)

**Q.** What are your thoughts on Ameren’s sensitivity analysis for the customer side make-ready program?

**A.** Ameren performed a sensitivity analysis for a three-year (2029-2031) total budget of $22 million to assess the cost effectiveness of having a customer side make-ready investment. For the three years, Ameren’s budget comes to an average of $7.3 million a year. I support the budgeted amount of $22 million over the 3-year period because that aligns with actual historical make-ready costs (with an allowance for escalation). In order to evaluate the reasonableness of the $22 million budget, I used Ameren’s historical EV infrastructure spend to calculate the actual spend for 2025 which is $6,373,554 and what the estimated costs for 2029-2031 will be with an escalation rate of 2.5%. The results showed $7,035,211 for 2029, $7,211,091 for 2030 and $7,391,368 for 2031 which comes to a total of $21,637,670. This aligns with what Ameren has for its customer side make ready budget which I think is reasonable for the Commission to adopt.

However the sensitivity analysis showed a net loss of about $7 million and a BCR of 0.68. (Ameren Ex. 23.0, 17; Ameren Ex. 19.0, 27.)
