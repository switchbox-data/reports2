Attachment LI-NG-1-8-1 does not explicitly show estimated monthly bills by electric heat technology, because bills do not vary by customer end use technology. (b) Please see Attachment LI-NG-1-8-2, which shows average usage for each of the last 36 months (January 2021 through December 2023) for: (i) Rate R-1 customers not enrolled in the Company's air source heat pump program;[^1] (ii) Rate R-1 customers who enrolled in the Company's air source heat pump program between 2019 and 2020; (iii) Rate R-2 customers not enrolled in the Company's air source heat pump program;[^2] and (iv) Rate R-2 customers who enrolled in the Company's air source heart [sic] pump program between 2019 and 2020. Average monthly usage data for these customer categories is not available for 2020. The Company does not track customers using electric resistance heat and, therefore, cannot provide average monthly usage data for this group.

(c) The proposed Electrification Pricing option is intended to alleviate the uneconomic effects of per kWh delivery rates on residential customer bills. Under the current default Rate R-1 delivery rate, customers face an incremental base distribution charge for every kWh of usage regardless of whether the incremental usage is associated with incremental contribution to distribution system costs. The proposed Electrification Pricing option serves to address this problem by effectively providing a volumetric discount where savings relative to the default rate increase with each incremental kWh of usage. Because the Rate R-2 discount is a percentage of the total bill, it provides the same effect of savings increasing with each incremental kWh of usage, thereby reducing the cost per kWh of usage.

For example, under the proposed default Rate R-1, a customer who electrifies and increases their average monthly usage from 600 kWh to 1,100 kWh would pay $0.35 per each kWh of additional usage, or $175 more per month. The Electrification Pricing option would reduce the cost of electrification to $0.28 per kWh, or $142 per month. The Rate R-2 discount at the 32 percent discount level reduces the customer's cost to electrify to $0.24 per kWh, or $119 per month, and to $0.16 per kWh, or $79 per month, at the 55 percent discount level. Please see Attachment LI-NG-1-8-1, Sheet 1, cells A46:E51, for the calculations supporting this example.

(d) The Company's long-term rate design strategy seeks to move toward more cost-reflective, fair, and grid beneficial rate designs. Because utility system costs do not

<!-- PDF page 3 (printed "Page 3 of 4") -->

vary by customer end use technology, technology-specific rate design would represent movement away from, rather than toward, more cost-reflective rate design. Further, as noted in the response to part (c), above, the proposed Electrification Pricing option is designed to begin to address the problem of volumetric delivery rates, resulting in customers with high per kWh consumption and low demands overpaying relative to their contribution to delivery system costs. Although the Commonwealth's electrification policy goals enhance the urgency of addressing this inefficiency as it pertains to heat electrification, all customers with these load profile characteristics are unfairly affected under the current Rate R-1 delivery rate design, regardless of their specific end uses.

As a practical matter, technology-specific rates may cause confusion for customers with multiple technologies impacting their usage profile, especially if the Company were to offer multiple pricing options each designed for specific end uses. In addition, technology-specific rates are complex and costly to implement, requiring verification of eligible technologies and submetering of appliances. This would also add to the administrative burden for customers and may prevent some who would benefit from opting in.

(e) Yes, the Electrification Pricing option is proposed to be available to all Rate R-1 customers. Electrification Pricing technically would be economic for all customers with usage higher than the residential class average. However, customers with usage levels just slightly higher than the class average would receive savings of only a few dollars or less per month, which for some customers may not justify opting in if there is a risk their usage may fall below the class average.

(f) Yes, Electrification Pricing is proposed as a technology-neutral pricing option and, therefore, would be available to customers using electric resistance heating. As described above in the responses parts (c) and (d), above, the Company's proposed Electrification Pricing option supports the Commonwealth's electrification goals by serving as a first step toward addressing the uneconomic impact of per kWh delivery rates on the cost to operate heat electrification technologies.

**Revised Response:**

Please see Attachment LI-NG-1-8-1-Revised. This attachment has been revised as follows:

- On Sheet 1, row 28, Rate R-1/R-2 annual kWh deliveries has been added to calculate the Rate R-1/R-2 revenue shift on a per kWh basis in row 47.
- On Sheet 2, the formulas in columns D-I, N-P, and U-W, which contain the Electrification Pricing bill impact calculations for Rate R-1 participating and non-participating customers; Rate R-2 customers at the 32 percent discount level; and Rate R-3 [sic; possibly intended "R-2"] customers at the 55 percent discount level, respectively, have been revised to correctly capture the impacts of the Revenue Decoupling Mechanism ("RDM") revenue shift on these customers.
