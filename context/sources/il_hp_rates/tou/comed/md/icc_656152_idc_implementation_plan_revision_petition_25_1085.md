## VERIFIED PETITION FOR APPROVAL OF A WAIVER AND REVISION TO INTEGRATED DISTRIBUTION COMPANY IMPLEMENTATION PLAN

Commonwealth Edison Company ("ComEd"), pursuant to 83 Ill. Admin. Code Part 452, Subpart B ("IDC Rules") and Sections 10-101 and 10-113 of the Illinois Public Utilities Act ("PUA"), 220 ILCS 5/10-101 and 5/10-113, files this Petition requesting the Illinois Commerce Commission ("ICC" or "Commission") approve a waiver and revision to ComEd's Integrated Distribution Company ("IDC") Implementation Plan and allowing ComEd to promote, advertise and market its residential supply time of use ("STOU") program. In support of this Petition, ComEd states as follows:

## I. Introduction and Background on Rate BEST – Basic Electric Service Time-of-Use Pricing

1. The IDC Rules are rules utilities must follow if they elect to structure their business as an IDC, rather than a Functionally Separated Utility. At a high level, an IDC is a utility that does not actively participate in the retail electric supply market and is prohibited from attempting to obtain or retain electric supply customers. See generally 83 Ill. Admin. Code Part 452, Subpart B §§ 452.200 et seq. The IDC Rules require electric utilities to file written plans describing the systems, policies and practices the electric utility will use to implement the IDC Rules. See 83 Ill. Admin. Code § 452.220.

[p. 2]

2. ComEd has previously elected to be an IDC. ComEd filed its Implementation Plan with the Commission on May 24, 2002. The Commission approved that Implementation Plan by Order in ICC Docket No. 02-0379. Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket No. 02-0379, Order Approving Plan (July 2, 2002).

<!-- EPON: goal — stakeholder (consumer, environmental, public-interest) request that ComEd use AMI to offer a tariff encouraging customers to shift usage from peak to off-peak -->

3. Beginning with the initial installation of advanced metering infrastructure ("AMI"), which enables granular tracking of energy usage, stakeholders (including consumer advocates, environmental organizations, and other public interest groups) have requested that ComEd utilize its AMI network to develop and implement a tariff with rates that encourage customers to shift usage from high demand, peak periods to lower demand, off peak periods.

<!-- EPON: bundling,goal — history of Rate BEST: CUB-EDF requested a residential supply TOU in the 24-0378 RDI; on rehearing the Commission authorized a *bundled* supply and delivery residential TOU named Rate BEST; compliance filing Aug. 22, 2025, effective with June 2026 monthly billing period; Commission directed ComEd to file an IDC Plan modification once marketing materials are developed -->

4. Most recently, in ComEd's 2024 Rate Design Investigation ("RDI") proceeding (ICC Docket No. 24-0378), the Citizens Utility Board and Environmental Defense Fund encouraged, and the Commission agreed, that ComEd should investigate a residential supply time-of use ("TOU") rate. See Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket No. 24-0378, Order (Jan. 16, 2025) at 189-190. On Rehearing, ComEd requested, and the Commission granted, authorization for ComEd to make a compliance filing implementing a bundled supply and delivery residential TOU rate that ComEd named Rate BEST – Basic Electric Service Time of Use Pricing ("Rate BEST"). See Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket No. 24-0378, Order on Rehearing ("RDI Order on Rehearing") (July 17, 2025) at 36. ComEd made its compliance filing that included tariff sheets implementing Rate BEST on August 22, 2025, effective with the June 2026 monthly billing period. See Rate BEST, ILL. C.C. No. 10, 1st Revised Sheet No. 483 et seq. The Commission also directed ComEd to "file for a modification to its IDC Implementation Plan, or in the alternative seek clarification that no modification is necessary once it has developed these marketing materials [for Rate BEST] so

[p. 3]

that the Commission can conduct a proper evaluation." RDI Order on Rehearing at 38, 47. ComEd is filing this Verified Petition in compliance with that directive.

<!-- EPON: goal — statutory/policy framing: CEJA contemplates utility STOU rates; CRGA (SB 0025, enrolled Oct. 30, 2025) would mandate that utilities offer TOU rates (new 220 ILCS 5/16-107.8) and explicitly authorizes utilities to market/promote them (16-119A(e)); CRGA effective June 1, 2026 -->

5. The Illinois General Assembly has recognized the importance of time-of-use rates, such as Rate BEST, that encourage customers to shift their usage by requiring ComEd to offer both real-time pricing (220 ILCS 5/16-107) and peak-time rebate (220 ILCS 5/16-108.6(g)) programs to all residential customers. In the same vein, the Commission has concluded that P.A. 102-0662, the Climate and Equitable Jobs Act, directly contemplates regulated utilities, such as ComEd, offering STOU rates. RDI Order on Rehearing at 42. Finally, the Clean and Reliable Grid Affordability Act, SB 0025 ("CRGA"), enrolled on October 30, 2025,[^1] would mandate that utilities offer time-of-use rates. See SB 0025 Enrolled, Section 90-35 (adding Section 220 ILCS 5/16-107.8 to the PUA). CRGA contains an explicit grant of authority for utilities to market and promote their TOU rates. See SB 0025 Enrolled, Section 90-35 (codifying revisions to Section 16-119A(e) of the PUA). This provision shows that the General Assembly intends for utilities, like ComEd, to not only offer TOU rates, but market them.

<!-- EPON: goal,bundling,midday-peak — ComEd's description of Rate BEST: four-period bundled supply and delivery TOU (morning, mid-day peak, evening, overnight); purpose is to shift load from high-demand/high-price hours to low-demand hours; customer who shifts lowers both supply and delivery costs (the profile-typical customer is assumed able to shift) -->

6. As approved by the Commission, Rate BEST provides for a four-period bundled supply and delivery time-of-use rate with price differentiation between morning, mid-day peak, evening, and overnight periods. The purpose of Rate BEST is to encourage customers to shift load from high demand hours when energy prices may be higher to low demand hours when energy prices are lower. A customer on this rate that is able to shift their energy usage out of the higher priced periods will lower their own supply and delivery costs.

[p. 4]

<!-- EPON: ev-hp,goal — Rate BEST explicitly pitched at EV charging: intended to encourage off-peak EV charging, lower supply costs and reduce system demand, possibly avoid/delay distribution investment related to EV growth, and lower cost of EV ownership. No mention of heat pumps or other electrification loads -->

7. Rate BEST also supports the public interest because it is intended to encourage electric vehicle ("EV") charging during the off-peak periods in order to lower supply costs and reduce system demand. In addition to lowering the cost of EV ownership, this may also allow certain distribution system investments related to EV growth to be avoided or delayed. It will also increase EV owners' satisfaction by offering an additional pricing product in which the customer has the opportunity to lower their operating costs if charging is done during periods when energy pricing is the lowest.

## II. Rate BEST Is a Permissible IDC Service and ComEd's Customer Education Campaign Is Consistent with the IDC Rules

<!-- EPON: bill-comparison,midday-peak,lockout — the customer education materials (Attachment A, not in this PDF) will explain variable TOU ("Time-of-Day"/"TOD") pricing, the current pricing periods and their relative prices, potential benefits, enrollment eligibility, FAQs, and "how it might impact them individually"; success depends on customer enrollment -->

8. The success of Rate BEST in providing customers an opportunity to reduce their electricity costs and shift electricity usage is dependent on customer enrollment in the Rate. This will require a customer education campaign to provide customers with materials that inform them of the existence of Rate BEST, how it works, how they might benefit from enrolling, and how it might impact them individually. Attached to this Petition as Attachment A are illustrative copies of ComEd's proposed customer education materials. These materials explain the variable nature of TOU pricing (identified in the education materials as "Time-of-Day" or "TOD" pricing) and the current pricing periods on their relative prices compared to each other, potential benefits to customers from the rate's pricing, the customer's enrollment eligibility, and answers to other frequently asked questions. See Illustrative Rate BEST Customer Education Materials, Attachment A.

9. ComEd's ability to effectively engage in the type of education campaign that is needed to make Rate BEST successful necessitates a grant of waiver from certain provisions of the IDC rules. The IDC Rules prohibit IDCs, such as ComEd, from promoting, advertising and marketing any Retail Electric Supply Service, which is defined simply as "the retail sale of

[p. 5]

electricity, whether bundled or unbundled." 83 Ill. Admin. Code § 452.200. However, the IDC Rules permit ComEd to promote permissible IDC services[^2] ("Permissible IDC Services") other than retail electric supply services and to engage in legitimate customer education efforts subject to Commission approval of revisions to the utility's IDC Implementation Plan. Therefore, through this Petition, ComEd seeks a finding that Rate BEST is to be considered and treated as a Permissible IDC Service.

## III. ComEd's Proposed Revisions to Its IDC Implementation Plan Should Be Approved

10. ComEd proposes to revise its IDC Implementation Plan (as is permitted by Section 452.220(f) of the IDC Rules) to make clear that one of the practices that ComEd will be allowed to engage in are the promotion, advertising and marketing of Rate BEST. ComEd's proposed revisions to its IDC Implementation Plan are attached to this Petition as Attachment B and marked in track changes. A clean version of the revised section is also attached to this Petition as Attachment C. Customer education regarding Rate BEST is consistent with the IDC Rules and promotes the public interest.

<!-- EPON: goal,midday-peak — ComEd's argument that Rate BEST is a demand-response program like Peak Time Savings: customers curtail or delay usage in response to price signals; part of the purpose is to reduce peak demand -->

11. The IDC Rules permit the marketing of Permissible IDC Services other than retail electric supply service (see Section 452.200 and Section 452.240), which include load curtailment and interruption programs. Rate BEST contains many of the same elements and value-drivers as a load curtailment and interruption program, such as ComEd's Peak Time Savings ("PTS") program. Fundamentally, both Rate BEST and PTS are demand-response programs in which customers may curtail or delay their energy usage in response to economic incentives or price signals. In addition, a portion of the value and purpose of TOU rates and other load curtailment and interruption programs is to reduce peak demand.

[p. 6]

Thus, Rate BEST is conceptually similar to ComEd's existing load curtailment and interruption tariffs and should be considered a Permissible IDC Service.

<!-- EPON: goal,bill-comparison — consumer-education rationale: educate the customer on their individual consumption patterns, appliance usage characteristics, cost of power and energy, and supply/demand interplay; claims Rate BEST "will assist in educating all customers about ways to manage their energy costs" -->

12. Similarly, the IDC Rules permit "legitimate consumer education efforts." 83 Ill. Admin. Code § 452.240(b)(4). Consumer education is a key element and value-driver of Rate BEST. As described above, an education campaign will be needed to bring awareness of this new rate to the attention of customers and help them decide whether to enroll. In addition, a major focus of Rate BEST is to educate the customer as to the customer's individual consumption patterns, the usage characteristics of various appliances, the cost of power and energy, and the interplay of supply and demand in the energy markets. As such, Rate BEST will assist in educating all customers about ways to manage their energy costs.

<!-- EPON: goal,midday-peak — public-interest argument: more enrollment means more individual savings and collectively reduced system demand for ComEd's residential delivery classes, deferring or reducing distribution investment -->

13. It is also in the public interest to allow for customer education of Rate BEST because the more customers who enroll in Rate BEST, the greater potential there is for individual customer savings. Collectively, customers engaging in this behavior could reduce the overall system demand for ComEd's residential delivery classes, in turn deferring or reducing the need for distribution system investments that would have otherwise been made to meet demand.

## IV. The Commission Should Grant ComEd's Petition for a Waiver

14. While ComEd's customer education campaign is consistent with the IDC Rules, the Commission has previously granted waivers from the IDC Rules for the purposes of marketing and promotion of products that, like Rate BEST, provide benefits related to demand-side resources. In ICC Docket Nos. 08-0411 and 11-0546, ComEd sought a waiver from the IDC rules so that it could market, promote and advertise its RRTP. In both instances, the Commission approved the waiver as reasonable and appropriate. See Commonwealth Edison

[p. 7]

Co., ICC Docket No. 08-0411, Order (Oct. 8, 2008) at 3-4; Ill. Commerce Comm'n v. Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket No. 11-0546, Order (May 29, 2012) at 7-8.

Similarly, in ICC Docket No. 09-0263, ComEd sought a waiver to market some novel rate options as a part of its AMI Pilot program. All parties recognized the need for ComEd to be able to market those programs in order for them to enroll customers for a successful pilot and the Commission ultimately approved the waiver request. See Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket No. 09-0263, Order (Oct. 14, 2009) at 44-46.

Finally, ComEd proposed and received a waiver to market its Rate RTOUPP – Residential Time of Use Pricing Pilot program, which was an STOU pilot program that enabled ComEd to better inform its design of Rate BEST. See Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket Nos. 18-1725 / 18-1824 (cons.), Order (Oct. 2, 2019) at 54-55.

<!-- EPON: lockout,bundling — marketing scope and switching: no marketing of Rate BEST to customers on a RES or ComEd's hourly supply rate (primarily marketed to bundled flat-rate customers); ComEd makes no effort to prevent customers from signing up with or switching to RES service; Commission found RESs not harmed by a bundled STOU -->

15. No party will be adversely affected by the granting this Petition. In particular, Retail Electric Suppliers ("RESs") will not be adversely affected. The Commission determined in approving Rate BEST that RESs would not be harmed by ComEd offering a bundled STOU rate. RDI Order on Rehearing at 46. ComEd will not market Rate BEST to its customers who take supply service from a RES or from ComEd's hourly supply rate. See Id. at 16 (noting that ComEd's position was that it planned to primarily market Rate BEST to its bundled flat rate customers and not its customers taking supply service from a RES or under ComEd's hourly pricing). As ComEd currently does with RRTP, ComEd commits that it will make no effort to prevent customers from signing up with or switching to RES service.

[p. 8]

16. ComEd will be able to market Rate BEST without a waiver beginning on June 1, 2026, CRGA's effective date. There is thus no harm in the Commission granting ComEd's Petition for a waiver until such time as the statutory grant of authority becomes effective.

<!-- EPON: bill-comparison — stakeholder review of illustrative education materials; ComEd's approach uses multiple formats incl. its bill comparison tool (see fn 3); no stakeholder comments received -->

17. Consistent with the Order on Rehearing issued in ICC Docket No. 24-0378, ComEd provided parties, including Staff of the Commission, the Office of the Illinois Attorney General, Citizens Utility Board, Environmental Defense Fund, the Environmental Law and Policy Center, Vote Solar, Union of Concerned Scientists, Illinois Competitive Energy Association, and Retail Energy Supply Association with the opportunity to review and comment on its proposed illustrative customer education materials, copies of which are attached to this Petition as Attachment A.[^3] See RDI Order on Rehearing at 42. ComEd received no comments on the materials contained within Attachment A that it presented to stakeholders.

18. The Commission has ample authority to grant a waiver from those rules and, as discussed, has previously done so in both the RRTP, AMI Pilot, and RTOUPP matters.

WHEREFORE, ComEd respectfully requests that the Commission (1) finds that Rate BEST is to be considered and treated as a Permissible IDC Service; (2) approves ComEd's modified IDC Implementation Plan (provided as Attachment B (redline) and Attachment C (clean)) to allow ComEd to promote, advertise and market Rate BEST; and (3) grant ComEd's waiver of the IDC Rules so that it may market and promote Rate BEST.
