**Executive Summary**

**Program Participation and Impact Highlights - 2017:**

The second half and full year 2017 demonstrated the continuing strength of BGE's Smart Energy Savers Program®, the umbrella for programs supporting EmPOWER Maryland. Based on a solid foundation of the traditional residential and commercial energy efficiency and PeakRewards℠ programs and augmented by BGE's more recent innovative Smart Energy Rewards®, Smart Energy Manager®, and Conservation Voltage Reduction programs, BGE delivered significant energy savings and demand reductions to its customers' homes and businesses. The second half of 2017 also saw BGE beginning deployment of new, cutting edge Smart Thermostats across multiple EmPOWER platforms to equip BGE's customers with yet another tool for saving energy and reducing their energy costs.

BGE's traditional Residential and Commercial Energy Efficiency programs produced nearly 215,000 MWh annualized gross wholesale savings for the third and fourth quarters compared to the target of 177,000 MWh approved by the Commission (including Commission approved changes). Full year savings were over 370,000 MWh compared to a target of 351,000 MWh for the year. Residential program savings were 106,000 MWh for the second half and nearly 176,000 MWh for the full year representing 127% and 112% of targets, respectively. Commercial programs continued the trend of representing a greater portion of energy savings

[PDF p. 2 | printed p. 2]

representing 55% of combined traditional energy efficiency savings during 2017. For the second half of 2017, savings from Commercial programs were 109,000 MWh and 195,000 MWh for the full year representing 117% and 101% of the 2017 targets, respectively. Combined, Residential and Commercial savings for the full year were over 105% of the 2017 target.

Savings from Smart Energy Manager®, BGE's residential behavioral energy efficiency program, contributed energy savings of over 145,000 MWh (160% of target) for the year and the CVR or Conservation Voltage Reduction program savings added nearly 138,000 MWh (135% of target). Total energy savings from all programs for 2017, as illustrated below, were over 668,000 MWh. This equates to 2.05% of 2013 weather normalized gross wholesale retail sales.

As of December 31, 2017, the potential and installed capacity demand reduction from all BGE programs was 1,294 MW. Of this total, Energy Efficiency and PeakRewards℠ programs accounted for 511 MW and 357 MW, respectively and the Smart Energy Rewards®, BGE's peak time rebate program, contributed 330 MW with an additional 97 MW from other programs.

In addition to reducing electric usage, BGE's EmPOWER programs helped customers reduce natural gas usage. During 2017, BGE's traditional residential and commercial energy efficiency programs reduced gas usage by approximately 1.1 million therms. Smart Energy Manager®, BGE's residential behavioral energy efficiency program, reduced gas usage by approximately 2.6 million therms.

Participation in the traditional energy efficiency programs continued to grow, with nearly 126,000 participants in the second half of 2017 and 213,000 for the full year 2017. For the full three year cycle participation was 677,000 and over 1,997,000 Program-to-Date. (Note that a customer generally is counted as a unique participant every time they get an incentive or participate in a particular program. See **Appendix J: Notes and Assumptions** for how lighting is counted). More than 405,000 unique residential customers participated in BGE's energy efficiency Program-to-Date, excluding the Lighting Program where unique customer information cannot be collected. As of year-end 2017, PeakRewards℠ had a total of more than 345,000 A/C and over 22,000 Water Heater customers participating with a total of over 367,000 active devices in both programs.

**Residential Energy Efficiency**

BGE's Residential program achieved 124% of its overall portfolio gross wholesale MWh forecast for the second half of 2017.

- For the full year, BGE achieved just under 176,000 gross wholesale MWh achieving 112% of its forecasted target.
  - The lighting markdown program achieved over 131,000 MWh which reflects over 127,000 participants. Over 3 million energy efficient bulbs were sold in 2017.
  - The Quick Home Energy Checkup program achieved nearly 15,000 MWh of energy savings achieving 115% of the forecast. Over 36,000 customers participated in this program with over 462,000 energy efficient measures being installed during the year.
  - The residential new construction program continued its success achieving 135% of its forecasted MWh reflecting over 3,200 energy efficient homes constructed.
  - The Recycling Program achieved 8,700 MWh which reflects just under 90% of the forecasted energy savings for 2017.

**Commercial and Industrial (C&I) Energy Efficiency**

The C&I portfolio had an outstanding year with most of its major C&I programs exceeding their forecast.

- During the second half of 2017, the C&I portfolio yielded 109,000 MWh compared to its forecast of 93,000 MWh achieving 117% of its second half forecast.
- For the full year, the C&I portfolio delivered 194,000 MWh in energy savings and achieving 101% of its 2017 forecast.
  - The Energy Solutions for Business (ESfB) Program continued to be a strong contributor towards BGE's C&I portfolio with over 92,000 MWh or 119% of its annual energy savings forecast.
  - BGE had two CHP projects which went online and started generating savings in 2017. The 2 MW projects at Baltimore Washington Medical Center and the University of Maryland St. Joseph's Medical Center provided over 25,000 MWh.
  - BGE's Commercial Midstream Lighting (Instant Lighting Discounts Program) contributed over 45,000 MWh and achieving 158% of its 2017 forecast.
  - The Building Tune-up and Building Operators Training programs greatly accelerated in the second half of the year to deliver over 13,000 MWh or 218% of the annual energy saving forecast.

[PDF p. 4 | printed p. 4]

**PeakRewards℠ Program**

BGE PeakRewards℠, BGE's residential demand response program, continued to operate smoothly with more than 312,000 Air Conditioning and 22,000 Water Heater customers receiving credits.

Coincident with the planning for the 2018 – 2020 three-year cycle, BGE revised the per device point estimate for PeakRewards℠ devices using data available through AMI meters. This resulted in an overall reduction in the estimated demand reduction available from these devices of approximately 40 MW.

**BGE Smart Energy Rewards®**

BGE Smart Energy Rewards®, BGE's residential behavioral peak time rebate program, included over one million customers with smart meters for the summer 2017 season. The program is designed to encourage customers to use less electricity during summer peak demand periods called Energy Savings Days (ESDs). On two separate occasions throughout the summer, BGE notified customers by email, text or phone call of an upcoming ESD. Customers earned $1.25 in bill credits for every kWh reduced, compared to their baseline.

**BGE Smart Energy Manager® (SEM):**

Smart Energy Manager® (SEM), BGE's residential behavioral energy efficiency program, has over 920,000 customers enrolled and over 14.8 million Home Energy Reports have been sent to customers since program inception.

**Conservation Voltage Reduction (CVR):**

Through the end of 2017 BGE has deployed CVR technology on 494 circuits. Savings from the CVR program deployments amounted to over 137,000 MWh for 2017. These savings are based on circuits deployed through 2017 based on evaluated reductions through the end of 2016. Since the beginning of CVR deployment, this program has provided reductions to BGE residential, commercial and industrial customers of nearly 331,000 MWh.

**Enhancements**

- Residential Energy Efficiency program enhancements
  - The HPwES Program instituted a significant overhaul in the incentive structure for the second half of 2017. **BGE's Smart Thermostat Programs:**

Per Order No. 88514 dated December 22, 2017, the information below responds to ordering paragraph (16); "That each Utility offering smart thermostats through multiple programs are directed to describe the interaction of the measures with the Utility's demand response portfolio as part of the forthcoming January 31, 2018 reports."

During 2017, BGE began offering new, innovative smart thermostats across multiple EmPOWER programs as another tool to help its customers achieve additional energy savings. The programs included PeakRewards℠ (direct install of a smart thermostat as part of participation), Quick Home Energy Check-up (cross-selling the PeakRewards℠ program and simultaneous customer delivery of both programs) and the Residential New Construction Program (installation of a smart thermostat in addition to the ENERGY STAR energy efficiency requirements).

Beginning in 2018 and as approved by the Commission through Order No. 88514, BGE will expand our 2017 offers to include multiple smart thermostat program options for our customers. There are four residential options and one small business option.

For residential customers electing to participate in PeakRewards℠, BGE offers an installed smart thermostat at no additional cost. For customers who are not participating in PeakRewards℠, beginning in 2018, BGE is providing financial incentives to residential customers who purchase eligible smart thermostats. Whether obtained as part of BGE's PeakRewards℠ program or purchased as part of the Smart Thermostat Program, residential customers can recognize additional energy savings by replacing a manually operated or conventional programmable thermostat. In addition, all qualified thermostats are eligible to enroll in the optimization tract, providing customer's additional energy savings.

In 2018 BGE's Small Business Energy Solutions Program added smart thermostats as an eligible measure. Smart thermostats capabilities make them ideal for small business energy efficiency applications.

**PBI True Up Evaluation Update:**

- Commission Order 88007 approved the Work Group's request to transition the Home Performance with ENERGY STAR (HPwES) program to a performance-based incentive structure. The Order specified that the program's "actual energy savings should be tracked in real time, or at a minimum on regular intervals, and should not rely solely on year-end data for impact evaluations." In addition, the Order stated that the utilities must "provide quarterly true-up reports and rely on best practices and normalization methods when evaluating the modeled versus measured energy savings that result from HPwES installations."

[PDF p. 30 | printed p. 17]

- The evaluation team will analyze 12 months of pre- and 12 months of post-measure utility billing data to evaluate energy savings generated by the program. The team will evaluate energy savings quarterly using a multifaceted approach that will inform utilities and implementers of any issues with ex-ante savings estimates. The team has implemented the following enhancements to the core billing analysis to ensure program level ex-ante savings estimates are reasonable, identify contractors or projects with inaccurate savings estimates, and produce measure-level realization rates (once adequate sample sizes have been achieved).[^p30-1]
  - Work collaboratively with the utilities and program implementers to ensure that program tracking data is complete, of high quality, and readily transferrable to the evaluation team. Good data quality and transferability are essential to satisfying the Commission's request for faster feedback regarding the performance-based incentive structure.
  - Conduct due-diligence reviews of a sample of the program implementers' energy savings estimation models to ensure that project-specific data inputs and assumptions are reasonable. The team will conduct these reviews on a quarterly basis and will alert the utilities and program implementers immediately of any observed anomalies.
  - Use pre-installation billing data to calibrate savings for a sample of participating projects to ensure the modeled energy savings estimates are reasonable. The team will calibrate savings on a quarterly basis and will alert the utilities and program implementers immediately of any observed anomalies.
  - Leverage existing program- and project-level evaluation results to conduct additional QC checks on modeled energy savings. The team has compiled a repository of thousands of evaluated projects (i.e., homes that participated in the EmPOWER HPwES program) with site-specific billing data and can use the data to conduct reasonableness checks on modeled energy savings at the project and measure levels.
  - Conduct quarterly billing analyses of all participating projects with 12 months of post-installation billing data to develop evaluated energy savings and realization rates. The team will continue using existing evaluation methods, but on a more frequent basis, to develop evaluated results that the utilities and program implementers can use to provide quarterly true-up reports to the Commission and improve the accuracy of the implementers' energy savings estimation models.

[^p30-1]: The team has discussed the evaluation enhancements with the utilities, their HPwES program implementers, the Independent Evaluator, and the HPwES Work Group. These stakeholders agree that the enhancements will meet the reporting requirements specified in Order 88007.

[PDF p. 31 | printed p. 18]

- A key outcome of the evaluation enhancements discussed previously will be finer resolution evaluation results, produced on a quarterly basis, that the utilities and their program implementers can use to provide quarterly true-up reports to the Commission, improve the accuracy of the implementers' energy savings estimation models, and further optimize program performance.[^p31-2] The team has built effective working relationships with utility staff and HPwES program implementers and will leverage these relationships to ensure that evaluation findings are efficiently transferred to the utilities and their implementers.
- The evaluation team understands the nature of the Commission's request and the importance of providing faster feedback regarding modeled vs. actual energy savings estimates during the transition to the performance-based incentive structure. 2. Define what constitutes a participant and measure

   Following is a summary of Participants and Measures for BGE's Energy Efficiency Programs:

**Residential Energy Efficiency Programs**

**Residential Lighting Program**

Participant: A household that purchases discounted screw-in CFL or LED bulbs or fixtures from a participating retailer or who uses a coupon to purchase screw-in CFLs. This number is derived from the number of bulbs sold using the bulbs per household factor of 24 bulbs per household as used in BGE's August 12, 2012 filing.

Measure: CFLs and LEDs of various wattages

**Residential Appliance Rebate Program**

Participant: A residential customer who purchases a qualified appliance and submits a rebate application form. Note – if a customer purchases an appliance and submits a rebate application, that customer is counted as one participant. If that rebate application is for multiple appliances, that customer is still counted as a single participant. However, if later that same customer purchases another appliance and submits a new rebate application, they will be counted as another participant.

Measures: Qualified appliances under the program - Clothes Washer, Refrigerator, Room A/C, Water Heater, Freezer, Dehumidifier.

**Residential Appliance Recycling Program**

Participant: Residential Customer who requests removal of a qualified refrigerator, freezer or room A/C from home (the latter if already removing a refrigerator or freezer). Note – if a customer recycles an appliance and receives an incentive, that customer is counted as one participant. If that incentive is for multiple appliances, that customer is still counted as a single participant. However, if later that same customer recycles another appliance and receives an additional rebate, they will be counted as another participant.

Measure: Removal of refrigerator and/or freezer, and/or Room A/C

[PDF p. 121 | printed p. 66]

**Residential Quick Home Energy Check-Up (QHEC) Program**

Participant: A residential customer who has a walk-through of home by a check-up professional and accepts installation of at least 3 energy savings measures as recommended by professional.

Measure: CFLs of various wattages, low-flow showerheads, pipe insulation, faucet aerators, water heater tank wrap, water heater temperature turndown, smart strips

**Residential Home Performance with ENERGY STAR® (HPwES) Program**

Participant: A residential customer who receives a home energy audit by a Home Performance Contractor is counted under number of completed audits in the mini-table. A residential customer who installs energy efficiency improvements recommended by the above contractor is counted under completed projects when the job is finished.

Measure: Insulation, Duct Sealing, Air Sealing, CFL Installation, HVAC replacements or tune-ups, low-flow showerheads, faucet aerators, appliance replacements, and other energy efficient installations.

**Residential New Construction Program**

Single Family Housing:

Measure: New Multifamily low rise unit (Tier 1)
