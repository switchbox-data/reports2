Program Participation and Impact Highlights
Program Impacts
BGE’s EmPOWER Maryland (“EmPOWER”) portfolio of programs was successful in 2023.
Overall, in 2023, BGE’s EmPOWER program portfolio has exceeded its 2% goal legislatively
established for the EmPOWER programs for 2023. In 2023, the EmPOWER program portfolio
achieved 116% of the 2% annual MWh savings goal using only 80% of the overall 2023 program
budget. With regard to the MWh savings targets established in the Company’s 2021-2023
EmPOWER cycle filing, the 2023 portfolio achieved 97% of the 2023 filed target in 2023. This
places the portfolio in the “On Target” category for 2023. The entire portfolio achieved over
745,000 MWh in annualized electric energy savings. Natural gas savings 2023 were over 2.6
million therms. In addition to the energy savings realized and associated environmental benefits
customers generated by choosing more efficient alternatives, BGE customers received incentives
and bill credits of approximately $114 million. Estimated savings on customer bills over the life
of measures installed in 2023 are $365 million. Total economic benefits returned to BGE customers
are summarized below.

Appliance Rebate: The Appliance Rebate Program, for 2023, realized 197% of its 2023 MWh
savings goal, and 178% of its MWh cycle goal. Increased incentives and a new direct to
consumer channel aided in the program’s accomplishment.
Quick Home Energy Check-Up: The Quick Home Energy Check-Up Program delivered
strong results in 2023, led by increased customer awareness and contractor marketing. For 2023,
the QHEC program attained 225% of the 2023 goal, and overall has reached 147% of the 3 year
cycle goal.
The following commercial energy efficiency programs exceeded target expectations for
2023:
Custom: The Custom Program continues its success from 2022. Program participation results
were elevated by the use of a proactive approach through streamlined processes and simpler
incentive calculations, and a new Building Modernization Initiative. The program accomplished
228% of its 2023 goal in 2023, and has achieved 183% of the cycle target savings.
Small Business: For 2023, the Small Business Program delivered on its targets, and then some,
as a result of its “closed network” model and an increased business peak demand threshold. For
2023, the program achieved 227% of its 2023 goal and currently stands at 202% of the cycle
target.
Retrocommissioning: In 2023, the Building Tune-up (Retrocommissioning) Program has
recorded 134% of its 2023 MWh savings goal, and 140% of its cycle target. The Building
Tune-up Program’s success has been bolstered by a Virtual Commissioning program and
simplified implementation.

BGE provides options for customers to save energy and money by installing smart thermostats
through multiple residential programs. Smart thermostats are available through the commercial

\*\* Includes Marketplace/Online Store, Verified Instant, ESRPP, & traditional smart thermostat rebates
BGE’s Smart Thermostat Rebate Program offers a rebate of up to $100 to residential customers
who purchase an ENERGY STAR certified smart thermostat through one of several market venues.
Customers can obtain a post-purchase rebate after purchasing an eligible thermostat through any
retailer and can choose whether to receive their post-purchase rebate via a gift card or personal
check. Customers can receive an instant rebate on their purchase through BGE’s Online
Marketplace or an instant coupon at select retailers. BGE also offers a direct-to-consumer limited-
time offer to targeted customers through a simple-to-use online platform.
BGE offers additional residential smart thermostat rebates through the HVAC midstream, New
Homes, Quick Home Energy Check-up (“QHEC”), and Home Performance with ENERGY STAR
(“HPwES”) programs. Residential customers are eligible for up to three smart thermostat rebates
(per program cycle) across all residential programs. BGE claims additional energy savings through
cloud optimization services offered through eligible devices claimed through the programs
mentioned above.
BGE’s Small Business Energy Solutions (“SBES”) Program offers smart thermostats to eligible
commercial customers. Smart thermostat capabilities make the measure ideal for small businesses
because there is an opportunity to significantly reduce load when the building is unoccupied.
BGE will continue to refine and enhance the evaluation and reporting for smart thermostats as
participation grows, additional measures are incentivized, and other outlets are considered.

Home Optimization and Retrofit Programs
Quick Home Energy Check-Up
Under the Quick Home Energy Check-Up (“QHEC”) Program, a Building Performance Institute
(“BPI”)-certified technician conducts a walk-through of a customer’s home and visually inspects
elements of the home, including the insulation, heating and air conditioning system, lighting, and
appliances. The customer receives a report summarizing potential energy-saving improvements, as
well as recommendations for other BGE programs for which the customer would be a good
candidate. In addition, the QHEC professional may install the following energy-saving measures
as appropriate: LED light bulbs, faucet aerators, efficient-flow fixed or handheld showerheads,
water heater pipe insulation, smart power strips, a ShowerStart™ showerhead adapter, and a smart
thermostat. There is no additional charge for the QHEC or the installed measures outside of the
customer’s EmPOWER surcharge.

[PDF p. 25 | printed p. 21]

cycle total for QHEC to 59,607 MWh (annualized gross wholesale) which is 147% of the cycle
savings target for the full 2021-2023 cycle period. Strong customer awareness continued to drive
success for the QHEC program, especially as COVID restrictions lifted during the tail end of the

QHEC participants benefit from few barriers to entry and an easy-to-understand process. This

Residential New Construction (ENERGY STAR® New Homes) Program
The ENERGY STAR New Construction Program features builder incentives for the construction
of ENERGY STAR-qualified new homes and Zero Energy Ready Homes (“ZERH”) that meet the
Department of Energy’s ZERH certification in addition to ENERGY STAR certification.
Both single-family and multi-family style homes built to ENERGY STAR or ZERH standards
qualify. Builders must incorporate certain high-efficiency appliances, measures, and construction
techniques to meet the program’s qualifications. The program includes “Additive Measures” with
incentives for equipment (smart thermostats, air conditioners, heat pumps, and heat pump water
heaters) with efficiency levels above ENERGY STAR minimum requirements.
Below Target: The ENERGY STAR New Homes Program achieved 5,521 MWh gross annual
savings in 2023, or 72% of the energy savings goal for the 2023 program year. The program
achieved 14,646 MWh gross annual savings for the full 2021-2023 cycle, which amounts to 67%
of the savings goal for the cycle period. Added measures for 2023 totaled 106 heat pump water
heaters, 6 high-efficiency heat pumps, 418 high-efficiency air conditioners,1,465 smart
thermostats, and 110 Zero Energy Ready Homes.
Of the 115 homes that met ZERH construction standards in this program cycle, 106 had either a
gas furnace or a gas water heater. The program is looking to address the need for more efficient
electric equipment in new homes by leveraging a recently released higher-tier standard finalized by
EPA in late 2023. The ENERGY STAR NextGen Program is a higher-tier whole-home residential
construction standard that prioritizes efficient electric equipment. The New Homes program has
continued to focus on encouraging higher efficiency measures that go above the minimum levels
required by the core ENERGY STAR standards. The program is encouraged by the recent uptake
in ZERH construction and has already begun working with participating builders and home energy

[PDF p. 33 | printed p. 29]

raters to support a transition to NextGen-level construction planned for the 2024-2026 program
cycle.

home energy monitor are not feasible additions to the QHEC Program as direct install measures
within the scope of the QHEC service. Results indicated a lack of customer interest and a lack of
eligible dryers in customers’ homes. The pilot was unable to provide sufficient data to address
product-level savings for the dryer device. Regarding the home energy monitor, the pilot
encountered meter integration challenges and the monitors were subsequently removed from the
pilot. BGE consulted with meter engineers, implementers, technology providers, and the
Commission’s independent evaluator to identify possible substitutes, including consideration of
plug-load monitors and panel-level monitoring devices. These alternatives were deemed unsuitable
for the pilot.
Final quantity of installed devices and customers served: Four customers accepted the dryer
measure resulting in a total of four dryer control devices installed.
Associated service calls and/or issues: No associated service calls and/or issues were reported by
pilot participants. Product availability was an issue, but this was due to manufacturing and shipping
delays associated with the COVID-19 pandemic, and not associated with the functionality of the
products themselves.
kWh and kW savings associated with installed devices: The pilot was unable to provide sufficient
data to evaluate measure-level kWh and kW savings. The Commission’s independent evaluator
recommended adopting findings from a similar pilot conducted by DTE Energy in Michigan. BGE
appreciates the opportunity for knowledge sharing and will look to the DTE study if available for
additional insight and savings recommendations.
Customer & contractor survey results: Surveys were issued to participants who accepted the
measure and non-participants who declined the measure. Regular conversations were held with
contractors and technicians throughout the duration of the pilot. Results point to low customer
eligibility and low customer interest in the dryer control measure. Contractors found that most
dryers did not qualify for the device and customers were not aware if their machines already had
moisture sensing capabilities. Customers with eligible appliances voiced concerns about making
changes to their appliance and declined the installation.
Behavioral trends, program recommendations, and propensity modeling based on pilot product
data: Sufficient data was not available to evaluate these items, due mainly to low measure adoption
and insufficient participant sample size.

[PDF p. 87 | printed p. 71]

Cross-program enrollments based on recommendations and marketing outreach: Low interest and
lack of participation resulted in no verifiable cross-enrollments.
Recommendations for full-scale rollout and implementation of devices into the QHEC Program:
The Program does not recommend adding the piloted devices to QHEC at this time. Aside from
low customer interest and lack of eligible households, the length of time required for proper
installation made the dryer control device impractical. As with all QHEC direct install measures
(DIMs), technicians are required to verify all items are in good working order and operating as
expected during the appointment. Applying the same level of quality assurance to the dryer sensor
would require a technician to remain in the house for at least the duration of one drying cycle,
possibly longer. Average installation time for the dryer device was more than 45 minutes. The
advertised time for an entire QHEC is one hour. There may be ways to restructure the QHEC service
to support this type of measure, but a wholesale reassessment or reimagination of the QHEC was
outside the scope of this pilot.

APPENDIX F – GHG STUDY
The Maryland Utilities, as agreed to in the Future Programming Work Group, are currently funding
the Applied Energy Group, Inc. (“AEG”) to conduct the Maryland 2022 GHG Abatement
Study. The study will produce a database of detailed GHG abatement estimates by resource,
technology, utility service territory, and market achievable potential scenarios. The result will
establish a suitable representation of potential GHG abatement goals for EmPOWER Maryland
utilities.
The effort involves a Market and Baseline Study. This energy efficiency and building
electrification potential analysis will consider technical, economic and achievable potential.
Technical Potential is a theoretical construct that estimates the savings that would be attained if all
customers adopted the most efficient option available in the marketplace, regardless of cost.
Economic Potential represents the adoption of all cost-effective measures by all customers.
Achievable Potential provides an estimate of the amount of potential that could be cost-effectively
acquired under a specified set of conditions: overall budgets; level of rebates relative to incremental
costs; and expectations about customer participation. The study will also estimate the potential for
active and passive demand response. As part of the project scope, the AEG team will provide for
proactive stakeholder engagement to gain both buy-in and a diverse set of viewpoints representing
the various concerns in developing GHG abatement goals.
The overall Commission budgeted amount for the GHG Abatement Study is $1,479,313, of which
BGE's budgeted portion is $624,270. For 2022 and 2023, BGE has spent in total $602,027 for this
study. These costs will not be included in the Cost Program Detail worksheets (Appendix B);
however, the Commission has allowed the utilities to recover these costs through the EmPOWER
MD surcharge.
