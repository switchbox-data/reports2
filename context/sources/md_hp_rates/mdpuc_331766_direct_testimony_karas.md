## III. Rate Design Principles

An effective rate design incorporates the principles of **cost causation**, **intergenerational equity**, **price signaling**, **reasonableness**, **gradualism**, and both **inter-class and intra-class equity**. These are documented by experts within the area of utility ratemaking and are principles employed by the Commission in prior base rate case proceedings.[^3]

[^3]: _Principles of Public Utility Rates_, Second Edition 1988, by James Bonbright, Albert Danielsen and David Kamerschen.

### Cost Causation and Rate Design

Costs should be borne by the customers on whose behalf the costs are incurred. The Company's proposed electric base distribution revenue allocation uses a two-step approach, using the results of the ECOSS to move customer class returns closer to the system average return. The methodology also incorporates the principle of **gradualism**, whereby rates are moved by incremental steps rather than by drastic changes.[^4]

[^4]: The definition of the word gradualism per Merriam-Webster reads "the policy of approaching a desired end by gradual stages."

The Company's rate design should be consistent with the nature of the costs incurred:

- **Fixed and demand-related costs** (costs that do not vary with the total amount of electricity delivered) should be recovered through fixed monthly rates and rates that reflect a customer's demand on the system
- **Variable costs** (costs that increase or decrease as the total amount of electricity delivered changes) should be recovered through rates that vary based on the amount of electricity delivered

### Current Rate Structure

BGE's distribution rate structures primarily include:

- **Customer Charge**: The fixed, monthly charge intended to recover operating costs caused by customers connecting to the electric grid (billing, customer care, meter costs, service connection costs)
- **Demand Charge**: A charge for certain rate schedules based on the maximum load over a measured period, designed to recover costs driven by the customer class' peak loads
- **Delivery Service Charge**: A volumetric charge meant to recover costs caused by customers' energy usage

Current Customer Charges for the residential electric classes recover a portion of fixed costs but are not set at a level to recover all fixed costs. The ECOSS shows that volumetric rates should recover 68% of electric base distribution revenues for the residential classes, but currently recover approximately 84%.[^5]

[^5]: Re Baltimore Gas and Electric Co., Case No. 9326, Order No. 86060, p. 105 (Dec. 13, 2013).

### Intra-Class Inequities

Intra-class inequities are created when fixed costs are not recovered through fixed charges because different residential customers use varying levels of energy. When fixed costs are recovered through variable charges, customers with higher usage are paying for a higher portion of fixed costs than customers with lower usage — subsidizing the fixed costs of those customers with lower-than-average usage.

### Two-Step Revenue Apportionment

The revenue increase is apportioned such that each customer class's rate of return moves toward or within a reasonable band (+/- 10%) around the system average rate of return, consistent with Case Nos. 9326, 9406, 9484, 9645, and 9692.

**Step 1**: Move the RROR for classes below 0.90 closer to the system average. Schedule R (RROR of 0.65) is the only class below 0.90. The step one adjustment moves it 50% of the way to 0.90, resulting in RROR of 0.77.

**Step 2**: Remaining revenue increase allocated to existing rate classes in proportion to base distribution revenues after step one. Schedules PL and T are excluded (RRORs of 2.70 and 20.46). Schedule EVP is also excluded as its rates are market-based.

## VI. Tariff Proposals

### A. BGE FlexPay — Prepaid Service Program

BGE proposes a new prepaid service program, BGE FlexPay, in which customers can voluntarily participate. Under this program, customers prepay for their anticipated energy consumption and can track usage through regular communication and self-service digital tools.

Key differences from the prior Prepaid Pilot (2018):

1. Removes the maximum of 1,000 participants
2. Eligibility revised to include only residential customers receiving supply through BGE Standard Offer Service with an arrearage balance of $1,000 or less at enrollment
3. Participants are not eligible for Rider 6 (Vehicle Charging TOU), Rider 18 (Net Energy Metering), or Rider 32 (Community Solar Program)

Implementation within 17 to 20 months after Commission Order.

### B. Storm Restoration Expense Rider

Rider 30, the Storm Restoration Expense Rider, is a cost recovery mechanism for incremental storm restoration expenses. The Rider adjusts the total distribution rate to recover or refund the imbalance between actual incremental storm restoration O&M expense and the baseline level embedded in base rates.

The calculation methodology:

1. Compare incremental storm expenses in base rates with actual expenses for the 12-month period under review. Revenue requirement divided by 3 for recovery over three years, allocated per most recent rate case revenue proportions.
2. Each Adjustment included in Rider for three years with interest at authorized rate of return.
3. Annual true-up for over/under-collections.
4. Total amounts divided by estimated billing determinants per kWh or per fixture.

Initial review period: 11 months commencing February 1, 2027, reset annually on calendar year basis.

Consistent with recent BGE rate cases, the revenue increase is apportioned using a two-step process such that each customer class's rate of return moves toward or within a reasonable band (+/- 10%) around the system average. Customer Charges for Schedule R and Schedule G are increased closer to the level supported by the ECOSS.
