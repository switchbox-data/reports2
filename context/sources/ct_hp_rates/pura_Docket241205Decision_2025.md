**DECISION**

## I. INTRODUCTION

### A. SUMMARY

The Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA or Authority) examined process
improvements for the annual reviews of the Rate Adjustment Mechanisms (RAM) of The
Connecticut Light and Power Company d/b/a Eversource Energy (Eversource) and The
United Illuminating Company (UI; together, with Eversource, the electric distribution
companies or EDCs). Specifically, the Authority considered stakeholder input on
forecasted Locational Marginal Prices (LMPs) and the effect of volatility on the net cost
of Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) as well as on the cost allocation methodologies
for certain RAM components. As a result, the Authority identified certain filing
requirements to facilitate resolution of these issues in each of the EDCs’ next annual RAM
proceedings.

In future annual RAM reconciliation dockets, the Authority will consider both actual
and forecasted LMPs when evaluating LMP volatility on the net costs of PPAs.
Accordingly, the EDCs are directed to file relevant LMP information and calculations in
those proceedings for the Authority to determine the appropriate Non-Bypassable
Federally Mandated Congestion Charge (NBFMCC) rates. In addition, the Authority
outlines a two-step analysis for considering interim rate adjustments necessitated by any
significant LMP volatility.

Finally, the EDCs are directed to provide, in their next respective annual RAM
proceeding, certain information and calculations to allow the Authority to assess potential
future changes to the NBFMCC and SBC cost allocators, as discussed below, including
the phasing in of any such changes over a reasonable period.

### B. BACKGROUND OF THE PROCEEDING

Generally, an EDC may only charge rates that have been approved by the
Authority in a general rate proceeding under General Statutes § 16-19. However, the
General Assembly established several mechanisms that allow EDCs to recover specific
costs on an annual basis. See, e.g., General Statutes § 16-245l (authorizing PURA to
assess, and each EDC to collect a Systems Benefit Charge (SBC). Accordingly, the
Authority conducts an annual proceeding to determine rates for the RAM components
such that the rates reflect actual costs.

In 2020, the Authority redesigned the annual rate adjustment process for specified
RAM mechanisms.[^1] Decision, Dec. 2, 2020, Docket No. 20-01-01, Administrative
Proceeding to Review the Connecticut Light and Power Company’s Standard Service and
Supplier of Last Resort Service 2020 Procurement Results and Rates; Decision, Dec. 2,
2020, Docket No. 20-01-02, Administrative Proceeding to Review The United Illuminating
Company’s Standard Service and Supplier of Last Resort Service 2020 Procurement

[^1]: Relevant to the present proceeding, these mechanisms include, among others, the SBC, the NBFMCC; and the Revenue Decoupling Mechanism (RDM) (collectively, RAM Components).

[PDF p. 4 | printed p. 2]

Results and Rates (RAM Process Decisions). Under the current process, the Authority
reviews and provisionally approves rates effective May 1 for the ensuing 12-month period
using actual costs from the previous calendar year as a proxy for expected costs, while
adjusting for known and measurable changes. RAM Process Decisions, p. 1,
respectively. The Authority then performs a prudency review of actual revenues and
approved expenses from the prior calendar year and may, if appropriate, adjust the
approved rates effective September 1 to account for the Authority’s prudency
determinations. Id.

In 2022, the Authority clarified the known and measurable standard and directed
the EDCs to annually evaluate what they should include in the Known and Measurable
Filing as an “other” revenue or cost change of significance. Interim Decision, Apr. 13,
2022, Docket No. 22-01-03, PURA Annual Review of the Rate Adjustment Mechanisms
of The Connecticut Light and Power Company, p. 23; Interim Decision, Apr. 13, 2022,
Docket No. 22-01-04, PURA Annual Review of the Rate Adjustment Mechanisms of The
United Illuminating Company, p. 16. Specifically, the Authority explained that the EDCs

> shall continue to include discussion of differences that do not qualify under the
> strict known and measurable standard, but that are either known and will arise
> during the year or are changes that are reasonably likely to be a significant factor
> incorporated into the Company’s proposed May 1 rate adjustments. Such “other”
> revenue and cost changes may not be definitively measurable, but to the extent
> they drive or are expected to be the primary reason for a proposed increase or
> decrease in a rate component or a future rate component under- or over-recovery,
> they should be identified. In essence, the significant drivers of proposed
> adjustments to the different rate components should be highlighted and made clear
> even when the Company does not consider them definitively “measurable” as of
> their January 15 filing each year.

Id. Additionally in 2022, the Authority consolidated all then-existing orders pertaining to
its annual RAM review and provided guidance to streamline future RAM proceedings,
starting with the 2023 RAM filings. Decision, Dec. 21, 2022, Docket No. 22-09-08, PURA
Proceeding to Consolidate Guidance of the Rate Adjustment Mechanism Docket
Procedure and Filings (RAM Consolidation Decision), pp. 4–5, App. B.

### C. CONDUCT OF THE PROCEEDING

On December 11, 2024, the Authority initiated this proceeding for the purpose of
investigating further process refinements in the annual reviews of each EDCs’ RAM. By
Notice of Proceeding dated January 17, 2025, the Authority announced its intent to
examine (1) the drivers of, and mitigation strategies for, significant over- or under-
recoveries for various expenses included in the RAM components; (2) cost allocation
methodologies for various RAM components; and (3) the treatment of uncollectible
expenses included in the RAM components. Notice of Proceeding, p. 1. Across all topics
[PDF p. 5 | printed p. 3]
of interest, the Authority also expressed its plan to review, with a view toward better
aligning, the respective methodologies used by the EDCs.[^2] Id.

The Authority issued two notices of requests for written comments on January 17,
2025 and June 11, 2025. The Authority issued two sets of interrogatories to the EDCs on
June 11, 2025, and July 29, 2025. The Office of Consumer Counsel (OCC) issued
interrogatories to the EDCs on March 27, 2025 and April 8, 2025.

On September 20, 2025, the Authority held a technical meeting at the Authority’s
offices. See Notice of Tech Mt’g. At the technical meeting, the EDCs presented on the
implications of: (1) potential alterations to the timing of RAM rate changes, (2) RAM
component cost forecasting methodologies, (3) the alignment of net cost effects of PPAs
with Standard Service rates, and (4) cost allocation scenarios that have been investigated
in the instant proceeding. After the EDCs’ presentations, participants engaged in an
Authority-facilitated roundtable discussion of potential process changes under
consideration. Id.

Participants had the opportunity to file briefs on or before September 4, 2025.
The Authority issued a proposed final decision in this matter on October 24, 2025,
and provided an opportunity for participants to file written exceptions thereto.

#### 3. Authority Analysis

##### a. Incorporation of LMP Forecasts in Future RAM Reviews

Under the existing annual RAM reconciliation process, the EDCs are required to
submit actual incurred costs for each RAM component for the prior calendar year and to
[PDF p. 10 | printed p. 8]
identify any known and measurable adjustments for the upcoming rate period that would
otherwise deviate from the prior year actual costs that serve as a proxy. See RAM
Process Decisions. Though Eversource has supported a return to a prior RAM
methodology that relied on forecasts for all RAM components and reconciled rates at
January 1st of each year, the rate volatility, as described in Section II.A.1, above, is
primarily due to LMPs and the effect on the Millstone and Seabrook PPAs. Accordingly,
the Authority will consider forecasted LMP data in future RAM proceedings.

Several participants support incorporating actualized and forecasted LMPs (and
the resulting net cost effects on PPAs into rate adjustments, as well as for additional
adjustments if net PPA costs materially deviate from existing assumptions.[^7] See
Eversource Brief, pp. 4, 5–12; UI Comments, July 16, 2025, p. 4; OCC Comments, July
16, 2025, pp. 9-10; DEEP Comments, July 16, 2025, p. 3. However, incorporating
forecasted net PPA costs into rates carries risk. As Eversource notes, “neither the utilities,
PURA, nor anyone else, can fix the market or predict with perfect accuracy the direction
and magnitude of market.” Eversource Brief, p. 2. Despite this inherent risk of an inability
to predict the future, the dynamic nature of LMPs and their impact on PPAs weighs in
favor of using the latest forecasted prices for LMPs available to the EDCs. Such LMP
forecasts are not reliant on the EDCs’ internal forecasting methodologies, but are built on
third-party LMP market data that the EDCs use to reforecast the offsetting revenues
recouped for the generated energy purchased from the PPAs.[^8] While the LMP forecasts
used by the EDCs cannot perfectly predict the changes in market prices that will impact
NBFMCC rates for customers, the very volatility and unpredictability of the market renders
the use of LMP forecast data, based on current data and multiple inputs, a reasonable
exception to the general RAM component use of prior year actuals adjusted for known
and measurable changes.

Therefore, in future RAM proceedings the Authority will consider the net cost
effects of the PPAs by analyzing the most current actualized data and most recent LMP
forecasts provided by the EDCs. Specifically, the May 1st rate can incorporate the net
impacts on PPAs of actual LMPs for January through March and forecasted LMPs for the
balance of the calendar year. For the September 1st rate adjustment, the Authority may

##### b. Two-Step Analysis for Considering Interim Rate Adjustments

To further mitigate the potential accrual of significant over- and under-recoveries
driven by costs recovered in the NBFMCC, the Authority adopts a two-part analysis
designed to trigger a review of then-current rate components to assess whether interim
NBFMCC adjustments are necessary.

When prior year PPA costs built into the NBFMCC rate are low and even potentially
close to zero, a 5 or 10% cost deviation, as proposed by Eversource, UI, and DEEP, may
represent a relatively low absolute value, which would be triggered easily as the rate year
progresses. Even at a 10% trigger, based on the historical cost of the PPAs for each
EDC, the first test itself would rarely restrict an interim rate adjustment unless the total
net costs of the PPAs assumed within the previously set rate are much larger than what
transpired historically for total PPA costs.

The second step of Eversource’s proposed test, which evaluates a whole-bill
threshold, would therefore play an important role in determining whether an interim rate
adjustment is appropriate. Eversource proposed a 1% of total bill metric to determine
whether an interim rate change may be prudent. Eversource clarified that the 1% would
“likely [focus] on Residential Rate 1 customers. . ..” Tech Mt’g Tr., Aug. 14, 2025, 62:23
– 63:18. Eversource recognized that the net PPA costs could result in a net credit in costs
to customers, or even be zero, which is why the second portion of the test is appropriate.
Id., 71:4 – 71:17. If the embedded portion of net costs or credit due to the PPAs were
larger, there is a potential scenario where the first step of the test may not be triggered,
but the 1% total bill impact would. Id., 70:16 – 71:3. Eversource and UI indicated that a
1% change in costs would roughly equate to $50 to $75 million and $15 million,
respectively. Id., 72:21 – 73:10; Id., 74:13 – 74:20.

OCC offered that, “intuitively [it] makes sense to have the sequence of the two-part
test in the reverse of what Eversource is suggesting.” Tech Mt’g Tr., Aug. 14, 2025, 74:21
– 75:8. OCC, therefore, supports reviewing whether a 1% bill impact threshold has been
met, and then determining to what degree the PPAs have deviated away from original
assumptions. Id.

The 1% whole-bill impact test evaluates what portion of a residential customer’s
bill is the result of the net costs associated with the PPAs. Though there may be
circumstances in which one of the Two-Step Test steps is triggered and not the other,
there is value in the EDCs monitoring both deviations from rates and proportional impact
on customer bills. When combined into the Two-Step Test, the EDCs have a clear,
established method for requesting a review for a potential interim NBFMCC rate
adjustment, and the Authority would have an additional tool for considering options to
mitigate significant under- or over-recoveries. Additionally, the Authority may consider a
[PDF p. 12 | printed p. 10]
potential interim rate adjustment triggered by the Two-Step Test in concert with any
concurrent or near-term changes in Standard Service supply rates.[^9] For example, if an
EDC’s Two-Step Test results warrant submission of an interim NBFMCC rate adjustment
request, that Company would submit the supporting test analysis with supplemental
information regarding directional expectations for Standard Service rates effective
January 1st or July 1st, whichever is forthcoming at that time. In recent years, some RAM
component increases have been offsetting with Standard Service rate decreases, helping
mitigate customer rate shock. However, an interim rate adjustment may need to occur as
a standalone rate change separate from the regular changes in Standard Service rates.
In deciding whether or not to implement a PPA-induced interim NBFMCC rate change,
the Authority must balance the goal of reducing the total number of rate changes for
customers with the goal of reducing rate shock whenever possible.[^10]

Therefore, the Authority will consider, in future RAM review dockets, the following
two-step analysis for precipitating an interim NBFMCC rate change request:

1. If the projected end-of-**year** over- or under-recovery **of all PPAs within each EDC’s respective NBMFCC**[^11] mechanism is **10**% or greater than the cost (or credit) that the rate is designed to collect from (or return to) customers, and

Then, the Company would be expected to submit an interim NBFMCC rate change request, with evidence in support thereof, including directional information regarding forthcoming Standard Service rates, in the then-active applicable annual RAM review docket.

##### c. Authority Analysis

When the NBFMCC was originally created, the 12CP and 1CP demand allocators
were appropriate because the costs that were initially included were associated with the
New England Grid Operator — ISO New England. As time has passed, however, new
cost categories have been added to reflect public policy objectives, shifting the NBFMCC
away from a strictly demand-related mechanism to include costs that more appropriately
align with ratepayer energy consumption. For example, the NBFMCC mechanism
includes several PPAs that generate electricity and therefore are directly related to the
total amount of electricity produced and consumed by customers. Ever since non-
demand related costs (i.e., increasing public policy costs, such as PPAs) have been
included within the NBFMCC, residential ratepayers have been responsible for covering
a disproportionate share of the allocated costs for such non-demand related costs within
the NBFMCC given that the current demand allocator remains in use. Accordingly, the
revised scenarios in Table 4 above are more consistent with cost causation principles
than the current demand allocators for the NBFMCC approach.

OCC agreed with Eversource’s position that costs associated with mandated
energy purchases, customer produced energy, and miscellaneous and other mandates
should utilize an energy allocator that uses historical sales. OCC Written Comments,
Mar. 14, 2025, p. 5. Basing cost allocation on sales offers “simplicity and transparency in

[^19]: Interrogatory RRU-5 requested that the various scenarios be compared to the existing cost allocation for rates that went into effect for each company as of July 1, 2025.

[PDF p. 20 | printed p. 18]

ratemaking as well as better alignment across the EDCs.” Id. The Authority agrees with
OCC’s assessment.

Based on the foregoing, it appears appropriate to transition to the Scenario 1 cost
allocation methodology for the NBFMCC, which will allocate the New England Grid
Operator costs using a 12CP demand allocator and other NBFMCC costs using an energy
allocator to be implemented through, and starting with, the next annual RAM review
proceedings (i.e., Docket Nos. 26-01-03 and 26-01-04). Scenario 1 and Scenario 3
leaves the 12CP allocator unchanged for the New England Grid Operator costs for both
EDCs which supports existing practice and other participant feedback, whereas Scenario
2 would be an unnecessary deviation to 1CP for these costs. Additionally, Scenario 3
incorporated a 1CP allocator solely for costs that were related to peaking generation
mandated energy purchases which did not produce a materially different overall bill
impact result compared to Scenario 1. The Authority sees more value in simplifying the
allocation for the overall mandated energy purchases category of costs within the
NBFMCC than utilizing a partial allocator for different costs. The Authority recognizes
that this modification to cost allocation more significantly affects some commercial and
industrial ratepayer classes. Accordingly, in the next RAM review proceeding, the
Authority will consider an appropriate period (e.g. four or more years) during which to
phase in any modifications. To reiterate, the Authority intends to finalize determinations
as to both the NBFMCC cost allocations and as to any appropriate phase-in period(s)
during the 2026 annual RAM proceedings for rates beginning May 1, 2026.[^20]

Accordingly, UI allocates its SBC program costs based on rate type, by kilowatt hours

programs, and energy efficiency are fairly distributed among customers based on their
energy consumption.” Id., p. 9. UI recommended a four-year phase-in period to
mitigate the effects of any changes made to cost allocators. Id., p. 8.

Eversource indicated that it would support changing its SBC allocator to an energy
allocator but recognized concerns with disparate impacts across various rate classes in
making what amounts to an effective reallocation of the SBC’s costs. Eversource
Comments, Mar. 14, 2025, p. 8. Specifically, residential rate classes would reduce by
approximately 1 cent/kWh and commercial and industrial customers would subsequently
increase approximately 1 cent/kWh if it were to allocate the SBC costs based on energy.
Eversource Written Comments, Mar. 14, 2025, Att. 2, p. 1. Eversource recommended a
3-year phase-in period should any significant changes be adopted as it relates to cost
allocation.

OCC agreed that the SBC costs should use an energy allocator because the
purpose of the SBC is to support public policy goals that relate to all rate classes. OCC
Comments, Mar 14, 2025, p. 6. Other jurisdictions, such as Washington DC,
Massachusetts, and Illinois, allocate low-income programs broadly across all rate classes
in a similar fashion. Id., p. 7. OCC stressed the importance of evaluating total bill impacts
on any changes made to cost allocators, and that “improvement in cost reflectivity” should
be more important than any major impacts to certain rate classes. Id., pp. 8-9.

CIEC recommended that the SBC rate be designed to collect revenue from
ratepayers in a manner that is consistent with how the underlying costs are allocated to
the rate to begin with. CIEC Comments, Mar. 14, 2025, p. 7. CIEC concluded that if an
alternative allocator was considered for the SBC that a hybrid allocator utilizing more than
one allocator should be developed that better reflects cost-causation principles. Id.; CIEC
Written Comments, Jul. 16, 2025, p. 8.

##### b. Potential Bill Impacts for Eversource Customers

Currently, Eversource prorates its SBC revenue requirement across its rate
classes by utilizing the prior year actual costs that are tracked under each individual rate
class. 2023 Eversource RAM Decision, p. 29. UI, on the other hand, allocates its SBC
revenue requirement using the total annual kilo-watt hour energy usage from the
applicable test year by rate class. 2023 UI RAM Decision, p. 29. The Authority previously
indicated that it was inclined to implement a version of UI’s energy-based allocation and
would investigate the details of such a change. 2023 Eversource RAM Decision, p. 29;
2023 UI RAM Decision, p. 29. Accordingly, in the instant proceeding, the Authority
prompted Eversource to analyze the use of an energy allocator for its SBC mechanism.
Eversource’s initial response provided the rate impact by rate classes, however
the analysis primarily looked at the deviation of the rates based on the SBC rate alone,
not from a total-bill impact perspective. Eversource Interrog. Resp, RRU-1. OCC

The law requires that the Authority determine the amount of the Systems Benefits
Charge in a general and equitable manner, using methods of allocation in effect when the
law was enacted. General Statutes §16-245l(b). As Eversource’s current allocation
methodology relies on prior year SBC actual costs to prorate its allocation by rate class,
2023 Eversource RAM Decision, p. 29, its commercial and industrial customers are not
allocated the same level of SBC revenue requirement as other rate classes. That is,
Eversource’s commercial and industrial customers are currently receiving a much lower
allocation than UI’s commercial and industrial customers. This disparity is inequitable;
the relative allocation of state-mandated SBC costs should not depend on which EDC’s
service area a given commercial or industrial customer falls. Moreover, there is ample
[PDF p. 23 | printed p. 21]
support for parity across EDCs in the utilization of an energy allocator for the SBC by the
participants within this docket.

To advance the process of aligning the EDCs’ cost allocation methodologies, the
Authority directs Eversource to file, in the next RAM proceeding, a calculation[^21] of the
SBC revenue requirement using an energy cost allocation methodology. As with the
NBFMCC, the Authority directs Eversource to include in its next RAM filing an additional,
alternate calculation of its SBC revenue requirement that includes a suggested phased in
approach over a period of several years (e.g., four or more years).[^22] Thus, any change
to the cost allocation methodology for the SBC revenue requirement would be approved
and implemented in the 2026 annual RAM proceedings beginning May 1, 2026, with the
full phase-in occurring over a period of several years.

#### 4. Conclusion

In each EDC’s respective 2026 annual RAM review docket, the EDCs are directed
to submit, for the Authority’s review, an additional, alternate calculation for transitioning
the cost allocation methodology to Scenario 1 in Table 4 above (i.e. using a 12CP demand
allocator for New England Grid Operator Costs and using an energy allocator for all other
NBFMCC costs). Similarly, Eversource shall provide for the Authority’s review, an
additional alternate calculation of the SBC revenue requirement using an energy cost
allocator.

For both NBFMCC and SBC prospective alternate cost allocation calculations
discussed above, the EDCs are directed to provide, in their next respective annual RAM
review submissions, calculations that would phase in the above changes over a
reasonable period (e.g. four or more years). The Authority will conduct further
assessment of potential allocation change implementation strategies during each EDC’s
respective contested RAM proceedings.

### C. OTHER TOPICS

#### 1. Cost Allocation – RDM

During the 2023 annual RAM reconciliation proceedings, the Authority also began
to investigate differences in cost allocation factors and methodologies utilized by the
EDCs with respect to the RDM mechanism. Both companies currently allocate the over-
and under-collected distribution revenues using an energy allocator. 2023 Eversource
RAM Decision, p. 30, 2023 UI RAM Decision, pp. 29-30. In the 2023 RAM Decisions, the
Authority established that this methodology is not based on cost causation principles. Id.

Currently, the annual over- and under-recovery reported by the EDCs is evenly
distributed across all rate classes, producing the same rate for all ratepayers. These
under- and over-recoveries are not tracked individually by the rate classes that have
produced those over- and under-recoveries of distribution revenue, which would
constitute a more direct cost causation methodology. Eversource provided analysis in
the instant docket that applied the individual rate class over- and under-recoveries back
to their individual rate classes, effectively causing the rate classes that produce an over-
or under-recovery to pay for it themselves. Eversource Comments, Mar. 14, 2025, Att. 3.
This analysis indicated significant potential swings to the volumetric rate for certain rate
classes that would constitute extreme rate fluctuation. Id. Further analysis that split the
over- and under-recoveries between residential and commercial and industrial customers
flattened the effects but failed to support an equitable allocator for the RDM revenue
requirement. Interrog. Resp. RRU-1. Therefore, at this time, the current allocation
methodology used for the RDM is appropriate.

#### 2. Non-Hardship Uncollectible Expenses

Finally, the Authority used this proceeding to, in part, further explore disparities in
the treatment of non-hardship (NHS) uncollectible expenses between the two EDCs.
Eversource recovers NHS uncollectible expenses within its Generation Service Charge
(GSC), NBFMCC, ESI, and base distribution rates. 2023 Eversource RAM Decision, p.
26. UI, on the other hand, allocates the NHS uncollectible expenses within its GSC, TAC,
and base distribution rates. 2023 UI RAM Decision. While these divergent allocation
approaches persist, any changes to the NHS uncollectible allocations are best
implemented through each EDCs next base distribution rate case. A change made to the
allocation methodology that would either reduce or increase the allocation of non-
hardship uncollectible expenses to the GSC, outside of a traditional rate case, would
cause the EDCs to either not recover the full expense or recover more than the actual
expense. The non-hardship uncollectible expense within distribution rates remains static
until final costs are determined during a new rate case, making a distribution rate case
the most appropriate venue to address any allocation changes.

Authority desires further data on this issue in advance thereof. Accordingly, the EDCs
