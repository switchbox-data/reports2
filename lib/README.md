# Shared libraries (`lib/`)

`lib/` is a Python-installable package (`packages = ["lib"]` in `pyproject.toml`); after `just install`, its modules import directly (`from lib.rdp import fetch_rdp_file`). R files are sourced (`source("lib/ggplot/switchbox_theme.R")`).

Find the module that already does what you need here before writing new helpers. When you add or change a module, update its row.

| `lib.rates_analysis.rate_case_funcs`                                                                                     | Generalized rate-case analysis for Python reports (bill-change incidence, BAT/cross-subsidy, cost of service by subclass, tariff introspection), parameterized by state/batch/scenario. `load_master_bat_for_utility` filters a master BAT segment to one utility and optionally validates/selects required columns while preserving lazy execution. `segment_upgrade` reads the ResStock upgrade ID stored on a master-bills segment. `monthly_bill_components`/`annual_bill_components` pull one building's electric+gas bill (monthly, or annual with delivery split into fixed/volumetric) for representative-household charts; `load_delivery_mc_heatmap` loads and joins a utility's hourly bulk-tx/dist-sub-tx marginal costs into one 8760-row frame; `tariff_month_rate_table` builds a month-by-month volumetric-rate comparison table across any number of tariffs; `bat_component_delta` diffs arbitrary master-BAT columns between two segments (optionally filtered to one `utility`), for per-building revenue/marginal-cost/etc. deltas. `weighted_range_pcts` bins a value column into caller-defined `[lo, hi)` intervals (use ±inf for tails); `quadrant_pcts` is the fixed +/- $1k wrapper used for bill-change quadrant bars. Plotting helpers include `plot_bill_change_quadrants` (savings/loss quadrants by heating type), `plot_weighted_bill_change_hist` (weighted histogram of annual bill change, colored by quadrant), `plot_weighted_hist_by_group` (weighted histogram of any value column faceted by a group column, e.g. incremental kWh by heating type), `plot_mc_heatmap` (8760 day-of-year x hour-of-day marginal-cost heatmap, given any dataframe with `day_of_year`/`hour`/value columns), `plot_monthly_bill_components` (stacked monthly delivery/supply bars for one building), `plot_decomposed_bill_3bar` (three-bar decomposed annual bill: gas furnace vs. HP on the default rate vs. HP on a reform rate), and `plot_ratedesign_monthly_hist`/`plot_ratedesign_cdf` (rate-design-only, pre-retrofit monthly bill-change histogram/CDF). | `lib.rates_analysis.cos_funcs`                                                                                           | Incremental delivery revenue vs. marginal-cost analysis. `incremental_cost_delta_with_gap` reads delivery revenue from **master bills** (`elec_delivery_bill` and `elec_fixed_charge` as separate columns) and marginal cost from **master BAT** (`economic_burden_delivery`), producing per-building before/after/delta for each, plus `delta_gap`, `overpayment_volumetric` (excludes fixed charge), and `overpayment_volumetric_fc` (includes fixed charge). | `lib.rates_analysis.seasonal_mc`                                                                                         | Seasonal-split marginal-cost 8760s for top-N allocations (distribution `calculate_pop_weights`, PJM bulk transmission `allocate_pcaf`), built in post-processing so a sensitivity needs no CAIRO rerun. `build_seasonal_split_mc_8760(load, annual_cost_kw_yr, n_hours, winter_share, split=...)`: `split="cost"` puts share x of the dollars on the top N winter hours and 1−x on the top N summer hours (each load-weighted within its season, so a home's cost is linear in x); `split="hours"` uses round(x·N) winter hours plus the rest from summer, load-weighted together; `winter_share=None` reproduces the generators exactly; optional `winter_rank_load` ranks winter hours on another shape. `load_pjm_allocation_load` rebuilds the PJM utility load the generators allocate on, placing another load year onto the output calendar by date (as `md_hp_rates` stamps BGE's 2024 home loads onto 2025).
