### G. Summary of Conclusions and Recommendations

**Q.** Please summarize your conclusions and recommendations for the issues you have reviewed.

**A.** The Company’s Grid Plan generally aligns with certain elements of Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M), including program offerings related to time-of-use rates, non-residential tariffs, and managed charging. However, the Company has not provided a plan which fully demonstrates consideration of Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i) for make-ready investments. I have identified concerns with respect to the Company’s broader showing of proposed BE investment programs under Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M), and include detailed recommendations in my testimony below. Finally, I recommend the Commission direct the Company to provide in a compliance filing after the final order in this proceeding updated Benefit-Cost Analysis workpapers and grid plan project budget spreadsheets that reflect the findings adopted by the Commission. If the Commission were to adopt a different discount rate or project budget amounts than those proposed by the Company, then the Company should provide updated workpapers and budget spreadsheets to reflect those, so the approved values are all in one place. This is particularly important given the Company is now required to produce updated BCA workpapers with ex post cost-effectiveness results in each annual adjustment docket. So, it is important to have base level BCA workpapers to start from when comparing what was assumed and approved in the Grid Plan filing with the actual results. I discuss these issues and recommendations in greater detail below.

## II. Statutory Requirements: Beneficial Electrification

**Q.** As part of your review of the Company’s Grid Plan, did you assess whether the Company’s Grid Plan included the information directed by Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)?

**A.** Yes. Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M) is a new requirement added by the Clean and Reliable Grid Affordability Act (“CRGA”), Public Act 104-0458, which was enacted on January 8, 2026, and will be effective June 1, 2026. Because this requirement will become effective during the pendency of this proceeding, I provide my assessment now to enable the Commission to have a complete record of this issue. Specifically, I reviewed whether the Company’s Grid Plan included the following required information:

[p. 17, lines 265–296] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

(M) For plans that include a time period that is after January 1, 2029, a description of efforts to support transportation electrification through the following:

(i) make-ready investments and other programs to facilitate the rapid deployment of charging equipment throughout this State, especially deployment that targets medium-duty and heavy-duty vehicle electrification and multi-unit buildings; (ii) the development and implementation of (1) time-of-use rates and their benefit for electric vehicle users and for all customers, (2) optimized charging programs to achieve identified savings, and (3) new contracts and compensation for services in the optimized charging programs, through signals that allow electric vehicle charging to respond to local system conditions, manage critical peak periods, serve as a demand response or peak resource, and maximize renewable energy use and integration into the grid; and (iii) commercial tariffs utilizing alternatives to traditional demand-based rate structures that facilitate charging for light-duty, heavy-duty, and fleet electric vehicles.

For items (i) through (iii), the utility shall demonstrate methods of minimizing ratepayer impacts and exempting or minimizing, to the extent possible, low-income ratepayers from the costs associated with facilitating the expansion of electric vehicle charging. Investments, programs, and activities proposed to meet the obligations of this subparagraph (M) shall be evaluated and approved by the Commission using the same standards of cost-effectiveness, as described in paragraph (7) of subsection (d), and not be subject to evaluation standards applied to other investments, programs, and activities, such as energy efficiency programs.

220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(f)(2)(M). CRGA also made changes to the Electric Vehicle (“EV”) Act (20 ILCS 627/45) that eliminated the large electric utilities from having to file beneficial electrification (“BE”) plans, and instead the only remaining beneficial electrification requirements for electric utilities are those set forth in the Grid Plan statute as quoted above. It is my understanding that the last year of the Company’s BE Plan 2 approved by the Commission in Docket Nos. 24-0494/24-0578, 2028, will be governed by the Electric Vehicle Act (20 ILCS 627/45), whereas years 2029-2031 will be governed by the statutory requirements for the Grid Plan set forth in new Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M) of the Act. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 2.) It is these new statutory requirements covering the 2029-2031 time period that I focus my testimony on.

1. Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i)

**Q.** What does Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i) require?

**A.** Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i) places a new emphasis on “make-ready investments and other programs to facilitate the rapid deployment of charging equipment throughout this State, especially deployment that targets medium-duty and heavy-duty vehicle electrification and multi-unit buildings.” 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(M)(i).

**Q.** Please describe how the Company proposes to satisfy the requirements of Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i) in its GP2.

**A.** Ameren witness Eric Sackett states that the Company satisfies Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i) by integrating utility-side make-ready infrastructure into its standard operations through its existing line extension policy, which reduces upfront installation costs for electric vehicle charging infrastructure and supports deployment of charging equipment. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 19.) While witness Sackett explains that this same approach was relied upon in BE Plans 1 and 2 to reduce upfront utility-side make-ready costs and asserts that no separate program is required to address this subsection, the witness further states that, under prior BE Plans, where utility-side investments were not fully covered under the line extension framework, additional BE incentives were available to address the remaining costs.[^1] Witness Sackett states that such customer-side make-ready incentives are not proposed for continuation in the post-2028 period due to cost-effectiveness considerations. Id. In addition, witness Sackett explains that the Company proposes to address medium-duty and heavy-duty vehicle electrification and multi-unit buildings through education and outreach efforts, while also providing those customers with reduced upfront costs for line and service extensions under its existing policy. Id. at 19.

[p. 19, lines 319–338] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

[^1]: In its first BE Plan, Ameren originally proposed only covering utility-side make-ready and the Commission rejected that approach and directed Ameren expand to cover customer-side make ready, finding it may increase participation in Ameren’s Rider EVCP.” Ameren Ill. Co., ICC Final Order, Docket Nos. 22-0431/22- 0443, 58 (March 23, 2023).

**Q.** How does the Company propose to use this line extension policy to reduce make-ready costs for customers?

**A.** The Company’s standard line extension policy, set forth in its Standards and Qualifications for Electric Service (Ameren Ill. Co d/b/a Ameren Ill. Standards and Qualifications for Electric Service, ILL. C. C. No. 1, 1st Revised Sheet No. 4.006 Canceling Original Sheet No. 4.006, issued Mar. 19, 2019 and effective May 3, 2019.), is intended to either reduce or fully cover utility-side make-ready costs through the application of a revenue-based allowance tied to the newly connected electric vehicle load. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 12.) As described by witness Sackett, this approach is applied through a revenue test that uses expected revenues from new EV load to offset upfront utility-side make-ready costs. Id. at 12-13.

[p. 20, lines 339–360] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

**Q.** Does the Company provide information regarding the estimated costs, level of participation, or expected benefits with its proposed make-ready approach in the 2029-2031 GP2 years?

**A.** No. The Company does not provide any information regarding the estimated costs, expected level of participation or projected benefits associated with its proposed make-ready approach for the 2029-2031 plan years. (Staff Ex. 1.01, 4.) Witness Sackett explains that make-ready investments are treated as Category 2 investments and are addressed through the Company’s existing line extension policy, which is outside the scope of the BE program budgets. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 16.) However, Ameren does not provide information quantifying the anticipated level of make-ready investment for the 2029-2031 plan years, nor does he explain how any such level of investment was determined. (Staff Ex. 1.01, 4.)

**Q.** In your opinion, does the Company’s proposed GP2 meet the requirements of Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i) for years 2029-2031?

**A.** I do not yet have enough information to make this determination. Ameren’s plan for make-ready investments is in fact not an actual plan because as discussed above, the Company relies almost entirely on an existing line extension policy for its make-ready investments. In rebuttal testimony, Ameren should provide additional details that support how this approach, on its own, is sufficient to facilitate deployment of infrastructure to accommodate the growth of EVs. Please include a detailed education and outreach strategy, measurable goals including estimated costs per project and the number of customer projects expected to be delivered as well as identifying additional efforts that will work in concert with the make-ready policy to facilitate deployment of infrastructure. The Company should also discuss whether it evaluated alternative programmatic approaches outside of the approved BE plans that could supplement its make-ready framework. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 9-11.) A make-ready investments program should be its own designated program with a detailed plan, including budgets and anticipated level of investment and address how the program is designed “to facilitate the rapid deployment of charging equipment throughout this State…” 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i).

[p. 21, lines 361–382] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

**Q.** Are there other things the Company should address in rebuttal testimony? Yes. The Company proposes to address medium-duty and heavy-duty vehicles and multi-unit buildings through education and outreach efforts; however, the Company’s approach relies on future, undefined activities to address key statutory components and those efforts are not yet developed. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 19.) As described in witness Sackett’s testimony, education and outreach efforts appear to play a central role in the Company’s overall approach to utilizing its existing line extension policy as the framework for make-ready investments. Id. The Company states “while these education and outreach efforts have not been developed, they will build upon the efforts highlighted in the BE annual reports.” Id. However, the Company has not explained how customers will be informed of or encouraged to utilize make-ready opportunities under its existing line extension policy. Id. This is particularly relevant given that prior BE Plans included customer-facing incentives that may have driven participation.

[p. 22, lines 383–404] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

**Q.** How do you recommend the Company address the concerns you have identified with respect to Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i)?

**A.** I recommend that in rebuttal testimony, the Company provide a developed and transparent plan specifically for make-ready investments demonstrating how using its existing line extension policy will support facilitation of EV infrastructure deployment in the post-2028 BE period. This should include, at a minimum, reasonable estimates of the scale of EV deployment, such as the number of customers expected to utilize make-ready infrastructure and level of investment. Additionally in rebuttal testimony, Ameren should develop and clearly describe its education and outreach strategy for this new make-ready investments program including: defined program elements, target customer segments, and an explanation of how those efforts will be implemented to support deployment, particularly for medium-duty and heavy-duty vehicles and multi-unit dwellings. The Company should also identify how these education and outreach efforts will be supported, including any associated budget, and how those efforts are expected to drive participation in make-ready opportunities.

2. Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii)

**Q.** What does Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii) require?

[p. 23, lines 405–428]

**A.** Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii) requires the Company to include the development and implementation of (1) time-of-use rates and their benefit for electric vehicle users and for all customers, (2) optimized charging programs to achieve identified savings, and (3) new contracts and compensation for services in the optimized charging programs, through signals that allow electric vehicle charging to respond to local system conditions, manage critical peak periods, serve as a demand response or peak resource, and maximize renewable energy use and integration into the grid.

220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(M)(ii).

**Q.** Please describe how the Company proposes to satisfy the requirements of Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii) in its GP2.

**A.** Witness Sackett states that the Company proposes to satisfy Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii) through its ChargeSmart and FlexCharge programs, which together provide time-of-use rates and an optimized charging offering designed to support electric vehicle adoption and grid efficiency. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 20.) Witness Sackett explains that the ChargeSmart program includes time-of-use rate elements that encourage electric vehicle charging during periods that are beneficial to the grid, including delivery credits for charging during preferred charging periods and charges that discourage charging during peak periods. Id. In addition, witness Sackett describes the FlexCharge program as an optimized charging program designed to manage electric vehicle charging load by shifting usage to times that are beneficial to the grid and reducing peak demand impacts. Id.

[p. 24, lines 429–449]

**Q.** Have you identified any issues or concerns with the Company’s proposed approach to satisfying Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii)?

**A.** No. I have not identified any concerns that the Company has failed to address Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii), as the proposed ChargeSmart and FlexCharge programs align with the types of activities contemplated by the statute. The Company provides program descriptions, participation estimates, and associated budget levels for these offerings. (Ameren Ex. 9.1, Appendix X.) However, while the Company describes program design and provides participation estimates, additional explanation regarding how participation levels translate to expected system benefits would improve transparency. These observations do not provide a basis to conclude that the Company has failed to address Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii), but they introduce some uncertainty regarding the extent to which the proposed programs will achieve the intended outcomes.

**Q.** Do you recommend any modifications to the Company’s approach to Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii)?

**A.** At this time, I do not recommend modifications to the overall structure of the Company’s proposed programs under Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii), as the ChargeSmart and FlexCharge programs align with the types of activities contemplated by the statute. However, Staff recommends that the Company provide additional explanation regarding how expected participation levels will translate into system benefits, including load shifting and peak demand reduction in rebuttal testimony. Providing this information would support a more complete evaluation of the expected outcomes associated with the proposed programs. In addition, in my opinion, there are sufficient time-of-use offerings on the horizon. In particular, the Commission required the Company to collaborate with Staff and Stakeholders while creating a strawman distribution time-of-use (“DTOU”) rate to be filed as a compliance filing within 180 days of the Final Order from Ameren’s recent Rate Design Investigation (“RDI”). Ameren Illinois Co., ICC Final Order, Docket No. 25-0083, 74 (Aug. 7, 2025). Following this Commission Order, Ameren held a series of DTOU workshops with Staff and Stakeholders and filed the compliance filing on Feb. 3, 2026. (Staff Ex. 1.04.) Lastly, CRGA requires Ameren to develop a supply time of use rate in the new Section 16-107.8 of the Act. This supply time-of-use rate is required to be filed no later than 120 days after June 1, 2026, effectively September 29, 2026.

3. Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii)

**Q.** What does Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii) require?

**A.** Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii) requires the Company to include “commercial tariffs utilizing alternatives to traditional demand-based rate structures that facilitate charging for light-duty, heavy-duty, and fleet electric vehicles.” 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(M)(ii).

**Q.** Please describe how the Company proposes to satisfy the requirements of Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii) in its GP2.

[p. 26, lines 471–492]

**A.** Witness Sackett states that the Company proposes to satisfy Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii) through its ChargeSmart program. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 20-21.) Witness Sackett also states that the Company’s ChargeSmart is designed to support public charging for light-duty, heavy-duty, and fleet EVs and provides additional rate design features, such as the elimination of off-peak demand charges and the application of rate limiter credits, for certain public charging facilities. Id. at 21.

**Q.** Have you identified any issues or concerns with the Company’s proposed approach to satisfying Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii)?

**A.** No. The ChargeSmart program includes rate design elements that align with the requirements of the statute. I would also note that the ChargeSmart program reflects a continuation of rate structures previously reviewed and approved in prior Beneficial Electrification Plans through Rider EVCP. Ameren Ill. Co d/b/a Ameren Ill., ICC Final Order, Docket Nos. 24-0494/24-0578 (Cons.), 58 (Mar. 27, 2025). Based on my review, the Company has not proposed material changes to these rate design elements that would warrant a different conclusion regarding their alignment with Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii).

4. Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)

**Q.** Please summarize your understanding of the last paragraph of Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M).

**A.** I understand this cost-effectiveness requirement to mean that these Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M) BE programs should be evaluated under Ameren’s grid plan cost- effectiveness framework and utilize the same discount rate used for grid plan investments when performing a BCA.

[p. 27, lines 493–513] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

**Q.** Does the Company demonstrate how it will minimize ratepayer impacts as required by subsection (M)?

**A.** Ameren witness Sackett identifies efforts to reduce impacts on low-income customers, including references to compliance filings addressing measures to exempt or minimize costs for those customers. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 21.) The Company’s compliance filing indicates BE plan costs for low-income customers have been reduced from approximately $1.56 per month to $0.77 per month, with further reductions under Rider ESDA resulting in estimated costs of approximately $0.62, $0.31, and $0.16 per month for customers at 50%, 100%, and 200% of the federal poverty level, respectively. (Staff Ex. 1.03, 1.)

However, the record does not clearly demonstrate how the Company’s proposed BE programs minimize impacts for the broader customer base. Witness Sackett points to the direct testimony of Ameren witness Ryan Schonhoff who points to general affordability considerations and does not isolate or quantify the impact of the Company’s BE programs or explain how its proposed approach minimizes costs relative to alternative approaches. (Ameren Ex. 15.0, 21; Ameren Ex. 6.0.) In rebuttal testimony, the Company should provide a more detailed explanation of how the proposed BE investment programs are providing benefits to its customer base while minimizing the costs associated with these infrastructure investments.

[p. 28, lines 514–529]

**Q.** Does the Company demonstrate cost-effectiveness of its proposed BE programs as required by subsection (M)?

**A.** Yes, I believe the Company has provided the data required for a cost-effectiveness evaluation for the other BE programs. Ameren indicates its proposed BE programs (excluding make-ready) fall into Category 4 of its cost-effectiveness framework and I do not disagree with that assessment. Ameren witness Cottrell provides the net benefit analysis results showing the overall ChargeSmart, FlexCharge, and total BE portfolio (excluding Make-Ready and including administrative costs) as being cost-effective. (Ameren Ex. 11.0, 10.) I recommend the Company provide in rebuttal testimony the net benefit results of these programs broken out by customer sector. Furthermore, I recommend that the Company propose a BE make-ready program in its rebuttal as I discussed above, and the Company should explain how this program fits in its cost-effectiveness framework.
