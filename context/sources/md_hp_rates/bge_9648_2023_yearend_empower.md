# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

## Program Participation and Impact Highlights

### Program Impacts

BGE's EmPOWER Maryland ("EmPOWER") portfolio of programs was successful in 2023. Overall, in 2023, BGE's EmPOWER program portfolio has exceeded its 2% goal legislatively established for the EmPOWER programs for 2023. In 2023, the EmPOWER program portfolio achieved 116% of the 2% annual MWh savings goal using only 80% of the overall 2023 program budget. With regard to the MWh savings targets established in the Company's 2021-2023 EmPOWER cycle filing, the 2023 portfolio achieved 97% of the 2023 filed target in 2023. This places the portfolio in the "On Target" category for 2023. The entire portfolio achieved over 745,000 MWh in annualized electric energy savings. Natural gas savings 2023 were over 2.6 million therms. In addition to the energy savings realized and associated environmental benefits customers generated by choosing more efficient alternatives, BGE customers received incentives and bill credits of approximately $114 million. Estimated savings on customer bills over the life of measures installed in 2023 are $365 million. Total economic benefits returned to BGE customers are summarized below.

| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----------: |
| Direct rebates paid to customers for energy efficiency measures                    |  $87,000,000 |

Avoided generation from the over 745,000 MWh 2023 portfolio savings over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed in 2023 is over 3.9 million MWh.

### Program Highlights

There are a six programs that have exceeded their 2021-2023 cycle targets by 130% (or more) that are worth noting. The programs mentioned below will be discussed in greater detail in the "Program Specifics" section of this report.

**_The following energy efficiency programs in the residential portfolio were very successful:_**

**Home Performance with ENERGY STAR**: This program continues to employ deep energy retrofits and high efficiency HVAC systems to achieve success.in 2023. HPwES has achieved 171% of the 2023 target, and overall reached 130% of the cycle savings.

**Appliance Rebate**: The Appliance Rebate Program, for 2023, realized 197% of its 2023 MWh savings goal, and 178% of its MWh cycle goal. Increased incentives and a new direct to consumer channel aided in the program's accomplishment.

BGE's Smart Thermostat Rebate Program offers a rebate of up to $100 to residential customers who purchase an ENERGY STAR certified smart thermostat through one of several market venues. Customers can obtain a post-purchase rebate after purchasing an eligible thermostat through any retailer and can choose whether to receive their post-purchase rebate via a gift card or personal check. Customers can receive an instant rebate on their purchase through BGE's Online Marketplace or an instant coupon at select retailers. BGE also offers a direct-to-consumer limited-time offer to targeted customers through a simple-to-use online platform.

BGE offers additional residential smart thermostat rebates through the HVAC midstream, New Homes, Quick Home Energy Check-up ("QHEC"), and Home Performance with ENERGY STAR ("HPwES") programs. Residential customers are eligible for up to three smart thermostat rebates (per program cycle) across all residential programs. BGE claims additional energy savings through cloud optimization services offered through eligible devices claimed through the programs mentioned above.

BGE's Small Business Energy Solutions ("SBES") Program offers smart thermostats to eligible commercial customers. Smart thermostat capabilities make the measure ideal for small businesses because there is an opportunity to significantly reduce load when the building is unoccupied.

BGE will continue to refine and enhance the evaluation and reporting for smart thermostats as participation grows, additional measures are incentivized, and other outlets are considered.

**_Home Performance with ENERGY STAR®_**

Home Performance with ENERGY STAR ("HPwES") is a national program overseen by the Department of Energy and guided by the standards of the BPI. The program's goal is to enhance residential comfort and energy efficiency by addressing the house as a system, while improving the health and safety of its occupants. Participating home performance contractors conduct comprehensive audits and make customized recommendations designed to improve the comfort and efficiency of the BGE customer's home. These recommendations are provided in an audit report that summarizes and prioritizes the opportunities for improvement. The customer decides which measures they want installed and contracts with the participating contractor to complete the job. The final project inspection and diagnostic testing ensure the contracted work was completed properly. The program provides a subsidy for the cost of the home energy audit (customer pays $100 for an audit valued at $400), direct installation of energy efficient measures, and a rebate up to $7,500 for qualifying energy efficiency improvements.

**Quick Home Energy Check-Up New Measures Pilot**

**QHEC New Measure Pilot – Final Results**

The QHEC New Measure Pilot ended in Q3 of 2023, concluding that the smart dryer control and home energy monitor are not feasible additions to the QHEC Program as direct install measures within the scope of the QHEC service. Results indicated a lack of customer interest and a lack of eligible dryers in customers' homes. The pilot was unable to provide sufficient data to address product-level savings for the dryer device. Regarding the home energy monitor, the pilot encountered meter integration challenges and the monitors were subsequently removed from the pilot. BGE consulted with meter engineers, implementers, technology providers, and the Commission's independent evaluator to identify possible substitutes, including consideration of plug-load monitors and panel-level monitoring devices. These alternatives were deemed unsuitable for the pilot.

Final quantity of installed devices and customers served: Four customers accepted the dryer measure resulting in a total of four dryer control devices installed.

Associated service calls and/or issues: No associated service calls and/or issues were reported by pilot participants. Product availability was an issue, but this was due to manufacturing and shipping delays associated with the COVID-19 pandemic, and not associated with the functionality of the products themselves.

kWh and kW savings associated with installed devices: The pilot was unable to provide sufficient data to evaluate measure-level kWh and kW savings. The Commission's independent evaluator recommended adopting findings from a similar pilot conducted by DTE Energy in Michigan. BGE appreciates the opportunity for knowledge sharing and will look to the DTE study if available for additional insight and savings recommendations.

Customer & contractor survey results: Surveys were issued to participants who accepted the measure and non-participants who declined the measure. Regular conversations were held with contractors and technicians throughout the duration of the pilot. Results point to low customer eligibility and low customer interest in the dryer control measure. Contractors found that most dryers did not qualify for the device and customers were not aware if their machines already had moisture sensing capabilities. Customers with eligible appliances voiced concerns about making changes to their appliance and declined the installation.

Behavioral trends, program recommendations, and propensity modeling based on pilot product data: Sufficient data was not available to evaluate these items, due mainly to low measure adoption and insufficient participant sample size.

BGE 2023 Year-End EmPOWER Maryland Report Page 70

[PDF p. 87 | printed p. 71]

Cross-program enrollments based on recommendations and marketing outreach: Low interest and lack of participation resulted in no verifiable cross-enrollments.

Recommendations for full-scale rollout and implementation of devices into the QHEC Program: The Program does not recommend adding the piloted devices to QHEC at this time. Aside from low customer interest and lack of eligible households, the length of time required for proper installation made the dryer control device impractical. As with all QHEC direct install measures (DIMs), technicians are required to verify all items are in good working order and operating as expected during the appointment. Applying the same level of quality assurance to the dryer sensor would require a technician to remain in the house for at least the duration of one drying cycle, possibly longer. Average installation time for the dryer device was more than 45 minutes. The advertised time for an entire QHEC is one hour. There may be ways to restructure the QHEC service to support this type of measure, but a wholesale reassessment or reimagination of the QHEC was outside the scope of this pilot.

**Notes**

**PeakRewards Energy Savings (MWh)**

The as filed Annualized Energy Savings for PeakRewards was made up of two components:

- Annualized Energy Savings (MWh) from installed smart thermostats. These energy savings are not associated with PeakRewards events; and,
- Energy savings coincident with the hours during PeakRewards calls.

Prior to 2018, Commission Staff required a mini-table, the DRDP Call Log, which included energy savings associated with the PeakRewards events. The current cycle DRDP Call Log eliminated MWh savings but instead includes MW reductions.

Accordingly, to be consistent with the requirements of the DRDP Call Log, BGE is only reporting Annualized Energy Savings from smart thermostats.

**Smart Energy Rewards Energy Savings (MWh)**

The as-filed energy savings for SER included energy savings associated with Energy Savings Days. To be consistent with the reporting for PeakRewards energy savings, as discussed above, BGE did not report energy savings coincident with event days.
