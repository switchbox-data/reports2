## II. PURPOSE AND FUNCTION OF FILING

### A. Overview of Rate Design

**Q.** Generally, what is rate design?

**A.** A rate design is a framework that establishes a balanced pricing structure that accurately reflects the overall cost of providing electric service to customers. There are two central components of a rate design – cost allocation and a rate structure.

First, the cost allocation process allocates the overall cost of providing service among customer groups, also known as customer classes, in a fair and practical manner related to the costs of providing service to each class. This involves studying how much energy different customer classes use and the facilities that are needed to meet the energy needs of the various classes. Consistent with previous Commission orders adopting Embedded Cost of Service Study ("ECOSS") models for cost allocation, ComEd utilizes an ECOSS model in rate design. See e.g., the two most recent RDI proceedings (2017, 2020), ICC Docket Nos. 17-0049 and 20-0832. This model is discussed further in the direct testimony of ComEd witness John L. Leick (ComEd Ex. 2.0).

[p. 4]

<!-- EPON: fixed-vs-volumetric — ComEd's description of a typical rate structure: kWh-based distribution facilities charge (DFC) vs kW demand charge vs fixed customer/meter charges; rates structured on usage characteristics of the class -->

Second, a rate structure recovers appropriate costs from each customer class. Rates are structured based upon the usage characteristics of the class. A typical ComEd rate structure includes a distribution facilities charge ("DFC") that can be a kilowatt-hour ("kWh") based charge determined by the amount of electricity a customer uses in a given billing period, or a kilowatt ("kW") demand charge based on the customer's highest electricity demand within a billing period, and charges set at fixed amounts for meter costs (e.g., meter maintenance, billing systems) and customer costs (e.g., customer support).

**Q.** What is ComEd's objective in its rate design structure?

<!-- EPON: goal — ComEd's general rate-design objective: cost causation and "fair, just, not discriminatory, and reasonable" rates using practical Commission-approved allocation methodologies -->

**A.** As a general matter, in establishing a rate design, ComEd takes a holistic approach and seeks to ensure that its rates reflect cost causation, are fair, just, not discriminatory, and reasonable, using practical allocation methodologies approved by the Commission.

**Q.** Do you support the rate design proposals submitted herein?

<!-- EPON: goal — rate design proposals (which include the DTOU) justified as the designs that "make the most sense to empower ComEd's customers to efficiently manage their electric consumption" and as meeting Commission directives "in the best way possible" -->

**A.** Yes. The proposals that ComEd has submitted herein are the rate designs that make the most sense to empower ComEd's customers to efficiently manage their electric consumption. ComEd submits that with the changes proposed herein, ComEd has met the Commission's directives in the best way possible. Given those directives, with the changes proposed herein, ComEd's rate design will reflect the principles of cost causation, and will result in rates that are fair, just, not discriminatory, and reasonable.

[p. 5]

**Q.** Did ComEd take any stakeholder feedback into account in developing its RDI proposals?

<!-- EPON: goal — stakeholder outreach credited for adding "innovative, customer-centric rate design components" (origin of ComEd's "customer-centric" framing) -->

**A.** Yes. As explained in more detail in both ComEd witness Leick's testimony and ComEd witness Kremer's testimony, ComEd met with numerous different stakeholders on several different occasions to discuss ComEd's proposed approaches to, and to collect feedback on, some of the rate design proposals in this proceeding. This stakeholder outreach moves ComEd and its customers toward a more balanced pricing structure that more accurately reflects the overall cost of providing electric service to customers. It also helped ComEd add innovative, customer-centric rate design components.

### B. Statutory Requirement

**Q.** Why is ComEd making this filing?

**A.** ComEd makes this filing in accordance with Section 16-105.5 of the PUA which provides the option for a separate revenue neutral RDI filing to occur no later than three years after the date on which the Commission entered the electric utility's most recent final rate design order. 220 ILCS 5/16-105.5 (a) and (c)(1). ComEd filed a Multi-Year Rate Plan on January 17, 2023, Docket Nos. 22-0486/23-0055 (cons.). The Commission issued its most recent Commission Order in a ComEd RDI on May 27, 2021, in Docket No. 20-0832 ("2020 RDI"). Accordingly, ComEd is making this separate revenue requirement neutral tariff filing after filing a Multi-Year Rate Plan and within the 3-year period elapsed since the conclusion of ComEd's last RDI.

[p. 6]

**Q.** What is a revenue requirement neutral tariff filing?

<!-- EPON: fixed-vs-volumetric — definition of "revenue requirement neutral": allocation among classes only; no change in revenue requirement; the revenue-neutrality limit later used to argue a supply TOU is out of scope -->

**A.** A revenue requirement for a given year, including any reconciliations related thereto, is the total amount the Commission approves to be collected by ComEd from customers pursuant to a docketed proceeding. A revenue requirement neutral tariff filing, like the instant Petition, has no impact on ComEd's revenue requirements; rather, it concerns the allocation of the Commission-authorized revenue requirements among ComEd's customer classes. The purpose of this filing is to assign revenue requirement responsibilities among customer classes in a reasonable manner, in accordance with cost causation principles.

**Q.** What documentation is ComEd presenting concerning the revenue requirement neutral changes in cost allocation among ComEd's customer classes?

**A.** ComEd is presenting ECOSS models with associated delivery service charges from rate design models to show how cost allocations and revenue responsibility assignment scenarios differ under proposed or potential changes to cost allocation. See e.g., ComEd Exs. 2.01 and 2.02. The ECOSS and rate design models are based upon the models submitted in ComEd's Adjusted Rate Plan dated March 15, 2024 in ICC Docket No. 24-0181, and ComEd's Refiled Grid Plan dated March 13, 2024 in ICC Docket Nos. 22-0486/23-0055.

**Q.** Is ComEd filing any revisions to Rate MRPP in this filing?

**A.** Yes. ComEd is proposing a revision to the language on Sheet No. 614 from Rate MRPP. A copy of the revised redline version is attached to my testimony as Exhibit 1.01. Other revisions to Rate MRPP related to time-of-use distribution rates are presented in the testimony of ComEd witness Kathleen Kremer (ComEd Ex. 3.0).

[p. 7]

**Q.** What is the timeline for this proceeding?

**A.** The PUA requires that the Commission provide an order "approving, or approving with modification, the proposed changes to the tariffs within 240 days after the electric utility's filing." 220 ILCS 5/16-105.5(c)(1). Therefore, the Commission must issue a final order in this proceeding by January 18, 2025.

**Q.** When will any such cost allocation or rate design changes adopted by the Commission take effect?

**A.** The PUA provides that any such changes would take effect the following year, in January 2026. 220 ILCS 5/16-105.5(c)(1) ("any changes ordered by the Commission shall become effective at the commencement of the first January monthly billing period that begins no earlier than 30 days after the Commission issues its order adopting such changes.").

## III. COMED'S ACTIONS IN RESPONSE TO PRIOR COMMISSION DIRECTIVES

**Q.** Are there any prior Commission directives that relate to ComEd's rate design?

<!-- EPON: goal — origin of the DTOU: a directive from ComEd's first Beneficial Electrification Plan (BE Plan) docket 22-0432/22-0442 requiring data on a distribution TOU offering -->

**A.** Yes. Four prior Commission directives relate to ComEd's rate design. Two of the directives originate from ComEd's 2020 RDI: the Railroad ("RR") Flow-Through Adjustment issue, and the transfer of overhead primary facilities from residential customers issue. One directive resulted from ComEd's First Beneficial Electrification Plan ("BE Plan"), Docket Nos. 22-0432 and 22-0442 (cons.). and it requires ComEd to present data on a distribution the time of use ("TOU") offering. The fourth and final directive arises from the Illinois Commerce Commission Low-Income Discount Rate Study Report to the

[p. 8]

Illinois General Assembly regarding a low-income discount ("LID").[^1] I will address each of these items in my direct testimony below. I note as a preliminary matter that only two of the four can be addressed in this RDI – the RR Flow-Through Adjustment and the TOU; the other two directives are being addressed in separate docketed proceedings.

### A. Railroad Flow-Through Adjustment

**Q.** What is the RR Flow-Through Adjustment?

**A.** The RR Flow-Through Adjustment is a revenue requirement reduction to the RR Delivery Class as a result of electricity flowing through RR-owned substations to serve non-RR customers in ComEd's service territory. This reduction took effect in 2011 after the Commission approved an adjustment amount to compensate for ComEd's use of RR facilities. Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket No. 10-0467, Final Order (May 24, 2011).

**Q.** In the 2020 RDI, what did the Commission direct with respect to the RR Flow-Through Adjustment?

**A.** The Commission issued two directives regarding the Flow-Through Adjustment associated with the RR Delivery Class. The Commission directed ComEd to "initiate [a] working group, including any additional interested stakeholders, to determine a present dollar value of compensation for prior use of RR facilities, if any, and provide a report, including an explanation for how such amount was developed, in the next rate design investigation proceeding." Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket No. 20-0832, Final Order (May 27,

[p. 9]

2021. at 19. The Commission observed that "the Flow-Through of RR Class substations has been declining as ComEd's system is reconfigured and redesigned." Id. at 18. Therefore, the Commission also directed ComEd to determine "how to reduce and/or phase-out the Flow-Through Adjustment, or, if facts warrant, to discontinue the Flow-Through Adjustment in its entirety." Id. at 18.

**Q.** How did ComEd address the Commission's directives with respect to the RR Flow-Through Adjustment amounts?

**A.** Per the Commission's directive, ComEd initiated a series of meetings with interested stakeholders to discuss and determine the present dollar value for its use of RR substations in prior years, specifically from 1958 through to 2010, and to determine how to reduce and/or phase out the Flow-Through Adjustment amount going forward. The direct testimony of ComEd witness John Leick (ComEd Ex. 2.0) explains how ComEd addressed the Commission's directive.

### B. Transfer of Overhead Primary Facilities from Residential Customers

**Q.** What did the Commission direct in the 2020 RDI with respect to the transfer of overhead primary facilities from residential customers?

**A.** Presently, residential customers own, maintain, and operate their overhead service lines where the length of the overhead primary service connection exceeds the standard 150 feet. In the 2021 RDI, the Commission directed the ICC Staff ("Staff"), ComEd, and other interested stakeholders to engage in discussions to evaluate alternatives to customer-owned residential overhead primary service connections (i.e., the transfer of overhead primary facilities from residential customers). The Commission also directed Staff to prepare a

[p. 10]

report on or within 120 days from the date of the 2020 RDI Final Order, describing the issues and setting forth the parameters of a possible future proceeding in which this issue could be addressed.

**Q.** What is the status of this directive from the 2020 RDI Final Order?

**A.** ComEd participated in a workshop hosted by Staff on July 29, 2021, which led to Staff publishing a report on this issue on September 20, 2021. The ICC then opened Docket No. 21-0719 on September 30, 2021, to allow for collaboration between the interested parties on this issue. ComEd filed its direct testimony on February 18, 2022, and Staff filed its direct testimony on August 16, 2022. ComEd filed rebuttal testimony on November 4, 2022. Since that time ComEd and Staff have been engaged in discussions to resolve the open issues in that docket.

**Q.** Does this rate design petition address the transfer of overhead primary facilities from residential customers?

**A.** No. That particular topic is being discussed in Docket No. 21-0719.

### C. Delivery Time-of-Use

**Q.** What is a delivery Time-of-Use ("TOU") rate?

<!-- EPON: midday-peak,fixed-vs-volumetric — ComEd's definition of a delivery TOU: delivery charges vary with time of delivery; higher in peak demand periods, lower in off-peak (no period hours stated here; see Kremer Ex. 3.0) -->

**A.** A delivery TOU rate is a rate structure where cost of electric delivery charges varies depending on the time of day it is delivered. Under this structure, delivery charges are higher during peak demand periods and lower during off- peak periods.

[p. 11]

**Q.** What was the Commission's directive regarding a TOU rate?

<!-- EPON: goal,ev-hp — Commission directive in BE Plan 1 Final Order (Mar. 23, 2023, at 263): develop a TOU distribution rate for residential and small commercial customers as part of the 2024 RDI -->

**A.** In the Final Order in the BE Plan Docket, the Commission directed ComEd to work with Staff and stakeholders to develop a proposed TOU distribution rate for residential and small commercial customers as part of ComEd's 2024 RDI. Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket Nos. 22-0432 / 22-0442 (Cons.), Final Order (Mar. 23, 2023) at 263.

**Q.** Is ComEd presenting proposed TOU distribution rates in this proceeding?

**A.** Yes. ComEd witness Kathleen Kremer (ComEd Ex. 3.0) explains how ComEd addressed the Commission's directives and presents ComEd's proposal for its distribution TOU rates.

### D. Low Income Discount Program

**Q.** What is the Commission directive regarding LID?

**A.** In its Low-Income Discount Rate Study Report, the Commission recommended, in part, that large electric utilities like ComEd "should offer a tariffed low-income discount rate." Illinois Commerce Commission Low- Income Discount Rate Study Report, at 63. In ICC Docket Nos. 22-0486/23-0055 (consol.), the Commission authorized ComEd to file its LID proposal separately from its RDI in order to permit implementation prior to January 2026. Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket Nos. 22-0486/23-0055 (Consol.), Final Order (Dec. 14, 2023) at 504. Accordingly, ComEd filed a petition along with direct testimony to initiate a proposal for its LID program on March 11, 2024, in ICC Docket No. 24-0163.

**Q.** Does this rate design petition address the LID?

**A.** No. ComEd's LID proposal will be determined in ICC Docket No. 24-0163.
