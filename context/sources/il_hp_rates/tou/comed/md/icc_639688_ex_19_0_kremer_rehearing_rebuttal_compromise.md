## II. COMED'S RATE BEST COMPROMISE PROPOSAL

**Q.** Generally, how did ComEd determine which modifications to make to its strawman proposal to create the Rate BEST Compromise Proposal?

**A.** As discussed by ComEd witness Perkins, ComEd has engaged in collaborative discussions with certain Intervenors regarding its strawman Rate BEST proposal. ComEd has also considered feedback provided in parties' direct testimony on rehearing. The modifications that ComEd has made to the strawman proposal to create Rate BEST reflect stakeholder input on the ease of customer use while preserving design elements that are important to a bundled time of use rate. Perkins RH Reb., ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 4:52 – 5:81. While ComEd

[p. 3, lines 43–63]

<!-- EPON: capacity-mdppec, fixed-vs-volumetric, bundling — ComEd still prefers its demand-based (kW) capacity charge but says recovering capacity in the volumetric rate is also "prudent customer-centric" -->

continues to support its original Rate BEST proposal with a demand-based capacity charge recovery mechanism, recovering these charges through the volumetric rate – as is proposed in the Rate BEST Compromise Proposal – also provides a prudent customer-centric time of use supply rate design.

**Q.** What modifications were made to the strawman proposal to result in the Rate BEST Compromise Proposal?

<!-- EPON: capacity-mdppec, midday-peak — Compromise change 1: 100% of capacity costs placed in the Mid-Day Peak period under kWh-based recovery (was a separate demand-based kW charge) -->

**A.** ComEd's Compromise Proposal is different from the strawman proposal in two ways. First, the Compromise Proposal puts 100% of the capacity costs in the Mid-Day Peak period under kWh-based recovery (also referred to as a usage-based, or volumetric rate). This approach was suggested by CUB-EDF witness McCann. See, e.g., McCann RH Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 4.0 CORR at 25:474-479. This is a modification to ComEd's proposal presented on rehearing that recovered capacity charges via a separate, demand-based kW charge.

<!-- EPON: tmc — Compromise change 2: transmission and miscellaneous procurement costs recovered through a single flat kWh rate (was PJM-pass-through kW or kWh by charge type) -->

Second, in the Compromise Proposal, the transmission and miscellaneous procurement costs are recovered based upon a single fixed kWh-based rate. A kWh recovery approach was generally suggested by ELPC-VS-UCS and CUB-EDF witnesses. See, e.g., Kenworthy RH Dir., ELPC-VS-UCS Ex. 1.0 at 8:174-176; McCann RH Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 4.0 CORR at 25:474-479. This is a modification from ComEd's proposal presented in its direct testimony on rehearing that recovered these transmission and miscellaneous services procurement charges as PJM bills ComEd – either on a kW or kWh basis, depending on the charge.

[p. 4, lines 64–82]

**Q.** Why is it reasonable to put 100% of the capacity costs in the Mid-Day Peak period under kWh-based recovery?

<!-- EPON: capacity-mdppec, midday-peak — rationale: concentrating all capacity cost in the 1–7 pm kWh rate raises the Mid-Day Peak price to strengthen the shift incentive; "apples to apples" with all-kWh supply rates -->

**A.** Placing 100% of the capacity costs in the Mid-Day Peak Period increases the \$/kWh rate in this period, thus providing greater incentive for customers to shift usage to other periods. Additionally, with the exception of Rate BESH, kWh-based recovery is used for all other supply rates in the ComEd service territory today; therefore, the provision of an entirely kWh-based Rate BEST provides for an "apples to apples" comparison of other supply rate options.

**Q.** Why is a single flat kWh charge a reasonable approach for the recovery of the transmission and miscellaneous procurement costs?

<!-- EPON: tmc — rationale for one flat kWh charge for transmission & miscellaneous procurement: customer simplicity, avoids extra billing line items -->

**A.** A single flat kWh charge is a reasonable approach for the recovery of the transmission and miscellaneous procurement costs because it provides for customer simplicity and avoids the complexity and administrative costs of additional billing line items and related billing system programming.

## III. RATE BEST TIME PERIODS

<!-- EPON: midday-peak — intervenor proposal (ELPC-VS-UCS Kenworthy) to shorten Mid-Day Peak from 1–7 pm to 4–7 pm -->

**Q.** Should the Mid-Day Peak period, currently from 1 pm to 7 pm, be shortened to 4 pm – 7 pm, as suggested by ELPC-VS-UCS Witness Kenworthy? Kenworthy RH Dir., ELPC-VS-UCS Ex. 1.0 at 9:197 – 10:202.

**A.** No. Shortening the Mid-Day Peak period would result in customer confusion, administrative challenges (and costs), and a very lopsided rate design, and therefore should not be adopted for Rate BEST.

**Q.** How would a shortened Mid-Day Peak period of 4 pm – 7 pm for Rate BEST cause customer confusion?

<!-- EPON: bundling, stacking, midday-peak — Rate BEST is "half" of a bundled TOU offering; supply and delivery (DTOU) use the identical four periods, so the 1–7 pm peak window is the same in both -->

**A.** Rate BEST is only half of ComEd's bundled time of use rate offering. Customers enrolling in Rate BEST will take delivery service under ComEd's residential Distribution Time of Use ("DTOU") rate, recently approved by the Commission in this docket. Final Order at 147. Rate BEST is specifically designed to reflect and harmonize the DTOU rate. In particular, Rate BEST uses the exact same four daily time periods. That way, a customer enrolled in the bundled TOU option only needs to be aware of the four daily time periods.

<!-- EPON: stacking, midday-peak — "set it and forget it" rationale; six-period alternative considered confusing -->

Even if a Mid-Day Peak period of 4 pm – 7 pm were adopted for Rate BEST, the DTOU rate would still have a Mid-Day Peak period from 1 pm – 7 pm. This misalignment would likely cause customer confusion. It would also undermine the objective of Rate BEST, which is to provide customers with an easy-to-use energy management tool that allows them to "set it and forget it." Instead, customers would have to remember that there are actually six daily periods, and would have to become familiar with how distribution and supply rates are designed, and which one has the largest impact on their monthly bill.

[p. 7, lines 111–131]

It would also create uncertainty as to whether the period of 1 pm – 4 pm is still a "peak" period.

**Q.** How would shortening the Mid-Day Peak cause administrative issues?

**A.** There would be several administrative issues if the Rate BEST Mid-Day Peak were to differ from the DTOU Mid-Day Peak. As part of the bundled rate, there are corresponding administrative efficiencies associated with marketing and programming the two TOU rates with the same time periods. Creating a misalignment between these two rates would result in additional administrative costs.

For example, a misalignment between the TOU rates' daily periods will require additional billing system programming. The kWhs will already be computed for 1 pm – 7 pm for the DTOU rate; under witness Kenworthy's proposal, another separate set of interval data would have to be summed for the 1 pm – 4 pm period to accommodate this smaller 4 pm – 7 pm "critical peak." There would also be additional billing line items. Both the billing line items and billing system programming would drive increased costs. Misaligned time periods would also hamper efforts to market and educate customers about the TOU rates jointly, and would also increase costs.

**Q.** Why would the shorter Mid-Day Peak create a "lopsided" Rate BEST design?

<!-- EPON: capacity-mdppec, midday-peak — ComEd's own sensitivity: compressing capacity into 4–7 pm would raise the Mid-Day Peak price to 29.880 ¢/kWh summer and 28.836 ¢/kWh nonsummer -->

**A.** A shorter Mid-Day Peak would compress the capacity costs into a smaller window, which would create a higher Mid-Day Peak price as high as 29.880 cents/kWh during the summer and 28.836 cents/kWh during nonsummer. This "lopsided" result may be a disincentive for customers to sign up for the rate.

[p. 8, lines 132–151]

**Q.** Does the existing Mid-Day Peak period of 1 pm – 7 pm provide other system benefits?

<!-- EPON: midday-peak, capacity-mdppec — 100% of PJM and ComEd peaks since 2018 fall within 1–7 pm (Ex. 17.06); capacity cost embedded in the volumetric Mid-Day Peak rate falls if peak-hour load falls -->

**A.** Yes. The current 1 pm – 7 pm Mid-Day Peak period reflects when the PJM and ComEd peaks occur. Since 2018, 100% of both the PJM and ComEd peaks have occurred within the 1 pm – 7 pm time frame. See ComEd Ex. 17.06. As ELPC-VS-UCS witness Kenworthy noted, as PJM capacity prices increase, it is important to send accurate but manageable price signals. Kenworthy RH Dir., ELPC-VS-UCS Ex. 1.0 at 13:256-257. If load is reduced during those PJM and ComEd peaks, the capacity cost contained within the volumetric rate will decrease, providing benefits to all Rate BEST customers in the form of reduced charges.

<!-- EPON: midday-peak — most peaks fall 4–7 pm but early (1–4 pm) peaks still occur -->

While the majority of peaks have occurred within the 4 pm – 7 pm period, early peaks between 1 pm – 4 pm still occur. Limiting the Mid-Day Peak period to 4 pm – 7 pm would increase the likelihood that a peak might occur outside of the Mid-Day Peak period.

**Q.** Should ComEd confine the Rate BEST Mid-Day Peak period to the summer months, as recommended by CUB-EDF witness McCann? McCann RH Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 4.0 CORR at 25:481-483.

<!-- EPON: midday-peak — rejects summer-only Mid-Day Peak; same 1–7 pm window all days and all seasons -->

**A.** No. Since ComEd intends to focus most of its customer education materials on TOU time periods, it is beneficial to maintain a consistent profile year-round. As structured, Rate BEST already provides an easily understandable, simple year-round model. Customers will learn to avoid the Mid-Day Peak period on all days and in all seasons. They won't need to adopt new behaviors based on the season.

[p. 9, lines 152–170]

**Q.** Is ComEd open to revisiting the time periods for Rate BEST in the future?

<!-- EPON: midday-peak — ComEd acknowledges solar is shifting system peaks to later hours -->

**A.** Yes. With more solar installations coming online, ComEd has seen system peaks shift to later hours in recent years. ComEd will continue to monitor trends and ComEd remains open to revisiting the time periods, if warranted.

## IV. RATE BEST IMPLICATIONS FOR SPECIFIC CUSTOMERS

**Q.** How will Net Metering customers be credited for excess generation under the Rate BEST Compromise Proposal? See Wright RH Dir., ICEA-RESA Ex. 1.0RH at 5:100-103.

<!-- EPON: capacity-mdppec, midday-peak — net-metering export credit equals the full hourly supply charge, including all capacity cost in the Mid-Day Peak rate -->

**A.** Net Metering customers who are eligible for Rate BEST will receive the value of their excess generation, calculated by multiplying the kWh sent to the grid in each hour multiplied by the applicable Electricity Supply Charge (Morning, Mid-Day Peak, etc.) for that hour. This will include full compensation of all of the capacity costs in the Mid-Day Peak period from 1 pm – 7 pm.

<!-- EPON: capacity-mdppec — worked example: capacity component \$0.13432/kWh inside the \$0.18219/kWh Mid-Day Peak rate; energy portion \$0.48 vs capacity \$1.34 per 10 kWh -->

For example, assume that the Rate BEST prices include a capacity component equal to \$0.13432/kWh in the Mid-Day Peak period. If a net metering customer generates 10 kWhs in the Mid-Day Peak period during a nonsummer month [sic; the \$0.18219 rate used is the Figure 1 _summer_ Mid-Day Peak charge of 18.219 ¢/kWh; the nonsummer charge is 16.814 ¢/kWh], they would receive a credit of \$1.82 (10 kWhs x \$0.18219) based upon the illustrative rates provided in Figure 1. In that example, \$0.48 of the credit would be related to energy and \$1.34 would be related to capacity.

[p. 10, lines 171–182]

**Q.** Will customers receiving the Low-Income Discount be eligible to enroll in Rate BEST?

<!-- EPON: goal — Rate BEST open to low-income-discount customers; paired with ComEd's LID program -->

**A.** Yes. Rate BEST is intended to be a tool to assist all customers, including low-income customers, in managing their energy usage, along with other programs ComEd offers. See also Perkins RH Reb., ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 6:103 – 7:111. As CUB-EDF witness McCann mentions, Rate BEST, when combined with ComEd's recently-approved LID program, will provide financial support to low-income customers while preserving the price signals needed for an effective TOU rate. See McCann RH Direct, CUB-EDF Ex. 4.0 CORR at 24:457 – 25:473.
