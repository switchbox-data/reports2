## Summary of Issues and Major Conclusions

- The Commission should accept BGE's proposed two-step revenue allocation, which uses results from the Company's 2024 electric cost of service study ("ECOSS") and moves customer classes' relative rates of return ("RROR") closer to the system average.
  - Staff's proposed four step electric revenue allocation process should be rejected as it is overly elaborate and achieves substantially similar results as the Company's two step revenue allocation.
  - The Commission should balance any reduction in Schedule T revenue with the overall revenue requirement to ensure that other customers are not disproportionately affected.
- The Commission should approve the Company's proposed Customer Charge increases for Schedules R and G. The Company's proposal moves Customer Charges closer to those supported by the Company's ECOSS and aligns similarly situated customers in Schedules R/RL and Schedules G/GS.

## II. ELECTRIC REVENUE ALLOCATION

[PDF page 5; printed page 2]

**Q. WOULD YOU PLEASE DESCRIBE STAFF WITNESS DELGADO'S PROPOSAL FOR THE FOUR-STEP ALLOCATION OF THE ELECTRIC REVENUE INCREASE?**

A. Staff Witness Delgado proposes a four-step allocation method based on the results of Staff Witness Bhave's electric cost of service study ("ECOSS") and using the recommended revenue requirement of Staff Witness Ostrander. Staff Witness Delgado states in his Direct Testimony that his overall rate design goal is to move classes to the relative rate of return (the "RROR"), between 0.90 and 1.10 and avoid moving existing "steady-state" classes away from this +/-10 percent band.[^1] He uses a four-step process to achieve these goals while ensuring classes remain within the 0.90 to 1.10 RROR range.[^2] In step one, Staff Witness Delgado excludes "highly" over-earning classes (Schedules T, PL, and GS) with a RROR of more than 1.75 and reduces Schedule T's revenues by $600,000. In the second step, classes with an RROR between 1.0 and 1.75 are allocated the system percentage increase in revenue in proportion to each class's test year base distribution revenue at current rates. In the third step of his proposed revenue allocation, Staff Witness Delgado allocates revenue to the under-earning classes, which he identifies as residential Schedule R and Schedule RL, by a factor of 1.15 multiplied by the system percentage increase in distribution revenue which results in a revenue increase of 1.09%. In the final and fourth step, Staff Witness Delgado

[PDF page 6; printed page 3]

allocates the remaining revenue to all rate classes excluding classes with an RROR above 1.75 based on each class's proportion of test year base distribution revenue at current rates.[^3]

**Q. DO YOU AGREE WITH STAFF WITNESS DELGADO'S FOUR STEP REVENUE ALLOCATION METHODOLOGY?**

A. No. I respectfully disagree with Staff Witness Delgado's proposed four-step revenue allocation because it is overly elaborate and complicated. The proposed four-step process can be achieved in two steps because the final result of Staff Witness Delgado's proposed revenue allocation is largely consistent with the result of my two-step proposal, as shown in Table 1, which compares the percentage of revenue allocated to each class under each approach.

As stated in my Direct Testimony, the Commission has approved a two-step revenue allocation process in Case Nos. 9326, 9406, 9484, 9645 and 9692.[^4] In Case No. 9692, the Company's last base rate proceeding, the Commission stated that it "is concerned, however, by the elaborate approach taken by Staff, which may prove difficult to rely on as a principled methodology in future cases."[^5] Furthermore, unlike the Company's approach, which relies on a targeted dollar adjustment, Staff's methodology is dependent upon a specific revenue requirement and system-average percentage increase. If the Commission adopts a different revenue requirement, the multipliers and resulting allocations no longer achieve Staff Witness Delgado's stated objectives without further modification. I recommend that the Commission continue to approve a two-step revenue allocation process for BGE because the two-step methodology is transparent, repeatable, consistent with prior Commission orders, and achieves similar allocation outcomes.

**Q. WHAT IS YOUR POSITION ON THE REVENUE REDUCTION MR. DELGADO PROPOSES FOR SCHEDULE T?**

A. I do not oppose Staff Witness Delgado's recommendation that Schedule T receive a revenue reduction. The Company is supportive of a decrease in revenues for Schedule T; however, any decrease in Schedule T revenues should be balanced with the overall revenue requirement awarded. Staff proposes a reduction of $600,000 to Schedule T, which is about 5% of the overall Staff proposed revenue requirement. In Case No. 9692, the Commission's approved reduction of $600,000 to Schedule T was 0.5% of the overall revenue requirement

[PDF page 8; printed page 5]

in 2024.[^6] While I do not object to a reduction in Schedule T's revenue requirement, it should be weighed against the overall revenue requirement since that reduction for Schedule T will increase the amount to be recovered by other rate schedules.

**Q. STAFF WITNESS DELGADO PROPOSES IN STEP THREE OF HIS REVENUE ALLOCATION A MULTIPLYING FACTOR OF THE SYSTEM PERCENTAGE INCREASE IN DISTRIBUTION REVENUE OF 1.15 TIMES THE SYSTEM AVERAGE INCREASE TO SCHEDULES R AND RL. DO YOU SUPPORT THIS PROPOSAL?**

A. No. Setting a factor of 1.15 is not grounded in any factual or constructive context that guides under-earning classes towards achieving parity with the system average rate of return in a reasonable time frame.[^7] Staff Witness Delgado's proposed third step multiplying factor is entirely dependent upon the overall revenue increase recommended by Staff Witness Ostrander. Staff Witness Delgado's proposal will not function as he intends it to if a higher or lower revenue requirement than that proposed by Staff Witness Ostrander is approved, as the multiplier would need to be adjusted up or down accordingly to achieve Staff Witness Delgado's intended results. For these reasons, I oppose Staff Witness Delgado's proposed step three multiplier which is applied to the revenue allocation of Schedule R and Schedule RL. The Company's proposal is based on a targeted approach that aims to move under-earning classes incrementally closer to parity. A set dollar amount is a defined and straightforward way to allocate the revenue requirement to under-earning rate classes as it will not fluctuate based on the results of the overall award in this case. The Company

[PDF page 9; printed page 6]

excluded Schedule RL from the step one revenue allocation because its current RROR falls within the +/-10% reasonableness band. The Company continues to believe that excluding Schedule RL from the step one allocation is appropriate and consistent with the objectives of its revenue allocation methodology.

**Q. DO YOU AGREE WITH STAFF WITNESS DELGADO'S PROPOSAL TO EXCLUDE SCHEDULE GS FROM THE REVENUE ALLOCATION?**

A. No. While Schedule GS is currently overearning, its level of overearning is less significant than that of Schedule PL and Schedule T. As discussed in my Direct Testimony, Schedule G and Schedule GS have a similar relationship to R and RL. The two small commercial rate schedules have the same eligibility requirements and customers may elect service under either schedule. Customers that take service on Schedule G can choose to switch to Schedule GS or vice versa. Excluding Schedule GS from the step two allocation while including Schedule G may result in similarly situated customers being treated differently in this proceeding. Therefore, the Company recommends that both Schedules G and GS be included in the step two allocation based on their comparable RRORs.

**Q. WHAT ARE THE RECOMMENDATIONS OF WALMART WITNESS MARCHAND RELATED TO THE ELECTRIC REVENUE ALLOCATION PROCESS?**

A. Walmart Witness Marchand does not oppose the Company's proposed revenue allocation.[^8] He states that if the Commission approves a revenue requirement less than proposed by the

[PDF page 10; printed page 7]

Company, the reduction should be applied in a manner that further moves classes towards cost of service.[^9]

**Q. DO YOU AGREE WITH THE RECOMMENDATIONS OF WALMART WITNESS MARCHAND RELATED TO THE ALLOCATION OF THE ELECTRIC REVENUE REQUIREMENT?**

A. Yes, in part. The Company supports moving closer to a situation where costs are borne by the appropriate customers, as shown in the results of the Company's proposed ECOSS. The revenue allocation proposed by the Company aims to move classes towards the system average RROR and reduce the inter-class inequities. Therefore, the Company agrees that the step one allocation should remain as proposed, even if there is a reduction in the revenue requirement awarded. However, if there is a reduction in BGE's proposed revenue requirement, the Company proposes that the second step should still allocate the remaining deficiency to all classes based on each class's proportion of revenue after step one. If the Commission were to authorize a revenue requirement less than the amount of the step one allocation, the step one allocation should be adjusted to 50% of the total revenue requirement so that the overall allocations result in logical step two allocations. This would avoid an allocation in the first step that was greater than the overall revenue requirement which would require reductions to all other classes in remaining allocation steps.

**Q. PLEASE SUMMARIZE THE RESPONSE OF DOD-FEA WITNESS GORMAN REGARDING THE COMPANY'S PROPOSED ELECTRIC REVENUE ALLOCATION.**

DOD-FEA Witness Gorman states that the class revenue allocation should be designed to bring all classes closer to cost of service using gradual movement .[^21] DOD-FEA Witness Gorman states that the Company's proposed class revenue spread fails to move classes closer to the cost of service in the current proceeding[^22] and in past proceedings because there has continued to be little to no movement in Schedule R's RROR.[^23] He proposes to make modifications to the Company's two step revenue allocation to include both Schedule R and Schedule RL in the first step of the revenue allocation, moving Schedule R to an RROR of 0.90 and Schedule RL to an RROR of 1.0.[^24] The second step would remain consistent with

**Q. DO YOU AGREE WITH THE RECOMMENDATIONS OF WITNESS GORMAN?**

A. No, not entirely. The Company's proposed two-step revenue allocation methodology is designed to move all rate schedules closer to their respective cost of service. Schedule R's lack of improvement over time is not attributable to the Company's two-step revenue allocation methodology. Rather, the amount allocated to Schedule R in step one, pursuant to Commission Orders issued in prior proceedings[^26], was lower than the amount proposed by the Company in those proceedings, resulting in a lack of improvement in Schedule R's RROR.

As discussed in my Direct Testimony, Schedule R experienced meaningful improvement in its RROR only once in the last 15 years, in Case No. 9326, the Commission directed that Schedule R receive a step one increase equal to 50% of the overall revenue increase. Since Case No. 9355, however, Schedule R's RROR has continued to deteriorate.[^27]

The Company's revenue allocation proposal in this proceeding is intended to move Schedule R halfway toward an RROR of 0.90. This gradual approach advances Schedule R toward the system rate of return while mitigating potential rate shock for customers.

As previously stated, the Company excluded Schedule RL from the step one revenue allocation because its current RROR falls within the +/-10% reasonableness band. The

[PDF page 14; printed page 11]

Company continues to believe that excluding Schedule RL from the step one allocation is appropriate and consistent with the objectives of its revenue allocation methodology.

**Q. DO YOU AGREE WITH OPC WITNESS NELSON'S STEP ONE REVENUE INCREASE THAT 10% OF THE OVERALL REVENUE REQUIREMENT SHOULD BE ALLOCATED TO SCHEDULE R?**

[PDF page 15; printed page 12]

A. No. As previously stated, the Company's step one revenue allocation proposal is based on a targeted approach that aims to move under-earning classes incrementally closer to parity. A set dollar amount is a more defined way to allocate the revenue requirement to under-earning rate classes as it will not fluctuate based on the results of the overall award in this case. ## III. ELECTRIC CUSTOMER CHARGE

**Q. WHAT ARE THE PROPOSED ELECTRIC CUSTOMER CHARGES IN THIS PROCEEDING?**

A. The Company proposes a residential, Schedule R and Schedule RD, Customer Charge of $11.00 and a Schedule G Customer Charge of $17.20. Both Staff Witness Delgado and

[PDF page 16; printed page 13]

OPC Witness Nelson recommend that the Customer Charge not be increased.[^31] Staff Witness Delgado does not provide an explanation for his recommendation to maintain the current customer charges. OPC Witness Nelson asserts that increasing the residential Customer Charge is inconsistent with the Commission's policies of energy conservation and energy efficiency.[^32] Additionally, he argues that increasing fixed charges has a disproportionate bill impact on low-usage customers, who also tend to be low-income customers.[^33]

**Q. DO YOU AGREE WITH STAFF WITNESS DELGADO'S AND OPC WITNESS NELSON'S PROPOSED CUSTOMER CHARGES RECOMMENDATIONS?**

A. No. Customer charges are intended to recover a portion of the fixed costs BGE incurs to provide electric service to customers. These costs typically include customer-related expenses such as the costs of services, meters, meter reading, and customer service. As discussed in my Direct Testimony, when fixed costs, or costs associated with connecting and maintaining a customer's account, are not recovered through fixed charges, intra-class inequities are created or allowed to persist. This occurs because customers with higher-than-average usage are subsidizing the fixed costs of serving those customers with lower-than-average usage. Staff Witness Delgado's and OPC Witness Nelson's focus on maintaining the Customer Charge at its current level is inconsistent with cost causation principles and undermines the important effort to reduce intra-class inequities. Additionally, as Company Witness Manuel explains in his Direct Testimony, current

[PDF page 17; printed page 14]

customer charges are well below the customer-related costs reflected in the results of the Company's ECOSS.[^34]

**Q. HAS THE COMMISSION ALSO CONSIDERED THE PRINCIPLE OF COST CAUSATION IN PRIOR CUSTOMER CHARGE INCREASES FOR RESIDENTIAL CUSTOMERS?**

A. Yes. The Commission has repeatedly recognized the principle of cost causation and has approved increases to the residential customer charge in several of the Company's prior base rate proceedings, including the Company's recent multi-year rate plan ("MYP") proceedings, Case Nos. 9645 and 9692. In both proceedings, the Commission approved customer charge increase for Schedules R and RD that are comparable to those proposed in this proceeding. In Case No. 9645, the Commission found the Company's proposed increase to be "reasonable" stating that it "balances cost causation against the principle of gradualism, while continuing to provide an incentive for customers to conserve and have more control over their bills."[^35] The Commission reaffirmed that approach in Case No. 9692, finding that the approved customer charge increase was a reasonable balance between the need to reflect costs causation and the need for gradualism.[^36] The Commission also recognized in both Case Nos. 9645 and 9692 that the increase in the customer charges remained below the level of customer-related costs reflected in the Company's ECOSS.[^37] Consistent with that finding, in Case No. 9692, the Commission found "a policy of zero movement on customer charges that are already well below the estimated cost of service

[PDF page 18; printed page 15]

would unreasonably elevate one principle of ratemaking above all others and would result, given rising rates overall, in an effective decrease in the customer charge, moving it still further out of alignment with cost causation."[^38] Thus, the Company's proposal is consistent with recent Commission precedent.

**Q. WILL THE COMPANY'S PROPOSAL TO INCREASE ITS SCHEDULES R AND RD CUSTOMER CHARGES DISPROPORTIONATELY IMPACT LIMITED-INCOME CUSTOMERS AS ASSERTED BY OPC WITNESS NELSON?**

A. No. The Company's proposal would not have a significant impact on limited-income customer bills. The Company tracks usage for limited-income customers identified by the Maryland Office of Home Energy Programs ("OHEP") as receiving energy assistance. On average, the usage is not significantly different between limited-income customers and customers who are not identified as having limited income.[^39] Therefore, the bill impact of my proposed gradual increase of the residential Customer Charge aligns the rate design with cost causation and is not disproportionate for limited-income customers as OPC Witness Nelson contends. Rather, the proposal appropriately advances cost causation principles while maintaining a reasonable and gradual transition.

**Q. PLEASE SUMMARIZE THE RATE DESIGN PROPOSALS FOR SCHEDULE GL.**

A. The Company has proposed to allocate 55% of the revenue increase to the Demand Charge, and 45% to the Delivery Service Charge for Schedule GL.

Staff Witness Delgado recommends no changes to Schedule GL demand charges; however, he provides no explanation or support for that recommendation.[^40]

In contrast, Walmart Witness Marchand contends that the Company could go further to improve the rate design for Schedule GL. Specifically, he recommends that the Commission accept the Customer Charge proposed by the Company and allocate all of the remaining revenue increase be applied to the Demand Charge.[^41] According to Walmart Witness Marchand, recovering demand-related costs through energy charges violates cost causation principles.[^42] He argues that shifting demand-related costs through energy

[PDF page 20; printed page 17]

charges shifts the demand cost responsibility from lower load factor customers to higher load factor customers.[^43]

**Q. PLEASE RESPOND TO WALMART WITNESS MARCHAND'S SCHEDULE GL RATE DESIGN PROPOSAL.**

As stated in my Direct Testimony, the Company aims to gradually move the rate design for these schedules closer to the level supported by the ECOSS.[^44] While the Company agrees with Walmart Witness Marchand that there are cost causative reasons to increase the Demand Charge at a higher percentage than the Delivery Service Charge, the Company does not support his recommendation to allocate 100% of the revenue increase to the Demand Charge because it is not a sufficiently gradual enough transition.

The Company's approach towards allocating less than 100% of the revenue increase to the Demand Charge for Schedule GL is consistent with the rate design that was accepted by the Commission in Case Nos. 9406, 9326, 9299, 9230, 9645, and 9692 in Order Nos. 87591, 86060, 85374, 83714, 89678, and 90948, respectively.

### B. Schedule RD On to Off-Peak Ratio

[PDF page 23; printed page 20]

**Q. PLEASE DESCRIBE OPC WITNESS NELSON'S ISSUE WITH THE SCHEDULE RD ON-PEAK AND OFF-PEAK RATIO.**

A. OPC Witness Nelson is concerned that the peak to off-peak price ratio may be increasing and, if the methodology previously approved by the Commission is maintained in this proceeding, that the lower peak to off-peak price ratio could be lost before there is opportunity to increase enrollment in Schedule RD.[^45] OPC Witness Nelson recommends that the Company provide the peak to off-peak ratio in its rebuttal testimony.[^46]

**Q. PLEASE RESPOND TO OPC WITNESS NELSON'S CONCERNS REGARDING THE PEAK TO OFF-PEAK PRICE RATIO.**

A. As discussed in my Direct Testimony, Schedule RD was established through the Public Conference 44 ("PC44"). The initial rate structure of Schedule RD recovered all primary distribution costs through the peak distribution rate. In the DRIVE proceeding, Case No. 9761, the Company proposed, and the Commission approved in Order No. 91917, to shift 40% of the primary distribution costs to be recovered in all hours and the remaining 60% to be recovered through peak hours because the initial peak rate served as a barrier for customers to take service under Schedule RD. Based on the rates effective at the time, the on to off-peak ratio on a total bill basis was 2.8 to 1 compared to 3.2 to 1. The Company's Schedule RD rate design in this proceeding is still well below the 3.2 to 1 ratio that existed prior to the 40/60 rate shaping. In fact, the peak to off-peak ratio on a total basis under the Company's proposal is 2.6 to 1[^47] which is lower than the ratio when the 40/60 rate shaping

[PDF page 24; printed page 21]

methodology was approved in 2025. The Company believes that the on- to off-peak ratio is still at a level to promote enrollment in Schedule RD while also providing enough of a price signal to encourage a shift in usage to off-peak hours.

### C. Residential Heat Pump Rate Schedule

**Q. PLEASE DESCRIBE THE PROPOSAL FOR HEAT PUMP RATE DESIGN FROM CCAN WITNESS VELEZ.**

A. CCAN Witness Velez proposes that the Company create a new residential rate schedule for electric customers who use a heat pump for heating and cooling which would reflect a lower delivery service charge.[^48] CCAN Witness Velez also proposes that the rate schedule reflect lower transmission and EmPOWER rates.[^49] CCAN Witness Velez's testimony addresses whether generation, distribution, transmission and EmPOWER costs are consistent with cost causation for customers with heat pumps.[^50]

**Q. IS THE REVIEW OF GENERATION, TRANSMISSION AND EMPOWER APPROPRIATE IN THIS PROCEEDING?**

A. No. Review of generation, transmission, and EmPOWER costs is not appropriate and is outside the scope of a distribution base rate proceeding because these costs are recovered through separate regulatory mechanisms or proceedings and, in the case of transmission, are largely subject to federal regulatory oversight.

**Q. DOES THE COMPANY CURRENTLY HAVE ANY BARRIERS IN IMPLEMENTING AN ELECTRIC HEAT-PUMP SPECIFIC RATE SCHEDULE?**

[PDF page 25; printed page 22]

A. Yes. The Company currently does not have specific knowledge of customer heating sources and does not have sufficient data to support an electric heat-pump rate design for customers in BGE's service territory at this time. CCAN Witness Velez's analysis relies on BGE data identifying "heating" and "non-heating" indications that the Company uses for internal classifications and not on actual verified BGE heat-pump customers. To determine whether a customer qualifies for an electric heat-pump-specific rate schedule, the Company would need a Commission-approved mechanism for verifying that the customer meets the eligibility requirements for the rate. For example, under Schedule EV, BGE developed a Commission-approved process requiring proof of purchase or lease of an electric vehicle and installation of a smart meter capable of measuring hourly time-of-use data. Similarly, implementation of an electric heat pump-specific rate schedule would require a Commission-approved verification process that BGE would need to utilize to determine if a customer installed a high-efficiency heat-pump at the home and met eligibility requirements to enroll under the new rate schedule. In addition, if electric supply time-of-use ("TOU") pricing were approved by the Commission, participants would be required to have smart meters capable of measuring hourly TOU data. Overall consideration of a heat-pump rate design would be better considered in a more comprehensive proceeding, potentially at the state level.
