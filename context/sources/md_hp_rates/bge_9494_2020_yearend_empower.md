## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Program Participation and Impact Highlights

#### Program Impacts

2020 has been a very productive and successful year for BGE’s EmPOWER Maryland (“EmPOWER”) program, despite COVID-19. The achievements of the Commercial and Industrial program portfolio helped lead the way to this success. During 2020, the EmPOWER program portfolio has achieved 134% of the 2% annual target savings goal using only 73% of the overall 2020 program budget. The entire portfolio achieved over 856,000 MWh in annualized electric energy savings. Natural gas savings were over 4.8 million therms. Avoided generation from these savings over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed during 2020 is over 6.5 million MWh. This represents a reduction of over 4.6 million metric tons of carbon dioxide gas emissions according to the EPA Greenhouse Gas Equivalents Calculator, over 2.5 million tons of coal not burned or over 1 million vehicles not driven for a year. In addition to the energy savings realized and associated environmental benefits customers generated by choosing more efficient alternatives, BGE customers received incentives and bill credits of approximately $93 million. Estimated savings on customer bills over the life of measures installed during 2020 are $685 million. Total economic benefits returned to BGE customers are summarized below.

The EmPOWER cycle performance has been outstanding. For the 2018-2020 cycle, the EmPOWER program portfolio has achieved 127% of the 2% annual target savings goal using only 83% of the overall 2018-2020 program budget. The entire portfolio achieved over 2.4 million MWhs in annualized electric energy savings. Natural gas savings were over 16 million therms. Avoided generation from these savings over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed during the 2018-2020 cycle is over 18 million MWh. This represents a reduction of over 12.8 million metric tons of carbon dioxide gas emissions according to the EPA Greenhouse Gas Equivalents Calculator, over 7 million tons of coal not burned or over 2.7 million vehicles not driven for a year. BGE customers received incentives and bill credits of approximately $302 million over the cycle. Estimated savings on customer bills over the life of measures installed during the 2018-2020 cycle are approximately $1.9 billion.

#### Program Highlights

A few program accomplishments are worth noting at this point. The programs mentioned below will be discussed in greater detail in the “Program Specifics” section of this report.

The residential portfolio of energy efficiency programs had the following stand-out programs. These four programs met or exceeded their MWh targets for both 2020 and the 2018-2020 cycle.

**Lighting:** The Lighting Program continued its strong performance into 2020. Special promotional pricing on multi-pack configurations, pallet displays, and other off shelf product placements resulted in robust sales activity in 2020. Distribution to non-profits, including the Maryland Food Bank, helped the program succeed.

**New Construction:** The New Construction Program faired very well in 2020, due to a strong pipeline of new construction. This program accomplished a significant milestone in 2020, incentivizing its 25,000th home.

**Smart Energy Manager (“SEM”):** The SEM Program achieved strong results in 2020 to out-perform its 2020 target savings. The defaulting of new BGE customers into receiving high usage alert (“HUA”) emails helped to increase these savings. Additionally, the SEM Program launched the Play-and-Save Gamification Pilot, which is designed to utilize advanced metering infrastructure (“AMI”) data within BGE’s mobile app to educate customers about their energy consumption and encourage energy reduction behaviors. The SEM Program has yet to see any impact on program performance related to the COVID-19 pandemic.

**Appliance Rebate:** The Appliance Rebate Program exceeded its 2020 MWh target early in 2020. The move to the ENERGY STAR® (“Energy Star”) Retail Products Program (“ESRPP”) in 2019 for various appliance measures (clothes washers, clothes dryers, and refrigerators) helped to bring about the program’s success. In the latter half of 2020, BGE started incentivizing online ESRPP sales with a “ship-to” zip code in the BGE service territory, which helped to contribute to the program’s savings.

The commercial and industrial portfolio of energy efficiency programs had the following noteworthy results for 2020. These four programs exceeded their MWh targets for both 2020 and the 2018-2020 cycle.

**Prescriptive:** The Prescriptive Program exceeded its 2020 MWh target early in 2020. The program’s success can be attributed to a concentrated effort to promote lighting fixtures and controls replacements, and a more focused marketing campaign, which has resulted in a strong pipeline of active projects.

**Small Business Program:** The Small Business Program ran successfully in 2020 while adhering to all COVID-19 safety protocols. LED fixture, lamps and retrofits comprised the bulk of savings achieved.

**Midstream Products:** The Instant Discount (Midstream) Program incentivizes commercial businesses to choose efficient options for their building maintenance. 2020 was a very successful year for this program. The majority of program savings derived from LED and TLED installs/replacements.

#### COVID-19 Impacts and Program Modifications

The PSC, by Order No. 89669 on November 25, 2020, directed the EmPOWER Utilities[^1] to include in their year-end reports details regarding impacts and modifications to their respective programs due to COVID-19. To ensure customer and contractor safety amid rapidly changing guidance from the CDC, BGE temporarily suspended EmPOWER programs where BGE contractors must enter a customer’s home or place of business. Other programs were modified to adhere to COVID-19 protocol and all programs were cautiously reopened through the year. Even with the difficulties presented by COVID-19, the EmPOWER program was still able to meet its overall Residential and Commercial portfolio goals. The Residential portfolio (including the behavioral program) achieved 123% of the forecasted target MWh savings in 2020, while using 59% of the forecasted budget. The Commercial portfolio achieved 158% of the forecasted target MWh savings in 2020, while using 103% of its budget. Overall, in 2020, the EmPOWER suite of programs achieved 121% of target using 73% of the forecasted budget. The innovative approaches/modifications made in response to COVID-19 are detailed in each of the affected program’s narratives in this report.

#### Residential Energy Efficiency Portfolio

The residential energy efficiency portfolio exceeded its 2020 goal in light of many challenges due to COVID-19. While COIVD-19 created difficulties, it also offered opportunities to meet BGE customers’ needs in new ways. The residential portfolio delivered gross wholesale MWh savings of over 209,000 MWh or 113% of the goal across all measures while only spending 73% of the planned incentive budget.

The Lighting, Appliance Rebate, and New Construction programs all exceeded their annual savings targets. The Quick Home Energy Check-Up (“QHEC”), Home Performance with Energy Star ("HPwES"), and Appliance Recycling programs were all paused for a period in 2020 as a result of COVID-19. These in-home programs reopened in the third quarter of 2020 and did fall short of annual savings goals for 2020. The HVAC and Smart Thermostat programs fell below the goal. While these programs were not paused, marketing tactics, consumer behavior, and inventory issues due to COVID-19 and lower savings on measures contributed to the savings shortfall.

Critical successes in 2020 included:

- BGE's Residential Appliance Rebate Program achieved 299% of the 2020 MWh savings goal in 2020.
- The Lighting Program achieved 127% of the 2020 MWh savings goal for 2020.

Challenges in 2020 included:

- The pause of in-home programs included QHEC, HPwES, and Appliance Recycling. BGE and its implementor started reopening the programs during the summer with enhanced safety measures to protect customers and technicians going into customer homes.
- There were changing customer purchasing patterns throughout the year. These changes led to higher than usual demand for lighting, appliances and new construction. As these patterns shifted, BGE worked to meet the customer where they were shopping.
- Supply chain issues posed unique challenges for different programs. This included shortages of HVAC equipment and appliances at times.

For the 2018-2020 cycle, BGE's Residential programs exceeded goals of achieving 117% of the cycle goal, while spending 84% of the incentive budget. BGE continues to work with its implementors to address program and COVID-19 challenges while delivering savings to customers.

#### Pilot Updates

**Smart Home Pilot**

For the 2018-2020 EmPOWER cycle, BGE and three other Maryland utilities developed smart home pilots. Each utility sought to test different hypotheses with their respective pilots. BGE's pilot focused kWh savings that could be realized through self-installed smart home devices that included smart plugs, smart lighting, and other sensors. The full report for the BGE Smart Home Pilot can be found in Attachment 1 – BGE Smart Home Pilot Final Report. This attachment displays the full report for the pilot (referenced in Appendix D).

**Advanced Evaluation, Measurement and Verification Behavioral Disaggregation Pilot**

Last year, BGE launched an experiment to test whether disaggregated energy insights can be used to increase (1) program participation in other EmPOWER programs, (2) digital engagement with the email Home Energy Report (HER), and (3) customer satisfaction. The pilot leveraged customers’ AMI data and machine learning (appliance disaggregation and detection) to identify HER recipients most likely to benefit from a particular EmPOWER program. That customer segment was then divided into a treatment and control group: the treatment group received a promotion for the EmPOWER program alongside their appliance disaggregation insights in the eHER and the control group only received the relevant EmPOWER program promotion in the eHER.

The pandemic and subsequent suspension of many of BGE’s energy efficiency programs delayed the launch of this pilot. BGE waited to launch the pilot to ensure customer and contractor safety amid rapidly changing guidance from the CDC.

The BGE pilot treatment campaigns reached over 40,000 unique customers in the first two campaigns. As of January 2021, only two of the three campaigns have been completed, with the second campaign having just sent its post campaign communication. As additional data is ingested and analyzed for the second campaign, the results may differ from the current analysis.

**Preliminary Results**

The program participation rate was higher among the customers targeted for this pilot compared to the general population of customers in BGE’s HER program.[^3] This demonstrates that appliance disaggregation combined with customer segmentation is helpful to identify good candidates to target for specific EmPOWER programs. However, among the treatment and control groups of those customers in the pilot, BGE did not observe any significant difference in the program participation rate.

No statistically significant difference of click rates was observed between the treatment and control groups of the pilot. Among open-rates of the follow-up communication for each campaign, approximately one-quarter were from customers who had not opened the original promotion communication. Interest in the programs may be sensitive to program context and the timing of promotion engagement.

Customer interest in these communications was high, as measured by digital user feedback modules in the follow-up communication, although there was no statistically significant difference in customer interest between treatment and control customers. Knowing this, BGE can consider omitting the user feedback module in future campaigns in favor of a call-to-action focused on promoting participation in other EmPOWER programs.

This preliminary analysis will be updated ahead of the Spring 2021 EmPOWER Maryland semi-annual hearing and BGE will provide a full pilot report out in the 2021 mid-year semi-annual report. BGE looks forward to leveraging lessons learned from the pilot for the new 2021–2023 EmPOWER Maryland cycle.

**Automated Window Shading System Pilot**

COVID-19 paused the Automated Window Shading System Pilot for about three months and pilot activities resumed in late summer after the relaunching of the HPwES Program. Approximately 10-15 BGE customers will participate to quantify potential energy savings from automated cellular and roller blind systems. BGE is working with fabricators/installers (Hunter Douglas, Rollease Acmeda and Illinois Window Shades) who will provide window blind coverings, automation and blind installation for the pilot. ICF will recruit the pilot participants and will conduct billing analysis of each participant’s energy consumption data (12 months pre- and post-blind installation) to evaluate potential energy savings during the winter and summer seasons. Solar heat gain from unshaded western and southern facing windows can be substantial during the summer and beneficial in the winter months. Having automated shades that open and close per a set schedule can potentially reduce energy usage, save customers money on their BGE bills, reduce HVAC maintenance costs, optimize climate consistency, improve comfort, and extend HVAC equipment life. ICF will provide a final report of the pilot results.

It should be noted that the customers participating in the Automated Window Shading System Pilot will not be required to participate in the current HPwES Program. If the pilot results prove beneficial, it is expected that the smart window shading incentives may be offered through the HPwES Program.

### Definitions of Participants and Measures by Program

The most current and complete description of all BGE EmPOWER programs, eligibility requirements, measures and available incentives for BGE customers can be found at the BGE SESP website at [https://bgesmartenergy.com/](https://bgesmartenergy.com/).

#### Residential Efficiency Energy Products Programs

**Energy Efficiency Products**

**Residential Lighting Program**

**Participant:** A household that purchases discounted screw-in light-emitting diode (“LED”) bulbs or fixtures from a participating retailer (brick and mortar or on-line) or a household that receives bulbs from a participating food bank through BGE’s outreach to donate LED bulbs for limited income customers. This number is derived from the number of bulbs sold or distributed using an estimated bulbs per household factor of 24 bulbs per household which is consistent with the Department of Energy estimate used in BGE’s initial EMPOWER filing.

**Measure:** LEDs or fixtures of various wattages

**Residential Appliance Rebate Program**

**Participant:** Downstream Retail Sales – A residential customer or a landlord of a residential customer who purchases a qualified appliance and submits a rebate application form.

Note: If a customer purchases an appliance and submits a rebate application, that customer is counted as one participant. If that rebate application is for multiple appliances, that customer is still counted as a single participant. However, if later that same customer purchases another appliance and submits a new rebate application, they will be counted as another participant.

Midstream Retailer Incentives – Retailer sales through the ENERGY STAR® (“Energy Star”) Retail Products Program (“ESRPP”) are incentivized with a payment to the retailer for stocking the qualifying Energy Star measure. Every qualifying measure sold that is incentivized by BGE counts as a single participant. Any qualifying instant or distributor rebate given at the register and funded by BGE also qualifies as a single participant.

**Measures:** Qualified appliances under the program (clothes washers, electric clothes dryers, hybrid heat pump water heaters, pool pumps, refrigerators, and dehumidifiers)

**Residential Appliance Recycling Program**

**Participant:** A residential customer who requests removal of a qualified refrigerator, freezer, or room air conditioner (“A/C”) from home (the latter if already removing a refrigerator or freezer).

Note: If a customer recycles an appliance and receives an incentive, that customer is counted as one participant. If that incentive is for multiple appliances, that customer is still counted as a single participant. However, if later that same customer recycles [PDF p. 102 | printed p. 88] another appliance and receives an additional rebate, they will be counted as another participant.

**Measure:** Qualified appliances in working condition - refrigerator and/or freezer, dehumidifier, and room air conditioner. Limit of three large units (refrigerator and/or freezer) and four small units (room air conditioners and/or dehumidifiers) per BGE residential household.

#### Home Optimization and Retrofit Programs

**Residential Quick Home Energy Check-Up Program**

**Participant:** A residential customer who has a walk-through of their home by a Quick Home Energy Check-Up (“QHEC”) professional and accepts installation of at least 1 energy savings measure as recommended by the QHEC professional.

**Measure:** LED light bulbs, faucet aerators, efficient-flow fixed or handheld showerheads, ShowerStart™ showerhead adapter, water heater pipe insulation (installed on hot and cold pipes for six feet from water heater), smart power strips.

**Residential Home Performance with ENERGY STAR® Program**

**Participant:** A residential customer who receives a home energy audit by a Home Performance with Energy Star (“HPwES”) contractor is counted under the number of completed audits in the mini-table. A residential customer who installs energy efficiency improvements recommended by the above contractor is counted under completed projects when the job is finished.

**Measure:** Audits: LED bulb installations, faucet aerators, efficient-flow showerheads – fixed mount or handheld, water heater pipe insulation, smart power strips and ShowerStart™ showerhead adapter.

Completed Jobs: Measures eligible for rebates include air sealing, insulation measures and duct sealing, heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (“HVAC”) and air conditioning equipment rebates, smart thermostats.

**Residential HVAC Rebate (Heating and Cooling Program)**

**Participant:** A residential customer who purchases new HVAC equipment.

**Measure:** Central Air Conditioning Tier 1 and Tier 2, Air-Source Heat Pump Tier 1 and Tier 2, Gas Furnace, Multi- and Single-Zone Mini-Split, Furnace Blower Motor (ECM) Factory-Installed, and Geothermal Heat Pump (closed loop).

Note: Midstream Incentives – HVAC incentives are paid to participating distributors to lower prices on high efficiency HVAC measures to make them more competitive with standard efficiency offerings.

**Smart Thermostat and Optimization Program**

**Participant:** A residential customer or landlord of a residential customer who purchases a smart thermostat for their residence or a residential customer who participates in the Smart Thermostat Optimization service.
