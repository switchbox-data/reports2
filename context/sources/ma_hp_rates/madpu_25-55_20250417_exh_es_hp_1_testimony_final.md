{11} a seasonal, residential electric heat pump rate available to customers who install

## {1} III. PROPOSED RATE R-1HP/R-2HP

{2} **Q.** **Please summarize the Company's proposed seasonal heat pump rate design.**

{3} **A.** The proposed Rate R-1HP and Rate R-2HP (collectively, "Heat Pump Rate") would
{4} be available to customers eligible for service under currently effective Rate R-1 and
{5} Rate R-2. However, such customer must be utilizing a heat-pump to heat their
{6} home in part or in its entirety. Rate R-2HP is the same as Rate R-1HP except that
{7} a 42% discount is available for eligible low-income customers consistent with the
{8} otherwise applicable Rate R-2. The Heat Pump Rate would have seasonally
{9} differentiated pricing for distribution and transmission with lower prices in the
{10} winter to mitigate the bill impact of higher electric usage during this time.

{11} **Q.** **Please explain the purpose of the Heat Pump Rate.**

{12} **A.** The Company is offering the Heat Pump Rate to mitigate higher electric costs
{13} associated with operation of a heat pump. The intent is to provide customer rate
{14} relief in furtherance of the Commonwealth's climate goals while remaining cost
{15} reflective.

{16} **Q.** **When does the Company envision it will implement Rate R-1HP?**

{17} **A.** The Company estimates that it will take approximately five months from the date
{18} of approval for the Company to implement the proposed Heat Pump Rate in its
{19} billing system. Although the Company is not seeking approval by June 1, that
{20} would be the timeframe for regulatory approval necessary for the Company to begin
{21} enrolling customers for November billing. In its Order in D.P.U. 23-80/D.P.U. 23-

[PDF p. 12 | printed p. 10]

{1} 81, the Department granted Unitil six to nine months to implement its new heat
{2} pump rates.

{3} **Q.** **Please describe the construction of the seasonally differentiated base
{4} distribution rate for the Heat Pump Rate.**

{5} **A.** The Company utilized the basic concept approved by the Department for Unitil's
{6} heat pump rate. Exhibit ES-HP-2, Schedule 1, shows how the Company adjusted
{7} test year sales for heat pump usage. This was done by converting average gas
{8} customer heating load to the kWh required to operate an air source heat pump. This
{9} adjustment was done on a monthly basis to arrive at new electric usage for a
{10} customer operating an air source heat pump. That monthly usage is then grouped
{11} into the proposed summer and winter periods and compared to the usage prior to

[PDF p. 15 | printed p. 13]

{1} the heat pump adjustment. For the winter, this represents an adjustment of 2.86
{2} times the original load. For the summer, there is a nominal adjustment reflecting
{3} some efficiency gains over central air conditioning usage.

{4} The revenue target for Rate R-1/R-2 is held constant to produce revenue neutral
{5} seasonal rates. The currently effective Rate R-1/R-2 base distribution rate is
{6} multiplied against test year sales for the summer period and then divided by the
{7} adjusted summer period sales to arrive at a new summer period base distribution
{8} rate. That new summer period base distribution rate is then multiplied by the
{9} adjusted summer period sales to derive summer period revenue. The summer
{10} period revenue is subtracted from the annual volumetric revenue target to arrive at
{11} the winter revenue requirement. The winter revenue requirement is divided by
{12} adjusted winter period sales to yield a winter base distribution rate. These
{13} calculations are shown in Exhibit ES-HP-2, Schedule 1.

{14} **Q.** **Please describe how you developed the seasonal transmission rate.**

{15} **A.** Summer and winter pricing for transmission rates were calculated in similar fashion
{16} to base distribution rates. The summer seasonal transmission price is established
{17} first based on the annual rate and the adjusted summer period sales. The winter
{18} seasonal transmission price is then calculated to collect the remaining revenue
{19} requirement over the adjusted winter period sales.

[PDF p. 16 | printed p. 14]

{1} **Q.** **How would you establish the seasonal differentiation for transmission when a
{2} new annual revenue requirement is established?**

{3} **A.** Similar to the performance-based ratemaking ("PBR") adjustment for seasonal base
{4} distribution pricing, the transmission rates would be adjusted based on the same
{5} percentage change applied to the otherwise applicable transmission rate under Rate
{6} R-1/R-2. This would allow for the seasonal differentiation to be maintained on a
{7} consistent basis.

{8} **Q.** **Why did the Company develop a seasonal transmission rate?**

{9} **A.** The Company is proposing to introduce a seasonal component to the transmission
{10} price to better mitigate the customer bill impact of increased electric usage due to
{11} heat pump operation. The Company finds that seasonally differentiating
{12} transmission remains cost reflective. Transmission costs are generally set in
{13} relation to system peak demand which typically happens in the summer. Therefore,
{14} a higher summer period transmission rate is acceptable under current conditions
{15} where the electric system remains summer peaking.

{18} **A.** As directed by the Department, the Company's proposed rate is similar to the

{20} with the approach taken by these EDCs, rates were seasonally differentiated on a
{21} revenue neutral basis by adjusting the test year sales underlying the proposed rate
{22} on the basis of heat pump usage. Exhibit ES-HP-1 [sic; the schedule is in Exhibit ES-HP-2 per the preceding testimony], Schedule 1 at Line 18 shows
{23} that winter usage was adjusted by a factor of three.

{11} **Q.** **Does the Company's proposal conflict with recommendations from the IRWG

{4} earlier, the Company supports seasonal price differentiation for transmission as it
{5} has some merit in cost causation. However, the Company does not currently
{6} support a wholesale blanket seasonal differentiation across all delivery rate
{7} components for the sake of achieving bill reductions. This violates adherence to
{8} cost causation in the design of rates. Moreover, the Company has concerns over
{9} creating a price differential that sends inappropriate price signals over the long
{10} term. The Commonwealth's climate goals target electrification which means that
{11} the electric grid will eventually be dual or winter peaking. This is also
{12} acknowledged by the IRWG recommendations where seasonal pricing is
{13} considered to be a short-term transitional solution. Price differentiation where
{14} winter rates are sharply discounted are likely to remain in place perpetually because
{15} it would be a challenge to remove customers from such a rate without triggering an
{16} undesirable bill impact.
