## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Program Participation and Impact Highlights

#### Program Impacts

2019 has been an extremely productive year for BGE's EmPOWER Maryland ("EmPOWER") program. The energy efficiency program portfolio achieved approximately 125% of its energy savings targets at 96% of the program budget (excluding Smart Energy Rewards and Peak Rewards expenditures). The entire portfolio achieved over 841,000 MWh annualized electric energy savings. Natural gas savings were over 5.4 million therms. Avoided generation from these savings over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed during 2019 is over 6.2 million MWh. This represents a reduction of over 4.4 million metric tons of carbon dioxide gas emissions according to the EPA Greenhouse Gas Equivalents Calculator, over 2.4 million tons of coal not burned or over 935,000 vehicles not driven for a year. In addition to the energy savings realized and associated environmental benefits customers generated by choosing more efficient alternatives in the way they use electricity and natural gas, BGE customers received incentives and bill credits of approximately $107 million. Estimated savings on customer bills over the life of measures installed during 2019 are $666 million. Total economic benefits returned to BGE customers is summarized below:

#### Program Highlights

During 2019, a few program accomplishments are worth noting. The programs mentioned below will be discussed in greater detail in the "Program Specifics" section of this report.

**The residential portfolio of energy efficiency programs had the following stand-out programs for 2019.**

**Lighting**: The lighting program continued its strong performance in 2019, exceeding its forecast for the year. A few of the reasons for this success include markdowns at major retailers, increased demand for light-emitting diodes ("LEDs"), and special promotional pricing.

**Quick Home Energy Check-Up ("QHEC")**: The QHEC Program far exceeded its forecast for 2019. A new marketing campaign and media exposure helped to bring about this success.

[PDF p. 6 | printed p. 5]

**Smart Energy Manager Program ("SEM")**: The SEM Program posted strong numbers in 2019 to beat its target savings. Targeted marketing modules for Home Energy Reports ("HERs") helped boost savings.

**Appliance Rebates**: The Appliance Rebate Program exceeded its 2019 target. 2019 was the first year of participation in the ENERGY STAR Retail Products Program ("ESRPP") for this program. Appliance measures (clothes washers, clothes dryers and refrigerators) were incented through ESRPP effective April 1, 2019.

**The commercial and industrial portfolio of energy efficiency programs had the following "above target" programs for 2019.**

**Prescriptive**: The Prescriptive Program had a successful 2019, exceeding its targets. The program's success is attributed to a concentrated effort to promote lighting fixtures and controls replacements.

**Instant Discount (Midstream) Program**: The C&I Instant Discount (Midstream) Program's 2019 success in exceeding its targets can be attributed to such actions as incorporating tubular LEDs ("TLEDs") into the program, selective incentive levels, and increased program awareness through marketing efforts.

**Small Business Program**: The Small Business Program exceeded its targets for 2019. This improved performance was a result of adding an additional contractor and smart thermostats to the program, and increased contractor training and customer awareness.

**Retrocommissioning**: The Retrocommissioning Program had exceptional results for 2019. The use of building analytics reports, increased incentive caps, and improved customer awareness, all contributed to this successful program.

#### Limited Income Participation

All BGE EmPOWER programs are available for participation by all customers regardless of income. BGE can only identify those limited income customers having self-identified themselves as qualifying for the Maryland Electric Universal Service Program ("EUSP"). Self-identified customers participating in various BGE energy efficiency programs received incentives which amounted to approximately $800,000 during 2019. The Residential Lighting Program provided approximately 500,000 energy efficient LED bulbs to local food banks for distribution to their clients as part of BGE's outreach to limited income residents. Additionally, within BGE's SEM Program, BGE marketed the QHEC Program to limited income customers while also addressing energy savings myths regarding thermostat use that was driven by customer feedback. A module was developed to address both use cases and a segmentation strategy was used to target both customer groups. Furthermore, BGE continues to support the efforts of the Maryland Fuel Fund to assist limited income customers by including a holiday message in the December email Home Energy Report promoting the program for the holiday season.

As directed per PSC Order No. 89189 on July 11, 2019, collaboration and cross-marketing efforts have been made between the utilities and the Maryland Department of Housing and Community Development ("DHCD"). Cross-marketing efforts promoting DHCD to limited income customers include language on BGEsmartEnergy.com and the Lighting Food Bank Flyer created for the Maryland Food Bank ("Food Bank"). BGE also included language promoting DHCD on the Smart Energy Savers Program ("SESP") overview brochure and QHEC brochure. BGE has also begun promoting energy efficiency to limited income customers through the utility's social media channels. These posts direct customers to learn more ways to save, which include links to DHCD.

As directed per PSC Order 89404 on December 20, 2019, BGE will take the following steps to further the goal of increased participation in the limited income programs.

BGE continues to enhance the customer experience and participation for limited income customers. While these customers must self-identify, BGE is aware that this population is much larger than those who have done so. On the customer experience side, BGE has looked at how the needs of these customers can be better served. On the participation side, BGE continues to make sure its programs are marketed and available to all customers.

BGE continues to look for ways to not only promote its programs but also the programs that DHCD offers. BGE has been actively involved in the Limited Income Working Group. BGE continues to look for ways to cross-promote BGE and DHCD EmPOWER programs. BGE is looking to move

beyond distributing DHCD marketing materials and will be adding information about DHCD programs in its EmPOWER offerings booklet later this year.

BGE's lighting program has committed to distributing 500,000 light bulbs a year to the Food Bank over the 2018-2020 cycle. The lighting program has looked to increase its commitment to reaching limited income customers through new avenues. BGE has engaged local non-profits which work with limited income customers, making light bulbs available to them. BGE continues to expand sales locations for lighting, adding smaller local stores in communities with higher numbers of limited income customers. In addition, BGE has expanded efforts to engage with limited income and hard to reach communities at festivals and events that they may attend in 2020.

The Quick Home Energy Checkup (QHEC) Program continues its outreach efforts to reach all participants who have not had a QHEC. BGE has expanded evening and weekend hours for QHECs to make them more accessible to customers. Contractors in the program have been given DHCD literature, which can be presented to the customer in addition to material about other BGE EmPOWER programs. The BGE Behavioral team continues to work on messaging that targets limited income customers. The messaging includes savings tips for these customers which are at little to no cost to the customer.

Customers participating in this program will not only see the savings of a smart thermostat they purchased, but also up to $100 in first year rewards from participating in the demand response program. BGE has engaged directly with DHCD on this program and discussed how the two can collaborate in the future.

### Residential Energy Efficiency Portfolio

The residential energy efficiency portfolio had a strong 2019. Across all measures, the residential portfolio gross wholesale MWh savings exceeded forecast, delivering over 400,000 MWh or 134% of the goal, while only spending 81% of the incentive budget. The Lighting, Appliance Rebate, Appliance Recycling, QHEC and New Construction programs all exceeded the 2019 targets. The Home Performance with Energy Star ("HPwES") Program was on target. The HVAC and Smart Thermostat program fell below target.

Key successes in 2019 included:

- BGE's Residential Appliance Rebate Program achieved 311% of the 2019 MWh savings goal. As of April 1, 2019, appliance measures (washers, dryers and refrigerators) were incented through ESRPP. The program achieved 262% of the total 2019 measures goal.
- 500,000 LED light bulbs were distributed for low income customers to local food pantries through the Food Bank.
- The Appliance Recycling Program exceeded the filed 2019 MWh savings goal at 118% and participant goal at 111%.

Challenges in 2019 included:

- Below target results for the HVAC program, but full transition of the program to midstream should lead to stronger future performance of the program.
- Smart thermostat sales and savings were lower than expected. A lower than expected finish in 2019 combined with an earlier change in the evaluation reference led to low program performance.

BGE continues to work with its implementors to address these challenges and enhance its programs across the board. Overall, the residential energy efficiency portfolio is on track to meet its goals for the overall 2018-2020 program cycle.

### Commercial and Industrial Portfolio

BGE's C&I energy efficiency portfolio exceeded its energy savings target for 2019. The program achieved 130% of its annual energy savings target during 2019. BGE saw strong performance in its Prescriptive, Building Tune-Up, Small Business and Instant Discount (Midstream) programs, with the Building Tune-Up and Instant Discount programs already achieving their three-year goals. The strong performance of these programs has more than offset the slower performing Custom Program. There were no Combined Heat and Power ("CHP") projects completed in 2019, but BGE is expecting completion of two large CHP projects in the first half of 2020. BGE's solid performance in 2018 and 2019 and its continued strong application pipeline for 2020 will enable BGE to meet its three-year full cycle target for the C&I portfolio.

##### Automated Window Shading System Pilot

The Home Performance with Energy Star ("HPwES") Program will be conducting an automated window shade pilot in 2020 with approximately 10-15 BGE customers to quantify potential energy savings from automated cellular and roller blind systems. BGE is working with two window blind manufacturers who will be providing window blind coverings, automation and blind installation for the pilot. ICF will recruit the pilot participants and will conduct billing analysis of each participant's energy consumption data (12 months pre- and post-blind installation) to evaluate potential energy savings during the winter and summer seasons. Solar heat gain from unshaded western and southern facing windows can be substantial during the summer and beneficial in the winter months. Having automated shades that open and close per a set schedule can potentially reduce energy usage, save customers money on their BGE bills, reduce HVAC maintenance costs, optimize climate consistency, improve comfort and extend HVAC equipment life. ICF will provide a final report of the pilot results.

It should be noted that the customers participating in the Automated Window Shading System Pilot will not be required to participate in the current HPwES Program. If the pilot results prove beneficial, it is expected that the Automated Window Shading Program would be offered through the HPwES Program.

#### Home Performance with ENERGY STAR®

Home Performance with Energy Star is a national program overseen by the Department of Energy ("DOE") and guided by the standards of the BPI. The program's goal is to improve residential comfort and energy efficiency by addressing the house as a system, while ensuring the health and safety of its occupants. Participating home performance contractors conduct comprehensive audits and make customized recommendations designed to improve the comfort and efficiency of the BGE customer's home. These recommendations are provided in an audit report that summarizes and prioritizes the opportunities for improvement. The customer decides which measures they want installed and contracts with the participating contractor to complete the job. The final project inspection and diagnostic testing ensure the contracted work was completed properly. The program provides a subsidy for the cost of the home energy audit (customer pays $100 for an audit valued at $400), direct installation of energy efficient measures and a rebate up to $7,500 for qualifying energy efficiency improvements.

[PDF p. 20 | printed p. 19]

**On Target**: In 2019, the BGE HPwES Program had a strong year, and achieved 3,558 MWh or 90% of the annual MWh gross wholesale electric energy savings forecast. This is a 17% improvement in MWh energy savings compared to the 2018 program year. More customers and participating contractors are recognizing the importance of the HPwES Program as a comprehensive energy efficiency program as they continue to bundle HVAC system upgrades in their whole-house energy makeovers.

When comparing 2019 to 2018, program results show:

- In 2019, 19% of projects were reported as including at least one rebate-eligible HVAC measure. This amounts to 193 projects and over 660 MWh reported in 2019, which both categories realized a 200% increase from 2018 statistics.
- The average HPwES project annualized kWh savings increased by 25% (up from 2,209 to 2,762) in 2019. The electric savings increase is attributable to an increased share of jobs completed in electric homes with HVAC system upgrades in the BGE service territory.
- The program was successful in integrating heat pump water heaters into the list of eligible measures within the PBI structure in the second half of 2019. By the end of the year, 11 projects included this upgrade.
- A 7% improvement in the savings-to-incentive ratio; hence, more savings per incentive dollar.
- Maintenance of a 36% audit-to-job conversion rate.

Currently, the BGE HPwES Program has 44 participating HPwES contractors.

The BGE Energy Coach service is improving the home performance customer experience by providing guidance and technical knowledge to help customers make more informed decisions throughout the home performance journey from scheduling an audit to moving forward and completing a job. #### Definitions of Participants and Measures by Program

The most current and complete description of all BGE EmPOWER programs, eligibility requirements, measures and available incentives for BGE customers can be found at the BGE SESP website at [https://bgesmartenergy.com/](https://bgesmartenergy.com/).

##### Residential Efficiency Energy Products Programs

###### Energy Efficiency Products

**Residential Lighting Program**

**Participant**: A household that purchases discounted screw-in light-emitting diode ("LED") bulbs or fixtures from a participating retailer (brick and mortar or on-line) or a household that receives bulbs from a participating food bank through BGE's outreach to donate LED bulbs for limited income customers. This number is derived from the number of bulbs sold or distributed using an estimated bulbs per household factor of 24 bulbs per household which is consistent with the Department of Energy estimate used in BGE's initial EMPOWER filing.

**Measure**: LEDs or fixtures of various wattages

**Residential Appliance Rebate Program**

**Participant**: Downstream Retail Sales – A residential customer or a landlord of a residential customer who purchases a qualified appliance and submits a rebate application form.

Note: If a customer purchases an appliance and submits a rebate application, that customer is counted as one participant. If that rebate application is for multiple appliances, that customer is still counted as a single participant. However, if later that same customer purchases another appliance and submits a new rebate application, they will be counted as another participant.

Midstream Retailer Incentives – Retailer sales through the ENERGY STAR® ("Energy Star") Retail Products Program ("ESRPP") are incentivized with a payment to the retailer for stocking the qualifying Energy Star measure. Every qualifying measure sold that is incentivized by BGE counts as a single participant. Any qualifying instant or distributor rebate given at the register and funded by BGE also qualifies as a single participant.

**Measures**: Qualified appliances under the program (clothes washers, electric clothes dryers, hybrid heat pump water heaters, pool pumps, refrigerators and dehumidifiers)

**Residential Appliance Recycling Program**

**Participant**: A residential customer who requests removal of a qualified refrigerator, freezer or room air conditioner ("A/C") from home (the latter if already removing a refrigerator or freezer).

[PDF p. 128 | printed p. 70]

Note: If a customer recycles an appliance and receives an incentive, that customer is counted as one participant. If that incentive is for multiple appliances, that customer is still counted as a single participant. However, if later that same customer recycles another appliance and receives an additional rebate, they will be counted as another participant.

**Measure**: Qualified appliances in working condition - refrigerator and/or freezer, dehumidifier and room air conditioner. Limit of three large units (refrigerator and/or freezer) and four small units (room air conditioners and/or dehumidifiers) per BGE residential household.

##### Home Optimization and Retrofit Programs

**Residential Quick Home Energy Check-Up Program**

**Participant**: A residential customer who has a walk-through of their home by a Quick Home Energy Check-Up ("QHEC") professional and accepts installation of at least 1 energy savings measure as recommended by the QHEC professional.

**Measure**: LED light bulbs, faucet aerators, efficient-flow fixed or handheld showerheads, ShowerStart™ showerhead adapter, water heater pipe insulation (installed on hot and cold pipes for six feet from water heater), smart power strips.

**Residential Home Performance with ENERGY STAR® Program**

**Participant**: A residential customer who receives a home energy audit by a Home Performance with Energy Star ("HPwES") contractor is counted under the number of completed audits in the mini-table. A residential customer who installs energy efficiency improvements recommended by the above contractor is counted under completed projects when the job is finished.

**Measure**: Audits: LED bulb installations, faucet aerators, efficient-flow showerheads – fixed mount or handheld, water heater pipe insulation, smart power strips and ShowerStart™ showerhead adapter.

Completed Jobs: Measures eligible for rebates include air sealing, insulation measures and duct sealing, heating, ventilation, and air conditioning ("HVAC") and air conditioning equipment rebates, smart thermostats.

**Residential HVAC Rebate (Heating and Cooling Program)**

**Participant**: A residential customer who purchases new HVAC equipment.

**Measure**: Central Air Conditioning Tier 1 and Tier 2, Air-Source Heat Pump Tier 1 and Tier 2, Gas Furnace, Multi- and Single-Zone Mini-Split, Furnace Blower Motor (ECM) Factory-Installed, and Geothermal Heat Pump (closed loop).

Note: Midstream Incentives – HVAC incentives are paid to participating distributors to lower prices on high efficiency HVAC measures to make them more competitive with standard efficiency offerings.
