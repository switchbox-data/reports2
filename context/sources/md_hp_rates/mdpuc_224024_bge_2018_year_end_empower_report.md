## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Program Participation and Impact Highlights

#### Program Impacts

2018 was an excellent year for BGE’s EmPOWER Maryland programs with the entire portfolio
achieving over 738,000 MWh annualized electric energy savings or over 2.3% of 2016 baseline
sales. Natural gas savings were over 5.6 million therms. Avoided generation from these savings
over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed during 2018 is
over 5.3 million MWh. This represents a reduction of nearly 4 million pounds of carbon dioxide
gas emissions according to the EPA Greenhouse Gas Equivalents Calculator, over 2.2 million
tons of coal not burned or over 846,000 vehicles not driven for a year. In addition to the energy
savings realized and associated environmental benefits customers generated by choosing more
efficient alternatives in the way they use electricity and natural gas, BGE customers received
incentives and bill credits of over $101 million. This was over 71% of BGE’s total EmPOWER
Maryland spend for the year. Estimated savings on customer bills over the life of measures
installed during 2018 are nearly $572 million. Total economic benefits returned to BGE
customers is summarized below;

2018 also marks the tenth year of BGE’s EmPOWER programs and a decade of EmPOWER
Maryland achievements in reducing electricity usage, natural gas consumption and greenhouse
gas emissions. The numbers are impressive. Since 2009, over 438,000 unique participants have
taken advantage of BGE energy efficiency programs. In addition, at the end of 2018, BGE’s
PeakRewards program had over 317,000 unique participants with over 318,000 participants in the
A/C program and nearly 23,000 participants in the Water Heater Program. BGE customers have
purchased over 316,000 appliances and recycled over 77,000. Over 290,000 customers have
invited BGE into their homes to perform a Quick Home Energy Checkup (“QHEC”) and have
allowed BGE to install almost 4,000,000 energy saving measures during those visits. BGE’s
customers have constructed nearly 21,000 new homes to more efficient standards incentivized by
EmPOWER Maryland. They have replaced over 23,000 inefficient HVAC units with new units
to cool and heat their homes while reducing energy consumption and have installed over 48,000
state of the art smart thermostats offered by multiple energy efficiency programs and
PeakRewards to ensure that their HVAC units deliver comfort with the best use of energy. The
above participant numbers do not include BGE’s lighting program because BGE cannot capture
the customer’s information, but BGE has incentivized customers to purchase over 33,000,000
energy efficient CFL and LED bulbs and fixtures.

[PDF p. 6 | printed p. 5]

BGE’s commercial customers were quick to recognize the value of energy efficiency to their
business. Nearly 17,000 small business owners and over 22,000 larger businesses have
participated in BGE’s commercial programs. Ten Combined Heat and Power (“CHP”) projects
have been completed with more in the works. A rather recent commercial program, the Instant
Discount – Mid-stream program, provides incentives at the distributor level to encourage the
purchase of more efficient lighting and other products and has already resulted in the purchase of
over 2,000,000 such items.
Because of their enthusiastic support for the EmPOWER Maryland programs, BGE’s customers
realize real dollar savings on their bills. Those savings approximate over $4.6 billion over the
expected lives of the measures they have installed. Additionally, their decisions to opt for energy
efficient products and services to reduce energy usage has generated over $570 million in
incentives. BGE customers who have taken advantage of BGE’s Smart Energy Rewards peak
time rebate program have received nearly $58 million in bill credits and those customers who
have elected to participate in BGE’s PeakRewards program have received nearly $250 million in
bill credits plus the value of the thermostats and switches installed in their homes of over $158
million. In total, that adds up to nearly $5.7 billion.

#### Program Highlights:

During 2018, a few program accomplishments were worth noting:
The residential New Construction Program had the greatest number of new homes constructed
since the initiation of the program. In addition, the incorporation of a smart thermostat option for
participants has been well received with a significant number of homes taking advantage of the
opportunity to add a smart thermostat.
BGE successfully launched the new residential Smart Thermostat Program in the first quarter of
2018, offering customers a variety of in-store and on-line options to get a smart thermostat rebate.
Over 13,800 ENERGY STAR certified smart thermostats were incentivized with an up to $100
rebate. Smart thermostats continue to be the leading instant rebated product sold on BGE
Marketplace representing 67% of all smart thermostat rebates. Customers have embraced BGE
Marketplace and value the convenient instant rebate process when purchasing energy efficient
products. In June 2018, BGE rolled out the Thermostat Optimization Program (“Optimization
Program”) which provides customers the opportunity to achieve additional energy savings above
their regular smart thermostat savings. Nearly 9,300 customers opted into BGE’s Optimization
Program in 2018 producing 511 MWh annualized energy savings.
BGE successfully executed three Smart Energy Rewards (“SER”) Program Energy Savings Days
(“ESDs”) on July 27, 2018, August 7, 2018 and August 29, 2018, which demonstrated the ability
of BGE customers to reduce their energy use when called upon. In return, these customers
received bill credits of over $11.6 million.
Three commercial programs had exceptional results which are discussed in detail in the program
section of this report:

#### Limited Income Participation

As noted at mid-year, all BGE EmPOWER Maryland programs are available for participation by
all customers regardless of income. BGE can only identify those limited income customers
having self-identified themselves as qualifying for the Maryland Electric Universal Service
Program. Those customers participating in various BGE energy efficiency programs received
incentives amounted to ~$950,000 during 2018. Of note was BGE’s QHEC program where these
customers accounted for over 9% of all QHEC participants. The Residential Lighting Program
provided nearly 500,000 energy efficient LED bulbs to local food banks for distribution to their
clients as part of BGE’s outreach to limited income residents. Additionally, within BGE’s Smart
Energy Manager (“SEM”) Program, BGE partnered with the Maryland Department of Housing
and Community Development (“DHCD”) to identify eligible customers who potentially qualified
for the Weatherization Assistance Program (“WAP”) and Limited Income Energy Efficiency
Programs (“LIEEP”) but had not applied. BGE used the SEM Program to send target messaging
through its Home Energy Reports (“HER”) to encourage those customers to apply. The results
are discussed in the program section of this report.

#### Demand Reduction Capability:

As noted when BGE provided the Q3 2018 EmPOWER Maryland results, the summary tables
reflect a declining capability of BGE’s Demand Response (DR) for the PeakRewards and Smart
Energy Rewards Programs. With the implementation of Advanced Metering Infrastructure
(“AMI”), BGE can more accurately measure the load-reducing capabilities of its DR programs.
Prior to sufficient AMI data, BGE and PJM used R&D impact tables developed through pilot
programs. As home use technologies improve and conservation efforts continue to climb, those
impact tables quickly became dated. BGE is getting to the point of having sufficient historical
AMI data to re-estimate the impacts that our DR programs have on our customers’ electric
consumption. BGE’s recent AMI-enabled impact estimations for our PeakRewards Program are
about one-third lower than the former estimates developed in the 2008-2011 Smart Energy Pilots.
We attribute the reductions to the great successes of conservation in Maryland. Successful
conservation reduces the base from which DR can reduce. Over the last 5 years alone, BGE’s
residential customers have realized a 10% reduction in electric energy use, and their peak demand
measured by the peak load contribution (“PLC”) has declined 17%. These magnitudes of
conservation result in less opportunity to achieve peak-load reductions in DR programs.

### Potential Portfolio Challenges Over the Three-Year Cycle

As BGE has conveyed to the Commission in the past, energy efficient lighting represents a
significant (>60%) portion of the total portfolio’s energy savings. Given the significant challenge
this represents and its possible impact on our portfolio, BGE wants to keep the Commission well
informed of developments within the lighting industry to ensure we are well positioned to
recommend strategies that will allow us to continue to meet the legislated EmPOWER Maryland
goals in a cost-effective manner. Below is a summary of BGE’s latest discussions referencing
changes in the lighting industry.
On August 15, 2017, DOE published a notice of data availability (“NODA”) and request for
information seeking data for general service incandescent lamps (“GSILs”) and other
incandescent lamps. The purpose of this NODA was to assist DOE in making a determination
regarding amending standards for GSILs. Comments submitted in response to the NODA lead
DOE to re-consider the decisions it had already made with respect to the definitions for General
Service Lamps (“GSLs”) and GSILs. On December 13, 2018 DOE formally submitted a proposal
to withdraw the revised definitions of GSLs and GSILs that would otherwise take effect on
January 1, 2020, to reduce the regulatory burdens on stakeholders.
A public meeting to discuss the GSL rulemaking is scheduled for February 28, 2019. Once the
final rulemaking is issued, litigation is likely to follow as either National Electrical Manufacturers
Association “NEMA” (as a representative of the lighting manufacturers) or the many advocacy
groups, including and not limited to the Natural Resources Defense Council (“NRDC”),
Appliance Standard Awareness Project (“ASAP”) and the American Council for an Energy-

[PDF p. 13 | printed p. 12]

Efficient Economy (“ACEEE”) will argue against the final decision. BGE’s consultant will be
conducting a survey amongst our manufacturer partners to get their insights and opinions on any
changes they are making on the manufacturing side leading up to 2020 and the eventual outcome
of the rulemaking and litigation. We will continue to monitor the situation.
If there is no 2020 backstop or expanded GSL Definition, halogen bulbs would remain in the
market for all bulb types, extending the life of residential lighting programs. The viability of
lighting programs would be determined based on market saturation in individual service
territories. Under this scenario, all current lighting products would continue to be included in the
program and BGE would closely monitor market saturation in the service territory to determine
the appropriate time to move away from lighting incentives.
If there is a 2020 backstop but no expanded GSL Definition, then only standard LEDs and some
decorative lamps would be impacted. Standard LEDs and some decorative LEDs would no
longer be cost effective or necessary to incentivize; all directional and most decorative LEDs
would still be eligible for incentives. Under this scenario the lighting program would only offer
incentives on eligible specialty LEDs.

##### Home Performance with Energy Star (HPwES)

HPwES is a national program overseen by the Department of Energy (“DOE”) and guided by the
standards of the (“BPI”). The program’s goal is to improve residential comfort and energy
efficiency by addressing the house as a system, while ensuring the health and safety of its
occupants. Participating home performance contractors conduct comprehensive audits and make
customized recommendations designed to improve the comfort and efficiency of the BGE
customer’s home. These recommendations are provided in an audit report that summarizes and
prioritizes the opportunities for improvement. The customer decides which measures they want
installed and contracts with the participating contractor to complete the job. The final project
inspection and diagnostic testing ensure the contracted work was completed properly. The
program provides a subsidy for the cost of the home energy audit (customer pays $100 for an
audit valued at $400), direct installation of energy efficient measures and a rebate up to $7,500
for qualifying energy efficiency improvements.

##### BGE Smart Energy Manager (SEM)

A multi-channel delivery platform that gives customers insights into their energy usage, made
available through smart meter data, to drive energy savings. The SEM program reports, alerts,
and online tools are designed to educate customers about their energy consumption, provide a
reference point for their use relative to their peers and their own usage over time, and encourage
customers to act to reduce their usage, thereby lowering their bills. This energy savings
translates to real dollars saved by BGE customers.
Exceeding Target: The SEM program is exceeding its energy savings target by achieving 128%
of the annual MWh gross wholesale electric savings at year end. BGE attributes exceeding target
to an increase in the number of eligible households that can participate in the program and the
enrollment of customers in emailed high usage alerts.
General media and email marketing campaigns were used to attract customers to create a My
Account profile and encourage existing My Account customers to reengage with the online
energy management tools. In 2018, 85% of My Account customers were satisfied with SEM’s
online energy management tools.
Segmented marketing campaigns were developed within SEM’s HERs to target limited income
customers and customers who have not completed their home energy profile. These campaigns

[PDF p. 26 | printed p. 25]

encouraged customers to participate in income eligible programs and act to reduce their usage. In
2018, 93% of customers recall receiving HERs, which BGE attributes to the effort of developing
segmented marketing campaigns. Additional details of these campaigns are below.

###### Limited Income Campaign

BGE worked with the DHCD to identify eligible customers who potentially qualified for
WAP and LIEEP but had not applied. SEM target messaged these customers in their print
or email HERs to encourage them to apply. The targeted group consisted of 12K paper
recipients and 5K email recipients. The open rate for email HER was 39.4%.
BGE also worked with the Fuel Fund of Maryland to support their giving campaign in
December 2018 by including messaging through print and email HERs. The open rate for
the email HER was 49%.

The SEM program sent email HERs customers a personalized marketing module to encourage
them to complete their Home Energy Profile. The marketing module displayed what additional

campaign ran in May 2018 and by the end of July 2018, a total of 24,598 customers accessed their
Home Energy Profile to provide additional details about their home. BGE followed up with those
customers that accessed their Home Energy Profile and provided another personalized marketing

### C&I Midstream HVAC Proposal

In Order No. 88514, issued on December 22, 2017, the Commission approved BGE’s Midstream
Program for the 2018-2020 EmPOWER Maryland Program Cycle. This approval included
BGE’s request to expand its current Midstream Lighting Program to include additional equipment
such as HVAC, plug load, and commercial kitchen equipment. Subsequently, in Order No.
88964, issued on December 31, 2018, it stated that “proposed measures to be added to a Utility’s
Midstream Program shall be included in the Utility’s Semi-Annual Report for the Third and
Fourth Quarter of 2018”.
BGE currently operates a highly successful Midstream Program for lighting products. This
program, called Instant Lighting Discounts has far exceeded its yearly goals while continuing to
be one of the most cost-effective programs in BGE’s C&I portfolio. As opposed to other C&I
programs, this program requires no customer application and yet still collects sufficient data for
meaningful EM&V. As a result, it has received both high customer satisfaction, high scores and
high realization rates from the Maryland evaluators.
In 2018, the BGE C&I Prescriptive Program had a very limited penetration into the HVAC
market, awarding incentives on only 128 (non-chiller) units. In light of BGE’s success in
midstream lighting, BGE hereby requests Commission approval to add HVAC measures to the
current Midstream Program to increase HVAC participation. The proposed midstream measures
mirror BGE’s prescriptive offerings in both scope, efficiency, and maximum incentive levels but
will allow service providers to obtain discounts directly from their HVAC distributors.
Per Order No. 88964, BGE requests that the Commission approve the following additions to
BGE’s existing Midstream Program:

- Single and Split Package HVAC Systems – incentivized up to $200 per ton
- Ductless Mini Split Systems – incentivized at up to $100 per ton
  BGE had already anticipated the possibility of adding these measures and included the associated
  budget for them in its 2018-2020 EmPOWER Maryland Program Filing. As a result, BGE is not
  requesting any additional budget to add HVAC measures to its C&I Midstream Program.

### Current Pilot Programs

#### Smart Homes

The BGE Smart Home Pilot intends to investigate the attainable energy savings associated with
installation of Smart Home Kits – a network of connected devices that incorporate a level of
automation that encourages customers to increase their energy efficiency. Once BGE Smart
Home Kits are installed, customers will be encouraged to engage with the mobile application to
create automated processes that aim to create customer convenience in parallel with reducing
HVAC runtimes, watt-draw from plug-load appliances, and lighting runtimes.
Pilot Program Status:
The BGE Smart Home Pilot (SHP) kicked off on January 8, 2018. Due to the two-year cycle of
this pilot, immediate attention was focused on selecting a smart home platform and vendor
selection, development of a ‘shite-label Smart Home kit and an associated SHP mobile
application. After a robust recruitment process, the team delivered 1,000+ DIY kits to BGE
customers who were screened and identified for participation in the pilot. Next steps include
encouragement and engagement activities to educate and aid customers to helping them use their
kit to better manage their utility bills. In addition, the team is working with the platform vendor

## Appendix G: Notes and Assumptions

All calculations are consistent with BGE’s 2018 – 2020 Maryland Public Service Commission
approved EmPOWER Maryland Filing and the most current Mid-Atlantic TRM as appropriate.

#### Notes

#### Smart Thermostats

In the preparation of the EmPOWER Maryland Filing, an estimate was used to calculate
the Annualized Energy Savings realized from the installation of smart thermostats. This
estimate was based on the best information at the time. The deemed savings prescribed in
the Mid-Atlantic TRM are lower than the values used for the forecast and have resulted in
a smaller saving per device and thus lower savings than the as-filed forecast.

#### PeakRewards Energy Savings (MWh)

The as filed Annualized Energy Savings for PeakRewards was made up of two
components:

- Annualized Energy Savings (MWh) from installed smart thermostats. These
  energy savings are not associated with PeakRewards events.
- Energy savings coincident with the hours during PeakRewards calls
  Prior to 2018, Commission Staff required mini-table, the DRDP Call Log, included energy
  savings associated with the PeakRewards events. The current cycle DRDP Call Log
  eliminated MWh savings but instead includes MW reductions.
  Accordingly, to be consistent with the requirements of the DRDP Call Log, BGE is only
  reporting Annualized Energy Savings from smart thermostats.

#### Smart Energy Rewards energy savings (MWh)

The as filed energy savings for Smart Energy Rewards included energy savings associated
with Energy Savings Days. To be consistent with the reporting for PeakRewards energy
savings, as discussed above, BGE did not report energy savings coincident with event
days.
