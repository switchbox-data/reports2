**By the Commission:**

## I. INTRODUCTION

On May 23, 2024, Commonwealth Edison Company (“ComEd” or the “Company”) filed with the Illinois Commerce Commission (“Commission”) a Petition pursuant to Section 16-105.5(a) and (c) of the Public Utilities Act (“Act”), 220 ILCS 5/16-105.5 (a) and (c), to approve its proposed revenue-neutral tariff changes and its proposed distribution time-of-use (“DTOU”) rate. The Commission issued a final Order on January 16, 2025 (“Final Order”), and subsequently granted rehearing on a limited basis to allow ComEd to present a supply time-of-use (“STOU”) rate and/or a bundled time-of-use (“TOU”) rate proposal that is harmonized with the approved DTOU rate.

## II. FACTUAL AND PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND

### A. The Parties

Staff of the Commission (“Staff”), the Office of the Illinois Attorney General, and the City of Chicago filed appearances. The following petitions for leave to intervene in this proceeding were granted: the Chicago Transit Authority; Citizens Utility Board (“CUB”); Retail Energy Supply Association (“RESA”); the Northeast Illinois Regional Commuter Railroad Corporation and the Commuter Rail Division of the Regional Transportation Authority (collectively known as “Metra”); Illinois Competitive Energy Association (“ICEA”); Nucor Steel Kankakee, Inc.; Illinois Industrial Consumers; Walmart, Inc.; Environmental Defense Fund (“EDF”); Environmental Law & Policy Center (“ELPC”), Vote Solar (“VS”), and Union of Concerned Scientists (“UCS”) (collectively known as “ELPC/VS/UCS”); Solar Energy Industries Association, the Coalition for Community Solar Access, and the Illinois Solar Energy Association (collectively known as “Joint Solar Parties”); Data Center Coalition; Building Owners and Managers Association of Chicago; CITGO Petroleum, Charter Dura-Bar, Inc., Magid Glove and Manufacturing Company, Metropolitan Water Reclamation District of Greater Chicago as members of the Coalition to Request Equitable Allocation of Costs Together (collectively known as “REACT”).

### B. Procedural History

The Commission issued a Final Order on January 16, 2025, approving ComEd’s proposed rate design, with modifications, and various revisions to its riders. On February
18, 2025, ComEd filed a verified Application for Rehearing (“Application”), seeking rehearing on certain provisions of the Final Order, including directives regarding the DTOU and STOU rates. ComEd contemporaneously filed a Motion to Stay (“Motion”), which requested that the Commission’s directives concerning implementation of the DTOU rate and the timelines for collaboration on customer education materials related to the DTOU be stayed if the Commission grants rehearing to consider an STOU rate or bundled time-of-use TOU rate. On March 6, 2025, the Commission granted, in part, ComEd’s Application “for the limited purpose of allowing ComEd to present an STOU and/or bundled TOU rate proposal that is harmonized with ComEd’s approved DTOU rate.” Regular Open Mtg. Tr. at 10-11 (Mar. 6, 2025). The Commission further granted ComEd’s Motion. A status hearing was held in this matter on rehearing via videoconference on March 27, 2025, before duly authorized Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”) of the Commission. In accordance with the established procedural schedule, ComEd submitted direct testimony on rehearing on April 4, 2025, in which it presented its initial STOU strawman proposal. On April 29, 2025, Staff, ELPC/VS/UCS, jointly CUB and EDF (“CUB/EDF”), and jointly ICEA and RESA (“ICEA/RESA”) filed direct testimony on rehearing. On May 12, 2025, ComEd filed rebuttal testimony on rehearing. An evidentiary hearing was held on May 14, 2025. On May 23, 2025, ComEd, Staff, CUB/EDF, ICEA/RESA, ELPC/VS/UCS, and REACT filed Initial Briefs on Rehearing (“IBR”). REACT filed a corrected IBR on May 27, 2025. On May 29, 2025, ComEd, Staff, CUB/EDF, ICEA/RESA, and REACT filed Reply Briefs on Rehearing (“RBR”). A Proposed Order on Rehearing was served on June 16, 2025. On June 25, 2025, Staff, REACT, and ICEA/RESA filed Briefs on Exceptions (“BOEs”). ICEA/RESA’s BOE included a request for oral argument. On June 30, 2025, ComEd, Staff, CUB/EDF, REACT, and ICEA/RESA filed Reply Briefs on Exceptions. On July 2, 2025, the Commission granted oral argument, which was held on July 9, 2025.

## III. LEGAL STANDARD

### A. Procedural Legal Issues

#### 1. ComEd’s Position

In direct testimony on rehearing, ComEd states that it presented and discussed its proposed strawman for a STOU rate that would be bundled with its previously approved DTOU rate. See generally ComEd Ex. 17.0. ComEd states that its strawman STOU proposal satisfies the Final Order’s directive to present in this docket a “strawman proposal for both an STOU and a bundled TOU rate.” Final Order at 189. It complies with the Commission’s outlined scope of rehearing, which was for ComEd to “present an STOU and/or bundled TOU rate proposal that is harmonized with ComEd’s approve [sic] DTOU rate.” Regular Open Mtg. Tr. at 10-11 (Mar. 6, 2025). ComEd further states that its proposal on direct demonstrates alignment with the Final Order’s suggestion that ComEd “consider developing and proposing an expanded TOU tariff in the near future as a means to advance the objectives of [Public Act (“P.A.”)] 102-0662 and enhance its response to changing grid needs.” Final Order at 190. ComEd also states that its engagement in discussions with interested parties, and consideration and incorporation of their feedback into Rate BEST – Basic Electric Service Time of Use Pricing (“Rate BEST”), satisfies the Final Order’s directive to engage with stakeholders on the potential structure and mechanics of an STOU and bundled TOU. Final Order at 189-190. ComEd notes that no party contests it has fulfilled the directives stemming from the Commission’s Final Order. ComEd maintains that Section 16-103(e) of the Act is not implicated on rehearing. ComEd freely volunteered in its Application to propose an STOU strawman if rehearing were to be granted. ComEd App. at 11. ComEd voluntarily presented its STOU proposals via testimony on rehearing, accompanied by supportive record evidence, for the Commission to consider and act upon as it sees fit. See ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 2. ComEd states that Section 16-103(e) of the Act prohibits the Commission from “requir[ing] an electric utility to offer any tariffed service other than the services required by [Section 16-103].” 220 ILCS 5/16-103(e). While there is strong policy support for a STOU rate (see Final Order at 189-190), ComEd states that there is no statutory mandate that ComEd provide such an offering. Therefore, ComEd states that in the initial phase of this proceeding, the Commission correctly found that it could not approve an STOU that ComEd had not proposed or otherwise agreed to offer. Final Order at 189-190 (declining to require ComEd to provide an STOU proposed by CUB/EDF and ELPC/VS/UCS that ComEd opposed, for various reasons); see also ComEd Ex. 4.0 at 18 (confirming that ComEd did not offer an STOU in the initial phase of the RDI proceeding); id. at 18 (opposing CUB/EDF and ELPC/VS/UCS’s requests to order ComEd to offer an STOU modeled on ComEd’s Rate RTOUPP – Residential Time of Use Pricing Pilot (“RTOUPP”)). ComEd states that Section 16-103(e) does not, and has never, prohibited the utility from agreeing to propose a new or modified service. 220 ILCS 5/16-103(e). In its Final Order, the Commission urged ComEd to “consider developing and proposing an expanded TOU tariff in the near future,” in addition to its requirement that ComEd produce a strawman. Final Order at 189-190. ComEd considered this recommendation and provided its Rate BEST proposal to efficiently meet both the Commission’s directive for a strawman proposal, and its suggestion for an expanded TOU tariff. Id. ComEd states that it has voluntarily proposed Rate BEST therefore Section 16-103(e) is not applicable on rehearing. See generally ComEd App. ComEd states that the Commission is free to consider ComEd’s proposal and exercise its judgment as to whether Rate BEST should be adopted at this time. Id. In doing so, ComEd states that the Commission should consider the record on rehearing, which demonstrates that Rate BEST is just and reasonable. ComEd states that the Commission should reject ICEA/RESA and Staff’s assertion that ComEd failed to “explicitly” seek rehearing of the Commission’s decision on whether an STOU is within the scope of the present docket, and therefore any proposed STOU is out of scope on rehearing. ICEA/RESA IBR at 4; Staff IBR at 4-5. ComEd states that
assertions by ICEA/RESA and Staff that STOU proposals are not within the scope of this proceeding are flatly contradicted by the Commission’s directives in this proceeding. First, ComEd states that the Commission never explicitly stated that an STOU is outside the scope of this docket, thus there was nothing for which ComEd had to seek rehearing. ComEd explains that nowhere in the Final Order does the Commission find that an STOU is out of scope for this proceeding. See generally Final Order at 19, 189-190. ComEd notes that, in fact, the Commission found that “[r]easonable anticipation of new services and tariffs in furtherance of P.A. 102-0662’s objectives support more inclusive rate design proceedings.” Final Order at 19. The Commission’s conclusion includes the statement that “[b]roadly, review of such [out of scope] issues is supported by consideration of: administrative efficiency, including conservation of already strained time and resources; prior directives to address specific issues in this proceeding; and P.A. 102-0662’s mandate that the impact of ComEd’s tariffs and rate design be holistically considered. 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(f)(4).” Final Order at 19. ComEd states that it predicated this rehearing, as Staff acknowledges, on administrative and resource efficiency. See Staff IBR at 5. ComEd explains that a finding that rate design proceedings have a more “inclusive” scope can be read harmoniously with the Commission’s later direction on the strawman and its encouragement that ComEd explore development of “an expanded TOU tariff.” Final Order at 19, 189-190. As such, ComEd states, ICEA/RESA and Staff’s arguments—predicated on seeking reconsideration of a finding that does not actually exist—deserve no weight. Second, ComEd states that the Commission’s granting of ComEd’s Application specifically articulates that the scope of rehearing is the STOU. As Staff states in its Initial Brief on Rehearing, the scope of rehearing is defined by the issues raised in the Application. Staff IBR at 4. ComEd asserts that it clearly articulated the grounds it was requesting rehearing: to propose an STOU for the Commission’s consideration. Application at 2-3, 9-11. ComEd notes that Chairman Scott affirmed this scope when he stated that the Commission was granting rehearing for ComEd to “present an STOU and/or bundled TOU rate proposal that is harmonized with ComEd’s approve [sic] DTOU rate.” Regular Open Mtg. Tr. at 10-11 (Mar. 6, 2025).

#### 2. Staff’s Position

In granting rehearing, Staff states that the Commission afforded ComEd a unique opportunity: the chance to present a strawman STOU for Commission consideration, notwithstanding the fact that ComEd offered no such proposal in the underlying docket. The Commission was responsive to ComEd’s argument that rehearing would “allow stakeholders [a] time-bound process to develop as quickly as practical a single, complete record for Commission action with respect to both supply and distribution a bundled TOU.” ComEd App. at 1. In the Final Order, the Commission directed ComEd “to submit a compliance filing in this docket that details the issues and topics raised by parties and provides a strawman proposal for both an STOU and a bundled TOU rate.” Final Order at 190. Staff recommends the Commission find that ComEd has complied with this directive and satisfied the Commission’s request that it “engage with stakeholders on the potential structure and mechanics of an STOU and a bundled TOU rate.” Final Order at 189-190.
In the underlying docket, ComEd argued that an STOU rate was out of scope because the Company did not propose such a rate, and the Commission had no authority to direct the Company to offer a rate it did not propose. Staff states that the Company made its position on this issue abundantly clear. See e.g., ComEd Ex. 4.0 at 18; ComEd Ex. 90 at 16. In fact, ComEd went so far as to move to strike testimony offered by CUB/EDF asking the Commission to order an STOU, arguing “ComEd has not offered and will not be offering a supply TOU in this case and the Commission does not have the statutory authority to require ComEd to do so.” ComEd Mt. to Strike at 11 (Aug. 12, 2024) (internal citations omitted); ComEd Init. Br. at 58. ComEd did not propose an STOU in the underlying docket and for that reason alone, Staff states, the grant of rehearing should be read narrowly to contemplate only development of a strawman proposal. According to Staff, it is axiomatic that an issue that is not within the scope of the initial proceeding cannot be within the scope of rehearing of that proceeding. The Commission’s rules of practice require that “[a]pplications for rehearing must state with specificity the issues for which rehearing is sought.” 83 Ill. Adm. Code 200.880. The scope of rehearing, in turn, is defined by the issues raised in the application for rehearing and the Commission has repeatedly and consistently held that issues not raised in an application for rehearing are outside the scope of rehearing. See e.g., Ameren Ill. Co. d/b/a Ameren Ill., Docket No. 12-0244, Order at 36 (Oct. 15, 2012). Limiting rehearing to issues raised in the application for rehearing is consistent with how an application for rehearing defines the scope of any appeal. “No person or corporation in any appeal shall urge or rely upon any grounds not set forth in such application for a rehearing before the Commission.” 220 ILCS 5/10-113(a). The Commission’s grant of rehearing should be read in a manner consistent with the resolution previously ordered, which is an exploration of the development of a strawman STOU or bundled TOU rate. Nothing in ComEd’s Application challenges this resolution as a matter of fact or of law; indeed, the Application is premised on administrative efficiency and cost savings for the Company. Staff states that before the Commission can consider approving an STOU on rehearing, it must first determine that the issue is properly raised in ComEd’s Application. It is not. As noted above, throughout the proceeding ComEd made clear that it was not offering an STOU, and it could not be directed to propose such a rate. In Staff’s view, it is not clear that the Company is proposing that the Commission approve its strawman STOU proposal. Indeed, any request for such approval would be directly contrary to the Company’s position in the original proceeding. While ComEd witness Perkins testified, “[i]n rehearing, ComEd is voluntarily proposing an STOU for the Commission’s consideration” (ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 2), the witness further explained the purpose of the testimony was, in part, to “introduce ComEd’s bundled TOU strawman proposal.” Id. The witness also asked that “the Commission find that ComEd has met its directive to provide an STOU and bundled rate [s]trawman within 120 days of the Final Order” (id. at 3), which is consistent with Staff’s understanding of the scope of rehearing. ComEd witness Perkins recommends the Commission find the Company has complied with the directive to develop a strawman and suggests the “Commission should consider the Rate BEST Compromise Proposal…” ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 2. Similarly, witness Perkins testified that the Company’s Rate BEST proposal in direct testimony on
rehearing “was designed to be a ‘strawman’ proposal’ which would serve as a starting point to which Staff and intervenors could react and respond.” Id. at 3. Staff asserts that this tentative language is clearly different from petitioning the Commission for approval of a new rate, even if such action was appropriate for the first time on rehearing. To support such a proposal, the Company would not only have to offer new evidence supporting an STOU, but also explain why it did not previously adduce that evidence. 83 Ill. Adm. Code 200.880(a). The Company cannot take that position or offer that evidence without contradicting the factual position it clearly took in the underlying docket, that it was not proposing and did not intend to propose a STOU for approval. Moreover, in addition to arguing an STOU could not be considered because it did not propose such a rate, the Company argued that a STOU rate for approval was outside the scope of the underlying docket because it is not related to the allocation of delivery services revenue. “This proposal has nothing to do with the allocation of delivery services costs and it is not revenue neutral. Indeed, it would require creation of an additional service that would require recovery of additional revenues.” ComEd Ex. 4.0 at 7; see also, ComEd Ex. 9.0 REV at 17. This issue remains. Nothing in the Application challenged this argument and thus it cannot be revisited on rehearing. Alternatively, if the Company takes the position that it is to propose an STOU for approval on rehearing pursuant to Commission directive, Staff states that it would be a complete reversal of its legal position and a waiver of the Company’s previous argument that the Commission cannot direct it to offer any rate. In the underlying docket, ComEd was clear that it would not and did not plan to offer an STOU rate in this proceeding. ComEd Ex. 4.0 at 2; ComEd Ex. 9.0 REV at 17. If ComEd seeks to offer an STOU for Commission approval for the first time on rehearing, such a proposal would be outside the scope of the docket, outside the scope of rehearing, and prejudicial to Staff and intervenors. According to Staff, the Commission’s grant of rehearing must be interpreted in a manner that is consistent with the issues properly within the scope of both the underlying proceeding and the request for rehearing and thus must be interpreted as limited to development of a strawman STOU proposal. Staff states that ComEd suggests the “Commission declined to adopt an STOU…” in the underlying proceeding. ComEd IBR at 7. This is a misstatement of the Final Order, in which the Commission noted “the lack of sufficient record evidence to formulate [an STOU] rate in this case.” Final Order at 189. The Commission did not “decline to adopt” an STOU; it found there was no evidence sufficient to formulate such a rate, even if it wanted to consider it. Indeed, ComEd notes “the Commission recognized that it lacked both the record and the statutory authority to require ComEd to adopt an STOU rate in the initial phase of the proceeding.” ComEd IBR at 4. ComEd also recognized the Commission could not order ComEd to propose an STOU and stated it “was not required by statute to offer an STOU and [it] was not voluntarily offering to provide an STOU.” Id. at 7. Both Staff and ICEA/RESA argue that ComEd did not ask the Commission to approve any STOU rate on rehearing. In its Initial Brief on Rehearing, ComEd continues to tiptoe around this procedural hurdle. As ICEA/RESA correctly noted, no ComEd witness “actually affirmatively request[ed] on behalf of ComEd that the Commission
approve the tariff.” ICEA/RESA IBR at 5 (emphasis in the original). ComEd states that it “has voluntarily presented an STOU proposal” but that is a far cry from an affirmative request for approval of a rate. Moreover, it does not remedy the obvious infirmity, that offering an STOU for consideration for the first time on rehearing, especially in light of the Company’s continued assertions in the underlying docket that it did not and would not offer an STOU proposal, is not appropriate, even if the Company clearly articulated its intention. ComEd suggests it considered the Commission’s recommendation and “provide[d] its Rate BEST proposal to efficiently meet both the Commission’s directive for a strawman proposal, and its suggestion for an expanded TOU tariff.” ComEd RBR at 5 (emphasis in the original). According to Staff, the former is properly within the scope of rehearing; the Commission directed ComEd to submit a compliance filing within 120 days, a process that was supplanted by rehearing. Final Order at 189. The latter is not. The Commission’s suggestion that ComEd “consider developing and proposing an expanded TOU tariff in the near future” (id. at 190) cannot be read as permission to allow ComEd to introduce a new rate proposal for Commission approval for the first time on rehearing, especially given ComEd’s repeated and unwavering assertions both that it did not intend to do so and that such a proposal is unrelated to the delivery services revenue requirement. CUB/EDF argue that “an STOU proposal was at issue in [the underlying] proceeding.” CUB/EDF IBR at 4 (emphasis omitted). This assertion is at odds with the record evidence. ComEd witness Perkins testified, CUB/EDF “has not proposed such a rate that can be adopted in this proceeding, and it is not appropriate to require the establishment of such a rate in this proceeding.” ComEd Ex. 9.0 REV at 16. Moreover, Mr. Perkins stated CUB/EDF “did not propose a new design for consideration by the parties or the Commission.” Id. In fact, the Commission “acknowledge[d] the arguments pertaining to the Commission’s statutory authority and the lack of sufficient record evidence to formulate such a rate in this case.” Final Order at 189. Staff asserts that the mere fact that CUB/EDF argued that ComEd should be directed to offer an STOU in the underlying proceeding does not make a new STOU proposal for approval appropriate on rehearing, especially given ComEd’s repeated arguments that an STOU was unrelated to the allocation of delivery services costs. ComEd Ex. 4.0 at 7; ComEd Ex. 9.0 REV at 17. CUB/EDF further argue that “Rate BEST is the product of exactly the consensusbuilding process the Commission set forth in the Final Order[.]” CUB/EDF IBR at 2. This argument fails to consider that the “product” the Commission envisioned resulting from a collaborative process was a strawman. Final Order at 189. Moreover, Staff states, there is no “consensus” on an STOU generally, let alone the strawman ComEd presented. Not only is ComEd not clearly requesting any STOU be approved, but only CUB/EDF and ELPC/VS/UCS appear to have been involved in its development and are the only parties that support it. Staff, ICEA/RESA, and REACT all expressed strong opposition to approval of Rate BEST.

#### 3. ICEA/RESA’s Position

ICEA/RESA argue that as an initial matter, consideration of an STOU rate is outside of the scope of this docket, as determined by the Commission, and thus outside
the scope of this rehearing. The Commission, according to ICEA/RESA, was clear as to the scope of the present docket: The Commission maintains that its authority under Section 16-105.5 is limited to consideration of revenue-neutral changes to the rate design and allocation of costs among ComEd’s rate classes, including possible changes to the rate design of Rate MRPP and other delivery services tariffs. Final Order at 19. ICEA/RESA posit that although the Commission did leave open that some topics that ComEd argued were out of scope might actually be within scope (see id.), the Commission later clarified specifically with regard to STOU rates that: “[t]he Commission acknowledges the arguments pertaining to the Commission’s statutory authority and the lack of sufficient record evidence to formulate such a rate in this case.” Id. at 189. ICEA/RESA highlight that the Commission then directed ComEd to collaborate outside of the docket and encouraged ComEd to consider proposing an STOU rate in the future. See id. According to ICEA/RESA, at no point did the Commission hold—explicitly or implicitly—in the Final Order that the Commission had or has authority to approve an STOU rate in the present docket. To the contrary, ICEA/RESA emphasize, the Final Order explicitly and implicitly held that an STOU rate is outside the scope of this docket. ICEA/RESA concede that ComEd’s Application did request that the Commission allow ComEd to propose an STOU rate for the purpose of the Commission approving that rate. See ComEd App. at 10. However, ICEA/RESA counter, ComEd’s Application does not allege that the Commission erred in deciding that STOU rates are outside the scope of the current proceeding—a position that ComEd itself advanced in briefing. See, e.g., ComEd R. Br. on Exceptions at 4 (“First, the ALJPO properly found certain proposals by [CUB/EDF] to go beyond the authorized scope of this proceeding, which is limited to revenue-neutral tariff changes to the allocation of delivery service costs among ComEd’s rate classes and possible changes to the rate design of Rate MRPP – Multi-Year Rate Plan Pricing (‘Rate MRPP’)”); ComEd R. Br. at 4; ComEd Init. Br. at 4, 9-10 (“This proceeding is not a catch-all for any and every concern with a filed rate or rider; it is a proceeding to address the revenue neutral cost allocation of ComEd’s Commissionapproved delivery service revenue requirement.”). At most, ICEA/RESA argue, ComEd’s Application attempts to harmonize ComEd’s argument about the application of Section 16-103(e) — not the scope of the current proceeding as authorized by Section 16-105.5. ICEA/RESA argue that because ComEd did not explicitly seek rehearing of the Commission’s decision that STOU is outside of the scope of the present docket, ComEd has waived that issue. “Any manner of issues may be implied, but the statutory language specifically requires express mention of grounds for review in the petition for rehearing.” Citizens Util. Bd. v. Ill. Com. Comm’n, 166 Ill.2d 111, 136 (1995) (“CUB v. ICC”). ICEA/RESA point out that in CUB v. ICC, the Illinois Supreme Court rejected CUB’s position that an argument it did not explicitly raise on rehearing (regarding retroactive ratemaking) was implicit in an argument that it did raise (regarding single-issue ratemaking) and thus was not waived. See id. ICEA/RESA note that mere mention is not enough to disturb the Commission’s Final Order—instead, ComEd was required to argue that the Commission’s findings
regarding scope were in error. ICEA/RESA argue that ComEd’s failure to argue that a legal decision by the Commission is in error prevents further review: Petitioners also forfeited their argument that the Commission erred by declining to take administrative notice of the designation of the Kreutzer House by Kane County. In their application for rehearing, petitioners mentioned the Commission's denial of the motion but did not assign error to it. Kreutzer v. Ill. Com. Comm’n, 404 Ill.App.3d 791, 809 (2d Dist. 2010). ICEA/RESA conclude that while ComEd fails to even mention the Commission’s viewpoint on the scope of the docket, even if ComEd arguably did it would still be insufficient because ComEd did not argue the Commission erred in determining in the Final Order that an STOU rate is outside the scope of the present docket. According to ICEA/RESA, Section 16-103(e) provides in full that “The Commission shall not require an electric utility to offer any tariffed service other than the services required by this Section, and shall not require an electric utility to offer any competitive service.” 220 ILCS 5/16-103(e). ICEA/RESA observe that the imposition of a new rate cannot be from the Commission. However, ICEA/RESA contrast, both ComEd’s direct and rebuttal testimony only request that the Commission find that ComEd described an STOU rate and thus satisfied ComEd’s obligations or that the rate ComEd describes is reasonable and prudent. See, e.g., ComEd Ex. 19.0 at 1 (requesting a finding that the described STOU rate is prudent and reasonable); ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 2 (requesting a finding that ComEd met its obligations under the Final Order regarding a strawman proposal); ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 2 (requesting a finding that ComEd met its obligations under the Final Order regarding a strawman), 15 (same). ICEA/RESA highlight that while ComEd’s testimony did allude to the potential approval of an STOU rate by the Commission, ComEd’s testimony is carefully and artfully worded so that despite any implication, no request is actually made for the Commission to approve any of the STOU rates that ComEd describes. #### 6. Commission Analysis and Conclusion

Any party may request rehearing following an order by the Commission. 83 Ill. Admin. Code 200.880(c); 220 ILCS 5/10-113. A rehearing request must state issues with specificity, contain an explanation of why new evidence was not previously adduced, and may allege new facts if filed with a verification. 83 Ill. Admin. Code 200.880(a)-(c). The scope of rehearing is limited to issues specified within the application for rehearing and subject to Commission discretion. See 220 ILCS 5/10-113(a) (any party may request rehearing “in respect to any matter determined in said action or proceeding and specified in the application for rehearing”). Nothing in the Act nor Commission rules dictates that the rehearing scope must be limited by how a party raised issues or its position in the underlying case. The Commission finds ComEd’s TOU proposal is within the scope of rehearing. CUB’s proposal that ComEd offer an STOU was addressed by many parties in the underlying case, including ComEd and Staff. See e.g., Final Order at 169-190. The Commission ultimately determined an STOU rate may be beneficial and directed ComEd to engage with stakeholders on a strawman proposal. Id. at 189-190. Within its application for rehearing, ComEd clearly stated its intent to propose a bundled STOU/DTOU rate. ComEd App. for Reh. at 10 (“To be clear: if rehearing is granted, ComEd commits to propose in direct testimony an STOU rate as part of a bundled rate... for the Commission’s consideration and approval in an order on rehearing”). The Commission notes ComEd’s Application included an offer of new evidence, verification, and its reasoning in compliance with 83 Ill. Adm. Code 200.880(a)-(c). The Commission granted rehearing “for the limited purpose of allowing ComEd to present an STOU and/or bundled TOU rate proposal that is harmonized with ComEd’s approved DTOU rate.” Regular Open Mtg. Tr. at 10-11, (Mar. 6, 2025).
The Commission disagrees with Staff and others that stakeholders did not receive appropriate notice of ComEd's proposal. An STOU was debated in the underlying proceeding, and expressly considered on rehearing. The Commission and parties adhered to all notice and scheduling requirements throughout this rehearing. Staff participated in this proceeding since its inception. ICEA and RESA, trade associations representing multiple ARES and other competitive market participants, participated at length in both the underlying proceeding and this rehearing. See Verified Pet. To Intervene, ICEA at 2, (Jun. 26, 2024) (“ICEA is a not-for-profit corporation established as an Illinois-based trade association to represent the interest of competitive energy suppliers... ICEA’s membership includes some of the most active competitive energy suppliers in Illinois”); See also Verified Pet. To Intervene, RESA at 1, (Jun. 26, 2024) (“RESA is a non-profit association of independent corporations that are involved in the competitive supply of electricity and natural gas”). The Commission and parties adhered to all notice and scheduling requirements throughout this rehearing. The Commission notes the notice afforded on this rehearing is comparable, if not more explicit, than the accepted process for tariff filings. The Commission finds Section 16-103(e) of the Act is inapplicable to this rehearing. Section 16-103(e) prohibits the Commission from “requir[ing] an electric utility to offer any tariffed service other than the services required by this Section[.]” 220 ILCS 6/16-103(e). The Commission’s Final Order expressly contemplated the limitations of 16103(e) and instead directed ComEd to engage with stakeholders on STOU rates. Final Order at 189-190. ComEd expressly stated its understanding that the Commission’s Final Order directed “information gathering” and did not “ord[er] ComEd to provide an STOU rate or bundled rate.” ComEd App. for Reh. at 11. ComEd voluntarily offered to make its STOU proposal in this proceeding because rehearing provided a more efficient and cost-effective method of designing the STOU rate than the stakeholder process contemplated in the Commission’s Final Order. ComEd App. for Reh. at 9-10. ComEd further stated rehearing would be preferable to preparing a tariff filing. Id. (“[I]f ComEd were to submit a tariff proposal... after developing the TOU strawman, the total length of that process would be 165 days” plus “litigation for an indeterminate length of time” if the tariff was suspended). ComEd clearly contemplated the options for its STOU rate proposal and implementation and requested the method it found best aligned with the Commission’s findings in its Final Order. Final Order at 189-190; See also ComEd App. for Reh. at 2. The Commission did not require the Company to offer a tariffed service, rather the Commission is evaluating a voluntarily presented proposal at ComEd’s request. As discussed in Section III.B., the Commission finds that a continued exploration of an STOU rate and ComEd’s proposal in this docket aligns with the objectives and considerations of P.A. 102-0662 and the Final Order. Accordingly, the Commission rejects Staff’s and REACT’s arguments regarding revenue neutrality made here and in Section III.B.

### B. Substantive Legal Issues

#### 1. ComEd’s Position

ComEd explains that the Commission reviews new and modified rates for approval under the “just and reasonableness” standard. 220 ILCS 5/9-201(c). A determination by
the Commission that a rate is “just and reasonable” must be based on a preponderance of the evidence in the record. See Ill.-Am. Water Co., Docket No. 24-0097, Order at 8 (Dec. 5, 2024) (“[T]he Commission is obligated to determine, from the proposals and evidence of record, just and reasonable versions of the proposed rates and practices.” (citing 5 ILCS 100/10-15)). ComEd states that its Rate BEST is just and reasonable under Section 9-201 of the Act. 220 ILCS 5/9-201(c). A determination by the Commission that a rate is “just and reasonable” must be based on a preponderance of the evidence in the record. See Docket No. 24-0097, Order at 8 (Dec. 5, 2024) (“[T]he Commission is obligated to determine, from the proposals and evidence of record, just and reasonable versions of the proposed rates and practices.” (citing 5 ILCS 100/10-15)). In support thereof, ComEd explains that Rate BEST aligns with previous Commission directives on TOU rates, namely that it contains differences between on-peak and off-peak periods that are significant enough to encourage customers to shift usage without deterring customers from signing up for the rate, and that its peaks coincide with the price signals sent by the supply market. See ComEd Ex. 19.0 at 4-5. ComEd states that its Rate BEST proposal on rebuttal is the result of collaborative discussions between parties and review of parties’ direct testimony on rehearing. ComEd witness Kremer calculated and presented illustrative rates in support of the Rate BEST Compromise Proposal on rebuttal. See id.; see also ComEd Exs. 19.02, 19.03. ComEd explains that Rate BEST also aligns with the objectives of P.A. 102-0662 and the benefits recognized by the Commission in a TOU rate design. ComEd notes that the Commission's Final Order highlighted that TOU rates lower risks compared to realtime pricing and provide for flexible rate design. Final Order at 189-190. ComEd notes that Rate BEST also helps support the Commission’s statutory objectives in P.A. 102-0662’s performance metric and grid planning statutes to consider TOU rates for both delivery and supply (see 220 ILCS 5/16-108.18(e)(2)(A)(v) (referencing metrics such as “offering a variety of affordable rate options, including demand response, time of use rates for delivery and supply, [and] real-time pricing rates for supply”)). ComEd states that Rate BEST bolsters P.A. 102-0662’s goals to promote “rate design options, technologies or programs that facilitate load flexibility” and “reduce energy usage generally and especially during times of greatest reliance on fossil fuels, and enhance customer engagement opportunities.” 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(a)(2), 16105.17(f)(2)(K). Furthermore, ComEd states that bundled rates with matching time periods help customers that shift usage to off-peak hours to maximize their savings because the benefits accrue to both the delivery and supply portions of their bills. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 5. ComEd explains that this consistency in time periods makes it easier to educate customers and for them to understand how their behavior impacts their total bill. Additionally, ComEd states that a bundled TOU rate creates implementation efficiencies by reducing the volume of billing and IT work across both TOU rates, an issue that ComEd raised in its Application. ComEd states that Staff and ICEA/RESA have raised in this rehearing the issue of a waiver of the Integrated Distribution Company Rules (“IDC” or “IDC Rules”). See Staff IBR at 8; ICEA/RESA IBR at 7-8. ComEd states that, unless and until the Commission approves Rate BEST, it is premature for ComEd to seek a waiver of the IDC Rules. See,
e.g., ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 13. Therefore, ComEd states that the IDC Rules do not prevent the Commission from considering Rate BEST in this rehearing. ComEd explains that utilities must follow the IDC Rules if they elect to structure their business as an IDC, rather than a Functionally Separated Utility. See ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 12. At a high level, an IDC is a utility that does not actively participate in the retail electric supply market and is prohibited from attempting to obtain or retain electric supply customers. See generally 83 Ill. Adm. Code 452.200 et seq. ComEd notes that, as a result, the IDC Rules prohibit utilities that elected to become an IDC from promoting, advertising, or marketing regarding the offering or provision of the retail sale of electricity. See 83 Ill. Adm. Code 452.240(a); ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 13. ComEd notes, however, that the Rules allow an IDC to promote programs for load curtailment and interruption if they are conducted pursuant to a Commission-approved tariff. 83 Ill. Adm. Code 452.230(a). ComEd states that, as proposed, Rate BEST allows customers to curtail or delay their energy usage in response to economic incentives (cheaper rates) or price signals (four daily periods), just like a demand response program. ComEd 16.0 at 13. Also like a demand response program, a well-designed TOU will ultimately help reduce peak demand over time. ComEd 16.0 at 13. Thus, ComEd states, Rate BEST is very similar to ComEd’s load curtailment tariffs (e.g., Rider PTR – Peak Time Rebate) and should be considered a permissible IDC service. ComEd Ex. 16.0; see also Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket No. 12-0484, Interim Order at 18 (Feb. 21, 2013) (ComEd not seeking waiver of IDC Rules to implement Peak Time Rebate program but agreeing to Staff’s proposal to submit marketing materials for review in Peak Time Rebate proceeding). ComEd notes that it plans to primarily provide direct marketing materials to Rate BES – Basic Electric Service (“Rate BES”) customers and will not market to customers that have elected service under Rate BESH – Basic Electric Service-Hourly Energy Pricing (“Rate BESH”) or with a retail electric supplier (“RES”). ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 14. ComEd witness Perkins testified that a customer education and information campaign will be needed to bring awareness of this new program to the attention of customers and allow them to decide to enroll. Id. at 13. ComEd states that, in addition, a major focus of an STOU program is to educate the customer as to the customer's unique consumption patterns, the usage characteristics of various appliances, the cost of power and energy, and the interplay of supply and demand in the energy markets. Id. at 13-14. In ComEd’s view, this means that the customer education surrounding Rate BEST will help lay the foundation for well-informed customers to learn to exercise choice more effectively and to exert control over their energy consumption. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 14. ComEd states that such outreach is important because without appropriate educational materials, customers may not understand or enroll in the rate, missing out on the opportunity to save money. See Id. at 14. ComEd states that if the Commission determines that the IDC Rules apply and Rate BEST is not within the category of permissible IDC services (related to load curtailment programs and customer education), ComEd will seek a waiver. Id. at 14. ComEd notes that it obtained a similar waiver when it implemented Rate RTOUPP – Residential Time of Use Pricing Pilot and Rider RRTP – Residential Real Time Pricing. See Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket No. 08-0411, Order at 3 (Oct. 8, 2008);
Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket Nos. 18-1725/18-1824 (consol.), Order at 54-55 (Oct. 2, 2019). See Staff IBR at 7-8; REACT IBR at 4-5. #### 4. CUB/EDF’s Position

Comm’n, 2015 IL App (2d) 140202, ¶ 79. #### 5. Commission Analysis and Conclusion

The Commission finds it is appropriate and efficient to consider issues related to Rate BEST in this proceeding and rejects Staff’s and REACT’s arguments regarding revenue neutrality. Section 16-105.5 of the Act governs this rehearing, as it did the underlying case. Under Section 16-105.5, utilities must file revenue neutral rate design proposals “consistent with the rate design and cost allocation across customer classes approved in the Commission's most recent order regarding the electric utility's request for a general adjustment to its rates entered under... subsection (e) of Section 16-108.5, or Section 16-108.18 of this Act, as applicable.” 220 ILCS 5/16-105.5(b). The Commission finds consideration and approval of proposals like ComEd’s Rate BEST are appropriate for several reasons. First, Section 16-105.5 does not define revenue neutrality, particularly in the context of P.A. 102-0662. This is a case of first
impression. Second, earlier interpretations of “revenue-neutrality” under Section 16-108.5 were made in the context of performance-based formula rates, not P.A. 102-0662. Such decisions are not automatically applicable here. Third, P.A. 102-0662 mandates holistic consideration of related investments, tariffs, and rate design options, to bolster regulatory efficiency. The statute expressly finds value in “time-variant or dynamic pricing” and “rate design that encourages efficient energy use.” 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(f)(4)(A) and (a)(6). Fourth, the potential costs (or savings) of Rate BEST are unclear at this time, and the record on that issue does not compel deferral of this proceeding. The Commission notes Section 16-105.5 does not define revenue neutral tariff changes related to rate design. See 220 ILCS 5/16-105.5(c)(1) and (2). The Commission further notes this is a case of first impression where a utility that elected to file a multiyear integrated grid and rate plan filed a revenue neutral rate design proceeding under Section 16-105.5. Historically, Illinois agencies’ interpretations of law have been given substantial deference where statutory language is ambiguous, undefined, or involves areas of specialized knowledge, as the informed source and expert in its field. See Provena Covenant Medical Center v. Dep’t of Rev., 236 Ill. 2d 368, 387 (2010); see also People ex rel. Raoul v. Ill. Com. Comm’n, 2019 IL App (1st) 180679-U. Illinois courts consistently defer to the Commission for interpretation of statutes governing the programs it is charged by the legislature with reviewing and enforcing. See Commonwealth Edison Co. v. Ill. Com. Comm’n, 398 Ill. App. 3d 510,514 (2nd Dist. 2009); see also Ill. Consol. Tel. Co. v. Ill. Com. Comm’n., 95 Ill. 2d 142, 152 (1983); People ex rel. Raoul, 2019 IL App (1st) 180679-U. “Rate design and cost-allocation issues, because of their complexity, are uniquely a matter for the Commission’s discretion.” Coal. to Request Equitable Allocation of Costs Together (REACT) v. Ill. Com. Comm’n, 2015 IL App (2d) 140202, ¶ 56. Parties throughout this proceeding have advanced multiple definitions of revenueneutrality. CUB/EDF contend ComEd’s Rate BEST is revenue neutral because it does not create a new service, but rather a new rate. CUB/EDF Ex. 2.0 at 2. In contrast, ComEd stated it previously defined revenue neutrality as any proposal that did not impact the revenue requirement; however, based on the Commission’s Final Order, it believes a broader scope is permissible to consider holistic planning under P.A. 102-0662. Oral Argument Tr. at 8:12-9:2 (July 9, 2025). ICEA/RESA provided several interpretations of revenue neutrality, including an alternative definition of “no change in the cost[s] that are being allocated across different recovery methods.” Id. at 28:11-18. The Commission finds party positions of record illustrate the ambiguity of Section 16-105.5 in this case of first impression. The language of Section 16-105.5 mirrors that of its predecessor statute, Section 16-108.5(e); however, the distinctive objectives and context of P.A. 102-0662 require an interpretation that reflects the overarching direction of the newer statute. See Coal. to Request Equitable Allocation of Costs Together (REACT), 2015 IL App (2d) 140202 (finding the Commission should consider the broad language of the Act as a whole when interpreting individual provisions). Prior to the passage of P.A. 102-0662, Section 16-108.5 governed ComEd’s rate design investigation proceedings. The Commission defined the scope of those proceedings as “limited to considering revenue neutral tariff
changes to allocation of delivery service costs among ComEd’s rate classes and possible changes to the rate design formula of Rate DSPP – Delivery Service Pricing and Performance.” Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket No. 13-0387, Order at 3 (Dec. 18, 2013). Further, the Commission notes that while Section 16-105.5 references Section 16-108.5, it includes an “or” not “and” signaling the Commission should read the statute to either refer to Section 16-108.18 (multi-year integrated rate plan) or Section 16-108.5(e) where applicable. As ComEd has a current multi-year rate plan in place, the Commission finds it appropriate to adhere to the provisions of Section 16-108.18 instead of Section 16-108.5(e). While the Commission’s prior rulings remain instructive, the Commission cannot ignore the General Assembly’s decision to define a new revenue-neutral provision within the confines of P.A. 102-0662, rather than referring to Section 16-108.5. The legislature explicitly concluded that performance-based formula rates, including Section 16-108.5, have “not been sufficiently transformative in urgently moving electric utilities toward the State's ambitious energy policy goals.” 220 ILCS 5/16-108.18(a)(4). That redirection produced a new rate design provision, instead of adoption of Section 16-108.5. Unlike Section 16-108.5, which places strict limits on revenue-neutral rate design proceedings, Section 16-105.5 authorizes a broader scope. See 220 ILCS 5/108.5(e). Accordingly, the Commission cannot disregard the directives set forth in P.A. 102-0662 requiring “holistic consideration of all related investments, planning processes, tariffs, rate design options, programs, and other utility policies and plans.” 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(f)(4). In these circumstances, the Commission finds rigid adherence to a narrow interpretation of revenue neutrality cannot be reconciled with P.A. 102-0662’s express mandate that the Commission “shall” consider TOU rates for both delivery and supply. See 220 ILCS 5/16-108.18(e)(2)(A)(v) and 105.17(f)(4)(A). Section 16-105.5, under which this proceeding was initiated, refers to Section 16-108.18, which expressly discusses a supply TOU. See 220 ILCS 5/16-108.18(e)(2)(A)(v). Additionally, the grid investment planning required by P.A. 102-0662 orders consideration of “the opportunities presented by nontraditional solutions to utility, customer, and grid needs that may be more efficient and cost-effective, and less environmentally harmful than traditional solutions” and requires evaluation of non-wires alternatives. See 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(a)(6) and (f)(2)(G). P.A. 102-0662 and prior Commission Orders consistently treated Section 16-108.18, the multi-year integrated rate plan provision, as intrinsically linked to and informed by related sections of P.A. 102-0662. See e.g., Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket Nos. 22-0486/23-0055 (Consol.), Final Order (Dec.14, 2023). P.A. 102-0662 further requires that Commission-approved plans shall, “[t]o the maximum extent practicable, align the 4-year investment plan and annual capital budgets with the electric utility's Multi-Year Integrated Grid Plan.” 220 ILCS 5/16-108.18(d)(3)(H). This is a clear admonition against unnecessary repetition or delay in Commission proceedings. The Commission is authorized to consider rate design solutions that may mitigate customer costs. 220 ILCS 5/16-108.18(d)(14) (“the Commission may, on its own initiative, take revenue-neutral measures to relieve the impact of rate increases on customers. Such initiatives may be taken by the Commission in the first Multi-Year Rate Plan... or in other instances described in this Act”). Customer affordability and cost-
effectiveness are foundational pillars of P.A. 102-0662. See 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(d)(1); (d)(2); (d)(7); and (d)(11); see also Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket Nos. 22-0486/23-0055 (Consol.), Order at 32 (Dec. 19, 2024) (P.A. 102-0662 contains “protective grid design and statutory objectives, including cost-effective investments, affordable rates, and coordinated grid planning at minimal costs”). Repeated discussion of TOU rates in P.A. 102-0662 exemplifies the General Assembly’s express interest in supply and delivery TOU rates for consideration in rate design and as potential mitigation to grid investments and resulting rate increases. The Commission finds TOU rates serve as a potential mitigative measure to customer bill impacts resulting from both the ongoing energy transition within the State and external factors, like Regional Transmission Organization market fluctuations. Accordingly, the Commission finds consideration of customer benefits appropriate to determine whether a proposal is revenue neutral pursuant to Section 16-105.5. The Commission acknowledges implementation of a bundled TOU rate may be accompanied by requisite incremental costs. The Commission finds, to the extent implementation costs exist for ComEd’s Rate BEST, the net costs may be de minimis in the context of grid investments and the record evidence is not sufficient to delay mandated initiatives for procedural reasons. The Commission finds the benefits and efficiencies gained from consideration of Rate BEST within this proceeding outweigh any support for the narrow interpretation of revenue neutrality proposed by certain parties. This determination is consistent with the Commission’s finding in the underlying case. “Reasonable anticipation of new services and tariffs in furtherance of P.A. 102-0662’s objectives support more inclusive rate design proceedings.” Final Order at 19. Accordingly, the Commission finds continued exploration of an STOU rate and ComEd’s proposal in this docket aligns with the objectives and considerations previously expressed in the Final Order and P.A. 102-0662. As an IDC, ComEd is generally prohibited from promoting, advertising, or marketing retail electric supply services under 83 Ill. Adm. Code 452.240(a). The Commission acknowledges the concerns raised by Staff regarding ComEd's yet to be developed outreach materials for Rate BEST. ComEd notes that the Commission granted ComEd’s petition to revise its IDC Implementation Plan with respect to outreach materials for Rate RTOUPP, a pilot program similar to the proposed Rate BEST. Though such revisions may also be appropriate here, it is premature to make a determination prior to the development of outreach materials. Once developed, if ComEd's Rate BEST materials are deemed to be for the purposes of promoting, advertising, or marketing the program, the Company must seek a waiver from the Commission. ComEd is directed to file a petition to modify its IDC Implementation Plan or, in the alternative, seek clarification that no modification is necessary no later than January 1, 2026. The Commission acknowledges ComEd has agreed to include its outreach materials on Rate BEST in the stakeholder engagement process defined for its DTOU in the underlying case. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 12; Final Order at 167. The Commission reiterates ComEd must provide adequate time for stakeholders to engage with the development of Rate BEST materials. The Commission further addresses ICEA/RESA’s tying argument in Section V.A.4 and the IDC Rules in Section V.B.4. The Commission’s conclusion regarding the justness and reasonableness of ComEd’s Rate BEST can be found in Section IV.B.5.

## IV. STOU PROPOSAL

### A. Proposal in ComEd Direct Testimony

#### 1. ComEd’s Position

ComEd has worked with CUB/EDF and ELPC/VS/UCS on changes to its Rate BEST proposal but remains supportive of its original plan (referred to as the “strawman proposal”) for a residential TOU rate. ComEd Ex. 17.0 at 34. The strawman proposal is a residential STOU rate that will be bundled with ComEd’s residential DTOU rate and is not intended to be available separately. ComEd states that its strawman proposal reflects the same four time periods approved for the DTOU rate by the Commission in the Final Order. The Morning period is from 6 a.m. – 1 p.m., the Mid-Day Peak period is 1 p.m. – 7 p.m., the Evening period is 7 p.m. – 9 p.m., and the Overnight period is 9 p.m. – 6 a.m. The periods have corresponding relative magnitude to each other (i.e., the Overnight period is the lowest charge, and the Mid-Day Peak period is the highest charge). While there will be seasonal differences in rates, there will be no change to the relative magnitude across seasons. ComEd Ex. 17.0 at 17. ComEd did not change the time periods or seasonal structure from the strawman proposal in the final Rate BEST Compromise Proposal. ComEd states that its strawman proposal would handle recovery of the capacity charge and certain components of the transmission and miscellaneous procurement charges on a kilowatt (“kW”) basis. ComEd Ex. 17.0 at 22, 30-31. This method of recovery is often referred to as a “demand-based charge,” and is different than “usagebased” volumetric charges that are recovered on a kilowatt-hour (“kWh”) basis. Id. at 2021. Demand-based charges are fixed and do not vary month to month, whereas volumetric charges change with the number of kWhs a customer uses. Id. at 20-21. Briefly, capacity charges represent the annual cost ComEd must pay to PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (“PJM”), based on a customer's Peak Load Contribution (“PLC”), which is determined by the customer's demand during the five highest peak hours of the PJM system during the previous year. Customers can reduce their PLC by lowering their demand during these peak times, which will be reflected on their bill the following year. Transmission charges are costs ComEd incurs to be part of the PJM transmission system, while miscellaneous procurement charges are costs ComEd incurs related to procuring electric power and energy from PJM-administered markets for retail customers. ComEd Ex. 17.0 at 18, 30. ComEd changed the way the capacity charge is recovered from the strawman proposal to the final Rate BEST as described in Section IV.B.2., below. ComEd also changed the way the transmission and miscellaneous procurement charges are recovered from the strawman proposal to the final Rate BEST, as described in Section B.3., below.

## V. IMPLEMENTATION

### A. Bundling with DTOU

#### 1. ComEd’s Position

ComEd states that Rate BEST is designed to complement the DTOU rate already approved by the Commission in this proceeding. For example, Rate BEST uses the same time periods as the DTOU rate time periods approved by the Commission in the Final Order. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 7; see also Final Order at 139-40, 147-48. ComEd states that this is one reason it proposes that Rate BEST be available only as a bundled option with ComEd’s DTOU rate. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 7. ComEd states that this complementary TOU design will benefit customers by synchronizing the time periods, which means that customers will only have to track one set of time blocks, but they will receive benefits on both their supply and delivery charges for shifting load out of peak times. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 7.
ComEd also states that, in addition to the benefits customers would realize from the bundled TOU, two additional efficiencies will be realized – design efficiencies and implementation efficiencies. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 10. ComEd explains that design efficiencies include using the same four time periods and seasonality approved for ComEd’s DTOU rate. This mirroring of design aligns the DTOU and STOU rate to make their bundling natural. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 10. ComEd also explains that there are two distinct implementation efficiencies achieved through the creation of a bundled TOU rate during this rehearing. First, efficiencies are created related to the IT work needed within ComEd to implement the bundled TOU. Second, customer education and marketing of the bundled TOU rate will be more efficient than marketing a DTOU and STOU separately. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 11. ComEd states that, if Rate BEST is approved by the Commission without modification, its harmonized design (i.e., using identical time blocks as the DTOU) should ultimately reduce the volume of billing and IT implementation work across both TOU rates. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 11. ComEd states that IT implementation work (i.e., development, programming, testing, and training) that is specific only to the DTOU is already underway. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 11. ComEd explains that IT work necessary to implement the STOU, or work necessary to create the bundled TOU, is on hold pending the order on rehearing. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 11. ComEd explains that, by proceeding with only the DTOU-specific IT work and pausing any potentially overlapping work that might change depending on the final order on rehearing, ComEd is reducing the risk of unnecessary work (if no STOU is approved), or rework (if the final STOU that is approved is different than ComEd’s proposal). ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 11. ComEd estimates these potential wasted costs could be between $5.7 million and $8 million, representing work on DTOU that could not be reused if ComEd had to program DTOU now and then had to reprogram it in the near future to address changes to align DTOU with the new bundled STOU/DTOU rate. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 11. ComEd states that customer outreach and education efficiencies are created by offering a bundled TOU rate, noting that a bundled TOU provides consistency in the peak and off-peak periods, which makes it significantly easier to educate customers and for them to understand how changes in their behaviors impact their total bill. ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 12. ComEd explains that, by bundling the TOU rate and rolling out the TOU offerings in 2026, ComEd will be able to use one set of marketing and educational materials, which will better enable ComEd to provide clear and consistent messaging to support informed customer rate decisions. Id. In addition, ComEd states that TOU information will be reflected in ComEd’s customer-facing online bill comparison tool. Id. ComEd anticipates customer outreach and education on the TOUs will begin in December 2025/January 2026 (to coincide with the launch of the DTOU) and ramp up through the first half of 2026, leading up to the June 2026 bundled TOU offering. Id. ComEd states that the Commission should reject ICEA/RESA’s argument that ComEd’s proposal to bundle Rate BEST with its DTOU rate is prohibited by Section 452.270 of the Commission’s Rules and the Antitrust Act. See ICEA/RESA IBR at 8-9 (citing 83 Ill. Adm. Code 452.270; 740 ILCS 10/3(4)). ComEd states that the plain language of Section 3 of the Antitrust Act does not apply to bundling ComEd’s STOU rate with its DTOU rate.
ComEd states that Section 3 of the Antitrust Act prohibits leasing or making a contract for the sale of services on the condition that the purchaser “shall not use or deal in the [service] of a competitor or competitors of the lessor or seller” where the effect of such condition “may be to substantially lessen competition or tend to create a monopoly in any line of commerce[.]” 740 ILCS 10/3(4) (emphasis added). ComEd explains that, under ICEA/RESA’s theory, the allegedly “tied” product is ComEd’s DTOU rate (i.e., to take service under the STOU, a customer must take service under the DTOU). See ICEA/RESA IBR at 8. However, ComEd notes that there is no competitor in ComEd’s service territory for electricity distribution service – ComEd is, by design and regulation, the monopoly provider of distribution service. ComEd states that there is thus no risk that bundling of Rate BEST with ComEd’s DTOU would “substantially lessen competition” for distribution service. Furthermore, ComEd explains that, under ICEA/RESA’s logic, any bundled supply rate that ComEd currently offers with its standard distribution service (e.g., Rate BES or Rate BESH) would be a violation of the Antitrust Act because customers must take distribution service from ComEd to choose those supply rates and that such a situation would be absurd. ComEd notes that a proposal to require customers to take supply from ComEd to participate in its DTOU rate would be a much different situation, as the tied product – supply service – is subject to retail competition in Illinois. However, that is not the scenario presented in this rehearing or raised by ICEA/RESA.

#### 2. ICEA/RESA’s Position

ICEA/RESA note that requiring a retail customer to take DTOU service when selecting the STOU service is prohibited tying under Section 452.270 of the Commission’s rules. ICEA/RESA cite to subsection (4) of Section 3 of the Antitrust Act which provides in relevant part that: Lease or make a sale or contract for sale of goods, wares, merchandise, machinery, supplies, or other commodities, or services (including master antenna television service), whether patented or unpatented, for use, consumption, enjoyment, or resale, or fix a price charged thereof, or discount from, or rebate upon, such price, on the condition, agreement, or understanding that the lessee or purchaser thereof shall not use or deal in the goods, wares, merchandise, machinery, supplies, or other commodity or service (including cable television service or cable television relay service), of a competitor or competitors of the lessor or seller, where the effect of such lease, sale or contract for such sale or such condition, agreement, or understanding may be to substantially lessen competition or tend to create a monopoly in any line of commerce; 740 ILCS 10/3(4). ICEA/RESA cite that the Illinois Supreme Court explained that “One violation set forth in this section [Section 4 quoted above] is commonly referred to as a tying arrangement whereby a party agrees to sell or lease an item (the tying item) only on the condition that the purchaser or lessee also purchase a different item (the tied item).”
People ex rel. Scott v. Schwulst Bldg. Ctr., Inc., 89 Ill. 2d 365, 370 (1982). ICEA/RESA further cite that the Illinois Supreme Court further determined that “the Illinois legislature intended that tying arrangements be dealt with harshly” and violations are on a per se basis and not evaluated through the lens of reasonableness. See id. at 376-377. ICEA/RESA conclude that no matter how reasonable or appropriate ComEd may argue (or the Commission may find) requiring taking a ComEd STOU and DTOU rate together, such a required pairing is per se tying where the purported reasonableness is not relevant to that determination.

#### 3. CUB/EDF’s Position

CUB/EDF concur with ComEd’s proposal to automatically enroll Rate BEST STOU customers in the DTOU and not automatically pair DTOU enrollees in the STOU. See ComEd Ex. 17.0 at 17; ComEd Ex. 18.01. The effect of this arrangement is to allow customers the option to enroll in a bundled TOU rate offered by ComEd (Rate BEST) or to pair the DTOU with any supply rate offering with which the customer is otherwise eligible. Thus, a ComEd customer who opts to switch can choose Rate BEST or can pair the DTOU with Rate BES (for a more gradual delivery-only TOU), Rate BESH (Real Time Pricing), or an ARES rate (providing ARES customers the full range of delivery rate options). This arrangement does not allow for a ComEd customer to take service on a ComEd-offered standalone STOU, but a customer interested in a lower-stakes TOU rate than Rate BEST would retain the option to enroll in the DTOU and a flat supply rate. CUB/EDF say the standalone DTOU offering thus obviates the need for ComEd to offer a standalone STOU rate option.

#### 4. ELPC/VS/UCS’s Position

ComEd’s bundling of the supply and delivery TOU rates is a crucial feature to this Rate BEST Compromise Proposal. CUB/EDF witness McCann notes that “[h]aving one portion of a customer’s bill determined on an hourly basis and another on a multipart time period basis complicates a customer’s decision rather than simplifies it.” CUB/EDF Ex. 1.0 at 7. ELPC/VS/UCS agree with witness McCann that failing to bundle the rates will create inconsistent price signals and confuse customers. Id. at 6. In contrast, pairing supply and distribution TOU rates assists customers in making “customer friendly” choices, because customers no longer need to track two varying price signal streams, instead they can focus on a single unified price and decide how to manage their energy usage. Id. at 7. Moreover, bundling the STOU and DTOU rates is consistent with the Commission’s Final Order in the initial part of this docket. Final Order at 147. A bundled TOU rate is also an efficient tool for promoting distributed energy resources (“DER”) participation. ELPC Ex. 1 at 5. As ELPC witness Kenworthy explained in his direct testimony, a bundled TOU rate can maximize the value of DER programs like Virtual Power Plants and non-wires alternatives. Id. at 5. Because bundled rates are more straightforward, customers can take simple actions like utilizing smart thermostats and shift or reduce their energy usage during peak time periods. Id. at 15. This will allow customers the option to lower their energy bills and decrease the load on the grid. Id. ELPC/VS/UCS urge the Commission to approve the bundling of the STOU and DTOU rates.

#### 5. Commission Analysis and Conclusion

The Commission approves ComEd’s proposal to bundle Rate BEST with ComEd’s approved DTOU rate. By bundling the two, there will likely be lower implementation costs for ratepayers in general. The Commission agrees with CUB/EDF that the pairing may also result in cost savings to individual Rate BEST customers who react to the aligned price signals to lower their energy bills. As a result, the Commission highlights that would decrease the load on the grid in furtherance of the policy objectives of P.A. 102-0662. The Commission rejects ICEA/RESA’s argument that bundling of Rate BEST with ComEd’s DTOU rate violates the Commission’s rules against tying. A ComEd customer will not be required to enroll in Rate BEST to obtain the DTOU, the product of which ComEd is the sole provider in its service territory. Instead, customers will continue to have the option to pair a different supply rate as they do currently. Moreover, P.A. 102-0662 directly contemplates regulated utilities offering STOU rates. See 220 ILCS 5/16-108.18(e)(2)(A)(v).

## VI. COST RECOVERY

### A. ComEd’s Position

ComEd explains that there are two categories of costs associated with implementing the STOU: (1) supply procurement costs and (2) administration costs
(which includes IT, implementation, customer education and marketing, and program administration costs). ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 9. ComEd states that these categories of costs will be recovered in two different ways. ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 9. Supply procurement costs will be recovered directly from STOU customers as a pass-through, just as they are recovered today for ComEd customers taking supply under ComEd’s other supply rate offerings. ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 9. ComEd states that, as with ComEd’s bundled flat rate (Rate BES) and hourly rate (Rate BESH) offerings, these costs will be reviewed and reconciled in ComEd’s annual Power Procurement reconciliation proceedings. ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 9. ComEd proposes to recover the Rate BEST administrative costs in the same manner as it will recover the DTOU administrative costs, which is through ComEd’s distribution revenue requirements. ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 9. ComEd states that it is appropriate to recover the STOU rate administrative costs through the delivery services rate because part of the synergies and efficiencies inherent to harmonizing the DTOU and Rate BEST STOU is that they can and will be jointly implemented and marketed to customers. ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 10; see also REACT Cross Ex. 2 on Reh’g (ComEd’s Response to REACT Request No. 10.02(b) (noting “[m]any, if not all, [Rate BEST] administrative costs will be incurred to jointly support the DTOU rate, on which Rate BEST customers must also take service”)). ComEd explains that this will help reduce overall project costs compared to if they had disparate designs and had to be implemented and marketed separately. ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 10. For example, ComEd explains that the IT development to create the kWh groups for DTOU will be leveraged to support Rate BEST too. Because the daily time periods are the same, ComEd states that the marketing and customer education materials will be developed so that they support both TOU offerings. Id. ComEd states that the two TOU offerings’ intertwined nature means that any attempt to allocate administrative costs between various cost-recovery mechanisms would be imprecise, unnecessarily complicated, and unwarranted. Id. ComEd states that recovering these joint administrative costs from all customers in distribution rates makes sense as both the DTOU and the STOU will be available to all residential customers. Id. Furthermore, ComEd states that recovering the administrative costs through distribution charges supports the future success of the STOU as it will not deter potential early adopters with prohibitively high rates. Id. ComEd states that REACT’s proposal whereby Rate BEST administrative costs would be recovered solely from residential supply customers is inappropriate. See REACT IBR at 8-14. Because the STOU and DTOU rates are bundled, ComEd states that an allocation of the administrative costs would be imprecise, unnecessarily complicated, and unwarranted. ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 10. Furthermore, ComEd notes that it is not always practical to create many different allocators or cost tracking mechanisms to assign costs with that level of precision when some of the rates/programs have the benefit of supporting larger statewide goals like load shifting, load reduction, clean energy, and assistance for low-income customers. ComEd notes that REACT takes issue with ComEd’s proposed allocation of Rate BEST administrative costs across all customers in distribution rates, rather than just being
allocated to residential customers. See REACT IBR at 8-10. But, as ComEd explains, its proposed allocation across all customers in distribution rates is consistent with how it recovers administrative costs in similar situations. ComEd notes that it currently offers several Commission-approved rates and riders for which it recovers administrative costs from its overall distribution customers, even if some of those rates and riders are not available to all customer classes. See REACT Cross Ex. 2 on Reh’g (ComEd Response to REACT Date Request 10.02(c) (providing examples of riders only available to residential customers, including Rider LID – Low-Income Discount and Rider RRTP – Residential Real Time Pricing Program, that recover administrative costs from all customers in distribution rates). ComEd notes that REACT also raises the specter of additional indirect costs because of hypothetical increased call volumes to ComEd’s Customer Call Center to inquire about Rate BEST. See REACT IBR at 11-14. ComEd states that REACT has not provided any evidence that such an increase in call volumes is likely to occur, or that additional indirect costs will result. ComEd states that, in fact, the record provides that ComEd does not anticipate that there will be materially higher call volumes directed to the Customer Call Center directly related to Rate BEST. REACT Cross Ex. 5 on Reh’g (ComEd Response to REACT Data Request 10.06). ComEd explains this is because:
(1) ComEd anticipates Rate BEST enrollment to be conducted online, not through the Customer Call Center; (2) the DTOU rate will have already been introduced in early 2026, so initial customer questions related to TOU rates will likely already have been asked; and (3) any calls received after the bundled TOU rate has been introduced will likely be about the TOU bundle, and not limited only to the Rate BEST STOU rate. REACT Cross Ex. 5 on Reh’g (ComEd Response to REACT Data Request 10.06). ComEd states that REACT reiterates various of its arguments related to Customer Call Center allocation that the Commission did not adopt in the underlying proceeding and regarding which Staff is currently conducting an undocketed investigation stemming from a directive in the Final Order. Final Order at 43. ComEd recommends that, to the extent REACT believes that substantive questions regarding ComEd’s allocation of costs between delivery and supply is currently inadequate, the better venue to raise those questions is Staff’s informal investigation.

### B. Staff’s Position

Staff states that the cost to implement an STOU, including system changes and marketing and promotional efforts, are unknown. It is also unknown how an STOU rate could impact the allocation between supply and delivery. Presumably, a new supply rate offering would lead to both inquiries by supply customers prior to enrollment and an increased need for customer assistance for customers after enrollment. These changes will directly impact customer care costs which are the subject of an ongoing investigation into the allocation between supply and delivery costs. REACT notes that ComEd proposes to recover administrative costs associated with an STOU or bundled TOU from “all customers”, but Rate BEST is only available to residential customers. REACT IBR at 8. Staff agrees this proposal violates cost causation principles.

Staff states that ComEd offered no evidence about a budget for an STOU, a customer need for an STOU, a benefit cost analysis supporting implementation of an STOU, or details about its proposed education and marketing initiatives. Absent that information, it is not possible to fully understand the impact on non-residential customers who are not eligible to enroll in Rate BEST or the impact on residential customers who take supply from ARES, making them ineligible to enroll in Rate BEST. It is also not clear what costs, rather direct or indirect, should be allocated to ComEd’s supply function rather than recouped from distribution customers.

### C. REACT’s Position

REACT notes that ComEd identified two (2) categories of costs associated with implementing Rate BEST: its supply procurement costs and direct administration costs. REACT identified a third category that the Commission should be concerned about: indirect costs. REACT maintains that ComEd appropriately proposes to recover direct supply procurement costs through its “Power Procurement” proceedings. See ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 9. However, REACT objects to the way in which ComEd proposes to recover its direct administrative costs as well as its indirect costs associated with Rate BEST. REACT explains that direct administrative costs that support a supply time of use rate should be recovered in ComEd’s supply rates to be consistent with the cost causation principle of ratemaking. REACT further explains that despite ComEd only making its STOU rate available to residential customers, ComEd improperly intends to force all ComEd customers to pay for the information-technology, implementation, customer education and marketing, and program administration costs through their delivery charges. See REACT IBR at 8, citing ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 10; see also REACT Cross Ex. 2 on Reh’g (ComEd Response to Data Request 10.02(b)). The costs associated with the residential rates STOU, DTOU, and Rate BEST should be recovered in rates from residential customers only since no customers other than residential customers can take service under STOU, DTOU, and Rate BEST. REACT notes that properly assigning the costs for the STOU and DTOU programs exclusively to ComEd’s residential customers would not present an unreasonable burden on those ratepayers, resulting in an increase of $0.000019/kWh or a monthly charge of $0.01122/residential account. REACT IBR at 10. REACT concludes that there are no material reasons for not assigning this de minimis cost to ComEd’s residential customers exclusively as they are the only consumers eligible for the rate. REACT notes that ComEd’s primary reason for proposing to force non-residential customers to pay the administrative costs of a rate only available to residential customers is that there are similar instances in the ComEd tariff where administrative costs for specific rates and riders that are available to only a portion of customers are simply added to burgeoning delivery rates for all customer classes. REACT maintains that regardless of whether ComEd has failed to accurately allocate administrative costs for other rates and riders, this approach is completely at odds with the cost causation principle and should not be perpetuated by the Commission. See REACT IBR at 10, citing 220 ILCS 5/1-105(d)(iii) (in defining the State’s equity goal: “the cost of supplying public utility services is allocated to those who cause the costs to be incurred”). REACT maintains that for these direct costs, ComEd has provided no
justification for delivery services customers to cross-subsidize ComEd’s supply rate; likewise, ComEd has not justified a rate where non-residential customers would subsidize administrative costs that are incurred solely for residential customers. REACT is also concerned that the deployment of the STOU, DTOU, and Rate BEST will result in the allocation of indirect costs to customers who are not eligible for those rates. REACT maintains that ComEd’s customer support services and Call Center costs exemplify the potential for indirect cost allocations resulting from ComEd offering a STOU under the terms of Rate BEST. REACT noted that each contact with the Call Center costs ratepayers approximately twenty dollars. REACT explains that under ComEd’s proposal, a Call Center interaction concerning ComEd’s STOU rate (a purely supply-related issue) would not be recorded as a “supply-related” cost and then recovered from the utility’s “supply” customers. Instead, under ComEd’s cost allocation approach for Call Center expenses, it appears that between $0 and $5.54 of the $19.98 cost for a supply-related contact to the ComEd Call Center would be allocated to ComEd’s “supply” function and between $14.44 and $19.98 would be allocated as “distribution” and recovered in delivery services rates from all ComEd consumers – even nonresidential customers that are prohibited from participating in the STOU rate. REACT recommends that the Commission avoid adding another improper cost allocation and instead adhere to cost causation principles by clearly articulating in its Order on Rehearing that all direct and indirect costs associated with STOU, DTOU, and Rate BEST are to be recovered from residential customer classes only. Further, costs that support the STOU rate should be recovered in ComEd’s supply rates.

### D. CUB/EDF’s Position

CUB/EDF say that in light of REACT’s meritless claim of having lacked the opportunity to respond to the Rate BEST proposal, it is surprising to see REACT propose a change to STOU administrative cost allocation for the first time in briefing on rehearing, having not filed testimony or briefing on the issue at any point prior in the docket. Specifically, REACT asks the Commission to order ComEd to allocate the direct costs of implementing Rate BEST to supply rates rather than delivery rates, and that they should be entirely allocated to residential customers. REACT Corr. IBR at 13-14. The allocation of these costs between supply and delivery rates is not ripe for adjudication here, for at least two reasons. First, REACT provides no record evidence in support of its proposal. Second, the Commission already ruled on the supply and delivery cost allocation question in the January Final Order: the Commission direct[ed] Staff to conduct an investigation into the appropriate allocation between supply and delivery service costs and to engage with the Company and interested stakeholders on the appropriate allocation method. Staff shall file a report with the Commission discussing its findings and providing recommended revisions or actions no later than one year of the date of this final Order. Final Order at 35.
As for the allocation of costs between classes, REACT argues only residential customers should pay these costs because only they are eligible to take service on Rate BEST. REACT Corr. IBR at 2, 13-14. CUB/EDF say the only evidence in the record on this question cuts against REACT’s reasoning. In response to a discovery request from REACT, ComEd explained that there are plenty other examples—nine, in fact—of ComEd rates and riders for which ComEd recovers administrative costs from ratepayers that are not eligible to participate. REACT Cross Ex. 2 on Reh’g at 2. CUB/EDF contend it further undermines REACT’s claim of unfair treatment against its members that the majority of these nine riders require residential customers to pay for administrative costs of rates for which they are not eligible. Id. CUB/EDF say REACT makes the general claim that the practice of collecting costs to administer a rate or rider for which only certain customer classes are eligible from all customer classes is unfair. REACT Corr. IBR at 2. CUB/EDF note REACT has made no objection in this rate design investigation docket, in which all relevant tariffs were open for review on initial hearing, to any of the existing instances of this practice, much less the several instances where residential ratepayers are, by REACT’s reasoning, unfairly subsidizing nonresidential customers like REACT’s members. CUB/EDF say if REACT’s concern truly lies with the fairness of this practice generally, disallowing the socialization of administrative costs for only this one rate, only to the benefit of nonresidential customers, cannot be said to serve any objective sense of fairness. CUB/EDF contend REACT was at liberty to raise this issue, as it pertains to ComEd’s tariffed rates and riders generally, seek an accounting of said costs, and propose an allocation method it considers fairer in this docket. CUB/EDF say instead, REACT seeks to leave a status quo it claims to be unfair intact, except in one specific instance that happens to work against its interest. CUB/EDF note that REACT, or any other party, is free to propose a holistic investigation into the allocation of costs to administer rates and riders for which only certain customer classes are eligible in a future rate design investigation. CUB/EDF state REACT similarly argues that indirect costs such as Call Center costs pertaining to Rate BEST should be allocated exclusively to residential customers. Id. at 11-14. In response to a REACT data request seeking the additional Call Center traffic and related costs from adding Rate BEST, ComEd responded that it does not expect them to be significant, as signups would be handled online and, as Rate BEST is automatically paired with the Commission-approved DTOU, any calls about Rate BEST necessarily would concern the already-approved DTOU as well and therefore not be additive. REACT Cross Ex. 5 on Reh’g. In its brief, REACT dismisses the Company’s explanation and dives into speculative hypotheticals. See id. CUB/EDF contend this is not a sufficient basis for a Commission order.

### E. Commission Analysis and Conclusion

The Commission notes that the ongoing investigation into the allocation of costs between supply and delivery services will affect more than just the Rate BEST tariff. Depending on the findings of the investigation, modifications may be required for the Rate BEST tariff, as well as other tariffs. The Commission finds that the possibility of a modification that may or may not occur is not sufficient reason to decline Rate BEST. Following the submission of Staff’s report in January 2026, the Commission will undertake
a comprehensive review of ComEd’s cost allocation procedures and address any necessary modifications through the appropriate proceedings. The Commission finds it appropriate to recover Rate BEST’s administrative costs through the delivery services rate. ComEd only proposes to recover administrative costs associated with the bundled STOU/DTOU rate in this manner. Supply procurement costs will be recovered directly from STOU customers as a pass-through, like all supply procurement costs recovered by ComEd. ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 9. ComEd specifies that its Rate BEST administrative costs recovered through the distribution rate will include only capital investments, such as IT infrastructure to support the DTOU and STOU rate. REACT Cross Ex. 2.0 on Rehearing at 2. ComEd notes the IT development to create the kWh groups for DTOU will be leveraged to support Rate BEST. ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 10. Further, since the daily time periods are the same, the consumer materials will support both TOU offerings. Id. Given the inherent shared nature of the recoverable administrative costs, the Commission agrees with ComEd that disaggregation and allocation between the supply and distribution rates may be arbitrary or imprecise and unnecessarily complicated. The Commission further agrees the bundled nature of the STOU/DTOU rate makes disaggregation inefficient and cost ineffective. Id. (“Part of the synergies and efficiencies inherent to harmonizing the DTOU and Rate BEST STOU is that they can and will be jointly implemented... this will reduce overall project costs compared to if they had disparate designs”). The Commission further finds it appropriate to approve ComEd’s proposed cost recovery method as all customers should benefit from the peak load reduction objective of Rate BEST. The Commission finds the statewide goal of peak load reduction ultimately benefits all customers much like the statewide goals of load shifting, clean energy adoption, and assistance for low-income customers. Accordingly, ComEd’s proposed recovery method for administrative costs is approved. The Commission rejects ICEA/RESA’s proposal to recover costs exclusively from STOU participants. Though the overall estimated budget for Rate BEST is relatively small, the Commission is concerned that early adopters’ bill impacts from ICEA/RESA’s proposal could act as a deterrent to enrollment. See ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 11. Comparatively, ComEd estimates the outreach costs will have a monthly impact of $0.007 per customer if its proposal is adopted. Id. ComEd’s proposed cost recovery method is not novel and has been previously approved by the Commission in other cases. ComEd stated the Company often recovers administrative costs from the delivery rate when allocating costs between supply and delivery would be imprecise or a standalone administrative cost recovery does not exist. Oral Argument Tr. at 7:16-8:11, (July 9, 2025) (noting the Residential Realtime Pricing Program and several riders recover administrative costs from the distribution charge). Similarly, ComEd currently offers nine programs that recover administrative costs across all customer classes despite being available to certain classes. REACT Cross Ex. 2.0 on Rehearing at 2. The Commission acknowledges parties’ cost causation concerns but finds there is no record evidence that supports deviating from the cost recovery method proposed by ComEd.
