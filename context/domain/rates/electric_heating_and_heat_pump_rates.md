# Electric heating and heat pump rates: catalog of what we know

**Last updated:** 2026-10-27. **Scope:** residential retail electric rates that were written for heat pumps, rates that are heat-pump-oriented but open to everyone, and older electric-heating rates that heat pump homes can take. Northeast and Mid-Atlantic only, plus pointers to pending proposals. This is our central list; add to it whenever we learn about a rate.

This file answers three questions for each rate: what is it, **which docket defined it**, and **how do we know**. The NEEP report's survey of TOU, EV, and low-income rates is cataloged in the sibling files: [`time_varying_rates.md`](time_varying_rates.md), [`ev_rates.md`](ev_rates.md), and [`low_income_and_affordability_programs.md`](low_income_and_affordability_programs.md). See [`README.md`](README.md) for the index.

## How to read this catalog

**Rate category**

- **A. Heat pump rate.** Written for heat pump customers (winter-discounted, seasonal, etc.). Eligibility is usually attestation or Mass Save enrollment, and the discount applies to the whole home's usage.
- **B. Heat-pump-oriented, open to all.** Not limited to heat pump owners, but designed with heat pumps in mind, and the utility or regulator says so. ConEd's Steady Use Rate is the main example.
- **C. Legacy electric-heating rate.** Pre-heat-pump rates for electric resistance or "electric space heating" homes. Heat pump homes can usually take them, but they were not designed for heat pumps. Many are closed to new customers.

**Two docket columns.** Utilities re-file these tariffs in every rate case, so "the docket on the current tariff sheet" is often not the docket that created the rate.

- **Defining docket:** the proceeding that created or approved the rate design. "Not found" means we have not located it, not that it does not exist.
- **Current governing docket:** the proceeding behind the tariff in force today, or where it is being changed.

**Evidence level** (how we know)

- **Primary:** we read the order, testimony, or tariff text itself. Local copies are linked where we have them.
- **Official page:** a utility or regulator web page or tariff.
- **Search summary:** a web search result summarized the source and we did not read the underlying document. Spot-check before relying on it.

## Massachusetts (MA-1, MA-2, MA-3)

All three electric utilities now have an optional seasonal heat pump rate, available to R-1 and R-2 customers who have installed a heat pump in all or part of the home. The rate lowers the winter (Nov–Apr) distribution charge, and from the 2026–27 heating season the transmission charge too. It applies to the whole home's usage, not only the heat pump. The DPU designed the rates to be revenue neutral, so no new cost-of-service study was needed. Eligibility is by customer attestation; Mass Save heat pump installs may be enrolled automatically.

| Utility       | Order that created it                          | Date         | Later dockets                                                   |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unitil        | D.P.U. 23-80 / 23-81 (rate cases), pp. 406–409 | 2024         | D.P.U. 26-72 (winter transmission charge, stamped Jul 31, 2026) |
| National Grid | D.P.U. 23-150 (rate case), pp. 509–513         | Sep 30, 2024 | D.P.U. 26-71 (winter transmission charge, stamped Jul 29, 2026) |
| Eversource    | D.P.U. 25-55                                   | Jul 29, 2025 | none yet                                                        |

- **D.P.U. 25-08** is the umbrella investigation (opened Jan 31, 2025 on a DOER petition). Its March 21, 2025 order told Eversource to file a rate like the other two; **D.P.U. 25-08-A** (Jun 1, 2026) told National Grid and Unitil to add winter transmission charges matching Eversource's.
- In D.P.U. 23-150 the DPU rejected National Grid's own proposed "heating electrification pricing option" and directed it to file a Unitil-style heat pump rate instead.
- R2HP is Eversource's low-income heat pump variant (42% discount on the total bill per Eversource's rate page).
- Unitil reported over 1,300 customers on the rate (Oct 2026).
- The DPU describes the rates as "designed to be revenue neutral" and says they "will not increase costs to other customers beyond the cost to serve those customers" (D.P.U. 25-08-A).

## Maine (ME-1, ME-2, ME-3)

The Sept 2022 orders in **MPUC 2021-00325** (a Commission-initiated investigation of T&D rate design to promote state policy, opened under the energy storage law, P.L. 2021 c. 298) approved separate stipulations for CMP and Versant.

- **CMP Seasonal Heat Pump rate (ME-1).** Pilot capped at 5,000 customers (peak ~3,134), higher fixed charge, very low winter delivery rate (Nov–Apr) and high summer rate. Started Jan 1, 2023. Enrollment closed Dec 31, 2024. Tariff sheet cancelled Feb 1, 2026. CMP asked to reinstate it in **MPUC 2026-00248**. The tariff sheet and CMP's compliance reports cite **2022-00152** (CMP's distribution rate case), which is where the pilot's extensions and evaluation are tracked.
- **CMP Electric Technology rate (ME-3).** Opt-in, open to any customer, higher fixed charge and lower per-kWh charge. Created in the same 2021-00325 order. Extended to Jan 2027; a further extension is in **MPUC 2026-00264**.
- **Versant Home Heating Eco Rate (ME-2).** Versant "adapted and expanded" an existing residential space-heating rate to cover heat pumps and heat pump water heaters, available in both the Bangor Hydro and Maine Public districts, when heat pumps/HPWHs account for at least 50% of electricity use (per Versant). In the Bangor Hydro tariff (A-20, July 2026) the delivery rate is declining-block in the heating season (Oct–Apr) and flat in the non-heating season. The pre-existing rate was used by about 13% of Versant customers (about 20,000) in 2022. We have not found the docket that created the original space-heating rate.
- Files: [`../../sources/me_hp_rates/`](../../sources/me_hp_rates/) (CMP tariffs, the reconstructed SHP schedule, CMP's Dec 2025 final report, `a-seasonal.xlsx` rate history).

## New York

### Con Edison Steady Use Rate (NY-1)

SC 1 Rate IV is an optional, demand-based residential delivery rate: a higher customer charge and no energy charge, with delivery billed on the average of the three highest off-peak and three highest peak kW readings. Supply is time-of-use. Heat pumps run steadily, so they often have a good load factor under it. Solar and net-metering customers are poor fits.

- **Created:** Case 19-E-0065, order Jan 16, 2020 ("Select Pricing Plan"). It was included in the 2019 joint proposal to address testimony that volumetric delivery rates recover fixed costs that geothermal heat pump customers do not cause. The 2026 order says it was initially made available to geothermal heat pump customers with pumps installed or qualifying through a Con Edison heat pump program. A Con Edison/NY-GEO deck says it was open to all residential heat pump customers with a cap of 5,000 non-heat-pump customers. We have not resolved this wording difference. We have not read the 2020 order itself.
- **Opened to all SC 1 customers:** Case 22-E-0064, joint proposal Feb 16, 2023, order Jul 20, 2023. The joint proposal removed the cap, required outreach, and added a one-year price guarantee for 500 ground-source and 500 air-source heat pump customers. Con Edison filed the required rate assessment on Dec 30, 2023.
- **Continued:** Case 25-E-0072, order Jan 22, 2026. Rate IV stays available to all SC 1 customers. The price guarantee now covers any heat pump customer first billed under Rate III or Rate IV during the 2026–2028 plan. Con Edison proposed raising the Rate IV customer charge from $29 to $34 per month (City of New York testimony, Jun 2025); EDF's proposed seasonal flat and seasonal TOU rates were offered as additions, not replacements.
- **Con Edison's assessment** (Case 22-E-0064 compliance, Dec 30, 2023): 80.1% of air-source and 88.9% of ground-source heat pump customers in the sample would have paid less on Rate IV than on Rate I; average savings among savers were $41/month (14%) and $103/month (18%).
- **Uptake is very low.** AGREE says fewer than 1% of households that installed heat pumps through Con Edison's Clean Heat program are on Rate IV, and attributes it to demand-rate complexity and weak outreach.
- This is the reason Rate IV belongs in the catalog even though it is "not explicitly a heat pump rate": regulators, intervenors, and the utility all describe it as the heat pump rate option in ConEd territory.

### Other NY

- **SC 1 Rate III (NY-2):** ConEd's optional seasonal TOU rate. Case 25-E-0072 commits ConEd to a study to improve its peak periods (due Nov 30, 2026) and extends the heat pump price guarantee to it. Its design traces to Case 13-E-0030 (order Feb 21, 2014); see [`time_varying_rates.md`](time_varying_rates.md).
- **PSEG Long Island Rate 580 (NY-3):** electric heating rate for homes heated entirely by electricity (including heat pumps). From October through May it gives a 40% delivery discount on use above 400 kWh/month (PSEG LI heat pump FAQ). It is a LIPA tariff, so there is no PSC rate-case docket. The DPS review of a LIPA rate request included a proposal (by LIPA's rate panel) to phase out residential electric space heating rates, and DPS testimony opposed it. We did not confirm the case number.
- Other NY utilities (Central Hudson, National Grid, NYSEG, O&R, RG&E): the NEEP survey found no heat pump or electric heating rate.
- NY HP work in this repo: see [`../../methods/ny_hp_rates_story_building_selection.md`](../../methods/ny_hp_rates_story_building_selection.md).

## New Jersey (NJ-1, NJ-2, NJ-3)

- **JCP&L RGT (NJ-1):** Residential Geothermal & Heat Pump Service. Available to residential customers whose primary heating is a qualifying geothermal system, heat pump, or room heat pump; electric resistance is excluded. Seasonal TOU: June–September on-peak/off-peak distribution charges; October–May one flat distribution rate (2025–26 tariffs). The BPU dockets on its tariff sheets (ER25010024 in 2025, ER26030090 in 2026) are annual compliance filings for transmission and other adjustments, not where RGT was created. JCP&L's Rider LRAM notice calls the class "RT/RGT".
- **PSE&G RHS (NJ-2):** seasonal electric heating rate (inclining block in summer, declining block in winter); closed to new customers. The 2023 combined rate case (ER23120924) lists it as "(Closed)".
- **RECO SC 3 (NJ-3):** seasonal TOU rate for electric water and space heating (peak hours 10 a.m.–10 p.m. weekdays per our search summary). Latest base rate case ER25060374 (order Jun 2026).
- ACE and the NJ summer inclining-block rates are not electric heating rates.

## Pennsylvania (PA-1, PA-2, PA-3)

- **Duquesne RA and RH (PA-1, PA-2):** Rate RH is the residential heating rate; Rate RA is the add-on heat pump rate (heat pump as a supplement to another heating system), closed to new customers. The Dec 2024 settlement in R-2024-3046523 raised both by 11.7% on a distribution basis.
- **PECO RH (PA-3):** residential heating rate for homes heated by electric resistance heaters or heat pumps, with seasonal distribution charges (winter lower than summer). The R-2024-3046931 settlement raised RH distribution revenue by $53.5M (30.1%) and set the fixed distribution charge at $11.25 effective Jan 1, 2025.
- Both utilities' rate origins are decades old; we have not located the original dockets.

## Maryland (MD-1)

- **BGE Schedule RH** (electric heat, non-TOU; tariff codes 42, 43) and **Schedule RL / RLH** (optional TOU; the RLH variant, code 45, is for electric heat). RL dates to 1984 and is available to single-family homes with electric central heating or air conditioning. BGE's current tariffs come out of Case 9692, its multi-year plan (Order 90948, Dec 14, 2023). Detail: [`bge_tou_rates.md`](../bge_tou_rates.md).
- Schedule RD (BGE whole-home TOU) and the DRIVE Act TOU tariffs (Case 9761, Order 91917) are TOU rates that heat pump homes can use but are not electric heating rates: see [`schedule_rd_tou_methodology.md`](../schedule_rd_tou_methodology.md), [`md_drive_act_tou.md`](../md_drive_act_tou.md), and [`../../methods/md_hp_rates_rd_rate_analysis.md`](../../methods/md_hp_rates_rd_rate_analysis.md).

## Connecticut (CT-1, CT-2)

- **Rate 5 (CT-1):** Eversource's current electric heating delivery rate (the EnergizeCT rate board labels Rate 1 as non-electric space heating and Rate 5 as electric space heating).
- **Proposed Rate 6 (CT-2):** in the company's July 14, 2026 rate review (PURA 26-05-10), Eversource proposes an optional residential space-heating rate that discounts incremental use by customers who adopt air-source heat pumps. Proposed eligibility is a residential customer that currently heats with a delivered fuel (natural gas conversions deliberately excluded) or a new home built after Jul 1, 2027; proposed effective date Jul 1, 2027; about 4.7 cents/kWh below Rate 1 for heating-season use above 700 kWh. Geothermal customers are excluded. The exhibits and CLF discovery responses are in [`../../sources/ct_hp_rates/`](../../sources/ct_hp_rates/).

## New Hampshire (NH-1)

Eversource NH has no heat pump rate. The only entry is the legacy Rate G space-heating provision (separately metered space heating, heat pumps and associated equipment included), available only at locations that received service under the old Transitional Space Heating Service rate (TSH) before customer choice. The 2022 tariff cites NHPUC DE 19-057 (Order 26,568), Eversource NH's 2019 distribution rate case. Eversource's heat pump rates R1HP/R2HP are Massachusetts rates, not New Hampshire rates (a source of confusion when searching). Liberty has no electric heating rate; Unitil has none.

## Rhode Island (RI-1)

Rhode Island has no heat pump rate. CLF has asked the PUC to adopt a seasonal heat pump rate in RIE's 2025 rate case (**Docket 25-45-GE**), and RIE opposes it. Our supporting files: [`../../sources/ri_hp_rates/`](../../sources/ri_hp_rates/) and [`../../methods/ri_hp_rates_rie_objections_and_responses.md`](../../methods/ri_hp_rates_rie_objections_and_responses.md).
