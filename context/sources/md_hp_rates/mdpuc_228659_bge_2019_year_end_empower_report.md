## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Program Participation and Impact Highlights

#### Program Impacts

2019 has been an extremely productive year for BGE’s EmPOWER Maryland (“EmPOWER”)
program. The energy efficiency program portfolio achieved approximately 125% of its energy
savings targets at 96% of the program budget (excluding Smart Energy Rewards and Peak Rewards
expenditures). The entire portfolio achieved over 841,000 MWh annualized electric energy
savings. Natural gas savings were over 5.4 million therms. Avoided generation from these savings
over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed during 2019 is over
6.2 million MWh. This represents a reduction of over 4.4 million metric tons of carbon dioxide gas
emissions according to the EPA Greenhouse Gas Equivalents Calculator, over 2.4 million tons of
coal not burned or over 935,000 vehicles not driven for a year. In addition to the energy savings
realized and associated environmental benefits customers generated by choosing more efficient
alternatives in the way they use electricity and natural gas, BGE customers received incentives and
bill credits of approximately $107 million. Estimated savings on customer bills over the life of
measures installed during 2019 are $666 million. Total economic benefits returned to BGE
customers is summarized below:

#### Program Highlights

During 2019, a few program accomplishments are worth noting. The programs mentioned below
will be discussed in greater detail in the “Program Specifics” section of this report.
The residential portfolio of energy efficiency programs had the following stand-out
programs for 2019.

- **Lighting:** The lighting program continued its strong performance in 2019, exceeding its
  forecast for the year. A few of the reasons for this success include markdowns at major retailers,
  increased demand for light-emitting diodes (“LEDs”), and special promotional pricing.
- **Quick Home Energy Check-Up (“QHEC”):** The QHEC Program far exceeded its forecast
  for 2019. A new marketing campaign and media exposure helped to bring about this success.

[PDF p. 6 | printed p. 5]

Smart Energy Manager Program (“SEM”): The SEM Program posted strong numbers in
2019 to beat its target savings. Targeted marketing modules for Home Energy Reports
(“HERs”) helped boost savings.

- **Appliance Rebates:** The Appliance Rebate Program exceeded its 2019 target. 2019 was the
  first year of participation in the ENERGY STAR Retail Products Program (“ESRPP”) for this
  program. Appliance measures (clothes washers, clothes dryers and refrigerators) were incented
  through ESRPP effective April 1, 2019.
  The commercial and industrial portfolio of energy efficiency programs had the
  following “above target” programs for 2019.
- **Prescriptive:** The Prescriptive Program had a successful 2019, exceeding its targets. The
  program’s success is attributed to a concentrated effort to promote lighting fixtures and controls
  replacements.
- **Instant Discount (Midstream) Program:** The C&I Instant Discount (Midstream) Program’s
  2019 success in exceeding its targets can be attributed to such actions as incorporating tubular
  LEDs (“TLEDs”) into the program, selective incentive levels, and increased program awareness
  through marketing efforts.
- **Small Business Program:** The Small Business Program exceeded its targets for 2019. This
  improved performance was a result of adding an additional contractor and smart thermostats to
  the program, and increased contractor training and customer awareness.
- **Retrocommissioning:** The Retrocommissioning Program had exceptional results for 2019.
  The use of building analytics reports, increased incentive caps, and improved customer
  awareness, all contributed to this successful program.

#### #### Limited Income Participation

All BGE EmPOWER programs are available for participation by all customers regardless of
income. BGE can only identify those limited income customers having self-identified themselves
as qualifying for the Maryland Electric Universal Service Program (“EUSP”). Self-identified
customers participating in various BGE energy efficiency programs received incentives which
amounted to approximately $800,000 during 2019. The Residential Lighting Program provided
approximately 500,000 energy efficient LED bulbs to local food banks for distribution to their
clients as part of BGE’s outreach to limited income residents. Additionally, within BGE’s SEM
Program, BGE marketed the QHEC Program to limited income customers while also addressing
energy savings myths regarding thermostat use that was driven by customer feedback. A module
was developed to address both use cases and a segmentation strategy was used to target both
customer groups. Furthermore, BGE continues to support the efforts of the Maryland Fuel Fund to
assist limited income customers by including a holiday message in the December email Home
Energy Report promoting the program for the holiday season.
As directed per PSC Order No. 89189 on July 11, 2019, collaboration and cross-marketing efforts
have been made between the utilities and the Maryland Department of Housing and Community
Development (“DHCD”). Cross-marketing efforts promoting DHCD to limited income customers
include language on BGEsmartEnergy.com and the Lighting Food Bank Flyer created for the
Maryland Food Bank (“Food Bank”). BGE also included language promoting DHCD on the Smart
Energy Savers Program (“SESP”) overview brochure and QHEC brochure. BGE has also begun
promoting energy efficiency to limited income customers through the utility’s social media
channels. These posts direct customers to learn more ways to save, which include links to DHCD.
As directed per PSC Order 89404 on December 20, 2019, BGE will take the following steps to
further the goal of increased participation in the limited income programs.
BGE continues to enhance the customer experience and participation for limited income customers.
While these customers must self-identify, BGE is aware that this population is much larger than
those who have done so. On the customer experience side, BGE has looked at how the needs of
these customers can be better served. On the participation side, BGE continues to make sure its
programs are marketed and available to all customers.
BGE continues to look for ways to not only promote its programs but also the programs that DHCD
offers. BGE has been actively involved in the Limited Income Working Group. BGE continues to
look for ways to cross-promote BGE and DHCD EmPOWER programs. BGE is looking to move

beyond distributing DHCD marketing materials and will be adding information about DHCD
programs in its EmPOWER offerings booklet later this year.
BGE’s lighting program has committed to distributing 500,000 light bulbs a year to the Food Bank
over the 2018-2020 cycle. The lighting program has looked to increase its commitment to reaching
limited income customers through new avenues. BGE has engaged local non-profits which work
with limited income customers, making light bulbs available to them. BGE continues to expand
sales locations for lighting, adding smaller local stores in communities with higher numbers of
limited income customers. In addition, BGE has expanded efforts to engage with limited income

The Quick Home Energy Checkup (QHEC) Program continues its outreach efforts to reach all

QHEC and DHCD’s programs.
The Home Energy Reports (“HERs”) that go to customers include messages on ways customers
can save on their utility bills. The BGE Behavioral team continues to work on messaging that
targets limited income customers. The messaging includes savings tips for these customers which
are at little to no cost to the customer.
The recently approved Bring Your Own Device Program will offer thermostat installation, at no
additional charge, to limited income customers, provided the qualifying thermostat is new in its
original packaging. Customers participating in this program will not only see the savings of a smart
thermostat they purchased, but also up to $100 in first year rewards from participating in the demand
response program. BGE has engaged directly with DHCD on this program and discussed how the

#### Residential Energy Efficiency Portfolio

The residential energy efficiency portfolio had a strong 2019. Across all measures, the residential
portfolio gross wholesale MWh savings exceeded forecast, delivering over 400,000 MWh or 134%
of the goal, while only spending 81% of the incentive budget. The Lighting, Appliance Rebate,
Appliance Recycling, QHEC and New Construction programs all exceeded the 2019 targets. The
Home Performance with Energy Star (“HPwES”) Program was on target. The HVAC and Smart
Thermostat program fell below target.
**Key successes in 2019 included:**

- BGE’s Residential Appliance Rebate Program achieved 311% of the 2019 MWh savings
  goal. As of April 1, 2019, appliance measures (washers, dryers and refrigerators) were
  incented through ESRPP. The program achieved 262% of the total 2019 measures goal.
- 500,000 LED light bulbs were distributed for low income customers to local food pantries
  through the Food Bank.
- The Appliance Recycling Program exceeded the filed 2019 MWh savings goal at 118% and
  participant goal at 111%.
  **Challenges in 2019 included:**
- Below target results for the HVAC program, but full transition of the program to midstream should
  lead to stronger future performance of the program.
- Smart thermostat sales and savings were lower than expected. A lower than expected finish
  in 2019 combined with an earlier change in the evaluation reference led to low program
  performance.
  BGE continues to work with its implementors to address these challenges and enhance its programs
  across the board. Overall, the residential energy efficiency portfolio is on track to meet its goals
  for the overall 2018-2020 program cycle.

#### Commercial and Industrial Portfolio

BGE’s C&I energy efficiency portfolio exceeded its energy savings target for 2019. The program
achieved 130% of its annual energy savings target during 2019. BGE saw strong performance in
its Prescriptive, Building Tune-Up, Small Business and Instant Discount (Midstream) programs,

#### Home Performance with ENERGY STAR®

Home Performance with Energy Star is a national program overseen by the Department of Energy
(“DOE”) and guided by the standards of the BPI. The program’s goal is to improve residential
comfort and energy efficiency by addressing the house as a system, while ensuring the health and
safety of its occupants. Participating home performance contractors conduct comprehensive audits
and make customized recommendations designed to improve the comfort and efficiency of the BGE
customer’s home. These recommendations are provided in an audit report that summarizes and
prioritizes the opportunities for improvement. The customer decides which measures they want
installed and contracts with the participating contractor to complete the job. The final project
inspection and diagnostic testing ensure the contracted work was completed properly. The program
provides a subsidy for the cost of the home energy audit (customer pays $100 for an audit valued
at $400), direct installation of energy efficient measures and a rebate up to $7,500 for qualifying
energy efficiency improvements.

[PDF p. 20 | printed p. 19]

**On Target:** In 2019, the BGE HPwES Program had a strong year, and achieved 3,558 MWh or
90% of the annual MWh gross wholesale electric energy savings forecast. This is a 17%
improvement in MWh energy savings compared to the 2018 program year. More customers and
participating contractors are recognizing the importance of the HPwES Program as a
comprehensive energy efficiency program as they continue to bundle HVAC system upgrades in
their whole-house energy makeovers.
When comparing 2019 to 2018, program results show:

- In 2019, 19% of projects were reported as including at least one rebate-eligible HVAC
  measure. This amounts to 193 projects and over 660 MWh reported in 2019, which both
  categories realized a 200% increase from 2018 statistics.
- The average HPwES project annualized kWh savings increased by 25% (up from 2,209 to
  2,762) in 2019. The electric savings increase is attributable to an increased share of jobs
  completed in electric homes with HVAC system upgrades in the BGE service territory.
- The program was successful in integrating heat pump water heaters into the list of eligible
  measures within the PBI structure in the second half of 2019. By the end of the year, 11
  projects included this upgrade.
- A 7% improvement in the savings-to-incentive ratio; hence, more savings per incentive
  dollar.
- Maintenance of a 36% audit-to-job conversion rate.
  Currently, the BGE HPwES Program has 44 participating HPwES contractors.
  The BGE Energy Coach service is improving the home performance customer experience by
  providing guidance and technical knowledge to help customers make more informed decisions
  throughout the home performance journey from scheduling an audit to moving forward and

## APPENDIX G: NOTES AND ASSUMPTIONS

All calculations are consistent with BGE’s 2018 – 2020 Maryland Public Service Commission approved
EmPOWER filing and the most current Mid-Atlantic Technical Resource Manual (“TRM”), as appropriate.

### Notes

**Smart Thermostats**
In the preparation of the EmPOWER filing, an estimate was used to calculate the Annualized
Energy Savings realized from the installation of smart thermostats. This estimate was based on the
best information at the time. The deemed savings prescribed in the Mid-Atlantic TRM are lower
than the values used for the forecast and have resulted in a smaller saving per device and thus lower
savings than the as-filed forecast.
**PeakRewards Energy Savings (MWh)**
The as filed Annualized Energy Savings for PeakRewards was made up of two components:

- Annualized Energy Savings (MWh) from installed smart thermostats. These energy savings
  are not associated with PeakRewards events; and,
- Energy savings coincident with the hours during PeakRewards calls.
  Prior to 2018, Commission Staff required a mini-table, the DRDP Call Log, which included energy
  savings associated with the PeakRewards events. The current cycle DRDP Call Log eliminated
  MWh savings but instead includes MW reductions.
  Accordingly, to be consistent with the requirements of the DRDP Call Log, BGE is only reporting
  Annualized Energy Savings from smart thermostats.
  **Smart Energy Rewards Energy Savings (MWh)**
  The as-filed energy savings for SER included energy savings associated with Energy Savings Days.
  To be consistent with the reporting for PeakRewards energy savings, as discussed above, BGE did
  not report energy savings coincident with event days.
