## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Program Participation and Impact Highlights

#### Program Impacts

BGE's EmPOWER Maryland ("EmPOWER") portfolio of programs continued its success into 2022, while continuing to deal with COVID-19 supply issues. Overall, in 2022, BGE's EmPOWER program portfolio has exceeded its 2% goal legislatively established for the EmPOWER programs for 2022. In 2022, the EmPOWER program portfolio achieved 127% of the 2% annual MWh savings goal using only 78% of the overall 2022 program budget. With regard to the MWh savings targets established in the Company's 2021-2023 EmPOWER cycle filing, the 2022 portfolio achieved 109% of the 2022 filed target. This places the portfolio in the "Exceeded Target" category for 2022. The entire portfolio achieved nearly 812,000 MWh in annualized electric energy savings. Natural gas savings in 2022 were over 2.4 million therms. In addition to the energy savings realized and associated environmental benefits customers generated by choosing more efficient alternatives, BGE customers received incentives and bill credits of approximately $92 million. Estimated savings on customer bills over the life of measures installed in 2022 are $364 million. Total economic benefits returned to BGE customers are summarized below.

Avoided generation from the nearly 812,000 MWh 2022 portfolio savings over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed in 2022 is nearly 4 million MWh.

#### Program Highlights

There are a few programs that achieved 148% or more of their filed 2022 MWh annual goals that are worth noting. These programs have all exceeded their 2022 targets. The programs mentioned below will be discussed in greater detail in the "Program Specifics" section of this report.

The following energy efficiency programs in the residential portfolio posted strong results:

**Residential Lighting:** The Residential Lighting Program achieved 148% of its 2022 MWh savings goal. Production was ramped up in 2022 in anticipation of the EISA policy enforcement which will take effect in 2023.

[PDF p. 6 | printed p. 2]

**Home Performance with ENERGY STAR:** Carrying forward the success of 2021, this program continues to promote deep energy retrofits, high efficiency HVAC systems and heat pump water heater upgrades. As a result, for 2022, this program reached 148% of its 2022 MWh savings goal.

**Appliance Rebate:** The Appliance Rebate Program, for 2022, achieved 156% of its 2022 MWh savings goal. This program has also exceeded its 3 year cycle MWh savings goal. Sales through the midstream RPP channel, and dehumidifier and air purifier markdowns offered through select retailers, helped this program succeed.

The following commercial energy efficiency programs were also successful:

**Custom:** The Custom Program was tremendously successful in 2022 as it achieved 258% of its 2022 MWh savings goal. A strong pipeline of open projects, streamlined processes, and simpler incentive calculations helped this program focus its efforts on projects that would yield substantial MWh savings. This program has already exceeded its three year cycle plan MWh savings target.

**Small Business:** The Small Business Program enjoyed great success in 2022, achieving 183% of its 2022 MWh savings goal, and 119% of its 3 year cycle MWh savings goal. The continued use of the "closed network" model of six dedicated direct service providers continues to bolster MWh savings.

**Retrocommissioning:** In 2022, the Building Tune-up (Retrocommissioning) Program has achieved 149% of its 2022 MWh savings goal. The Building Tune-up Program's lasting success is due primarily to its continued focus on both large and small building requirements.

BGE's Smart Thermostat Rebate Program offers a rebate of up to $100 to residential customers who purchase an ENERGY STAR certified smart thermostat through one of several market avenues. Customers can obtain a post-purchase rebate after purchasing an eligible thermostat through any retailer. Customers can choose whether to receive their post-purchase rebate via a gift card or personal check. Customers also have the opportunity to obtain an instant rebate on their purchase through BGE's Online Marketplace or via an instant coupon at select retailers. In December 2022, BGE also launched a direct-to-consumer limited-time offer to targeted customers through a simple-to-use online platform.

BGE offers additional residential smart thermostat rebates through the HVAC midstream, New Homes, Quick Home Energy Check-up ("QHEC"), and Home Performance with ENERGY STAR ("HPwES") programs. Residential customers are eligible for up to three smart thermostat rebates (per program cycle) across all residential programs. BGE claims additional energy savings through cloud optimization services offered through eligible devices claimed through the programs mentioned above.

BGE's Small Business Energy Solutions ("SBES") Program offers smart thermostats to eligible commercial customers. Smart thermostat capabilities make the measure ideal for small businesses because there is an opportunity to significantly reduce load when the building is unoccupied.

#### Residential Energy Efficiency Portfolio

The Residential Energy Efficiency Portfolio had a strong 2022 and continues to prepare for the phase-out of lighting programs in 2023. The residential programs achieved over 218,000 MWh of savings for the year, which is 126% of the annualized energy savings goal, while using 85% of the planned budget (excluding the Smart Energy Manager-Residential).

- The Lighting, Appliance Rebate, Quick Home Energy Checkup, Home Performance with ENERGY STAR ("HPwES"), and Smart Thermostat Programs exceeded their savings goals for 2022.
- The Appliance Recycling, HVAC, and New Construction faced challenges for the year. BGE continues to work with its program implementor to address these challenges.
- BGE continues to work with its implementor and program evaluators to make necessary changes to address the impending EISA changes for our lighting program.

The Residential Energy Efficiency Portfolio is on track to meet its 2021-2023 cycle goals and continue delivering customer savings.

#### Commercial and Industrial Portfolio

BGE's C&I energy efficiency portfolio was able to achieve over 186,000 MWh in energy savings during 2022, representing 113% of its 2022 annual energy savings target (exclusive of the Business Energy Manager Program). In 2022, successful performance in its Small Business, Building Tune-Up, and Instant Discount (Midstream) programs resulted in the C&I portfolio exceeding its 2022 targets. The Custom Program has also contributed with its stellar performance in 2022. Significant reasons for the C&I portfolio's achievement are the concentrated effort to create a strong pipeline of active projects, focused marketing efforts, and strategic partnerships with contractors and lighting distributors. BGE remains steadfast in its customer education and strategic marketing approach. BGE's solid performance in 2021 and 2022 and its continued strong pipeline for 2023 will help enable BGE to stay on track to meet its three-year full cycle target for the C&I portfolio.

#### Other Energy Efficiency Programs

The Conservation Voltage Reduction ("CVR") Program's energy savings achieved 101% of its 2022 energy savings forecast. During 2022, CVR was deployed on thirteen additional circuits, bringing the total to 871 circuits on which CVR is deployed.

##### BGE Smart Shade Pilot

BGE worked to conclude the analysis on its Automated Window Shade System ("AWSS") Pilot ("BGE Smart Shade Pilot") in the second half of 2022. This Pilot focused on potential energy savings during the winter and summer seasons captured because of automated window shading systems. Solar heat gain from unshaded western and southern facing windows can be substantial during the summer and beneficial in the winter months. Having automated shades that open and close per a set schedule can potentially reduce energy usage, save customers money on their BGE bills, reduce HVAC maintenance costs, optimize climate consistency, improve comfort, and extend HVAC equipment life.

Following a continuous twelve-month observation period of AWSS use within the homes of selected participants, weather normalized billing analysis was conducted to determine potential savings opportunities with these products. Analysis results across the fifteen participants indicated savings for electricity and natural gas, 13.7% and 2.4% savings respectively. However, due to the small participant pool and comparison groups, results should be interpreted with caution. While the results from the AWSS Pilot appeared to indicate savings for these systems within participants homes, BGE will continue to look for additional industry data and analytics to help future discussions and decisions on whether AWSS products should be considered for inclusion within the EmPOWER MD suite of programs.

In addition to the analysis, a final participant survey was conducted to better understand the customers' experience bringing the total number of customer surveys to a count of four throughout the Pilot's duration. Responses from these surveys were used to help better understand the customer interaction and potential affects in-home use had on potential energy savings.

##### Home Performance with ENERGY STAR®

Home Performance with ENERGY STAR ("HPwES") is a national program overseen by the Department of Energy and guided by the standards of the BPI. The program's goal is to enhance residential comfort and energy efficiency by addressing the house as a system, while improving the health and safety of its occupants. Participating home performance contractors conduct comprehensive audits and make customized recommendations designed to improve the comfort and efficiency of the BGE customer's home. These recommendations are provided in an audit report that summarizes and prioritizes the opportunities for improvement. The customer decides which measures they want installed and contracts with the participating contractor to complete the job. The final project inspection and diagnostic testing ensure the contracted work was completed properly. The program provides a subsidy for the cost of the home energy audit (customer pays $100 for an audit valued at $400), direct installation of energy efficient measures, and a rebate up to $7,500 for qualifying energy efficiency improvements.

**Exceeded Target:** The HPwES Program had an exceptionally strong year completing 3,998 audits and 1,584 jobs resulting in 4,610 MWh or 148% of its annual MWh gross wholesale electric energy savings target. By promoting deep energy retrofits, customers have taken advantage of the program rebate structure by including high efficiency HVAC systems and heat pump water heater upgrades in their jobs. In 2022, customers have bundled 329 equipment measures with insulation improvements that include 191 high efficiency heat pumps and central air conditioners as well as 131 heat pump water heaters.

### Background

BGE has partnered with industry-leading custom window treatment manufacturers: Hunter Douglas and Rollease Acmeda to offer customers up to 10 automated window shades for west- and south-facing windows at no additional cost as part of BGE Smart Shade Pilot Program. The pilot intends to reduce energy loss through the windows, lower heating and cooling bills and improve home comfort.

[PDF p. 66 | printed p. 2]

In total, 15 houses[^s1] benefited from the pilot and installed smart shades. Most homes installed 10 smart shades in areas like family/living rooms, kitchen and dining area and bedrooms.

### Data

BGE provided list of participants (and pool of non-participants) in the pilot along with flag identifier if they are also participants in Peak Rewards or Connected Rewards demand response programs along with demand response dates for the two programs. BGE also provided electric, and gas smart meter data associated with the two groups for a period sufficient for the purposes of this impact evaluation: at least one year of data pre- and post-installation, consecutive data-point, less than 10% missing data points, and no missing zip codes. No significant outliers were found in the AMI data. Duplicate service points (SP_ID) were found between the treatment and comparison group files and have been excluded from the comparison group. Also, service points flagged as moved out of their premises were excluded from the comparison group as new tenant behavior might differ from the original occupant.

### Comparison Group Matching

For pilot evaluation, it is important that estimates of program impacts are unbiased and internally valid. The use of randomized controlled trials (RCT) is recommended as it is a robust, statistically unbiased method for energy savings estimation. Because of the size of this pilot, an RCT comparison group was not created before the start of the pilot, a quasi-experimental approach is followed to create a comparison group from about 70 premises enrolled in the smart home program but not part of the smart shade pilot

This method is less robust and possibly biased as compared to RCT. We matched the comparison group from the pool of non-participants based on one-year-long pre-installation period total and hourly consumption. Using the hybrid (total and hourly) ensures the selection doesn't give false precision in the pre-installation equivalency check. While there're other methods of quasi-experimental design that use home characteristics, propensity score, zip codes, and other similar metrics, collection and binning of such data is infeasible with the small number of premises participating in this pilot and will create biased subsets.

We selected the electric comparison group to minimize the weighted average of the mean root of the squared difference between treatment and selected premises both annual and hourly. Figure 1 shows how close the comparison group to the treatment group. It is possible to get the distribution of annual consumption that is closer to the treatment group but will result in different consumption patterns which are key in evaluating smart shades' impact during the afternoons of summer days.

[^s1]: Pilot has 15 houses with 16 different customers; one customer moved out in the middle of the pilot.

### Methodology

Because of the limited number of homes that installed smart shades, we'll follow a two-stage approach to evaluate savings as outlined in NREL's Uniform Methods Projects Chapter 8: Whole Building Retrofit with Consumption Data Analysis Evaluation Protocol[^s2]. The approach allows us first to evaluate savings for each house individually, then use difference-in-differences method along with the comparison group to assess the average impact per premise. The method is similar to FFM except that it breaks analysis into two steps that allow for versatility in the model selection that makes use of the AMI data vs the fixed change point model used in FFM.

[^s2]: [NREL Uniform Methods Project Chapter 8](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy17osti/68564.pdf) (printed as the bare URL https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy17osti/68564.pdf)

[PDF p. 68 | printed p. 4]

For each premise in the analysis, whether in the treatment or comparison group, we followed the three steps below.

In step1, we fitted a premise-specific degree-day regression model separately for the pre-installation period (and post-periods for normalized energy savings). The model we chose for normalized meter energy consumption (NMEC) complies with CalTRACK Methods[^s3]. Each premise has its own model that allows change points to be determined for each premise individually. This model is suitable for smart shades as it might alter the point at which the home starts cooling or heating. In step2, we calculate the metered savings (MS) and weather-normalized savings (WNS). In step3, we used mean difference of differences regression to estimate the total impact. The model takes the form of

$$Savings_i = \gamma \, treat_i + \beta$$

where,

- $Savings_i$ = metered or weather-normalized savings for customer $i$
- $treat$ = 0/1 dummy variable, equal to 1 if premise $i$ is a participant, 0 if customer $i$ is in the comparison group
- $\beta$ = mean trend in the comparison group unrelated to the pilot
- $\gamma$ = mean of weather normalized savings

## APPENDIX C – PILOT PROGRAMS AND PROGRAM INVESTIGATION, DESIGN AND DEVELOPMENT

### Current Pilot Programs

#### Quick Home Energy Check-Up New Measures Pilot

The Quick Home Energy Check-Up ("QHEC") Program will serve as a platform to offer new measures, including a smart dryer control and/or home energy monitor, to select eligible customers receiving a QHEC. The pilot will test the feasibility of these products as future direct install measures for the QHEC Program.

#### BGE Smart Shade Pilot

The pilot employs an automated shading system that opens and closes per a set schedule. This system can potentially reduce energy usage, save customers money on their BGE bills, reduce HVAC maintenance costs, optimize climate consistency, improve comfort, and extend HVAC equipment life.

#### Midstream Appliance Recycling Pilot

BGE's Midstream Appliance Recycling Pilot Program focuses on intervening at the midstream level to prevent old, inefficient refrigerators from being resold in the secondhand appliance market.

The goals of the pilot are to:

- Claim energy savings by collecting units from secondhand appliance retailers
- Prevent old and inefficient units from being plugged back into the BGE grid
- Reduce greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions by following the U.S. EPA RAD-compliant recycling process
- Create a secondhand appliance market with more efficient choices for income eligible BGE customers
- Lower program marketing costs
- Decrease program transportation costs due to bulk pick-up

#### CEA Lending Pilot (Residential Financing Pilot)

In 2022, EmPOWER Maryland Utilities worked with the Maryland Clean Energy Center ("MCEC") and Montgomery County Green Bank ("MCGB") to launch the Commission approved Clean Energy Advantage Residential Lending Pilot Program ("CEA Pilot Program"), an off-bill financing program. The program is intended to provide an attractive financing option to customers, thereby increasing participation in the Utilities' Residential HPwES and HVAC programs.

BGE continued running the award-winning Fairy Tale campaign, highlighting key program messages that resonate with customers and capture their attention. ## APPENDIX E – GHG STUDY

The Maryland Utilities, as agreed to in the Future Programming Work Group, are currently funding the Applied Energy Group, Inc. ("AEG") to conduct the Maryland 2022 GHG Abatement Study. The study will produce a database of detailed GHG abatement estimates by resource, technology, utility service territory, and market achievable potential scenarios. The result will establish a suitable representation of potential GHG abatement goals for EmPOWER Maryland utilities.

The effort involves a Market and Baseline Study. This energy efficiency and building electrification potential analysis will consider technical, economic and achievable potential. Technical Potential is a theoretical construct that estimates the savings that would be attained if all customers adopted the most efficient option available in the marketplace, regardless of cost. Economic Potential represents the adoption of all cost-effective measures by all customers. Achievable Potential provides an estimate of the amount of potential that could be cost-effectively acquired under a specified set of conditions: overall budgets; level of rebates relative to incremental costs; and expectations about customer participation. The study will also estimate the potential for active and passive demand response. As part of the project scope, the AEG team will provide for proactive stakeholder engagement to gain both buy-in and a diverse set of viewpoints representing the various concerns in developing GHG abatement goals.

The overall Commission budgeted amount for the GHG Abatement Study is $1,479,313, of which BGE's budgeted portion is $624,270. For 2022, BGE has spent $500,738 for this study. In the future, these costs will not be included in the Cost Program Detail worksheets (Appendix B); however, the Commission has allowed the utilities to recover these costs through the EmPOWER MD surcharge.
