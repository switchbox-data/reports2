# ComEd Rate BEST + DTOU (ICC Docket 24-0378): source corpus and index

This directory is the ComEd-specific source corpus for **ICC Docket 24-0378** (ComEd's residential time-of-use offerings): **Rate BEST** (bundled supply TOU) and **DTOU** (delivery TOU, the time-differentiated Distribution Facilities Charge, DFC), compared against the **flat default** (Rate BES supply plus the flat DFC). It holds the tariffs, information sheets, ComEd testimony and exhibits, the illustrative workpapers that set the prices, and the published price sheets, each as a markdown extract with exact numbers. The original PDFs are not stored in the repo; `MANIFEST.csv` records each one's filename, page count, SHA256 and download URL so it can be re-fetched and verified.

It is built to answer four questions:

1. **What** did ComEd propose? See [Quick answers](#quick-answers) and [Rate structure](#rate-structure-in-one-paragraph).
2. **Why** did it propose it that way? See [Goals and non-goals](#goals-and-non-goals-as-stated-by-comed).
3. **How** are the rates derived? See [Methodology chain](#methodology-chain-how-a-rate-best-price-is-built).
4. Does the design likely **overcollect from EPON customers**? See [EPON overcollection hypotheses](#epon-overcollection-hypotheses) and the [EPON excerpt index](#epon-excerpt-index).

The policy background (what the ICC approved and why) is in the parent directory (`../icc_24-0378_order_on_rehearing.md`, `../icc_24-0378_final_order_part_*.md`) and in [`context/domain/il_tou_rates.md`](../../../../domain/il_tou_rates.md). Nonresidential Rate BEST (available January 2028) is covered by the parent file `../icc_erm_26-176_comed_rate_best_nonresidential_compliance_tariff.md`; it is not re-extracted here.

## What "EPON customers" means in this corpus

**EPON customers** is the project's working label for **electrification-heavy residential customers**: homes with heat pumps and/or electric vehicles whose hourly load shape differs from the **aggregate illustrative profile** that ComEd uses in Rate BEST Formula 1 and Formula 2 to set every period price. Typical features: winter-heavy load (heat pumps), large Morning and Evening loads, EV charging, and limited ability to move load out of the 1–7 p.m. Mid-Day Peak window. The acronym's expansion was not specified in the request, so none is asserted here. Nothing in ComEd's filings uses the term; every EPON statement below is an analytical inference and is labelled as one.

## Quick answers

**What did ComEd propose?** Two paired offerings. **DTOU** replaces the flat DFC with four period DFCs by delivery class (single/multi-family, with/without electric space heat); it started in January 2026 billing. **Rate BEST** replaces the flat supply price (PEC + PSC + PEA) with four period supply prices (BESTEC), a flat kWh Transmission and Miscellaneous Component (TMC, Info Sheet 72), and a monthly TPEA true-up. Rate BEST **requires** DTOU (mandatory bundling, Sheet 486 and Ex. 6.0); DTOU alone is allowed. Periods are the same for both: Morning 6 a.m.–1 p.m., Mid-Day Peak 1–7 p.m., Evening 7–9 p.m., Overnight 9 p.m.–6 a.m., every day. Summer is June–September for supply prices; delivery prices have no season.

**Why?** ComEd says it voluntarily proposed Rate BEST because the Commission invited a strawman supply TOU and because bundling supply and delivery TOU with identical periods gives a simple, customer-centric price signal ([goals table](#goals-and-non-goals-as-stated-by-comed)). The strawman recovered capacity on each customer's kW capacity obligation; ComEd then adopted a **Compromise** with CUB-EDF and ELPC-VS-UCS that moved **100% of capacity cost into Mid-Day kWh** and made the TMC a flat kWh charge (Ex. 19.0). The Commission approved this on July 17, 2025.

**How are rates derived?** From a **historical hourly PJM real-time residual LMP** for the ComEd Zone, weighted by the **"participants' aggregate illustrative profile"** (a 1,858-account Rate RTOUPP pilot load, calendar 2023, 18,265,086 kWh), grossed up for losses and uncollectibles, with PJM capacity cost added to every Mid-Day hour. See the [methodology chain](#methodology-chain-how-a-rate-best-price-is-built).

**Does it likely overcollect from EPON customers?** Not demonstrated in the record, but several design features create the conditions for it, and ComEd's own Ex. 17.0 documents the mechanism for the closely related case of low-capacity-obligation customers. The answer depends on the benchmark: against the **flat default bill**, a typical single-family shape still comes out well ahead (worked check below); against **cost causation** (the five summer weekday PJM peaks that set capacity obligations), the flat capacity adder over all Mid-Day kWh allocates more cost to winter Mid-Day load than the cost-causing events justify. See the [hypotheses](#epon-overcollection-hypotheses).

## Rate structure in one paragraph

Four periods, every day, Central Prevailing Time: **Morning** 6 a.m.–1 p.m., **Mid-Day Peak** 1–7 p.m., **Evening** 7–9 p.m., **Overnight** 9 p.m.–6 a.m. A **flat default** customer pays one Rate BES supply price (PEC + PSC + monthly PEA) and one flat DFC for their delivery class. A **DTOU** customer keeps the flat supply but pays four DFCs by period. A **Rate BEST** customer pays four supply prices (BESTEC) **plus** a flat PJM/Misc line (Info Sheet 72) **plus** the monthly TPEA, **and** the four DTOU DFCs. Supply prices differ by season (summer = June–September billing months); delivery prices have no season. Delivery has four residential classes (single/multi-family, with/without electric space heat).

## Methodology chain: how a Rate BEST price is built

This is the chain as documented; each step cites where to verify it.

1. **Fix the four periods and two seasons** (Sheet 483–484): eight cells, from Morning to Overnight, summer (June–September) and nonsummer (October–May).
2. **Take hourly PJM real-time residual LMP** for the ComEd Zone ($HLMP$, $/MWh) for a historical 12-month window (Sheet 489). The strawman cites Mar 2024–Feb 2025 prices (Ex. 17.0 fn. 5); the Compromise workpaper (Ex. 19.02) pairs CY2023 prices with CY2023 load (see the first rows of `workbooks/..._p120_first_102_hours_with_pjm_prices.csv`).
3. **Weight by the "participants' aggregate illustrative profile."** $HkW$ is the hourly kW of that profile and $E_P$ its kWh in period $P$ (Sheet 489–490). The Compromise profile is the CY2023 hourly load of **1,858 Rate RTOUPP accounts**, 18,265,086 kWh (Ex. 19.02 pp. 4–119; `workbooks/icc_639690_ex_19_02_rtoupp_hourly_profile.csv`).
4. **Gross up for losses** with a single participant-weighted DLF (0.0522 in Ex. 19.01/19.02), built from the delivery-class DLFs on Info Sheet 33 (Sheet 489, DLF definition).
5. **Formula 1 (HENC)**: for each period, $\sum(HkW \times HLMP \times (1+DLF)) / E_P$. **For every Mid-Day Peak hourly interval, HENC also includes the applicable capacity costs** (Sheet 489).
6. **Capacity (Compromise)**: annual capacity obligation of the 1,858 accounts = their January 2024 PLCs (4,899.33 kW) × zonal factor (1.1156) × forecast pool requirement (1.1170) = 6,105 kW (6.11 MW), × the 2025/26 PJM Net Load Price (\$270.35/MW-day, posted Aug 16, 2024) × 365 days = **\$602,456**. 100% is divided by the profile's Mid-Day kWh (4,566,998) = **\$0.13192/kWh** and added to every Mid-Day kWh in **both seasons** (Ex. 19.02 p. 2; Ex. 19.0). The "Remaining % for kW-based Capacity" is 0.0%.
7. **Formula 2 (EC)** $= (HENC + A) \times 100 \times SBUF \times ISUF$, where $A$ is an adjustment to reflect current market prices and TPEA true-up balances, and SBUF/ISUF are the residential supply uncollectible factors (Sheet 489; Rider UF). Illustrative result: Mid-Day Peak 18.219 ¢/kWh summer and 16.814 ¢/kWh nonsummer (Ex. 19.0).
8. **TMC (Compromise)**: transmission service and related PJM costs (\$215,046, including NITS of \$188,855) are divided by 19,219 MWh, which equals the 18,265 MWh forecast grossed up for the 0.0522 loss factor (18,265 × 1.0522 ≈ 19,218), giving **1.177 ¢/kWh**. Adding 0.066 ¢/kWh of miscellaneous procurement cost gives 1.243, and the uncollectible factors (SBUF 1.0128, then ISUF 1.0053) give the **flat 1.266 ¢/kWh** illustrative TMC, applied to every kWh in every period (Ex. 19.03). Live value: 1.875 ¢/kWh (Jun–Sep 2026), 2.039 ¢/kWh (Oct 2026–May 2027).
9. **Monthly TPEA** (Sheets 492–494): trues up Rate BEST supply revenue against PJM procurement expense, pooled across all Rate BEST customers, as a flat ¢/kWh on every kWh. Ex. 17.0 (p. 28) says the true-up is applied about four months later.

## EPON overcollection hypotheses

The record contains **no Rate BEST bill-impact analysis by customer type** (no heat pump, EV or electric-space-heat cut for Rate BEST). "Overcollect" means a customer pays more than the cost they cause, or more than the flat-rate alternative.

### Reconciling the CUB fact sheet

The CUB fact sheet pairs a "Standard" flat DFC (6.228 / 4.791 / 3.165 / 2.996) with TOU prices (4.009 / 10.712 / ...). The TOU prices equal the **base** DFCs on ComEd's Info Sheet 67 exactly. Compare **base to base** (`icc_388395_*` files) or **billed to billed** (the Oct 2026 guide). Any analysis that quoted 6.228 against 10.712 should be rechecked.

- Info Sheet 64.1 in the e-docket PDF and in the 2026 ratebook are identical, so no 2026 revision of the flat DFC was found. The 2025 flat DFC (5.269 for single-family no heat) shows the 2026 step is +8.1%.
- **Extraction conventions**: tables with figures that the PDF renders as images carry a `[DIAGRAM DESCRIPTION: ...]` block with values read from the plot, marked approximate. Spreadsheet-style exhibits were reproduced from embedded text and checked against rendered pages; none of the table numbers were reconstructed from memory.
