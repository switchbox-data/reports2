# Connecticut ratemaking standards: CLF counsel memo and primary-source checklist

## How to use this file

We asked Conservation Law Foundation (CLF) attorneys whether Connecticut has ratemaking principles we have to be aware of. They answered with a short memo, reproduced in full below. **This is a finding aid, not a source.** We don't cite it. We use it to find the statutes and PURA decisions behind each claim, then cite those directly.

What this file contains:

1. **A summary** of what the memo says and what it doesn't (Switchbox annotation).
2. **The memo text**, converted from Word. Wording is unchanged; the formatting is cleaned up and the two reviewer comments are kept as callouts.
3. **A checklist of primary sources**, with our verification of each statutory quotation against the current Connecticut General Statutes (checked 2026-11-10), the memo's two PURA decisions (now in the source library, with page pinpoints), and what the memo leaves out.

The memo is not privileged. It may be committed publicly, but we don't cite it.

## Summary (Switchbox note)

The memo identifies two layers of standards:

- **Statutes.** The six ratemaking principles in § 16-19e(a); the rate-class and return-on-equity factors in § 16-19gg; the performance-based regulation (PBR) mandate in § 16-244aa(b); and the requirement in § 16-2(m) that PURA decisions be "guided by" the state's energy plans and DEEP policy.
- **PURA's own articulation.** In the 2024 low-income rate decision (Docket 17-12-03RE11) PURA ties low-income rate design to principles (4) and (5) of § 16-19e(a) and to the restructuring findings in § 16-244. In the 2023 PBR decision (Docket 21-05-15) PURA adopts four regulatory goals, five "foundational considerations," and nine "priority outcomes," including equity, GHG reduction, and affordable service.

**Why this matters for heat pump rates** (our interpretation):

- § 16-2(m) is the most useful hook, per the reviewer's note in the memo: if DEEP and the state energy plans support heat pump deployment, PURA's rate decisions must be guided by that, so a heat pump rate is consistent with PURA's policy direction. We need to find the DEEP and plan language that says so. The memo doesn't quote it.
- § 16-244(4) says it is in the state's best interest "to prevent cross subsidization among customer classes." That text is about **inter-class** subsidies. Our argument is about **intra-class** cross-subsidies (heat pump vs. non-heat-pump homes within the residential class), so we should cite it carefully and not claim it directly addresses subclasses.
- The PBR decision's outcomes (GHG reduction, social equity, affordable service) give PURA-endorsed vocabulary for a rate that is cost-reflective and supports electrification.

**What the memo leaves out.** § 16-19e(b), the subsection of the same statute that deals most directly with rate structures. It directs PURA to investigate new pricing principles, including "long run incremental cost of marginal cost pricing" and "peak load or time of day pricing," to determine whether rate structures place an undue burden on persons of poverty status, and to be guided by DEEP's goals, the Comprehensive Energy Strategy, the Integrated Resources Plan, and the Conservation and Load Management Plan. See the checklist below.

Below is a summary of (1) statutory standards governing rate design and (2) additional rate design principles as applied or articulated by PURA in recent decisions. (not exhaustive; focus is primarily on standards that may be relevant to heat pump rates)

### 1. CT Statutory Standards that Govern Rate Design

#### Ratemaking & Regulatory Principles

Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19e(a) establishes the **core principles that govern PURA's ratemaking and regulatory decisions**. PURA "shall examine and regulate . . . the establishment of the level and structure of rates in accordance with" **six principles:** (1) clear public need for the service being proposed or provided; (2) the utility must be fully competent (i.e., technically, financially, and managerially expert and efficient) to provide efficient and adequate service; (3) PURA and utilities shall perform their responsibilities with economy, efficiency, and care for public safety and energy security, and so as to promote economic development in CT with consideration for energy and water conservation, energy efficiency and the development and utilization of renewable sources of energy and for prudent management of the environment; (4) the level and structure of rates should be sufficient, but no more than sufficient, to allow utilities to cover their operating and capital costs, to attract needed capital, and to maintain their financial integrity, and yet provide appropriate protection to the relevant existing and foreseeable public interests; (5) the level and structure of rates should reflect prudent and efficient management; and (6) rates, charges, conditions of service, and categories of service should not discriminate against customers using renewable energy sources or cogeneration technology. Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19e(a).

#### Factors Considered During Rate Proceedings

**When determining rates for individual rate classes** in an electric or gas rate proceeding, PURA must consider the energy cost of manufacturers by analyzing the following factors: "(1) the effect of different rates of return among rate classes upon manufacturers; (2) the use of different cost allocation methodologies; (3) the use of flexible pricing; and (4) any other issue deemed relevant by the authority." Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19gg(a).

**When determining a reasonable rate of return** during an electric or gas rate proceeding, PURA must consider the following factors: "(1) macroeconomic conditions at the time the rate amendment is pending before the authority; (2) the company's compliance with state law, regulations and the decisions and policies of the authority and the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection; (3) the burden of the public service company's costs on residential ratepayers, measured as a percentage of household income, under the current and proposed rate; (4) trends in the company's accrual of bad debt; (5) the rate impact on all residential and nonresidential customers; and (6) any other issue deemed relevant by the authority." Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19gg(b).

> **Reviewer comment (anchored on the two paragraphs above, i.e. the § 16-19gg discussion):** "Not so relevant to the HP rate context"

#### Performance-Based Regulation of EDCs

Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-244aa(b) required PURA to initiate a PBR proceeding to develop and adopt a PBR framework, which "shall: (1) [e]stablish standards and metrics for measuring such electric distribution company's performance of objectives that are in the interest of ratepayers or benefit the public, which may include, but not be not limited to, safety, reliability, emergency response, cost efficiency, affordability, equity, customer satisfaction, municipal engagement, resilience and advancing the state's environmental and policy goals, including, but not limited to, those goals established in section 22a-200a [the GWSA], in the Integrated Resources Plan approved pursuant to section 16a-3a and in the Comprehensive Energy Strategy prepared pursuant to section 16a-3d . . . ."

#### IRP, CES, and CLMP Guide PURA Decisions

Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-2(m) states: "Notwithstanding any provision of the general statutes, **the decisions of the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority, including, but not limited to, decisions relating to rate amendments** arising from the Integrated Resources Plan, the Comprehensive Energy Strategy, the Conservation Load Management Plan and policies established by the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection, **shall be guided by said plans and strategy and such policies**."

> **Reviewer comment (anchored on the last words of the § 16-2(m) quotation, "policies"):** "Shannon's note: This is a good hook because we can argue that DEEP's guidance supports HP deployment and a HP rate would therefore be consistent with DEEP policy direction."

### 2. Rate Design Principles as Applied by PURA

#### PURA Application of Statutory Standards

In its November 20, 2024, [Final Decision in Docket 17-12-03RE11](https://www.dpuc.state.ct.us/2nddockcurr.nsf/8e6fc37a54110e3e852576190052b64d/3aa5538fced855fc85258bdb0052ea40/$FILE/171203RE11-112024.pdf) (Investigation into Distribution System Planning of the Electric Distribution Companies – New Rate Designs and Rates Review), PURA stated that it has an obligation to establish the level and structure of low-income rates consistent with principles (4) and (5) in Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19e(a) (see above). Decision at 7.

PURA also explained that "in the context of restructuring the electric industry, the General Assembly explicitly recognized the important role of electricity in Connecticut and articulated additional principles that are broadly applicable and provide guidance in the Authority's oversight of the EDCs and the EDCs' obligations to the public." Decision at \_\_. [_The page number is blank in the original memo. The passage is on p. 7 of the decision (see the checklist below)._] The principles that PURA explicitly cited are:

"(1) The provision of affordable, safe and reliable electricity is key to the continuing growth of this state and to the health, safety and general welfare of its residents; . . . (4) It is in the best interest of the state to reduce rates for electricity to all customer classes [and] to prevent cross subsidization among customer classes . . . while retaining a regulated distribution system to ensure reliability; . . . (8) The assurance of safe, reliable and available electric service to all customers in a uniform and equitable manner is an essential governmental objective and a restructured electric market must provide adequate safeguards to assure universal service and customer service protections; . . . (12) It is in the best interest of the state for all customers to use electricity as efficiently as possible." Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-244.

#### PURA-Articulated Standards

In its April 26, 2023, [Final Decision in Docket No. 21-05-15](https://www.dpuc.state.ct.us/dockhistpost2000.nsf/8e6fc37a54110e3e852576190052b64d/b9fe7d37d6e33e8d85258db7005dd3b6/$FILE/210515-042623.pdf) (Investigation into a Performance-Based Regulation Framework for the Electric Distribution Companies), PURA adopted **four regulatory goals "to guide not only the remainder of the PBR investigation, but future proceedings and regulation for the electric utilities moving forward."** Decision at 14. The goals are: (1) excellent operational performance; (2) **public policy achievement**; (3) customer empowerment and satisfaction; and (4) **reasonable, equitable, and affordable rates**. Decision at 13. PURA stated, "The legacy goals of safe, reliable, and affordable service remain critically important to the state's regulatory framework and are reflected in the adopted regulatory goals and foundational considerations." Decision at 13. PURA recognized that there may be tension between the regulatory goals but this is an inherent aspect of utility regulation. Decision at 13.

In the same decision, PURA also established **five "foundational considerations . . . to serve as core tenets of any regulatory decision to be made in future proceedings and Authority deliberations."** Decision at 16. These considerations "are essential dimensions to the Authority's approach to EDC regulation – no decision should be made without initial consideration for these first principles." Decision at 16. The five foundational considerations are: safety, **equity**, economic opportunity, risk distribution, and transparency. Decision at 14-15.

Finally, PURA adopted **nine "priority outcomes" to guide the rest of the PBR investigation and future proceedings and regulation for the EDCs**. Decision at 19. The priority outcomes include: (1) business operations and investment efficiency; (2) comprehensive and transparent system planning; (3) distribution system utilization; (4) reliable and resilient electric service; (5) social equity; (6) **GHG reduction**; (7) customer empowerment; (8) quality customer service; and (9) **affordable service**. Decision at 16.

## Gap analysis against Velez Direct Testimony (Docket 26-05-10) (Switchbox note, 2026-11-10)

After filing direct testimony, we reviewed it against the standards in this memo to check whether any important ratemaking principles were missed. The testimony's core arguments — cost causation, intra-class equity, and building electrification policy — are thoroughly covered and grounded in Bonbright, the RAP manual, and the Company's own MCOS witness. The CIEC interrogatories (Set CIEC-02) do not surface any statutory-principle gaps. Assessment by priority:

### One genuinely important gap: § 16-19e(b)

The testimony's central argument is that incremental heat pump load should be priced at (closer to) marginal cost, and Section VIII argues that Rate 6's block design places a disproportionate burden on lower-income customers. **§ 16-19e(b) is a statute that directs PURA to do exactly both of those things** — investigate "long run incremental cost of marginal cost pricing" and "peak load or time of day pricing," and determine whether rate structures "place an undue burden upon those persons of poverty status." It is the most direct statutory basis for the kind of rate design innovation proposed in Rate HP, and it is not cited anywhere in the testimony.

**Where to use it.** If the Company's rebuttal argues that the Commission has no basis for adopting marginal-cost-based rate design, or that Rate HP is a novel departure from established ratemaking, § 16-19e(b) is a clean surrebuttal point. Either way, it belongs in the post-hearing brief, where the evidentiary record is tied to the legal standards the Commission applies. (It's a statute, not a factual claim, so it doesn't need to be in the evidentiary record to be cited in a brief.)

### Two secondary items worth knowing

**§ 16-2(m) — "decisions shall be guided by" DEEP plans and policies.** The testimony cites the substantive policies (GWSA, DEEP's GHG inventory, the New England Heat Pump Accelerator) but does not invoke the statute that makes them binding on PURA's rate decisions. The argument is all there; the statutory command isn't explicitly invoked. Worth adding in surrebuttal if the Company argues heat pump adoption is outside PURA's ratemaking lane, and in briefing regardless.

**PBR Decision (Docket 21-05-15) goals and outcomes.** PURA adopted "GHG reduction," "social equity," and "affordable service" as priority outcomes, and "public policy achievement" and "reasonable, equitable, and affordable rates" as regulatory goals. The testimony never cites the PBR decision. These are PURA's own articulated standards, useful for framing Rate HP in the Commission's own vocabulary — particularly in briefing.

### Items considered and not gaps

- **§ 16-19e(a)(6) — non-discrimination against renewable energy customers.** Heat pumps are not "renewable energy sources" in the statutory sense (aimed at solar/cogen). Citing it would be a stretch.
- **§ 16-244(4) — cross-subsidization among customer classes.** This is about inter-class subsidies; our argument is intra-class. The testimony wisely avoids claiming it as direct authority and instead relies on Bonbright and the Company's own MCOS witness.
- **§ 16-19oo and § 16-19zz.** Procedural hooks (PURA _can_ approve policy-driven rate amendments). Useful if jurisdiction is challenged, but that hasn't happened.
- **Revenue neutrality / who bears the revenue loss.** CIEC hammers this (questions 2, 3, 6, 7, 16). This is an implementation question the testimony deliberately left open (argue the overcharge should be eliminated; don't specify who absorbs it). A strategic choice, not a gap in principles.
