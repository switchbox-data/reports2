## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Program Participation and Impact Highlights

#### Program Impacts

BGE’s EmPOWER Maryland (“EmPOWER”) portfolio of programs continued their success in 2026. Beginning in 2025, the statutory target for the EmPOWER MD programs changed from MWh to lifecycle Green House Gas (“GHG”) reduction measured in Metric Tons of CO2e. For the first half of 2026, BGE’s EmPOWER MD program portfolio has achieved nearly 62% of its statutory GHG target using 36% of the overall 2026 program budget. Regarding the GHG reduction target established in the Company’s 2024-2026 EmPOWER cycle filing, the portfolio achieved over 547,000 lifecycle GHG metric tons, which represents 58% of the 2026 filed target for 2026. This places the portfolio in the “Exceeding Target” category for 2026. Additionally, the entire portfolio achieved nearly 348,000 MWh in annualized electric energy savings. Natural gas savings for the first half of 2026 were over 2.1 million therms. In addition to the energy savings realized and associated environmental benefits customers generated by choosing more efficient alternatives, BGE customers received incentives and bill credits of approximately $93 million. Economic benefits returned to BGE customers for 2026 are summarized below.

| BGE EmPOWER Maryland Economic Customer Benefits - YTD June 2026                    |       Amount |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----------: |
| Direct rebates paid to customers for energy efficiency measures                    | $ 82,500,000 |
| Demand Response (excl. SER) customer credits and signing bonuses paid to customers |  $ 8,700,000 |
| Smart Energy Rewards (SER) program credits paid to customers                       |          $ - |
| Value of PeakRewards devices installed in customers' homes                         |  $ 2,100,000 |
| Total value to BGE customers                                                       | $ 93,300,000 |

Avoided generation from the nearly 348,000 MWh 2026 portfolio savings over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed in 2026 is over 2.2 million MWh.

#### Program Highlights

There are four notable Energy Efficiency programs that have performed extremely well for the first half of 2026. These four programs are identified below and will be discussed in greater detail in the “Program Specifics” section of this report.

**The following energy efficiency programs in the residential portfolio achieved great success in 2026:**

**Residential New Construction (New Homes):** BGE’s support for builders advancing projects ahead of the June 30, 2026 expiration of the federal 45L New Energy Efficient Home Tax Credit resulted in the Residential New Construction Program delivering 404% of its filed annual lifecycle GHG savings target for the first half of 2026.

[PDF p. 6 | printed p. 2]

**Appliance Rebates:** BGE leveraged key drivers, including strong Heat Pump Water Heater (HPWH) sales across multiple channels, growth in the ENERGY STAR Retail Products Program (ESRPP) channel and updated Non-Profit Organization (NPO) Kit contents that deliver significant GHG savings at a cost-effective rate to achieved [sic] 62% of the 2026 filed lifecycle GHG annual savings target for the first half of 2026.

**The following commercial energy efficiency programs are posting strong results for 2026:**

**Retrocommissioning (Building Tune-Up):** For the first half of 2026, by way of customer education and coordination with state agencies, the Retrocommissioning Program achieved 119% of its 2026 lifecycle GHG annual savings target.

**Small Business:** Driven by coordination with the Connected Rewards Program, for the first half of 2026, the Small Business Program achieved 67% of its 2026 lifecycle GHG annual savings target.

Effective January 1, 2024, the Smart Thermostat Rebate Program was incorporated into the Appliance Rebate Program to align with the delivery structure of the other Maryland Utilities. BGE offers a rebate of up to $100 to residential customers who purchase an ENERGY STAR® certified smart thermostat through two channels in the Appliance Rebate Program - a mail-in or online rebate request or, a direct-to-consumer limited-time offer (“LTO”) to targeted customers through a simple-to-use online platform. The program offers a retailer rebate to stock and sell ENERGY STAR® certified smart thermostats through the ENERGY STAR Retail Products Platform (“ESRPP”) channel.

BGE also offers additional residential smart thermostat rebates through the HVAC Residential Midstream, New Homes, Quick Home Energy Check-up (“QHEC”), and Home Performance with ENERGY STAR® (“HPwES”) programs. Residential customers are eligible for up to three smart thermostat rebates (per program cycle) across all residential programs. BGE claims additional energy savings through cloud optimization services offered through eligible devices claimed through the programs mentioned above.

BGE’s Small Business Energy Solutions (“SBES”) Program offers smart thermostats to eligible commercial customers. Smart thermostat capabilities make the measure ideal for small businesses because there is an opportunity to significantly reduce load when the building is unoccupied.

##### Appliance Rebates

The BGE Residential Appliance Rebates Program encourages consumers to purchase high-efficiency appliances by offering rebates for select ENERGY STAR® certified products. Qualifying products offered through the Appliance Rebates Program include room air conditioners, air purifiers, clothes dryers and washers, freezers, refrigerators, smart thermostats, cooktops, and combo washer/dryers offered through the midstream ENERGY STAR Retail Products Platform (“ESRPP”), advanced power strips and dehumidifiers offered through an instant markdown at select retailers, energy efficiency kits distributed via nonprofit organizations, and heat pump water heaters (“HPWH”) and smart thermostats offered through multiple channels.

**Exceeding Target:** For the first half of 2026, the Appliance Rebate Program achieved 62% of its annual savings target, achieving 51,774 metric tons of lifecycle GHG savings. This performance was driven by strong HPWH sales across multiple channels, continued growth in the ENERGY STAR Retail Products Program (“ESRPP”) channel and updated Non-Profit Organization (“NPO”) Kit contents that deliver significant GHG savings at a cost-effective rate.

For the first half of 2026, 1,309 HPWHs were incentivized across the five channels in the Appliance Rebate Program offering HPWHs. HPWH sales increased by 215% compared to the same period in 2025.

**HPWH Highlights:**

- **Product Advisor Plus Channel** - Electrification incentives in the program launched through the Product Advisor Plus platform ([www.bgewaterheater.com](http://www.bgewaterheater.com/)). In the first half of 2026, BGE supported 31 customer transitions through the program’s electrification incentives.
- **Midstream HPWH Channel** - Electrification launched through the Midstream HPWH channel in Q2 of 2026. The enhanced HPWH incentive included a “make-ready” rebate to support the completion of the project and improve the affordability of electrifying. This is offered through BGE’s network of contractors and distributors already participating in the

[PDF p. 23 | printed p. 19]

HVAC midstream program. New contractors and distributors are being invited to participate also.

Other midstream HPWH enhancements were implemented in Q2 to align the HPWH midstream offering with the HVAC midstream processes. These enhancements include enabling contractors to submit applications, increased documentation requirements to ensure customer receipt of the rebate, BGE EmPOWER rebate attribution, and photos of before and after work for electrification projects.

Baseline HPWH incentives have increased from $1600 to $1700, allowing both distributors and contractors to retain a portion and pass the full $1600 to the customer. ##### Appliance Recycling

The Appliance Recycling Program offers residential customers an easy way to recycle inefficient, old working appliances. BGE offers customers two ways to participate through home pick-up appointments as well as community drop-off events. Customers can receive $50 for each large appliance (up to three refrigerators and/or freezers) and $25 for each small appliance, such as room air conditioners, dehumidifiers, or compact refrigerators.

**On Target:** For the first half of 2026, the Appliance Recycling Program achieved 47% of its annual savings target, achieving 4,205 metric tons of lifecycle GHG savings.

The harsh winter temporarily halted scheduled pickups and led to appointment cancellations. These weather-related disruptions reduced unit collections and slowed program savings. As the program entered its summer seasonal peak period, appliance collections increased as expected. Because appliance recycling is highly seasonal, with higher participation and collection volumes during the summer and fall months, the program is expected to continue gaining momentum through year-end. The program remains on track to achieve its annual savings goals by year-end.

In the first half of 2026, BGE completed the following initiatives:

- Implemented a four-week refrigerator/freezer LTO in March 2026 that increased incentives to $100 for these measures during the LTO period.
- Implemented a four-week dehumidifier LTO in May 2026 that increased the dehumidifier incentive to $50.
- Hosted an appliance drop-off event in Baltimore, in partnering with the Baltimore City Department of Public works, which collected 90 room air conditioners, dehumidifiers, and compact refrigerators. More drop-off events are scheduled for the second half of the year.
- Relaunched BGE’s Midstream Recycling Program which focuses primarily on small reseller businesses where savings can be captured from additional units through our efficient, bulk collection opportunities.

Under the Quick Home Energy Check-Up (“QHEC”) Program, a Building Performance Institute (“BPI”) certified technician conducts a walk-through of a customer’s home and visually inspects elements of the home, including the insulation, heating and cooling system, lighting, and appliances. The customer receives a report summarizing potential energy-saving improvements, as well as recommendations for other BGE programs for which the customer would be a good candidate. In addition, the QHEC technician may install the following energy-saving measures as appropriate: LED light bulbs, faucet aerators, efficient-flow fixed or handheld showerheads, water heater pipe insulation, smart power strips, a ShowerStart™ showerhead adapter, door sweeps, weatherstripping, and a smart thermostat. Outside of the customer’s EmPOWER Maryland surcharge, there is no additional charge for the QHEC inspection or the installed energy-saving measures.

**Below Target:** For the first half of 2026, the QHEC Program achieved 39% of its annual savings target, achieving 23,521 metric tons of lifecycle GHG savings.

Consistent with program expectations, GHG savings from lighting measures continued to decline. Although the program installed more than 60,000 LED bulbs, generating 1,928 MWh of reported lifetime electric savings, these measures accounted for 0.3% of reported lifecycle GHG savings during the reporting period. LED light bulbs will be fully phased out of QHEC by the end of 2026. Efficient-flow showerheads and faucet aerators remained the primary drivers of program savings, contributing 81% of reported lifecycle GHG savings, while door sweeps and weatherstripping contributed an additional 6% of lifecycle GHG savings.

The program also continued to connect customers with additional energy-saving opportunities, generating 1,146 DHCD referrals during the reporting period.

##### Home Performance with ENERGY STAR®

Home Performance with ENERGY STAR® (“HPwES”) is a national program overseen by the Department of Energy and guided by the standards of the BPI. The program’s goal is to enhance residential comfort and energy efficiency by addressing the house as a system, while improving the health and safety of its occupants. Participating home performance contractors conduct comprehensive home energy audits (“audits”) and use home energy modeling software to create customized recommendations designed to improve the comfort and efficiency of the BGE customer’s home. These recommendations are provided in a home energy audit report that summarize and prioritize the opportunities for home energy improvements. The customer decides which measures they want installed and contracts with a participating contractor to complete the home performance improvement (“job”). Upon completion of the job, a final project inspection is performed using diagnostic testing to ensure the contracted work was completed properly. The program provides a subsidy for the cost of the audit that allows the customer to receive an audit valued at $400 for only $100. For qualified moderate-income households, the cost of the audit is fully subsidized by the program. The program offers rebates up to $10,000 for qualifying energy efficiency improvements and up to $15,000 for projects that include electrification measures.

**On Target:** For the first half of 2026, the HPwES Program achieved 55% of its annual savings target, achieving 46,304 metric tons of lifecycle GHG savings. Additionally, the HPwES Program completed 3,183 audits and 1,705 jobs.

Year-over-year participation growth continued into the first half of 2026 with a 46% increase in completed jobs compared to the first half of 2025. The audit-to-job conversion rate also exceeded 50% – the highest conversion rate of any reporting period program-to-date.

The number of participants installing heat pumps as part of their project has also continued to increase. During the reporting period, 18% of projects included a heat pump water heater (309 HPWHs installed), and nearly 15% included at least one heat pump for space heating and cooling. A total of 293 Air Source Heat Pumps (“ASHPs”), including mini-splits, and one Ground Source Heat Pump (“GSHP”) were installed across 276 projects.

**Electrification:** BGE customers completed 226 electrification projects through the HPwES Program during the first half of 2026.

**Moderate Income Offering (“MIO”):** In the first half of 2026, 34 audits and 42 jobs were completed through the moderate-income pathway under the HPwES Program. During the reporting period, the program received 64 new applicants. Of those applicants, 20 qualified as moderate-income, while 32 self-identified at income levels consistent with DHCD's programs and were directed to move forward through that pathway. The remaining 12 applicants, who did not qualify for the MIO pathway, were directed to continue forward through the standard HPwES offer. MIO-participating contractors support both BGE and DHCD programs, helping ensure customers are matched with the program best suited to their income eligibility.

**Moderate Income Offering – Response to Order No. 92522**

In Order No. 92522, the Commission adopted OPC's recommendation that BGE enhance the visibility of its Moderate-Income Offering ("MIO") within the Home Performance with ENERGY STAR® ("HPwES") program and provide additional reporting regarding MIO program performance. BGE supports the Commission's objective of increasing awareness of assistance available to moderate-income households and has already launched additional marketing and updates to customer-facing materials.

For context, the MIO was developed during the 2024-2026 EmPOWER planning process in response to a recognized gap between market-rate HPwES offerings and DHCD's limited-income energy efficiency programs. During review of the proposed plans, stakeholders—including OPC and other parties—raised concerns around utility marketing of MIO due to concerns about customer routing, income verification, program overlap, and the need to ensure customers were directed to the most appropriate offering based on household circumstances. OPC specifically emphasized a "no wrong door" approach and highlighted the importance of coordination between utility and DHCD-administered programs.[^1]

Consistent with those stakeholder discussions, BGE's MIO implementation was structured as a coordinated offering designed to complement, rather than compete with, DHCD programs. As a result, early program implementation avoided any direct consumer marketing. Instead, focusing on contractor training, income screening, referral pathways, and coordination with DHCD. The program was intentionally designed to identify households that may qualify for enhanced assistance through DHCD and direct those customers accordingly.

Notably, OPC's review of early MIO performance found that referrals to DHCD were a significant program outcome. OPC reported that many MIO applicants were ultimately determined to be eligible for DHCD-administered programs and noted that approximately half of MIO inquiries were referred to DHCD's limited-income offerings. OPC further concluded that these referrals demonstrated that MIO was serving as an effective mechanism to ensure customers were directed to the appropriate program channel.[^2]

BGE recognizes that Order No. 92522 reflects the Commission's determination that additional MIO visibility may improve participation among eligible moderate-income households. Accordingly, BGE has already begun increasing marketing of MIO within HPwES program materials and customer-facing channels. In implementing these improvements, BGE intends to preserve the original objective of the program—to provide a seamless pathway between market-rate, moderate-income, and DHCD-administered offerings while minimizing customer confusion and ensuring that customers receive the level of assistance most appropriate for their household circumstances. Please see Appendix E - Marketing section for more information about HPwES MIO marketing activities.

#### Residential New Construction (ENERGY STAR® New Homes) Program

The ENERGY STAR® New Construction Program features builder incentives for the construction of “above-code” homes that meet or exceed ENERGY STAR® new home construction requirements. Both single-family and multi-family style homes qualify. Builders must incorporate certain high-efficiency appliances, measures, and construction techniques to meet program qualifications. The program offers bonus incentives for “Additive Measures” that exceed ENERGY STAR® minimum efficiency requirements. Additive Measures include high efficiency heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, air conditioners, smart thermostats, and HVAC verified quality installation. Beginning 2024, the program offered increased incentives for homes that qualify for higher ENERGY STAR® tiers, including ENERGY STAR® Version 3.2 and ENERGY STAR® NextGen with an added all-electric requirement for qualifying NextGen homes.

**Exceeding Target:** For the first half of 2026, the Residential New Construction Program achieved 404% of its annual savings target, achieving 44,729 metric tons of lifecycle GHG savings. The Program continued to exceed targets due to the mid-cycle transition to GHG calculations and the contribution of savings from long-lived measures that have exceeded the program’s initial forecasts.

The Program provided whole-house certification incentives for 873 homes and supported builders in advancing projects ahead of the June 30, 2026 expiration of the federal 45L New Energy Efficient Home Tax Credit.

The Program also continued to promote higher-quality HVAC installations, with 365 homes achieving the highest Grade 1 HVAC Installation designation under ACCA Standard 310, and four homes earning highest efficiency tier, ENERGY STAR® NextGen certification, with the additional requirement of all-electric construction.

While several builders have successfully transitioned to ENERGY STAR Version 3.2, many continue to face challenges meeting its more stringent whole-house requirements. Code+ supports continued progress while helping prevent backsliding to code-minimum construction. For builders not yet prepared to achieve full Version 3.2 certification, Code+ provides a flexible pathway to deliver meaningful energy and GHG reductions. It also allows the program to promote high-efficiency heat pumps and heat pump water heaters to a broader audience of builders. For example, the percentage of Code+ homes that included at least one high-efficiency heat pump doubled from 2025 (3%) to 2026 (7%). Importantly, Code+ requires homes to meet the same energy rating target as Version 3.2, ensuring comparable energy performance while allowing builders flexibility in how those savings are achieved.

### Commission Direction

In Order No. 92176, issued February 6, 2026, the Maryland Public Service Commission directed each utility to include in its 2026 Q1/Q2 Semi-Annual Report an assessment of the incentive structures within its Residential New Construction ("RNC") Program. Specifically, the Commission requested an analysis of whether program incentives appropriately compensate for higher levels of energy savings while minimizing free-ridership and ratepayer impacts.[^att1]

### Purpose of Assessment

This report evaluates the current incentive structure of BGE's Residential New Construction Program in response to the Commission's directive. The assessment examines the relationship between program incentives, evaluated energy savings, and greenhouse gas ("GHG") reductions associated with both performance-based program tiers and eligible additive measures.

The BGE RNC Program utilizes a tiered incentive structure designed to encourage residential builders to construct homes that exceed applicable energy code requirements. Higher incentive levels are available only when participating homes achieve progressively more stringent efficiency standards through verified improvements in overall home performance. In addition to these performance pathways, the Program includes a limited number of additive incentives that support the adoption of specific high-efficiency technologies and electrification measures.

### Assessment Approach

Reported energy savings, greenhouse gas (GHG) reductions, and incentive values were summarized using program participation data from the 2025 Program Year. For purposes of this assessment, representative residential energy models and engineering assumptions were used to evaluate the relative impacts of eligible additive technologies, including GSHPs, and provide a consistent basis for comparing incentive levels with expected energy and GHG benefits. Average savings vary by housing type and are influenced by factors including conditioned floor area, building configuration, fuel type, and installed equipment.

### Summary of Findings

Higher incentive levels are associated with progressively more stringent performance requirements and correspond to greater expected energy savings and customer benefits. The results of this assessment indicate that the current BGE Residential New Construction Program incentive structure aligns incentive levels with expected energy savings and greenhouse gas reductions. The Program's tiered framework appropriately differentiates incentives based on increasing performance requirements, while additive measure incentives provide targeted support for technologies that advance program and policy objectives.

Overall, the analysis demonstrates that the existing incentive structure continues to support measurable energy savings, meaningful GHG reductions, and above-code residential construction

### Considerations When Interpreting Savings Metrics

Energy savings and GHG reductions are influenced by factors including home size, housing type, building configuration, installed equipment, and fuel mix. As a result, homes achieving similar levels of efficiency improvement may produce different evaluated savings outcomes. For example, two otherwise identical homes that receive the same insulation and air sealing improvements will achieve comparable building envelope efficiency gains. However, if one home utilizes a heat pump and the other utilizes natural gas heating, the resulting GHG reductions may differ due to the emissions characteristics of the heating fuel rather than differences in the effectiveness of the efficiency improvements themselves.

[PDF p. 165 | printed p. 3]

This distinction is important when evaluating Program performance. The RNC tier structure is designed to reward verified improvements in overall home performance, while GHG outcomes may also reflect builder and homeowner fuel-selection decisions that are largely outside the Program's control. Accordingly, this assessment considers both energy savings and GHG reductions when evaluating incentive alignment.

### Factors Affecting Incentive Alignment

#### Program Design Framework

A foundational element of the RNC Program is its performance-based design. Rather than prescribing specific equipment selections, the Program establishes efficiency performance thresholds that permit builders flexibility in determining how those requirements are most cost-effectively achieved. Incentive levels increase as participating homes achieve progressively higher levels of verified energy performance. Achievement of higher Program tiers requires additional investments in building envelope performance, equipment efficiency, and construction practices necessary to attain more stringent performance thresholds.

As state energy codes continue to advance, maintaining meaningful differentiation between code-compliant construction and Program-qualified homes becomes increasingly important. The Program's tiered structure preserves this distinction while encouraging continued adoption of above-code construction practices. The Program has also evolved to support Maryland's greenhouse gas reduction and beneficial electrification objectives through incentives for technologies such as heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, and all-electric construction pathways.

#### Additive Measures

In addition to its performance-based tiers, the RNC Program includes a limited number of additive incentives intended to encourage adoption of high-efficiency electric technologies, including heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, and all-electric construction pathways. These incentives were introduced in recent program years to support Maryland's increasing emphasis on beneficial electrification and greenhouse gas reduction and are intended to complement the Program's long-standing whole-house performance framework. These incentives are intended to complement the Program's broader performance-based framework by encouraging technologies that support electrification objectives rather than serving as a separate mechanism for delivering or claiming Program savings.

#### Free-Ridership and Ratepayer Impact

In Order No. 92176, the Commission directed utilities to evaluate whether Residential New Construction Program incentives appropriately compensate for energy savings while minimizing free-ridership and ratepayer impacts. Within the RNC Program, this consideration is particularly relevant when evaluating additive incentives that are intended to influence technology adoption rather than compensate for separately claimed Program savings.

Unlike the Program's performance tiers, additive incentives do not correspond to a separate source of claimed savings beyond those already reflected in the home's verified performance and tier achievement. As a result, substantial increases in additive incentive levels may increase program costs without producing a proportionate increase in evaluated energy savings or greenhouse gas benefits.

[PDF p. 166 | printed p. 4]

Accordingly, additive incentive levels should be evaluated in the context of expected market impacts, technology adoption objectives, ratepayer costs, and the potential for free-ridership among participants who may have adopted the technology absent additional incentive support. This consideration is particularly important in maintaining alignment between incentive expenditures and expected customer and ratepayer benefits.

### Conclusion

The results of this assessment indicate that BGE's current Residential New Construction incentive structure aligns incentive levels with expected energy savings and greenhouse gas benefits. Higher Program tiers require progressively more stringent levels of verified energy performance and correspond to greater customer and environmental benefits, while additive incentives provide targeted support for technologies that advance electrification objectives. Overall, the analysis supports the conclusion that the existing incentive framework remains consistent with Commission direction and prudent stewardship of ratepayer-funded resources.
