## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Program Participation and Impact Highlights

#### Program Impacts

BGE's EmPOWER Maryland ("EmPOWER") portfolio of programs continued their success in 2025. Beginning in 2025, the statutory target for the EmPOWER MD programs changed from MWh to Lifecycle GHG reduction measured in Metric Tons of CO₂e. For 2025, BGE's EmPOWER MD program portfolio has achieved over 114% of its GHG target using 79% of the overall 2025 program budget. Regarding the GHG reduction target established in the Company's 2024-2026 EmPOWER cycle filing, the portfolio achieved over 954,000 lifecycle GHG metric tons, which represents 114% of the 2025 filed target for 2025. This places the portfolio in the "Exceeded Target" category for 2025. Additionally, the entire portfolio achieved over 686,000 MWh in annualized electric energy savings. Natural gas savings in 2025 were over 3 million therms. In addition to the energy savings realized and associated environmental benefits customers generated by choosing more efficient alternatives, BGE customers received incentives and bill credits of approximately $192 million. Economic benefits returned to BGE customers are summarized below.

**BGE EmPOWER Maryland Economic Customer Benefits - Year 2025**

| Benefit | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Direct rebates paid to customers for energy efficiency measures | $167,500,000 |
| Demand Response (excl. SER) customer credits and signing bonuses paid to customers | $22,000,000 |
| Smart Energy Rewards (SER) program credits paid to customers | $2,300,000 |
| Value of PeakRewards devices installed in customers' homes | $5,700,000 |
| Total value to BGE customers | $197,500,000 |

Avoided generation from the over 686,000 MWh 2025 portfolio savings over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed in 2025 is nearly 4.2 million MWh.

### Program Highlights

While many programs have exceeded their 2025 targets, there are four Energy Efficiency programs that have performed extremely well in 2025 that are worth noting. The four programs mentioned below will be discussed in greater detail in the "Program Specifics" section of this report.

The following energy efficiency programs in the residential portfolio achieved great success in 2025:

**Residential New Construction (New Homes):** The Residential New Construction Program achieved extremely strong results in 2026 [source discrepancy: the report covers 2025; "2026" as printed], attaining an astounding 775% of the filed lifecycle GHG annual savings target. Strong performance and the conversion methodology used to

**Appliance Recycling:** The Appliance Recycling program achieved 116% of the 2025 filed lifecycle GHG annual savings target. Key drivers of the performance include the institution of home appointments and flexibility at pick-up time as to additional items.

The following commercial energy efficiency programs are posting strong results for 2025:

**Retrocommissioning (Building Tune-Up):** For 2025, the Retrocommissioning Program achieved 139% of its 2025 lifecycle GHG annual savings target. The refinement of the Building Tune-up program to assist building owners in meeting the requirements of Maryland's Building Energy Performance Standard (BEPS) regulations, and a redesigned Building Operator Training Program, helped bring about this success.

**Midstream:** For 2025, the Midstream Program achieved 132% of its 2025 lifecycle GHG annual savings target. A new suite of offerings, and teaming up successfully with the school systems, brought about this increased GHG reduction.

### Residential Energy Efficiency Portfolio

In 2025, The Residential Energy Efficiency Portfolio achieved Over 397,000 metric tons of lifecycle GHG savings, equating to 112% of the filed annual savings target for the portfolio (excluding the Smart Energy Manager-Residential Program).

Some program highlights included:

- The HVAC Program achieved 111% of its filed annual savings targets. The results were driven by strong contractor participation as we saw incentivized Heat Pumps increase over 90% compared to 2024. ### Maryland Public Service Commission Requests

**Establish Deadlines and Guidance for GHG Savings Calculations and Final Verification and Cost-Effectiveness Reports**

During the current EmPOWER program cycle, the Independent Evaluator (IE) submitted multiple requests requiring utilities to revise greenhouse gas (GHG) savings and cost effectiveness calculation inputs. The IE team informed the utilities and DHCD on January 14, 2026 that the GHG emissions intensity factor was being updated and instructed to apply these updates to projects completed from July 2025 – June 2026, after the projects had already been completed and the savings were calculated for July – December 2025. This adjustment has had the overall effect of retroactively reducing the reported savings. This request was made shortly before the annual and semiannual report filing deadline, which is February 15, 2026. Implementing substantive changes under such time constraints is impractical and materially increases the risk of errors due to updating reporting under accelerated timelines. Retroactive adjustments that reduce reported savings after the period has closed undermines the utilities and DHCD's ability to manage their programs to meet the annual target and for customers to understand their savings impacts. These retroactive adjustments have adverse impacts on customers, as customers are expecting their projects to produce a certain amount of savings when the project is closed out and the invoice is paid. Retroactively going back and updating the savings, in many cases, reducing the savings, results in a negative customer experience. BGE implemented this update for the purposes of abiding by the Commission's reporting deadline, but felt it important to raise with the Commission that we disagree with the practice of retroactively applying revised GHG savings calculations after the reporting period closes, and the unreasonable timeframe for which the IE expects utilities and

[PDF p. 23 | printed p. 8]

DHCD to execute these updates and still meet rigorous data quality control and reporting standards that the Commissions expects. This update resulted in gross savings reduction of approximately 35,000 metric tons.

The Evaluation Advisory Group (EAG) Strategic Guidance provides that "the Independent Evaluator should inform the appropriate Evaluation Advisory Group member(s) in a timely manner when any material addition, update, or edit is made." BGE submits that the implementation of retroactive changes late in the reporting cycle is inconsistent with the intent of this guidance. Moreover, the Strategic Guidance does not establish defined deadlines for IE deliverables, specify timeframes for IE requests to utilities, or provide standards governing when such requests may be incorporated into filed reports.

Accordingly, BGE respectfully requests that the Commission adopt clear deadlines and procedural guidance addressing the following:

- The Commission to establish a deadline for the delivery of final verification and cost-effectiveness reports from the Independent Evaluator to the utilities and DHCD. These reports should be submitted no later than eight months after the end of the calendar year being evaluated (e.g., a November 1, 2025 deadline for the Calendar Year 2024 reports).
- Clear guidance that GHG savings calculations at the measure level must be established by the IE before the start of a program cycle and remain fixed for the entirety of the cycle. These GHG savings calculations should be provided to the utilities and DHCD 8 months before the plan filing submission date to align with HB 864's 8 month period for utilities and DHCD to develop their plans. This ensures consistency that the plans are modeled using the same assumptions that will later be applied to program implementation and evaluation.

#### Quick Home Energy Check-Up

Under the Quick Home Energy Check-Up ("QHEC") Program, a Building Performance Institute ("BPI")-certified technician conducts a walk-through of a customer's home and visually inspects elements of the home, including the insulation, heating and air conditioning system, lighting, and appliances. The customer receives a report summarizing potential energy-saving improvements, as well as recommendations for other BGE programs for which the customer would be a good candidate. In addition, the QHEC professional may install the following energy-saving measures as appropriate: LED light bulbs, faucet aerators, efficient-flow fixed or handheld showerheads, water heater pipe insulation, smart power strips, a ShowerStart™ showerhead adapter, door sweeps, weatherstripping, and a smart thermostat. There is no additional charge for the QHEC or the installed measures outside of the customer's EmPOWER surcharge.

#### Home Performance with ENERGY STAR®

Home Performance with ENERGY STAR® ("HPwES") is a national program overseen by the Department of Energy and guided by the standards of the BPI. The program's goal is to enhance residential comfort and energy efficiency by addressing the house as a system, while improving the health and safety of its occupants. Participating home performance contractors conduct comprehensive audits and make customized recommendations designed to improve the comfort and efficiency of the BGE customer's home. These recommendations are provided in an audit report that summarizes and prioritizes the opportunities for improvement. The customer decides which measures they want installed and contracts with the participating contractor to complete the job. The final project inspection and diagnostic testing ensure the contracted work was completed properly. The program provides a subsidy for the cost of the home energy audit (customer pays $100 for an audit valued at $400), with rebates up to $10,000 for qualifying energy efficiency improvements and up to $15,000 for projects that include electrification.

**Below Target:** In 2025, the HPwES Program completed 6,029 audits and 2,696 jobs, achieving 63,489 metric tons of lifecycle GHG savings. This equates to 76% of the program's 2025 GHG savings target. Despite falling short of the higher annual target, 2025 was the program's strongest year to date, with participation increasing 43% over 2024.

The program also saw continued growth in the installation of high‑efficiency heat pumps as part of bundled home improvement projects. In 2025, customers installed 305 air‑source heat pumps, 2 ground‑source heat pumps, and 444 heat pump water heaters. This reflects nearly three times the number of heat pumps installed during the same period in 2024 and an almost 80% increase in heat pump water heater installations.

**Moderate Income Offer:** In 2025, 59 audits and 21 jobs were completed through the moderate-income pathway under the HPwES Program. Of the 227 applications received in 2025, 88 applicants qualified as moderate-income, while 117 qualified as limited-income and were encouraged to move forward under DHCD's limited-income program. The remaining non-qualifying applicants were encouraged to move forward under the standard program offer.

**Electrification:** BGE launched the HPwES electrification offering on May 1, 2025, expanding the program to allow households to earn up to $15,000 for projects that include replacing existing fossil‑fueled space and water heating equipment with qualifying high‑efficiency heat pumps. Although introduced late in the first half of the year, the offering saw strong early participation with 133 electrification projects completed by the end of 2025.

### Commercial and Industrial Programs

The BGE commercial and industrial ("C&I") portfolio consists of a group of programs specifically designed to encompass the breadth of needs of BGE's C&I customers. This portfolio includes various programs to meet the diverse requirements of businesses of any size and sector while providing the flexibility required to accommodate new technologies and market trends. The

[PDF p. 44 | printed p. 29]

programs in the portfolio are divided into two major groups: Efficient Equipment and Building Operation and Performance. Programs in the Efficient Equipment group provide customer incentives for capital equipment installed as part of new construction or renovation that enhances a building's efficiency and reduce Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emissions. These programs include Small Business Energy Solutions (SBES), Instant Discount (Commercial Midstream), Prescriptive, and Custom. The SBES Program is a turnkey program that has been designed specifically to simplify energy efficiency for smaller customers. The Instant Discount and Prescriptive Programs provide incentives for common energy-saving measures like lighting, HVAC, and variable speed drives while the Custom Program provides incentives for complex projects that require custom engineering.

As opposed to the Efficient Equipment group, the Building Operation and Performance group concentrates on optimizing a building's performance through non-capital improvements like enhanced maintenance, building analytics, control system programming, and building operator education. These typically low-cost measures do not require capital budgeting and are generally implemented using the customer's maintenance budget. The Building Operation and Performance group includes the Building Tune-Up Program and its subprograms.

BGE designed two subprograms under the Building Tune-Up program expressly to help Maryland businesses meet Maryland's new Building Energy Performance Standards (BEPS). The first one, the Building Performance Tune-up is a turnkey service provided through preselected professional engineering firms that: 1) performs benchmarking required by BEPS regulations 2) provides an ASHRAE Level II audit of the facility to identify measures to help the business reduce Greenhouse Gas emissions and power consumption and 3) performs Retrocommissioning on the building to optimize building performance. BGE has also added a Building Owner/Property Manager Training program to specifically educate building owners and property managers on the BEPS requirements, benchmarking, energy efficiency, GHG mitigation strategies and the financial implications of these measures.

It should be noted that with the shift to GHG emissions reduction goals, the CHP program has been discontinued. While payments were made in 2025 to close out prior commitments, no savings were claimed in this year.

##### Energy Solutions for Business Program

The Energy Solutions for Business Program provides C&I customers with simple solutions to improve the energy efficiency of their facilities with the goal of producing verifiable savings from the adoption of more efficient technologies. CHP systems provide at least a portion of a facility's electrical load and capture waste heat from hot exhaust gases for use in space heating, domestic hot water, dehumidification, and/or process heating.

### Dynamic Pricing - BGE Smart Energy Rewards

Smart Energy Rewards ("SER") is a peak-time rebate program that provides customers with bill credits for reducing energy use during peak periods, known as Energy Savings Days ("ESD"). The program is available to all residential customers with a certified smart meter. Typically, the day before an event, customers receive a notification phone call, email, text, and/or mobile app alert to help them plan for adjusting their energy usage the following day. On the day of an event, customers receive a reminder notification – text, email and or/ mobile app alert. Within a few days after the ESD, BGE notifies customers by phone call, email, text, or mobile app alert to inform them how much they saved. This rapid dissemination of personalized results through the customer's preferred communications channel(s) provides customers with timely feedback on the financial impact of the behavioral changes they made during the ESD.

**On Target:** More than 1.2 million customers were eligible for the 2025 SER season. BGE activated one ESD on June 24, 2025. Results of the event included a participation rate of 63% and an average credit of $4.47 per participant, which totaled $3.2M in customer rewards (before normalizing PeakRewards customers). Combined with Connected Rewards, PeakRewards, and Smart Energy Rewards, the Energy Saving Day was able to provide 150MW of capacity reduction as compared to the PJM forecast for the day.

## APPENDIX E – MARKETING

### Residential Energy Efficiency Marketing

#### Overall

In the first half of 2025, BGE launched the "Come On In" campaign. "Come On In" invites customers into the real homes—and lives—of their neighbors, showing how BGE and the EmPOWER Maryland programs help real people take control of their energy use in ways that fit their lives. We recruited customers across the BGE territory to share their stories. Each participated in a different program: Quick Home Energy Check-up, HVAC Tune-up, Appliance Rebates, and Home Performance. The multichannel campaign is being delivered across TV, YouTube, streaming audio, online banners and more. The campaign drives to BGE's energy efficiency web pages featuring the four customers' stories, videos, and links to sign up for energy efficiency programs.

In January 2025, the website's residential program pages were translated into Spanish to increase program accessibility. As program changes and offers were updated throughout the year, the revised information was made available in English and Spanish.

In May 2025, BGE developed a magnet with five quick, easy energy-saving tips to hand out at limited- and moderate-income events. The magnet format allows customers a daily, visible reminder of the small yet impactful changes they can make to save energy.

In the second half of 2025, BGE continued running the "Come On In" campaign, which invites customers into the real homes—and lives—of their neighbors, showing how BGE and the EmPOWER Maryland programs help real people take control of their energy use in ways that fit their lives. This multichannel campaign continued to run across TV, YouTube, streaming audio, online banners and more. The campaign encourages customers to sign up for energy efficiency programs.

Also encouraging participation in BGE's residential energy efficiency programs, personalized email drip campaigns were deployed in September and December 2025. Drip campaigns allow customers to indicate where they want to learn more, and depending on their preferences, they receive information on the programs that best fit their interests. The campaign consists of a welcome email and emails for Appliance Rebates, HVAC, HVAC Tune-up, Home Performance with ENERGY STAR®, Connected Rewards and Appliance Recycling. The campaign also includes an educational email with articles on energy efficiency.

In November 2025, BGE released its fourth issue of The Efficient Home magazine. Content included interviews with a recycling associate from the Department of Public Works and a home energy contractor, apps that can help you save time and energy and low-energy recipes for every season. The articles highlight simple tips to help customers save energy and money as part of their everyday lifestyle, and the content is interspersed with ads for energy efficiency programs.

#### Appliance Recycling

BGE's Appliance Recycling Program gained momentum in the first half of 2025. Two new tactics, online banners and LinkedIn ads, launched in May to promote the multifamily recycling offering. These ads target property managers encouraging participation in bulks pickups for large refrigerators.

To boost additional participation, the program ran two limited time offers, one for large refrigerators and freezers and one for dehumidifiers. The large refrigerator and freezer limited time offer ran from March 3rd to March 31st, with an extension until April 14th, giving customers a $100 reward for recycling their large appliances. The dehumidifier limited time offer ran from May 1st to May 31st. The offer allowed for a dehumidifier to be picked up from the customer's home without a fridge or freezer and customers received a $50 reward. Emails and paid social media were deployed to market the offers.

### Notes

#### PeakRewards Energy Savings (MWh)

The as filed Annualized Energy Savings for PeakRewards was made up of two components:
