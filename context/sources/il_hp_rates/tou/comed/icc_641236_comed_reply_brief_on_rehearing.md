**REPLY BRIEF OF COMMONWEALTH EDISON COMPANY**

Commonwealth Edison Company ("ComEd") submits this Reply Brief to the Illinois Commerce Commission (the "Commission" or "ICC") under the Rules of Practice, 83 Ill. Admin. Code § 200.800, and the Administrative Law Judges' Notice of Continuance of Hearing and Notice of Schedule issued on March 31, 2025. This rehearing concerns proposals for a residential supply time of use ("STOU") rate proposal, specifically ComEd's proposed Rate BEST – Basic Electric Service Time of Use Pricing ("Rate BEST").[^1]

The Staff of the Illinois Commerce Commission ("Staff"); the Citizens Utility Board ("CUB"); the Environmental Defense Fund ("EDF" and collectively, "CUB-EDF"); the Environmental Law & Policy Center ("ELPC"); Vote Solar ("VS"); the Union of Concerned Scientists ("UCS" and collectively, "ELPC-VS-UCS"); the Illinois Competitive Energy Association ("ICEA"); Retail Energy Supply Association ("RESA" and collectively, "ICEA-RESA"); and the Coalition to Request Equitable Allocation of Costs Together ("REACT") filed initial briefs on rehearing ("RH IB"). ComEd's Reply Brief on Rehearing ("RH RB") responds to

[p. 2]

those briefs and is organized in accordance with the common outline agreed to by ComEd, Staff, and the Intervenors.

For the reasons provided below and in ComEd's Initial Brief, the Commission should consider the evidentiary record on rehearing, which supports adoption of Rate BEST, without modification.

## I. INTRODUCTION

<!-- EPON: goal,bundling — ComEd's framing of Rate BEST: "simple" supply TOU bundled with DTOU, "customer-centric", aligned with CEJA; claims support of CUB-EDF and ELPC-VS-UCS as the only parties engaging with rate design -->

The proposed Rate BEST has the support of all parties that actively engaged with the substantive design of the rate components of an STOU, specifically CUB-EDF and ELPC-VS-UCS. See CUB-EDF RH IB at 10; ELPC-VS-UCS RH IB at 5. Rate BEST is the result of the good faith and collaborative efforts of ComEd, CUB-EDF, and ELPC-VS-UCS to reach an agreed-upon rate design through numerous discussions and information exchanges. As demonstrated by the record on rehearing, the resulting Rate BEST is a simple supply time of use rate that, when bundled with the recently approved distribution time of use rate ("DTOU"), presents residential customers with an energy and bill management tool that is easy to use and understand. Rate BEST's customer-centric design will minimize participating customers' efforts while maximizing their savings opportunities. No party disputes that Rate BEST also aligns with the objectives of P.A. 102-0662 ("CEJA") and reflects the benefits that the Commission has recognized are inherent in a TOU design. As the record on rehearing reflects, ComEd's Rate BEST is a just and reasonable STOU rate and the facts and law presented on rehearing support the Commission's adoption of Rate BEST without modification.

In contrast, Staff, ICEA-RESA, and REACT did not meaningfully engage with the STOU proposal in testimony or briefs. Instead, they oppose ComEd's Rate BEST solely on various legal

[p. 3]

grounds, each of which is unfounded, and should not affect the Commission's decision on Rate BEST.

## III. LEGAL STANDARD

The Commission should review Rate BEST to determine if it is just and reasonable. 220 ILCS 5/9-201(c). This standard of review is not contested.

### A. Procedural Legal Issues

Staff, ICEA-RESA, and REACT assert that certain procedural issues raised in the initial proceeding, namely scope and Section 16-103(e), continue to bar the Commission's consideration of Rate BEST on rehearing. But their arguments rest on fundamental misunderstandings of the Commission's findings in the final Order ("Final Order") and the procedural posture of this rehearing. As ComEd explained in its Initial Brief, any procedural legal issues concerning the Commission's consideration of an STOU rate raised in the underlying proceeding have been fully addressed during this rehearing and no procedural legal issues remain outstanding that would hinder the Commission's consideration of the proposed STOU. ComEd RH IB at 7-9.

#### ComEd's Rate BEST Proposal on Rehearing Is Within the Scope of This Proceeding

ICEA-RESA and Staff assert that ComEd failed to "explicitly" seek rehearing of the Commission's decision on whether an STOU is within the scope of the present docket, and

[p. 4]

therefore any proposed STOU is out of scope on rehearing. ICEA-RESA RH IB at 4; Staff RH IB at 4-5. Assertions by ICEA-RESA and Staff that STOU proposals are not within the scope of this proceeding are flatly contradicted by the Commission's directives in this proceeding.

First, the Commission never explicitly stated that an STOU is outside the scope of this docket, so there was nothing for which ComEd had to seek rehearing. Nowhere in the Final Order does the Commission find that an STOU is out of scope for this proceeding. See generally Final Order at 19, 189-190. In fact, the Commission found that "[r]easonable anticipation of new services and tariffs in furtherance of P.A. 102-0662's objectives support more inclusive rate design proceedings."[^2] Final Order at 19. A finding that rate design proceedings have a more "inclusive" scope can be read harmoniously with the Commission's later direction on the strawman and its encouragement that ComEd explore development of "an expanded TOU tariff." Final Order at 19, 189-190. As such, ICEA-RESA and Staff's arguments—predicated on seeking reconsideration of a finding that does not actually exist—deserve no weight.

Second, the Commission's granting of ComEd's Application for Rehearing specifically articulates that the scope of rehearing is the STOU. As Staff states in its Initial Brief, the scope of rehearing is defined by the issues raised in the Application for Rehearing. Staff RH IB at 4. ComEd clearly articulated the grounds it was requesting rehearing: to propose an STOU for the Commission's consideration. ComEd Application for Rehearing at 2-3, 9-11. Chairman Scott affirmed this scope when he stated that the Commission was granting rehearing for ComEd to

[p. 5]

"present an STOU and/or bundled TOU rate proposal that is harmonized with ComEd's approve [sic] DTOU rate." Mar. 6, 2025 ROM Tr. (filed Mar. 27, 2025) at 10:9 – 11:14.

#### Section 16-103(e) Is Not a Bar to the Commission's Consideration of an STOU Rate on Rehearing

ICEA-RESA assert that because ComEd does not "actually recommend" approval of Rate BEST in testimony, Section 16-103(e) remains a barrier for Commission approval of Rate BEST. ICEA-RESA RH IB at 7. ICEA-RESA's position misconstrues Section 16-103(e) and should be rejected.

Section 16-103(e) prohibits the requiring of a utility to offer any tariffed service other than the services required by Section 16-103. 220 ILCS 5/16-103(e). It does not, and has never, prohibited the utility from agreeing to propose a new or modified service. Id. In its Final Order, the Commission urged ComEd to "consider developing and proposing an expanded TOU tariff in the near future," in addition to its requirement that ComEd produce a strawman. Final Order at 189-190. ComEd considered this recommendation and provided its Rate BEST proposal to efficiently meet both the Commission's directive for a strawman proposal, and its suggestion for an expanded TOU tariff. Id.

Section 16-103(a) also states, in relevant part, that "[n]othing in this subsection shall be construed as limiting an electric utility's right to propose, or the Commission's power to approve, allow or order modifications in the rates, terms and conditions for such services pursuant to Article IX or Section 16-111 of [the PUA]." 220 ILCS 5/16-103(a). Accordingly, Article IX of the PUA allows the Commission to approve rates that it determines to be "just and reasonable."[^3] 220 ILCS 5/9-201(c). In a prior decision that implicated Section 16-103(e), the Commission explicitly

[p. 6]

reserved the right under Section 16-103(e) to consider "future service proposals," such as Rate BEST, as they arise. Citizens Utility Board and Environmental Defense Fund, ICC Docket No. 15-0156, Order (July 18, 2015) at 12.

Whether to propose a TOU tariff has always been ComEd's decision. In the docket initially authorizing ComEd's advanced metering infrastructure ("AMI") plan, the Commission recognized that ComEd agreed to a dialogue with stakeholders about whether to propose a TOU tariff and that ComEd had the choice whether to make such a proposal. Commonwealth Edison Company, Petition for Statutory Approval of a Smart Grid Advanced Metering Infrastructure Deployment Plan pursuant to Section 16-108.6 of the Public Utilities Act, Order (June 22, 2012) at 44-45. Later, in oral argument in this rate design investigation proceeding, the Commission mentioned that TOU rates looked to be a "cost effective way" of handling increased demand on the customer side of the meter, utilizing prior investment in AMI technology. Jan. 7, 2025 Oral Argument Tr. (filed Jan. 10, 2025) at 117:21 – 119:17. ComEd responded that time-variant and dynamic pricing rates were "valuable options that need to be put forth for the customers to [sic] want to use them." Id. at 119:18 – 120:6. ICEA-RESA witness Wright even acknowledged in his rebuttal testimony in the underlying proceeding that there is not a per se bar to ComEd offering an STOU rate. See Wright Reb., ICEA-RESA Ex. 6.0 at 3:38-43 (acknowledging belief that "ComEd is not legally barred from [offering an STOU rate]") (emphasis in original).

ComEd has now voluntarily proposed Rate BEST. ComEd RH IB at 8; see generally ComEd Application for Rehearing. The Commission is thus free to consider ComEd's proposal and exercise its judgment as to whether Rate BEST should be adopted at this time. Id. In doing so, the Commission should consider the record on rehearing that demonstrates that Rate BEST is just and reasonable.

[p. 7]

Finally, to the extent that ICEA-RESA is arguing that the Commission cannot approve Rate BEST because ComEd has not "actually recommend[ed]" approval of Rate BEST in testimony, there is no basis in law or fact for such an assertion. ICEA-RESA RH IB at 7. ICEA-RESA has failed to cite to any statutory provision or legal precedent for this supposed requirement that ComEd must utter certain "magic words" in testimony for the Commission to consider the Rate BEST proposal. In fact, ComEd has consistently encouraged the Commission – in testimony and briefs – to consider the additional evidence ComEd and other parties submitted in rehearing regarding Rate BEST, which support adoption, without modification. See, e.g., ComEd RH IB, ComEd Exs. 16.0 – 19.0 and attachments.

### B. Substantive Legal Issues

#### The Integrated Distribution Company Rules Do Not Bar the Commission's Consideration of Rate BEST in this Proceeding

Staff and ICEA-RESA have raised in this rehearing the issue of a waiver of the Integrated Distribution Company Rules ("IDC" or "IDC Rules"). See Staff RH IB at 8; ICEA-RESA RH IB at 7-8. As ComEd has consistently explained during rehearing, unless and until the Commission approves Rate BEST, it is premature for ComEd to seek a waiver of the IDC Rules. See, e.g., Perkins RH Reb., ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 13:248-254. Therefore, the IDC Rules do not prevent the Commission from considering Rate BEST in this rehearing.

Utilities must follow the IDC Rules if they elect to structure their business as an IDC, rather than a Functionally Separated Utility. See Perkins RH Dir., ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 12:237-239. At a high level, an IDC is a utility that does not actively participate in the retail electric supply market and is prohibited from attempting to obtain or retain electric supply customers. See generally Ill. Admin. Code tit. 83, pt. 452, subpt. B, §§ 452.200 et seq. As a result, the IDC Rules prohibit utilities that elected to become an IDC from promoting, advertising, or marketing regarding the

[p. 8]

offering or provision of the retail sale of electricity. See Ill. Admin. Code tit. 83, pt. 452, subpt. B, §§ 452.240(a); Perkins RH Dir., ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 13:242-244. However, the Rules allow an IDC to promote programs for load curtailment and interruption if they are conducted pursuant to a Commission-approved tariff. Ill. Admin. Code tit. 83, pt. 452, subpt. B, §§ 452.230(a).

<!-- EPON: goal,midday-peak — ComEd characterizes Rate BEST as demand-response-like: customers curtail/delay usage in response to price signals across four daily periods; peak-demand reduction is the stated purpose -->

As proposed, Rate BEST allows customers to curtail or delay their energy usage in response to economic incentives (cheaper rates) or price signals (four daily periods), just like a demand response program. Perkins RH Dir., ComEd 16.0 at 13:247-251. Also like a demand response program, a well-designed TOU will ultimately help reduce peak demand over time. Id. at 13:251-254. Thus, Rate BEST is very similar to ComEd's load curtailment tariffs (e.g., Rider PTR – Peak Time Rebate) and should be considered a Permissible IDC Service. Id.; see also Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket No. 12-0484, Interim Order (Feb. 21, 2013) at 18 (ComEd not seeking waiver of IDC Rules to implement Peak Time Rebate program, but agreeing to Staff's proposal to submit marketing materials for review in Peak Time Rebate proceeding).

<!-- EPON: lockout,bundling — marketing/enrollment mechanics: direct marketing aimed at Rate BES (flat-rate bundled supply) customers; not marketed to Rate BESH or RES customers; education campaign to drive enrollment -->

ComEd plans to primarily provide direct marketing materials to Rate BES customers and will not market to customers that have elected service under Rate BESH or with a RES. Perkins RH Dir., ComEd 16.0 at 14:269-271. ComEd witness Perkins testified that a customer education and information campaign will be needed to bring awareness of this new program to the attention of customers and allow them to decide to enroll. Id. at 13:255-259. In addition, a major focus of an STOU program is to educate the customer as to the customer's unique consumption patterns, the usage characteristics of various appliances, the cost of power and energy, and the interplay of supply and demand in the energy markets. Id. at 13:259 – 14:262. This means that the customer education surrounding Rate BEST will help lay the foundation for well-informed customers to

[p. 9]

learn to exercise choice more effectively and to exert control over their energy consumption. Id. at 14:262-264. Such outreach is important because without appropriate educational materials, customers may not understand or enroll in the rate, missing out on the opportunity to save money. See Perkins RH Dir., ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 14:265-269.

If the Commission determines that the IDC Rules apply and Rate BEST is not within the category of Permissible IDC Services (related to load curtailment programs and customer education), ComEd will seek a waiver. Perkins RH Dir., ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 14:272-279. ComEd obtained a similar waiver when it implemented Rate RTOUPP – Residential Time of Use Pricing Pilot and Rider RRTP – Residential Real Time Pricing.[^4] See Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket No. 08-0411, Order (Oct. 8, 2008) at 3; Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket Nos. 18-1725/18-1824 (cons.), Order (Oct. 2, 2019) at 54-55.[^5]

#### The Commission Has Indicated that Proposals that Are Not Revenue Neutral Are Not Necessarily Barred from Consideration in this Proceeding

<!-- EPON: fixed-vs-volumetric — revenue-neutrality objection to Rate BEST (Staff, REACT) and ComEd's response that the DTOU itself was not revenue neutral; incremental administrative costs recovered via distribution revenue requirement -->

The Commission should reject Staff and REACT's assertions that the Commission cannot consider Rate BEST in this proceeding because it is not revenue neutral.[^6] See Staff RH IB at 7-8; REACT RH IB at 4-5. While the issue of the revenue neutrality of various proposals was heavily litigated in the underlying proceeding, the Commission in the Final Order ultimately concluded that certain "rate design-related issues should be addressed in this proceeding . . . [and] review of such issues is supported by consideration of: administrative efficiency, including conservation of

[p. 10]

already strained time and resources; prior directives to address specific issues in this proceeding; and [CEJA's] mandate that the impact of ComEd's tariffs and rate design be holistically considered." Final Order at 19; see also id. ("Reasonable anticipation of new services and tariffs in furtherance of [CEJA's] objectives support more inclusive rate design proceedings."); Jan. 16, 2025 ROM Tr. (filed Feb. 6, 2025) at 14:11 – 15:19 (approving the Final Order in the underlying proceeding with Commissioner Reddick noting the Commission "appropriately determined that it was efficient to consider several issues related to or affected by rate design").

Among the issues considered and approved by the Commission in the underlying proceeding that was not revenue neutral with respect to ComEd's distribution revenue requirement is the DTOU itself. See Perkins RH Reb., ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 11:217 – 12:235 ("While inherently related to rate design, the DTOU requires upgrades to ComEd's information technology and billing systems, as well as customer education, marketing, and program administration, all of which will incur incremental costs to be recovered through the distribution revenue requirement.").

<!-- EPON: bundling,goal — why ComEd sought rehearing: efficiencies of designing/implementing a bundled TOU rate with harmonized time periods and design features; Commission granted rehearing to "harmonize" STOU with DTOU -->

Cognizant of the Commission's expressed views on the issue of revenue neutrality and TOU rates in this proceeding, ComEd filed its Application for Rehearing to propose an STOU rate on rehearing. See generally Application for Rehearing. Among the reasons ComEd cited were the efficiencies to be gained from the ability to design and implement a bundled TOU rate that harmonized time periods and other design features. Id. at 7-10. The Commission, presumably with full knowledge that consideration of an STOU rate would entail incremental costs to design and implement similar to those needed to implement the DTOU, granted ComEd's Application and related Motion to Stay "for the limited purpose of allowing ComEd to present an STOU and/or bundled TOU rate proposal that is harmonized with ComEd's approved DTOU rate" and "better align the implementation of its DTOU with any STOU and/or bundled TOU that could be approved

[p. 11]

by the Commission in rehearing." Mar. 6, 2025 ROM Tr. (filed Mar. 27, 2025) at 10:23 – 11:14, 11:19-22; Notice of Commission Action (Mar. 6, 2025). Given the Commission's previously stated intention to consider rate design issues holistically and its review and approval of the DTOU, it should consider ComEd's Rate BEST in this rehearing and reject Staff and REACT's arguments regarding revenue neutrality.

## V. IMPLEMENTATION

### A. Bundling with DTOU

<!-- EPON: bundling — ComEd defends mandatory bundling of Rate BEST (STOU) with DTOU against ICEA-RESA's antitrust-tying claim (83 Ill. Adm. Code 452.270; 740 ILCS 10/3(4)) -->

The Commission should reject ICEA-RESA's argument that ComEd's proposal to bundle Rate BEST with its DTOU rate is prohibited by Section 452.270 of the Commission's Rules and the Illinois Antitrust Act ("Antitrust Act"). See ICEA-RESA RH IB at 8-9 (citing 83 Ill. Admin. Code § 452.270; 740 ILCS 10/3(4)). The plain language of Section 3 of the Antitrust Act does not apply to bundling ComEd's STOU rate with its DTOU rate.

[p. 15]

<!-- EPON: bundling — no supply-only option: to take STOU a customer must take DTOU; ComEd argues there is no competitor in distribution so no tying harm, and compares to Rate BES/BESH which also require ComEd distribution service -->

Section 3 of the Antitrust Act prohibits leasing or making a contract for the sale of services on the condition that the purchaser "shall not use or deal in the [service] _of a competitor or competitors of the lessor or seller_" where the effect of such condition "may be to substantially lessen competition or tend to create a monopoly in any line of commerce[.]" 740 ILCS 10/3(4) (emphasis added). Under ICEA-RESA's theory, the allegedly "tied" product is ComEd's DTOU rate (i.e., to take service under the STOU, a customer must take service under the DTOU). See ICEA-RESA RH IB at 8. However, there is no competitor in ComEd's service territory for electricity distribution service – ComEd is, by design and regulation, the monopoly provider of distribution service. There is thus no risk that bundling of Rate BEST with ComEd's DTOU would "substantially lessen competition" for distribution service. Furthermore, under ICEA-RESA's logic, any bundled supply rate that ComEd currently offers with its standard distribution service (e.g., Rate BES or Rate BESH) would be a violation of the Antitrust Act because customers must take distribution service from ComEd to choose those supply rates. Such a situation would be absurd.[^7]

Staff's argument that "ComEd [should] not be allowed to offer an STOU rate at least until ARES have the information that they need to develop supply TOU rates" is moot. Staff RH IB at 10. ComEd has previously acknowledged Staff's concerns and proposes to implement Rate BEST beginning in June 2026 – a full year after RESs are slated to begin receiving ComEd customers'

interval usage meter data under Rider IMUD – Interval Meter Usage Data. This will provide sufficient time for RESs to develop their own STOU products. See Perkins RH Dir., ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 6:113 – 7:119; Perkins RH Reb., ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 14:269-278.

### C. Rate BEST and Other Tariffs

### D. Retail Market Competition

<!-- EPON: bundling,goal — ICEA-RESA/Staff argue Rate BEST harms retail competition; ComEd says no record evidence and CEJA contemplates utility STOU rates; Wright quote: supply options between fully unbundled (BESH) and fully bundled (BES) for "niche customer preferences" should be left to the competitive market -->

ICEA-RESA and Staff argue that Rate BEST would adversely affect retail market competition in Illinois. See ICEA-RESA RH IB at 9-10; Staff RH IB at 10. These arguments lack any factual support in the record. They also contradict CEJA, which directly contemplates regulated utilities offering STOU rates.

Neither Staff nor ICEA-RESA provide any factual support demonstrating Rate BEST's hypothetical effect on the retail market. Instead, they merely gesture toward "concerns" with ComEd offering any additional supply rate and generally assert that ComEd should never offer an STOU rate. See Capellan Dir., Staff Ex. 8.0 at 3:64 – 4:65 (Staff witness Capellan testifying that "allowing ComEd to offer a supply TOU rate would be in direct conflict with the Commission's mandate to promote retail electric competition in Illinois") (emphasis added); Wright RH Dir.,

[p. 17]

ICEA-RESA Ex. 1.0RH at 3:58-61 ("I have a fundamental problem with ComEd's offering a rate such as Rate BEST. _Developing supply options other than completely unbundled (Rate BESH) or completely bundled (Rate BES) to meet specific or niche customer preferences should be left to the competitive retail marketplace_") (emphasis added). But mere opinion does not constitute record evidence. See, e.g., _Gray v. Carlton Midway Corp._, 2023 IL App (1st) 221636-U, ¶ 23 (affirming trial court's decision to disregard expert testimony when affidavit "contained only conclusory opinions that were unsupported by any specific facts" without evidence or analysis).

In contrast, there is evidence demonstrating that efforts are underway to enable ARES to provide their own supply TOU. See Perkins RH Dir., ComEd Ex. 16.0 at 6:110 – 7:119 (explaining that interval meter usage data will be available for RESs in June 2025); Perkins RH Reb., ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 14:269-27 [sic; possibly 14:269-278] (explaining that RESs will have access to interval meter usage data for 12 months before Rate BEST takes effect and will provide RESs sufficient time to design and offer TOU products). As Staff witness Capellan recognized, "we're looking at a completely different landscape in 2026" after Rider IMUD takes effect in June 2025. Oct. 10, 2024 Hr'g Tr. (filed Oct. 22, 2024) at 202:24-25. Furthermore, ComEd submitted annual reports regarding its Rate RTOUPP learnings that RESs can use to develop STOU products they intend to offer in the future. See generally Docket Nos. 18-1725/18-1824 (cons.).

<!-- EPON: goal — ComEd's evidence of customer demand/policy interest: CEJA contemplates utility STOU rates to lower costs and shift usage so peak demand is lowered; RTOUPP averaged ~1,800 participants vs 1,900 cap; >90% retention in other jurisdictions' TOU pilots -->

ICEA-RESA's claim (ICEA-RESA RH IB at 10) that ComEd has not established that there is a demand for an STOU should also be rejected. There is a demonstrated public policy and customer interest in an STOU. CEJA contemplates electric utilities offering STOU rates as a means of helping customers lower their energy costs and shift usage so that peak demand is lowered. See ComEd IB at 11-12 (citing statutory provisions contemplating utility STOU rates). The Commission has also expressed interest in STOU rates being offered in Illinois. See e.g.,

[p. 18]

Final Order at 189 ("The Commission agrees that there are many benefits to an STOU."); Jan. 7, 2025 Oral Argument Tr. (filed Jan. 10, 2025) at 117:19-25 (Commissioner Reddick noting that "the time of use rate can be effective in reducing peak demand and this looks like a very cost effective way of solving a very expensive problem"). Record evidence established as far back as intervenor direct testimony in the initial phase of the proceeding demonstrates there is a customer demand for an STOU rate. See McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 11:195 (referring to ComEd's Rate RTOUPP, an STOU pilot program that averaged 1,800 participants out of the Commission-approved cap of 1,900 participants, to establish that there is a customer demand for an STOU rate). Additionally, CUB-EDF witness McCann testified regarding TOU pilot programs in other jurisdictions in which greater than 90% of customers elected to remain on TOU rates after participating in pilot programs, which provides additional support for the proposition that TOU rates are popular among utility customers. See McCann RH Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 4.0 CORR at 5:83-90.

Finally, ICEA-RESA's concern (ICEA-RESA RH IB at 10) regarding Staff's undocketed investigation into ComEd's allocation of delivery and supply costs has no bearing on the Commission's consideration of Rate BEST in this rehearing. ComEd identified the categories of costs it expects to incur in developing and implementing Rate Best (see Perkins RH Reb., ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 9:168-181), and each of those categories is the same as the categories ComEd incurs in developing and administering its other supply rates. Staff is required to provide its informal investigation report in mid-January 2026 for the Commission's consideration. Final Order at 35. Any Staff recommendations subsequently adopted by the Commission could be directly applied to Rate BEST costs at that time. Therefore, there is no need to delay consideration of Rate BEST now to await those findings.

## VI. COST RECOVERY

<!-- EPON: fixed-vs-volumetric — administrative costs (IT, implementation, education/marketing, program admin) of the bundled TOU recovered through distribution revenue requirement, like DTOU admin costs; ComEd rejects REACT's proposal to charge residential supply customers only -->

ComEd has proposed to recover its administrative costs (which include IT, implementation, customer education and marketing, and program administration costs) in the same manner as it will recover the DTOU administrative costs, which is through ComEd's distribution revenue requirements. See Perkins RH Reb., ComEd Ex. 18.0 at 9:168-181. Such a recovery methodology is appropriate because the bundled TOU rate would be available to all residential customers and there are inherent synergies and efficiencies to developing and marketing a bundled TOU rate – which is expected to reduce overall project costs. See id. at 10:185-190.

<!-- EPON: bundling,goal — because STOU and DTOU are bundled, ComEd says separate admin-cost allocation is imprecise; cites statewide goals of load shifting, load reduction, clean energy and low-income assistance as justification for socializing program costs -->

In contrast, REACT's proposal whereby Rate BEST administrative costs would be recovered through solely from residential supply customers is inappropriate. See REACT RH IB at 8-14. Because the STOU and DTOU rates are bundled, an allocation of the administrative costs would be imprecise, unnecessarily complicated, and unwarranted. Id. at 10:194-196. Furthermore, it is not always practical to create many different allocators or cost tracking mechanisms to assign costs with that level of precision when some of the rates/programs have the benefit of supporting larger statewide goals like load shifting, load reduction, clean energy, and assistance for low-income customers.

REACT also takes issue with ComEd's proposed allocation of Rate BEST administrative costs across all customers in distribution rates, rather than just being allocated to residential customers. See REACT RH IB at 8-10. But ComEd's proposed allocation across all customers in distribution rates is consistent with how it recovers administrative costs in similar situations. ComEd currently offers several Commission-approved rates and riders for which it recovers administrative costs from its overall distribution customers, even if some of those rates and riders are not available to all customer classes. See REACT RH Cross Ex. 2.0 (ComEd Response to REACT Data Request 10.02(c) (providing examples of riders only available to residential

[p. 20]

customers, including Rider LID – Low-Income Discount and Rider RRTP – Residential Real Time Pricing Program, that recover administrative costs from all customers in distribution rates).

REACT also raises the specter of additional indirect costs because of hypothetical increased call volumes to ComEd's Customer Call Center to inquire about Rate BEST. See REACT RH IB at 11-14. REACT has not provided any evidence that such an increase in call volumes is likely to occur, or that additional indirect costs will result. In fact, the record provides that ComEd does not anticipate that there will be materially higher call volumes directed to the Customer Call Center directly related to Rate BEST. REACT RH Cross Ex. 5.0 (ComEd Response to REACT Data Request 10.06). The reasons for this include:

<!-- EPON: lockout — enrollment mechanics: Rate BEST enrollment expected to be online, not through the Customer Call Center -->

1. ComEd anticipates Rate BEST enrollment to be conducted online, not through the Customer Call Center;
2. the DTOU rate will have already been introduced in early 2026, so initial customer questions related to TOU rates will likely already have been asked; and
3. any calls received after the bundled TOU rate has been introduced will likely be about the TOU bundle, and not limited only to the Rate BEST STOU rate.

Id.

REACT reiterates various of its arguments related to Customer Call Center allocation that the Commission did not adopt in the underlying proceeding and regarding which Staff is currently conducting an undocketed investigation stemming from a directive in the Final Order. Final Order at 43. To the extent REACT believes that substantive questions regarding ComEd's allocation of costs between delivery and supply is currently inadequate, the better venue to raise those questions is Staff's informal investigation.
