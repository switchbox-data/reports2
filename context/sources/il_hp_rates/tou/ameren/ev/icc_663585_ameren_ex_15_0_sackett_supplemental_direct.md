## II. CRGA CONTEXT FOR BENEFICIAL ELECTRIFICATION

**Q.** Are you familiar with CRGA?

**A.** Yes. While I am not a lawyer, I generally understand it as comprehensive legislation that focuses on the modernization of the electric grid by introducing new requirements for beneficial electrification, energy efficiency, virtual power plants, distributed energy resources, customer data, and system access, while emphasizing affordability, reliability, and customer benefits.

**Q.** How does the passing of CRGA impact the Company's beneficial electrification programs?

**A.** CRGA revises the statutory framework governing beneficial electrification in Illinois and modifies the regulatory structure under which such programs are developed and implemented. Prior to the enactment of CRGA, the Company’s Beneficial Electrification Plans were filed and administered pursuant to the Public Utilities Act (PUA) and the Illinois Electric Vehicle Act (EV Act), developed outside of the 2028 Grid Plan.[^1] CRGA implemented updates to Section 45 governing beneficial electrification that require electric utilities to propose and include beneficial electrification programs as part of its Grid Plan rather than as a standalone BE Plan. These updates also impact certain BE-related responsibilities assigned to the Illinois Environmental Protection Agency beginning in 2029. Consistent with these statutory changes, Ameren Illinois will no longer be subject to the requirements of the EV Act and will instead comply with the updated beneficial electrification provisions established under CRGA.

[p. 4, lines 67–86] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

[^1]: The Company’s initial BE Plan filing under the Electric Vehicle Act (EV Act), separate from this Grid Plan, was approved by the Commission on March 23, 2023. (Ameren Illinois Beneficial Electric Plan 1, Docket 22-0431 / 22- 0443). The Company’s second BE Plan filing, also separate from this Grid Plan, was filed on July 1, 2024, and approved by the Commission on March 27, 2025. (Ameren Illinois Beneficial Electric Plan 2, Docket 24-0494).

**Q.** What is the Company's approach for addressing the updated BE requirements from CRGA?

Ameren Illinois will transition from BE Plan 2, which covers the program years of 2026 to 2028, to a portfolio of BE investments that will be implemented from 2029 to 2031 as part of the Company's Grid Plan. The Company's 2029-2031 BE Portfolio plans to meet the statutory requirements through proposed offerings and investments in this Updated Grid Plan that are targeted at transportation electrification.

**Q.** How will beneficial electrification be integrated into the 2028 Grid Plan?

**A.** Beneficial Electrification is incorporated into the Company’s Grid Plan and evaluated within the investment framework applicable to Grid Plan initiatives. The Company’s proposed beneficial electrification programs for 2029-2031 align with Category 4 Investments, as they are driven by policy and must provide positive net benefits. As a Category 4 investment, the BE Portfolio is subject to the same planning, analytical, and reporting requirements applicable to other Grid Plan components. This includes integration into the Company’s broader Grid Plan processes, cost-effectiveness and performance analysis, and ongoing reporting and evaluation. New BE offerings will be developed, proposed, and administered in accordance with the Grid Plan structure, ensuring consistency in evaluation, stakeholder transparency, and regulatory oversight established through the Grid Plan Pilot Framework. In addition, any new beneficial electrification pilot initiatives that would have previously been undertaken outside the Grid Plan construct will now transition into the Grid Plan framework and Pilot funding mechanisms. The Pilot Framework is detailed in Section 15 of the Grid Plan (Ameren Ex. 9.1).

[p. 5, lines 87–109] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

**Q.** Please provide more detail on how reporting for beneficial electrification will be addressed in the 2028 Grid Plan.

All former reporting requirements from the EV Act will be suspended, and Ameren Illinois will no longer be filing separate BE Reports. Instead, all reporting for beneficial electrification will occur through the Company's Consolidated Annual Grid Plan Implementation Report. In that report, Ameren Illinois will report on key metrics related to beneficial electrification such as expenditures, participation, and net benefit results.

**Q.** Are there aspects of the beneficial electrification programs proposed for 2029-2031 that make them unique compared to other Category 4 Investments?

Yes, there are nuances between the 2029-2031 BE Portfolio and other investments in the Grid Plan. The BE investments are customer-facing programs where spending is associated with delivering the programs' benefits to customers. This means that the 2029-2031 BE Portfolio includes budget set aside for delivering the programs' benefits, where expenditures are largely driven by customer participation as opposed to internal Company initiatives that drive other grid plan investments.

Yes. CRGA includes specific programmatic requirements for BE programming from 2029 onward.[^2] These requirements include:

[^2]: 20 ILCS 627/45.j.

(i) Make-ready investments and other programs to facilitate the rapid deployment of

> charging equipment throughout this State, especially deployment that targets medium-duty and heavy-duty vehicle electrification and multi-unit buildings;

> electric vehicle users and for all customers, (2) optimized charging programs to achieve identified savings, and (3) new contracts and compensation for services in the optimized charging programs, through signals that allow electric vehicle charging to respond to local system conditions, manage critical peak periods, serve as a demand response or peak resource, and maximize renewable energy use and integration into the grid; and

(iii) Commercial tariffs utilizing alternatives to traditional demand-based rate

> structures that facilitate charging for light-duty, heavy-duty, and fleet electric vehicles.

As a result of the statutory changes and the shift in certain program responsibilities, the Company will implement a streamlined portfolio of programs within its control that is designed to maximize benefits for customers while advancing the objectives established by CRGA.

## III. 2029-2031 BENEFICIAL ELECTRIFICATION OVERVIEW

**Q.** What did Ameren Illinois consider as it developed its proposed 2029-2031 beneficial electrification programs?

Ameren Illinois relied on several key considerations in developing its proposed 2029– 2031 BE programs, including statutory compliance with CRGA, implementation experience and lessons learned from prior BE plans, and the results of the net benefits analysis. First and foremost, the Company’s portfolio development was guided by the requirements established in CRGA. The statutory provisions informed which program types the Company prioritized in order to develop a compliant portfolio. In evaluating compliance, the Company considered the structure and offerings approved in Beneficial Electrification Plan 2 and assessed which programs best align with the updated statutory requirements for beneficial electrification in CRGA. Later in this testimony, I provide additional detail regarding how the proposed portfolio satisfies the applicable statutory compliance elements.

The Company also incorporated lessons learned from the implementation of BE Plan 1 and the implementation planning process for BE Plan 2. As part of preparing for BE Plan 2, the Company conducted a Request for Proposals (RFP) process to identify third party vendors to implement the programs. Through that process, the BE team worked to refine the implementation details of each BE Plan 2 program for purposes of communicating the program needs in the RFPs. In the review, the Company gathered valuable market information, implementation insights, and program design considerations that informed development of the proposed 2029–2031 BE programs.

Finally, the Company evaluated the portfolio using a net benefits analysis. Multiple portfolio configurations were assessed to evaluate customer benefits. The resulting proposed portfolio was selected because it provides positive net benefits.

[p. 10, lines 191–213]

**Q.** What specific insights were gathered from BE Plan 1 and BE Plan 2 that aided in the selection of beneficial electrification programs for 2029-2031?

Ameren Illinois incorporated the following insights into the development of the 2029-2031 BE Portfolio:

1. Attribution Feedback – Feedback received from intervenors during the BE Plan 1

> and 2 proceedings led the Company to reassess the costs and benefits of beneficial electrification, including cost-effectiveness modeling. Certain benefits associated with the removal of the gas powered internal combustion engine vehicles such as the avoided whole fossil fuel costs or avoided CO2 emissions from fossil fuel reductions were included in the benefit cost analysis methodology for BE Plan 1 and 2. But stakeholders expressed concern regarding whether the Company can attribute certain benefits and costs to the BE plan's influence on a customer's decision to purchase an electric vehicle.

2. Pilot Program Learnings – The Managed Charging Pilot was concluded after the

> BE Plan 2 filing. Ameren Illinois has translated the learnings from the pilot implementation into the design of the FlexCharge program. These learnings included program design, program cost, and participation volume. For example, in program design, there is greater customer value through optimized charging times and minimizing secondary peaks at the start of preferred charging periods.

Yes. The Ameren Illinois BE team was fully integrated into the Grid Plan planning process. **Q.** In addition to the two BE programs being proposed, what is Ameren Illinois's approach to support EV make-ready infrastructure cost reduction outlined in 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i)?

The CRGA requirement to support reductions in customer EV make-ready infrastructure costs is met through Ameren Illinois' long-standing standard line extension policy, which is included in the Standards and Qualifications for Electric Services and was similarly relied upon in BE Plans 1 and 2. As this policy provides reduced upfront utility-side make ready costs, it fully meets the requirements of CRGA, and a separate program is not required.

**Q.** When you refer to "make-ready" in this testimony, what specifically are you referring to?

In this testimony, unless specifically stated, I am referring to utility-side make-ready infrastructure; that is, the distribution system facilities, service extensions, and related equipment to serve electric vehicle charging load up to the point of interconnection with the customer's facilities.

**Q.** What is Ameren Illinois' long-standing standard line extension policy, referenced above, and how does it satisfy the make-ready requirement in CRGA?

Under this policy, as set forth in the Company's Standards and Qualifications for Electric Service, upfront utility side make-ready costs are reduced or fully offset through the application of a revenue-based allowance tied to the newly connected electric vehicle load.

In BE Plan 1, this revenue test was applied first to utility-side EV make-ready infrastructure. Where the revenue test did not fully cover those utility-side costs, BE Plan 1 incentives were available to address the remaining utility-side make-ready costs. Only after utility-side costs were fully covered could any remaining incentive amounts be applied to customer-side make-ready infrastructure. In practice, the revenue test covered all utility-side EV make-ready costs, which resulted in the full incentive amount being applied to customer-side infrastructure. This same framework continues to apply under BE Plan 2. The Company's reliance on the line extension policy in this plan reflects a continuation of that established framework while maintaining a clear distinction between utility-owned and customer-owned infrastructure.

[p. 13, lines 247–268] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

**Q.** Why does Ameren Illinois not propose customer-side make-ready incentives for 2029-2031?

While customer-side make-ready incentives were available under BE Plans 1 and 2, changes to the cost-effectiveness framework for the proposed 2029–2031 BE program result in these programs no longer having claimable benefits for purposes of the net benefits analysis. As discussed earlier in this testimony, attribution-related feedback led to the removal of key benefit streams that had previously supported the cost-effectiveness of the make-ready programs. Absent these benefits, the make-ready programs would retain substantial administrative and incentive costs without corresponding measurable benefits, causing the customer make-ready work to not be cost effective.

**Q.** Are there any other reasons for this approach?

Yes. Under CRGA, Ameren Illinois is required to prioritize cost-effectiveness and rate payer impact minimization. The Company's line extension policy appropriately balances these priorities in CRGA by preserving appropriate cost responsibility and aligning with core utility functions, while furthering the statute's clean energy goals and directly reducing upfront make-ready costs.

**Q.** Please explain the basis for the changes outlined in the chart above.

## IV. COMPLIANCE

**Q.** Has Ameren Illinois complied with the requirements of CRGA?

Yes, Ameren Illinois has developed a portfolio that meets all of the beneficial electrification requirements in CRGA. Below I discuss the specific elements of compliance.

**Q.** Please discuss how the Company is addressing 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(i): "make-ready investments to facilitate the rapid deployment of charging equipment throughout this State, especially deployment that targets medium-duty and heavy-duty vehicle electrification and multi-unit buildings."

Ameren Illinois integrates utility-side make-ready infrastructure as a standard component of its utility operations with its existing line extension policy. This streamlines the EV charging deployment process and significantly reduces installation costs for charging stations at typical sites while establishing a reliable foundation for both private and public investments.

Additionally, Ameren Illinois' approach specifically addresses medium-duty and heavy-duty vehicle electrification and multi-unit buildings through education and outreach while providing these customers with reduced upfront cost of line and/or service extensions. While the specific education and outreach efforts have not been developed, they will build upon the efforts highlighted in the BE annual reports. This strategic approach accelerates the development of Illinois' charging network, directly supporting the state's ambitious goal of reaching 1 million electric vehicles by 2030.

**Q.** Please discuss how the proposed beneficial electrification programs addresses 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(ii): "the development and implementation of (1) time-of-use rates and their benefit for electric vehicle users and for all customers, (2) optimized charging programs to achieve identified savings, and (3) new contracts and compensation for services in the optimized charging programs, through signals that allow electric vehicle charging to respond to local system conditions, manage critical peak periods, serve as a demand response or peak resource, and maximize renewable energy use and integration into the grid."

[p. 20, lines 360–382] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

The ChargeSmart program encourages residential and small non-residential customers to charge at times beneficial to the grid with a Preferred Charging Period Delivery Credit for usage occurring between 11 PM and 7AM. ChargeSmart also discourages customers from charging at times less beneficial to the grid by assessing a Peak Hourly Delivery Charge during the Non-Preferred Charging Period between 11 AM and 7PM. Large non-residential customers participating in ChargeSmart will not be billed their maximum demand during off-peak periods. ChargeSmart's encouragement of EV charging in off-peak periods supports greater use of renewable resources during peak production and renewable resource integration into the grid. To further support customers and address vehicle charging, the Company has included a Managed Charging Program (FlexCharge). FlexCharge is an optimized charging program to manage peak load periods and shift EV charging to times beneficial to the grid. FlexCharge is designed to provide residential customers with incentives for enrolling, and ongoing participation, in the load management program.

**Q.** Please discuss how the proposed beneficial electrification programs address 220 ILCS 5/16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii): "commercial tariffs utilizing alternatives to traditional demand-based rate structures that facilitate charging for light-duty, heavy-duty, and fleet electric vehicles."

The ChargeSmart program offers qualifying non-residential light-duty, heavy-duty, and fleet electric vehicles customers flexible alternatives to traditional demand-based rate structures through its optional commercial tariff. These customer-focused alternatives are tailored to different facility types and include off-peak demand relief, where maximum demand charges are waived during off-peak periods, and a rate limiter with a specialized rate structure designed to support cost-effective public charging for light-duty, heavy-duty, and fleet electric vehicles. This approach enables businesses to optimize their EV charging operations while managing electricity costs more effectively. For smaller fleets or light duty fleets, ChargeSmart offers bill incentives to facilitate charging, and delivery credits to facilitate charging during Preferred Charging Periods.
