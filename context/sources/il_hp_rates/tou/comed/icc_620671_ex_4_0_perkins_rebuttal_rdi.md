### B. Purpose of Rebuttal Testimony

**Q.** What is the purpose of your rebuttal testimony?

**A.** I address the scope of this rate design investigation ("RDI") and identify the issues raised by intervenors that are outside the scope of a revenue-neutral RDI. These issues are the subject of ComEd's Motion to Strike filed on August 12, 2024. Of these issues, I substantively address issues related to Rider SPWS REBATE – Solar Paired with Storage Rebate ("Rider SPWS") and certain aspects of ComEd's proposed distribution Time of Use rate ("TOU") offering. The remaining issues that are outside the scope of this proceeding are addressed in the rebuttal testimony of other ComEd witnesses that I introduce below. I'll note that the silence of myself, or other ComEd witnesses, on any other issues raised in Staff and Intervenor direct testimony should not be interpreted as agreement with that testimony.

### C. Summary of Recommendations

**Q.** What are the principal recommendations of your rebuttal testimony?

<!-- EPON: bundling,goal — ComEd's headline position in the initial phase: reject CUB-EDF's recommendation that ComEd offer a companion supply TOU; "a supply TOU offering is not appropriate to include in this proceeding" -->

**A.** The Commission should address in this RDI proceeding "revenue neutral tariff changes related to rate design." 220 ILCS 5/16-105.5(c). Issues outside that scope should be addressed, if the Commission decides to address them formally, outside of this proceeding. Should the Commission consider these issues here, it should:

1. reject the Coalition to Request Equitable Allocation of Costs Together ("REACT") witness Marhoefer's proposal and find that the currently approved methodology for calculating incremental costs under the Rider SPWS Rebate remains just and reasonable;
2. reject Citizens Utility Board ("CUB") and Environmental Defense Fund ("EDF") witness Richard McCann's proposal to deduct projected cost savings from reduced peak period use in allocating revenue requirements to the distribution time of use ("DTOU") rate; and
3. reject CUB-EDF witness McCann's recommendation for ComEd to offer a companion supply Time of Use ("TOU") and find that a supply TOU offering is not appropriate to include in this proceeding.

## IV. THE COMMISSION SHOULD REJECT REACT WITNESS MARHOEFER'S PROPOSALS REGARDING THE RIDER SPWS REBATE.

**Q.** What is the purpose of Rider SPWS?

**A.** The purpose of Rider SPWS is to implement paragraph (b-5) of Section 16-107.6 of the Act, which requires ComEd to provide compensation, via a rebate, to customers who install photovoltaic facilities paired with energy storage facilities ("ESS," or, more commonly, a battery) on or adjacent to their premises for the purpose of participating in the PJM frequency regulation market.[^4] Rider SPWS sets forth the methodology for the calculation of the amount of a rebate and the conditions under which such rebate is provided. Rider SPWS, ILL. C. C. No. 10, 5th Revised Sheet No. 306.

**Q.** What rebates does Rider SPWS provide?

**A.** Rider SPWS provides the following rebates to energy storage resources paired with solar photovoltaic facilities: (i) a Qualifying Costs of Installing Interconnection Facilities and Related Infrastructure ("QCIIFRI") Rebate and (ii) a Wholesale Demand Charge Rebate.

**Q.** When was Rider SPWS established?

**A.** ComEd's Rider SPWS was reviewed and approved by the Commission in Docket No. 21-0812. See Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket No. 21-0812, Final Order, (Feb. 3, 2022) ("SPWS Final Order") and Order on Rehearing (Aug. 11, 2022) ("SPWS Order on Rehearing"). The terms and conditions of Rider SPWS, as well the benefits of solar paired with storage and the methodology for calculating the value of the QCIIFRI and Wholesale

[p. 13]

Demand Charge Rebates, were fully litigated in that proceeding. Indeed, one of REACT's members that has intervened in this RDI proceeding, Magid Glove, was the only active party other than ComEd and Staff in that Rider SPWS proceeding, and the same REACT witness Marhoefer who is offering the testimony at issue in this proceeding offered testimony on behalf of Magid Glove in that Rider SPWS proceeding.

In the SPWS Final Order, the Commission noted that the statute did not intend for all interconnection costs to be rebated to customers that install solar paired with storage. See SPWS Final Order at 18. It further recognized that no information was provided to show "how infrastructure costs differ between an installation that does or does not participate in the frequency regulation market" but still allowed the recovery of these incremental costs. Id. The Commission then granted rehearing to (a) allow parties to provide evidence regarding how infrastructure costs differ between an installation that does or does not participate in the frequency regulation market, and (b) clarify which interconnection costs constitute the QCIIFRI Rebate. Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket No. 21-0812, Notice of Commission Action (Mar. 18, 2022) at 1. Ultimately, on rehearing, the ICC approved Staff's proposed methodology for calculating the QCIIFRI Rebate. SPWS Order on Rehearing at 25.

**Q.** What does REACT witness Marhoefer recommend regarding Rider SPWS?

**A.** REACT witness Marhoefer asserts that the differential approach approved by the Commission in the SPWS Order on Rehearing and used by ComEd to calculate the value of the QCIIFRI Rebate does not accurately reflect the incremental interconnection costs associated with a customer's participation in the frequency regulation market, i.e., the cost

[p. 14]

difference between a facility that participates in that market and one that does not participate in that market. Marhoefer Dir., REACT Ex. 3.0 at 10:214-221. He argues that batteries inject power to the distribution grid, resulting in a negative peak load contribution ("PLC") which in turn provides a credit on a transmission charge for ComEd customers. As a result of this implied benefit, he proposes that the incremental interconnection costs should be determined by: (1) calculating the interconnection costs of solar only; (2) calculating what the interconnection costs would be for facilities with solar plus storage; and (3) calculating the difference between these costs. Id. at 12:256-259. He therefore recommends that the Commission "at a minimum" direct ComEd to revise its implementation of the Rider SPWS Rebate in order to "properly compensate" customers who are participating in the PJM frequency regulation market. Id. at 5:95-98.

**Q.** Are REACT witness Marhoefer's recommendations to modify Rider SPSW [sic; Rider SPWS] appropriate?

**A.** No, REACT witness Marhoefer's recommendations are contrary to the Commission-approved calculation of incremental costs under the Rider SPWS rebate. In the Rehearing Order in ICC Docket No. 21-0812, the Commission ordered ComEd to calculate incremental interconnection costs by: (1) calculating what interconnection costs would be for an ESS assuming it would not participate in the PJM frequency regulation market; (2) calculating what the interconnection costs would be for the same ESS assuming it intends to fully participate in the PJM frequency regulation market; and (3) subtracting the non-frequency regulation costs from the frequency regulation costs. SPWS Order on Rehearing at 25. Thus, REACT witness Marhoefer's proposed method to calculate incremental

[p. 15]

interconnection costs only accounts for the ESS in one portion of the calculation – the interconnection costs for a battery that is participating in the PJM frequency regulation market. It completely excludes the ESS from the non-frequency regulation cost calculation and ignores any contribution of the ESS to potential adverse system impacts in that scenario. The Commission's Order in ICC Docket No. 21-0812 explicitly requires the same ESS to be used in both portions of the calculation. Id.

**Q.** Are there additional problems with REACT witness Marhoefer's recommendations?

**A.** Yes. First, the Commission's Final Order on Rehearing in the Rider SPWS docket requires the ESS to be included in both portions of the incremental interconnection cost calculation. Specifically, ComEd is required "to calculate incremental interconnection costs by: (1) calculating what interconnection costs would be for an Eligible Energy Storage Facility assuming it would not participate in the PJM frequency regulation market; (2) calculating what the interconnection costs would be for the same Eligible Energy Storage Facility assuming it intends to fully participate in the PJM frequency regulation market; and (3) subtracting the non-frequency regulation costs from the frequency regulation costs." Id. (emphasis added). Further, by excluding the ESS from the non-frequency regulation portion, of the incremental interconnection cost calculation, REACT witness Marhoefer's recommended approach to calculating the incremental interconnection costs incorrectly assumes that, in every use case except for frequency regulation, an ESS paired with solar will have no adverse system impact beyond the impacts of the solar itself. This simply is not technically correct. ESS use cases are much broader than the handful cited in REACT witness Marhoefer's testimony; moreover, additional use cases may surface through the

[p. 16]

Commission's investigation of Value of DER Compensation and the Single Source Opportunities rulemaking. Because the use cases for ESS are so broad, it is inappropriate to utilize a single scenario for the non-frequency regulation portion of the calculation that, conveniently, assumes no adverse system impacts from the ESS. In order to address the broad set of non-frequency regulation ESS use cases, the calculation of interconnection costs without frequency regulation must be based on the full technical and operational capabilities of the ESS to determine the adverse system impacts and corresponding interconnection costs.

## V. THE COMMISSION SHOULD REJECT CUB-EDF WITNESS MCCANN'S PROPOSAL REGARDING COMED'S PROPOSED DTOU

**Q.** What does CUB-EDF witness McCann assert regarding the potential cost saving from customer participation in ComEd's DTOU?

<!-- EPON: goal,midday-peak — McCann's premise that any DTOU participation yields system-wide savings via deferred distribution capital and reduced peak-hour energy purchases (and that this reduces revenue requirement) -->

**A.** CUB-EDF witness McCann believes that any level of customer participation in DTOUs creates system-wide savings for all customers through deferred capital investments in distribution and reduced energy purchase costs during peak hours. McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex.1.0 at 20:322-324. He believes that ComEd has not accounted for these alleged savings and argues that not accounting for these alleged savings results in an inflated revenue requirement, which will result in excess revenues for ComEd. Id. at 20:328-331. Therefore, CUB-EDF witness McCann proposes a deduction to ComEd's Commission-approved MYRP revenue requirement commensurate with those alleged savings. Id. at 25:409 - 411

[p. 17]

**Q.** Is CUB-EDF witness McCann's proposal to reduce ComEd's distribution revenue requirement as a result of DTOU reasonable?

<!-- EPON: goal,fixed-vs-volumetric — ComEd position: system benefits of DTOU are "entirely speculative"; ComEd has no data on uptake or customer behavior under DTOU; any benefit flows through annual reconciliation -->

**A.** No. While ComEd hopes that time-varying rates will yield benefits for the customers who choose them, whether ComEd's DTOU program will have the effect of reducing or deferring future distribution system investments is entirely speculative. ComEd has no data on uptake rates and no data yet on whether customers taking service under a DTOU will behave in a manner that yields the type of benefits CUB-EDF witness McCann notes. Accordingly, it is not appropriate to adjust ComEd's distribution investment plan and revenue requirements associated therewith because any potential benefit to the grid of DTOU is entirely too speculative at this juncture.

In addition, CUB-EDF witness McCann's argument seems to suggest that if the forecasted revenue requirements are not adjusted, customers will not realize the benefits of any avoided or deferred investments, resulting in a windfall of excess revenue for ComEd. Id. at 20: 329 - 333. That is simply not the case. To the extent there are any avoided or deferred investments resulting from DTOU or for any other reason, the revenue requirement benefits related thereto will be realized for customers through ComEd's annual distribution rate reconciliation processes, which ensures ComEd customers pay not a penny more than ComEd's actual prudent and reasonable costs.

[p. 18]

## VI. THE COMMISSION SHOULD REJECT CUB-EDF'S ATTEMPT TO FORCE COMED TO OFFER A SUPPLY TOU

**Q.** How does CUB-EDF witness McCann rationalize his recommendation that ComEd offer a supply TOU?

<!-- EPON: bundling — CUB-EDF's initial-phase proposal: ComEd offers a supply TOU alongside the DTOU so that customers enrolling in the supply TOU are automatically enrolled in the DTOU (origin of the bundled STOU+DTOU concept) -->

**A.** CUB-EDF witness McCann asserts that since ComEd offers several time variant rates that include supply costs, it is only "intuitive" for ComEd to provide supply time variant rates together with its proposed distribution time variant rates. CUB-EDF witness McCann recommends the Commission order ComEd to offer a supply TOU alongside its proposed DTOU to enable customers who enroll in the supply TOU be automatically enrolled in the DTOU.[^5]

**Q.** Has ComEd proposed a supply TOU in this proceeding?

<!-- EPON: bundling,profile,goal — ComEd has not proposed a supply TOU here; cites Rate RTOUPP pilot (June 2020–June 2024, capped at 1,900 participants at any time) as the foundation for future supply-TOU discussions; reserves Section 16-103(e) rights but is "open to engage and collaborate with stakeholders outside this proceeding" -->

**A.** No. I note, however, that ComEd offered a pilot residential TOU supply rate under its Rate RTOUPP – Residential Time of Use Pricing Pilot ("Rate RTOUPP" or "RTOUPP"). The pilot begun in June 2020 and ended in June 2024 with a capped number of 1,900 participants at any time. The lessons learned from RTOUPP have provided a deeper understanding of the dynamics involved in offering a supply TOU rate and can serve as a strong foundation for future discussions. Without waiving its rights under Section 16-103(e) on what services it will offer, ComEd is open to engage and collaborate with stakeholders outside this proceeding to discuss supply TOU rates.
