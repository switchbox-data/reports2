## Part 1. Supplemental testimony of Mark A. Mader (Exhibit JC-2 Supplemental No. 1): overall 12+0 base rate request

**EXHIBIT JC-2 Supplemental No. 1**

**BEFORE THE NEW JERSEY BOARD OF PUBLIC UTILITIES**

**JERSEY CENTRAL POWER & LIGHT COMPANY**

**Supplemental Direct Testimony of Mark A. Mader**

**Re: Update to Amortization of Deferred Storm Expense**

_(Page footer: LEGAL\65189990\1)_

<!-- PDF page 2; printed page 1 -->

**I. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND**

**Q.** **Please state your name and business address.**

A. My name is Mark A. Mader and my business address is 300 Madison Ave., Morristown, NJ 07960.

**Q.** **Have you previously submitted testimony in this proceeding?**

A. Yes. My direct testimony (Exhibit JC-2) was filed on March 16, 2023 as part of Jersey Central Power & Light Company’s (“JCP&L” or the “Company”) Verified Petition.

**Q.** **What is the purpose of your supplemental direct testimony?**

A. My supplemental direct testimony addresses an adjustment to the amortization of deferred storm expense as a result of the Company’s 12+0 update, which includes actual data through June 30, 2023. Specifically, JCP&L has reduced the requested increase in the deferred storm balance amortization by $2.5 million annually. The purpose of the reduction in the proposed amortization of deferred storm balance is to moderate the impact of the overall rate increase on JCP&L’s customers.

**II. ADJUSTMENT TO AMORTIZATION OF DEFERRED STORM EXPENSE**

**Q.** **Why is the Company making an adjustment to the amortization of deferred storm expense at this time?**

A. The Company is filing its 12+0 update based on actual data through June 30, 2023, the end of the test year. When the Company filed its Verified Petition on March 16, 2023, the Company proposed a test year of the twelve months ending June 30, 2023, and included six months of actual data and six months of forecasted data (“6+6”), which supported a base rate increase of $184.95 million. On June 2, 2023, the Company filed a “9+3” update with actual data through March 31, 2023, which

<!-- PDF page 3; printed page 2 -->

supported a base rate increase of $192.9 million. The Company’s 12+0 update now reflects actual data for the entire test year and is the last update of data for the Company’s pending base rate case. Based on the 12+0 update, the test year actual data would support a base rate increase of approximately $194.7 million on an annual basis.

**Q.** **What is the reason for making an adjustment to the amortization of deferred storm expense?**

A. The Company is aware of the current economic conditions as the State emerges from the COVID-19 pandemic, including continued inflationary pressures. Under these unique conditions, the Company has elected to moderate its total request for a base rate increase. The Company proposes to reduce the amount of its requested increase in its amortization of deferred storm expense by $2.5 million to moderate its total base rate increase request. The downward adjustment to the deferred storm cost amortization results in a requested base rate increase for its 12+0 update of $192.2 million, which approximates the Company’s requested base rate increase filed in the 9+3 update.

**Q.** **Based on JCP&L’s updated data in the 12+0, can you elaborate on the changes the Company is proposing to the amortization of deferred storm expense?**

A. In my prior testimony, I explained that, in its 6+6 and 9+3 filings, JCP&L was requesting to increase recovery of its deferred storm balance by $30 million from $29 million to $59 million per year, which resulted in approximately a 5-year amortization period for its storm-related regulatory asset balance of $310.2 million (as of December 31, 2022). The Company now seeks to reduce this requested increase by $2.5 million, resulting in a request to increase recovery of its deferred

<!-- PDF page 4; printed page 3 -->

storm balance by $27.5 million from $29 million to $56.5 million per year. This results in approximately a 5-year amortization period for its storm-related regulatory asset balance of $296.6 million (as of June 30, 2023). This adjustment is reflected in Exhibit JC-3 (Direct Testimony of Carol A. Pittavino), Schedule CAP-2 (12+0), p. 17 of 31. This is an adjustment to the Company’s prior request based on the updated data in the 12+0.

**Q.** **Does this conclude your supplemental direct testimony?**

A. Yes.

**SUPPLEMENTAL DIRECT TESTIMONY NO. 1 OF CAROL A. PITTAVINO**

**I. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND**

**Q.** **Please state your name and business address.**

A. My name is Carol A. Pittavino and my business address is 800 Cabin Hill Drive, Greensburg, PA 15601.

**Q.** **Have you previously submitted testimony in this proceeding?**

A. Yes. My direct testimony (Exhibit JC-3) was filed on March 16, 2023 as part of Jersey Central Power & Light Company’s (“JCP&L” or “Company”) Petition.

**Q.** **What is the purpose of your supplemental direct testimony?**

A. My supplemental testimony addresses revisions to the JCP&L revenue requirements resulting from the Company’s: (1) adding a new adjustment number 29 relating to the removal of an injury and damage accrual; (2) revised items removed in (Column 2) to Schedule CAP-1 for transmission revenues and reclassification of assessment fees; (3) allocation of Operating Reserve amounts to transmission that were historically previously 100% distribution.

**II. REVENUE REQUIREMENTS**

**Q.** **Please provide an overview of the revised Schedules to your testimony.**

A. Schedule CAP-2 Adjustment No. 29 – In June 2023, there was an injury and damage accrual posted in A&G FERC account 925 “Injuries and Damages” in the amount of $8 million. The $8 million injury and damage accrual amount relates to an accident that occurred on the Garden State Parkway. Adjustment No. 29 removes the distribution portion using a Salary & Wage allocation of 91.55% resulting in

<!-- PDF page 9; printed page 2 -->

$7,324,000 being removed from the distribution income statement as the Company is not seeking distribution recovery at this time.

**Q.** **Based on your updated revenue requirements analysis, is JCP&L requesting changes to the amounts removed in the Non-Distribution Adjustments?**

A. Yes. As set forth in Schedule CAP-1 (12+0) Column 2, JCP&L is requesting to align the transmission and distribution treatment for certain revenues deemed to be transmission revenues, which resulted from the transmission 2020 “ATRR” Actual Transmission Revenue Requirement preliminary challenge process. JCP&L agreed to allocate a portion of revenue from FERC account 451 “Miscellaneous Service Revenue” and 454 “Revenue Rent from Electric Property” from distribution to transmission. Specifically, for distribution JCP&L removed GT annual transmission amounts of $74,289 related to rents for transmission facility charges from Miscellaneous Service Revenue that resulted from the transmission preliminary challenge 2020 NJRC-PC-19 and 2021 NJRC-PC-25. JCP&L also for distribution removed a transmission allocated share of rent revenue general ledger account numbers 454011 and 454012 applying a pole attachment allocation percentage of 2.5%, resulting from the transmission preliminary challenge 2020 NJRC-PC-20 and 2021 NJRC-PC-08 . In addition related to these PC’s, JCP&L also removed for distribution a transmission allocated share of affiliated rent general ledger account 454204 using a Salary & Wage allocator of 8.45%. The transmission revenue portion of these three amounts was $270,266 included in Column 2 “Non-Distribution Adjustments”, which increases the distribution revenue requirement by the same amount. See the tables below for a tabular depiction of these three transmission adjustments.

**Q.** **Were there any additional transmission adjustments made in the (12+0) update to the income statement.**

A. Yes, a reclass was made to remove Rate Counsel Assessment fees from FERC 930.2 “A&G Miscellaneous Regulatory Expense” to FERC 928 “A&G Regulatory Commission Expense” to address the Company’s transmission 2021 ATRR Preliminary Challenge NJRC-PC-38. Historically, JCP&L allocated a transmission share of the expense in FERC account 930.2 to transmission using a Salary & Wage allocation. However, the reclass to FERC account 928 results in JCP&L taking 100% of Rate Counsel assessment fees as part of Distribution expense. The revenue requirement impact in the (12+0) update is an increase to distribution expense in the amount of $76,545, which increases the distribution revenue requirement by the same amount.

**Q.** **Were there any additional transmission adjustment made in the (12+0) that would impact Distribution Rate Base?**

A. Yes. In the Company’s 2020 ATRR Preliminary Challenge NJRC-PC-11 JCP&L agreed to allocate a portion of the Operating Reserve accounts 228102 “General Liability” and 228104 “Workers Compensation” to transmission based upon a Salary & Wage allocator. Historically, JCP&L used 100% of these balances (net

<!-- PDF page 11; printed page 4 -->

of tax) as part of the Operation Reserve reduction to the Distribution Rate Base. Therefore, in order to align transmission with distribution the Company has applied the distribution Salary & Wage allocator of 91.55% to the two afore mentioned accounts (net of tax), which are now included as part of the Operating Reserve balance deduction from Distribution rate base. The impact to the distribution rate base (12+0) update Operating Reserve balance (net of tax) was $197,612, which impacts the (12+0) update by multiplying the amount by the Company’s Weighted Average Cost of Capital “WACC” in this case by 9.71% results in increasing the revenue requirement by $19,188.

**Q.** **Does this conclude your supplemental direct testimony?**

A. Yes.
