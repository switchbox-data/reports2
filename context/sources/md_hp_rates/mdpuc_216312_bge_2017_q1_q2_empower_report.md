## Executive Summary

### Program Participation and Impact Highlights

In the first half of 2017, total savings from all BGE’s Smart Energy Savers Program® produced were over 277,000 MWh annual gross wholesale savings. This represents a slight increase over savings reported for the same period in 2016 and is 85% of 2013 sales (the full year 2017 interim target is 1.94% of 2013 sales - see chart below). Program-to-Date (PTD), gross wholesale annual energy savings totals from all programs are over 3.1 million MWh. Program savings include BGE’s traditional residential and commercial energy efficiency programs and other programs such as BGE’s Smart Energy Manager®, BGE’s Smart Energy Rewards®, PeakRewardsSM, the Conservation Voltage Reduction program, the DHCD managed limited income program and others. Program level results can be found in the Summary Tables included as part of this report. Savings numbers reflect changes made as a result of the latest evaluation report performed by BGE’s EM&V consultant, Navigant Consulting, including changes to the realization rates and, where applicable, Net-to-Gross Ratios. (see below for details).

**Additional Accomplishments in the First Half of 2017**

In April, BGE was honored with its 7th consecutive ENERGY STAR® Partner of the Year – Sustained Excellence Award

BGE received the ENERGY STAR Certified Homes Market Leader Award acknowledging BGE’s continued excellence in supporting and building ENERGY STAR new homes – 4,400 homes contributed in 2016.

### Residential Energy Efficiency

- BGE’s residential energy efficiency portfolio achieved 102% of the forecasted MWh in the first half of 2017 (Gross Wholesale).
- The residential portfolio saw over 81,000 participants with nearly 1.4 million energy efficient measures installed.
- The residential lighting markdown program achieved over 52,520 MWh (Gross Wholesale) which reflects 106% of the forecasted energy savings.

[PDF p. 3 | printed p. 3]

- The residential new construction program achieved 2,530 MWh (Gross Wholesale) in the
  first-half of 2017 or 122% of the forecasted energy savings.
- BGE’s Residential Retrofit programs continue to perform well with nearly 2,500 HPwES
  audits and jobs completed and 16,000 QHECs completed resulting in over 193,000 measures
  installed.

### Commercial & Industrial (C&I) Energy Efficiency

- During the first half of 2017, the Energy Solutions for Business (ESFB) Program re-engaged
  the contractor network to promote the Prescriptive Program. Even though the program
  relaunched with revised guidelines and service provider qualifications, a revised incentive
  structure, adjusted incentive measures and required pre-approvals on all projects regardless
  of size, BGE was able to stay on track to meet its annual MWh savings forecast.
- The Instant Lighting Discount (Midstream) program is performing well above expectation
  and has achieved 172% of its Q1/Q2 savings forecast.
- Baltimore Washington Medical Center celebrated the commissioning of a 2 MW CHP
  reciprocating machine at their hospital complex in the spring of 2017. The project received a
  $350,000 incentive through the Program. They are using the thermal heat to preheat boilers,
  steam heating, domestic hot water heating, and space heating.
- The Small Business Pilot savings analysis was performed monthly during the pilot and after
  the pilot’s conclusion. The overall savings results showed estimated savings across the
  treatment group of 0.5% with a margin of error of +/- 0.7%. These savings were not
  statistically significant and therefore it cannot be concluded the savings occurred as a result
  of the pilot. Based on this lack of savings alone, BGE will not be recommending launch of
  this pilot as a full program under the 2018-2020 portfolio.

### PeakRewardsSM

- The BGE PeakRewards Program continued to operate smoothly with over 327,600 customers
  receiving credits at June 30, 2017. During the first half of 2017, BGE selected a new
  program thermostat, ecobee 3 lite, for its PeakRewards program. At the end of Q2 there
  were 500 of these devices installed in customers’ homes.

### BGE Smart Energy Manager®/BGE Smart Energy Rewards®

- BGE Smart Energy Manager® (SEM), BGE’s residential behavioral program, continued to
  roll out to residential customers with 904,000 customers enrolled and over 12.2 million
  Home Energy Reports sent to customers through June.
- BGE Smart Energy Rewards®, BGE’s peak time rebate program, included over 1 million
  customers with smart meters at the start of the summer 2017 season. The program is
  designed to encourage customers to use less electricity during the summer peak demand
  periods called Energy Savings Days (ESDs). During events, customers will earn $1.25 in bill
  credits for every kWh reduced, compared to their baseline, from 1pm to 7 pm on Energy
  Savings Days. During the first half of 2017 BGE did not have any Energy Savings Days as
  temperature and loads were not conducive to calling an event.

[PDF p. 4 | printed p. 4]

### Conservation Voltage Reduction

- During the first half of 2017, savings from CVR program deployments amounted to nearly
  49,000 MWh. These savings are based on circuits deployed through 2016 based on current
  sales. Since the beginning of deployment, this program has provided cumulative savings of
  almost 230,000 MWh for BGE’s residential and commercial customers.

### Enhancements

- Residential Energy Efficiency program enhancements
  - In the second half of 2017, BGE’s lighting food bank program will donate an
    additional 38,000 ENERGY STAR LED bulbs to local food pantries. The anticipated
    2017 distribution will be over 77,000 bulbs, which will more than double the 2016
    distribution amount of 20,000 bulbs.
  - In the second half of 2017, BGE will host two additional room air conditioner and
    dehumidifier appliance recycling events to help increase the measure count and target
    customers who would not otherwise be able to participate in the recycling program
    without a qualifying refrigerator or freezer as a primary unit that is eligible for a
    home pick-up.
  - The HPwES Program began transitioning a rebate structure change from cost-based
    incentives to performance-based incentives. This program update will allow the
    HPwES Program to potentially increase customer participation as project approval
    would not be restricted based on savings-to-investment requirements. The
    performance-based incentive structure will also allow the HPwES Program to be
    better stewards of the EmPOWER MD funds by more accurately incentivizing the
    reduction of residential electric consumption.
- Commercial Energy Efficiency program enhancements
  - BGE will initiate a “thank you note” campaign to follow-up on all Instant Lighting
    Discount customers. This letter will thank them for their purchase, emphasize the
    value that the program provided and encourage future sales.
  - Based on the successful use of Building Analytics in our Building Tune-Up Program,
    BGE has begun using an ICF proprietary tool called Sightline Analytics to better
    target Small Business Energy Solutions customers. Rather than having the service
    providers blindly inquire door-to-door, this tool collects, and stitches together
    multiple utility and non‐utility data sets such as current and historical customer and
    billing data, physical attributes of properties, and previous participation data from our
    broader platform.

### Evaluation, Measurement & Verification EY6 Results Implementation

The implementation of the revised realization rates, Net-to-Gross Ratios (NTGR) and deemed values recommended in the EmPOWER Maryland 2017 Final Evaluation Report from Navigant Consulting, BGE’s EM&V consultant, resulted in an increase of approximately 11,780 MWh in reported gross energy savings and a decrease of 0.454 MW of reported gross demand savings. As required, the realization rates and NTGR were applied retroactively to calculated savings during Evaluation Year 7 (June 1, 2015 through May 31, 2016). Revised deemed values were used in all savings calculations beginning June 1, 2017. In addition, preliminary realization rates were developed and used to adjust the savings for the period June 1, 2016 through May 31, 2017 and will be adjusted, as necessary, subsequent to the issuance of the 2018 Evaluation report. These changes to previously reported values are reflected in the Program-to-Date and Cycle-to-Date numbers. A summary table of the realization rates and NTGR assumptions used to update the previously reported energy and demand savings is located in the Notes and Assumptions section of this report.

## C&I Building Tune-Up (formerly Retrocommissioning) Program

Building Tune-up is the process of optimizing a building’s performance by adjusting or “tuning-up” its existing systems as opposed to replacing equipment. Almost all buildings, even buildings with relatively new and efficient equipment, can benefit from the Building Tune-up. This process is so cost effective that it is typically funded out of an organization’s operating and maintenance budget as opposed to the capital budget. This means that projects can often be executed rather quickly without the need for extensive multiyear budgeting. Due to the significant weather related HVAC component, Building Tune-up is a seasonal program that typically shows greater savings in the second half of the year.

**Residential Appliance Rebate Program**

Participant: A residential customer who purchases a qualified appliance and
submits a rebate application form. Note – if a customer purchases an
appliance and submits a rebate application, that customer is counted as
one participant. If that rebate application is for multiple appliances,
that customer is still counted as a single participant. However, if later
that same customer purchases another appliance and submits a new
rebate application, they will be counted as another participant.
Measures: Qualified appliances under the program - Clothes Washer,

another appliance and receives an additional rebate, they will be
counted as another participant.
