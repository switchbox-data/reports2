# Maryland's DRIVE Act TOU Provisions: IOU Compliance, Automatic Enrollment, and the July 1, 2026 Reports

## TL;DR

- **All four Maryland IOUs already had some form of TOU rate before the DRIVE Act, but their starting points differed sharply.** BGE, Pepco, and Delmarva (the "Exelon/PHI" utilities) had legacy whole-home and EV TOU schedules dating to the 1980s–2010s and only had to _modify_ them; Potomac Edison had essentially no residential TOU rate except an EV-charger rider and had to _build a new one from scratch_ — which it launched April 20, 2026 using dual/quad-register meters because it lacks AMI.
- **The PSC has NOT exercised its DRIVE Act authority to automatically (opt-out) enroll beneficial-electrification incentive recipients into TOU tariffs as of mid-2026.** Every DRIVE Act TOU tariff approved in Case No. 9761 (Order No. 91917, Oct. 21, 2025) remains strictly opt-in; the broader opt-out transition question was deferred to the July 1, 2026 utility reports and a PSC report to the General Assembly due Dec. 31, 2027.
- **The July 1, 2026 reports WERE filed on time** by all four IOUs in Case No. 9761 (ML 331710, ML 331718, ML 331727), and the Commission opened a comment period on July 14, 2026 (ML 332115). **The reports have now been obtained and are summarized in Section 3 below.** Their unanimous headline: all four IOUs **recommend against transitioning customers to TOU on an opt-out basis at this time**, and none quantifies a firm TOU-driven distribution-capital-deferral number — TOU is characterized as a behavioral, non-dispatchable, non-locational tool best suited to system-wide load shaping and customer education, while any actual deferral value is reserved for dispatchable, locationally targetable resources (demand response, managed charging, storage, VPPs). The PHI/Exelon joint report was prepared by **The Brattle Group**.

## Key Findings

### 1. Pre-DRIVE-Act TOU landscape and what each utility had to do

The DRIVE Act (HB 1256/SB 959, 2024; Chs. 475/476; codified at PUA §§ 7-1001 to 7-1007) required each IOU by **July 1, 2025** to file one or more opt-in TOU tariffs for appropriate customer classes with a sufficient off-peak discount, plus an enrollment target for January 1, 2028. The PSC implemented this through Case No. 9761 ("DRIVE Act Implementation") and Order No. 91218 (July 11, 2024). Both the statute and the Department of Legislative Services Fiscal and Policy Note for HB 1256 note that "Electric companies may currently offer TOU rates to customers, including residential customers" — so the Act built on existing offerings rather than creating TOU from nothing.

Order No. 91917 (Oct. 21, 2025) documents each utility's pre-existing schedules and the modifications ordered:

- **BGE — already had multiple offerings; only modified.** Schedule RD (whole-home delivery+supply TOU, developed 2019 under the PC 44 TOU Pilot, ~1,200 customers); Schedule RL (a legacy residential TOU introduced in 1984, over 50,000 customers); Schedule EV (whole-home EV TOU, created 2013, ~190 customers); and Rider 6 / Vehicle Charging TOU (EV-only submetered adjustment, created 2020, ~3,700 customers; marketed as "EVsmart" with an advertised ~$120/yr saving vs. Schedule R). BGE lowered the Schedule RD on/off-peak ratio from 3.2:1 to 2.8:1 and strengthened Schedule RL from 1.3:1 to 1.9:1 (applying Schedule EV rate-shaping). Per Order No. 91917, "the company's estimated budget of $369,000 in incremental costs is moderate… these costs are related only to marketing and education; BGE does not have system programming [costs]," and BGE aims "to enroll an additional 3,200 customers in its revised Schedules RL and RD by January 2028." BGE also proposed (in Case No. 9478, EV Phase II) to close Schedule EV to new enrollment and retain Rider 6.
- **Pepco (PHI) — already had offerings; modified R-TOU-P, deferred R-TM.** Schedule R-TOU-P ("Residential TOU Pilot," developed 2019 under PC 44) and legacy Schedule R-TM ("Residential Time-Metered," introduced 1984, closed to new customers since July 1, 2000, ~52,646 customers), plus its "Whole House TOU" and Plug-in Vehicle (R-PIV) offerings. Pepco modified R-TOU-P (ratio ~4.5:1 down to ~2.7:1) and left R-TM unchanged, deferring any strengthening to its next base rate case because of Bill Stabilization Adjustment revenue-erosion concerns.
- **Delmarva (PHI) — already had offerings; modified R-TOU-P, studying R-TOU-ND.** Schedule R-TOU-P and Schedule R-TOU-ND ("Residential TOU Non-Demand," reopened to new customers in 2023, ~16 customers). Modified R-TOU-P identically to Pepco; the Commission directed Delmarva to bring the Rate Design Work Group an analysis (within six months) of adding an SOS TOU component to R-TOU-ND, whose ~1.35:1 ratio Staff found too weak.
- **Potomac Edison (FirstEnergy) — the outlier; built a new rate.** It had **no** general residential TOU rate, only an EV Charger TOU Rider from its EV Phase I portfolio in Case No. 9478. It had to _create_ a brand-new Schedule R-TOU. Because Potomac Edison has no AMI in its Maryland territory and no AMI deployment timeline, it uses dual/quad-register meters. The Commission directed shifting the summer on-peak window one hour earlier (from a proposed 5–9 p.m. to 4–8 p.m.) to align with PJM's five coincident-peak (5CP) hours; note that the filed tariff (Electric P.S.C. Md. No. 54) states an EDT on-peak of 4–9 p.m. weekdays and an EST on-peak of 3–8 p.m., a wording that should be reconciled against the Order's four-hour directive. Potomac Edison's new residential TOU rate launched **April 20, 2026**; per its April 20, 2026 press release, on-peak electric supply and variable distribution charges cost "about 16 cents per kilowatt-hour" versus off-peak "about 9 cents per kilowatt-hour, roughly 44% less" through May 31, 2026 (rising to roughly 17¢/10¢ on June 1, preserving a ~7¢ spread). Potomac Edison serves "about 285,000 customers in seven counties in Maryland and about 155,000 customers in the Eastern Panhandle of West Virginia"; enrollment requires a meter replacement, is voluntary, and excludes customers on the EV Charger TOU Rider.

**Relevant dockets and filings**: Case No. 9761 (DRIVE Act TOU tariffs and EDSSS pilots); Case No. 9478 (EV portfolio, where EV-specific TOU rates are addressed and BGE's/PHI's EV Phase II proposals live); Case No. 9715 (energy storage); PC 44 (electric-distribution transformation, origin of the TOU pilots). Key July 1, 2025 maillogs: BGE VPP/TOU filing ML 320089; PHI EDSSS/TOU filing ML 320092; Potomac Edison EDSSS application ML 320115 and TOU rate schedule ML 320116. Compliance tariff filings followed (e.g., DP&L/Pepco revised tariff pages ML 323857/323858, Oct. 31, 2025; Potomac Edison revised R-TOU tariff ML 324615, Nov. 20, 2025, accepted by Letter Order ML 325953, Jan. 8, 2026; BGE revised residential delivery+energy TOU tariff ML 329078, accepted ML 330315, May 20, 2026).

### 2. Automatic (opt-out) enrollment — authority NOT exercised

The statute provides that the PSC "may require an investor-owned electric company to automatically enroll customers that receive an incentive from the company as part of a beneficial electrification program in a TOU tariff," subject to a mandatory opt-out. This is **permissive, not mandatory**.

**As of mid-2026, the PSC has not exercised this authority.** In Order No. 91917 the Commission approved all TOU offerings strictly as opt-in and made no move to default beneficial-electrification incentive recipients (EV charger rebate recipients, heat pump incentive recipients, etc.) into a TOU tariff. The DRIVE Act's broader opt-out question is deliberately staged: each IOU had to file a July 1, 2026 report evaluating "the merits and feasibility of transitioning all customers to a TOU tariff on an opt-out basis," and the PSC must report to the General Assembly by **December 31, 2027** on the impacts of opt-in TOU tariffs, the "timeline, feasibility, and merits of transitioning all customers to a TOU tariff on an opt-out basis," and "whether a full transition to TOU rates is justified." No opt-out default enrollment is occurring yet, and none is pending as an active enrollment mechanism — it is under study only.

Legislative history reinforces this: the originally introduced HB 1256/SB 959 contained a **mandatory** opt-out transition plan (transition all customer classes to a TOU tariff by ~Sept. 1, 2028); that provision was amended out, and the enacted law made TOU opt-in with opt-out available only as a discretionary PSC option. Utility testimony opposed a mandate — Pepco/Delmarva noted their completed opt-in whole-house TOU pilot benefited a very small number of customers at high implementation cost, and BGE stated its whole-bill TOU pilot achieved under 2% enrollment with significant attrition despite participation incentives.

### 3. The July 1, 2026 reports — filed on time; now obtained and summarized

The DRIVE Act required each IOU by **July 1, 2026** to submit a report evaluating (1) the potential to avoid/defer distribution capital projects via TOU rates, DR/demand-side programs, and renewable on-site generation, and (2) the merits and feasibility of an opt-out TOU transition.

**Filed in Case No. 9761 on July 1, 2026:**

- **ML 331710** — Baltimore Gas and Electric Company, Potomac Electric Power Company, and Delmarva Power & Light Company, joint **"Deferring Distribution System Upgrades Report,"** prepared by **The Brattle Group** (Ramakrishnan, Kavlak, Sergici, Hledik) on behalf of Pepco and Delmarva (BGE is a joint signatory but files its substantive analysis separately in ML 331727).
- **ML 331727** — Baltimore Gas and Electric Company, **"Drive Act Capital Deferral Potential July 1 Report."**
- **ML 331718** — The Potomac Edison Company, **"Drive Act Report."**

So all four IOUs met the deadline: the three Exelon utilities filed a joint distribution-deferral report (with BGE adding a company-specific capital-deferral report), and Potomac Edison filed separately. To standardize these reports, the Commission had issued **Order No. 92158** (Jan. 27, 2026, ML 326582) "on DRIVE Act Reporting Requirements" and **Order No. 92422** (May 29, 2026, ML 330633) "on Common Metrics for DRIVE Act July 2026 Reporting Requirements" (preceded by a March 24, 2026 Notice/RFP for standard evaluation metrics, ML 328202). Order 92422 adopted six Staff-recommended program metrics (peak load reduction potential; annual cost per kW; locational targeting potential; time-of-day performance; other practical considerations; distribution-deferral screening capability) and directed reliance on EmPOWER analytical methods where appropriate. On **July 14, 2026** the Commission issued a **"Notice of Request for Comments on Drive Act Reports"** (ML 332115), opening the review window.

**The unanimous bottom line.** All four IOUs reach the same two conclusions: (1) no utility quantifies a firm, constraint-level distribution-capital-deferral number attributable to TOU rates specifically — TOU is characterized as behavioral, non-dispatchable, and hard to target to a constrained feeder, and is demoted to a system-wide load-shaping and customer-engagement tool, while dispatchable/locational resources (demand response, managed charging, front-of-the-meter storage, C&I/aggregator programs, VPPs) are where deferral value is said to live; and (2) each recommends **against** an opt-out TOU transition at this time, citing weak behavioral response under default enrollment, adverse bill impacts, affordability concerns, and (for Potomac Edison) the absence of AMI.

**BGE (ML 331727).** Grounded in its 2026 Annual System Plan Update, BGE identified **190 forecasted feeder overloads and 33 forecasted substation transformer overloads** over the 2027–2036 horizon, totaling **547 MW (feeders) + 393 MW (substations)** of overload. But ~90% of that MW is **6+-hour (long-duration)** overload (494.2 of 547 feeder MW; 342.5 of 393 substation MW) — which BGE says is "not well suited to behavioral demand response or many current energy storage configurations." Only ~**10%** is short-duration (≤5 hrs) and potentially addressable by non-wires resources. Summer overloads fall in the afternoon/early evening (aligned with existing DR); **winter overloads occur in the early morning (~4–8 a.m.) and late evening (8 p.m.–midnight)**, which BGE flags as an emerging need tied to growing **heat pump** penetration. Critically, BGE **excluded the constraint-level "peak load reduction potential of NWS programs" metric**, stating it "does not currently evaluate demand-side program impacts at the individual constraint level" and that its NWS evaluations to date have used **utility-owned front-of-the-meter storage only**. Its program screen rates TOU "Poor" scalability / "Low" locational signal; the only behavioral evidence cited is the **Brattle PC44 Schedule RD pilot (−9.3% summer weekday peak, −4.9% non-summer weekday peak)**, discounted as opt-in and incentivized. On opt-out, BGE **recommends against** it for distribution-capital purposes: it cites out-of-state results (peak reductions ~~1.3–8.0%; **PSEG Long Island's 900k-customer default reached 97% participation but only ~1.3% avg summer weekday peak reduction**), warns that high enrollment ≠ durable load reduction, and notes its own pilot bill-impact analysis found **40% of 100,000+ customers would have seen higher bills (~~$4/month) had they not shifted usage.** _(BGE's full analysis is summarized in the companion doc `bge_tou_rates.md`, Part 8.)_

**Pepco & Delmarva (ML 331710, Brattle).** Delmarva's ten-year forecast has **no** feeder or substation constraints; Pepco has **six** (four feeder overloads in 2026, one in 2027, one substation — Kingswood — in 2030). None passed PHI's two-part NWS screen (remediation cost > $500k **and** in-service date > 36 months out); all will be handled by **load transfers to adjacent feeders**, so PHI identified **no NWS** for any current constraint. The report instead gives Order-92422 screening metrics for each program: **Maryland Energy Storage Program** FTM batteries (MESP-1: 10.5 MW Pepco / 4 MW Delmarva; MESP-2: 21 MW / 8 MW; 4-hr batteries at 58% PJM ELCC) — the most dispatchable/locational resource; **Smart Charge Management** (existing ~2.81 MW Pepco / 0.21 MW Delmarva, scaling toward 49 MW / 4 MW by 2030 at ~$157–$233/kW-yr); **DRIVE/DSSS battery VPP** (proposed, ~3.28 MW Pepco / 0.93 MW Delmarva by pilot year 2, up to $300/kW-yr performance payments); load management, C&I/aggregator, BTM solar, and EmPOWER EE. On opt-out, PHI concludes it is **not the right time**, and details the heavy transition machinery required (customer communications, marketing, digital tools, customer protections, decoupling, metering, IT, and transition costs) if the Commission ever mandates it.

**Potomac Edison (ML 331718).** Its constraints are mostly **winter transformer overloads** (spanning the winter peak, several coincident with the winter morning peak) plus a high-risk asset and an n-1 contingency sub-transmission overload; for every one, Potomac Edison reports **"no viable NWA identified."** It rejects the JEU/Brattle standardized-metrics framework as ill-suited to its constraint-driven, non-AMI planning process. On opt-out, it concludes a territory-wide transition is **"technically feasible but not meritorious or recommended"** under current conditions: it would require re-metering **~250,000 residential** customers (dual-register meters, which can't support more than a single two-period rate) and migrating **~35,000 non-residential** customers to MV-90 metering, plus **removing declining-block non-residential rates**; total metering cost alone would run into the **low hundreds of millions of dollars**, with AMI a practical prerequisite. It leans on the Kahn-Lang et al. (Resources for the Future, 2024) meta-analysis showing highly variable time-based-rate impacts (both increases and decreases) to argue benefits are too uncertain to justify the cost.

**Cross-cutting caveats the reports themselves stress:** all lean heavily on the still-in-progress **UBCA** benefit-cost framework (Case No. 9764) and the **DRIVE Act DSSS/VPP pilots** to generate the operational evidence needed before any resource can be relied on for planning-grade deferral; several requested metrics were expressly deferred to later EM&V cycles. For directional context, note that the aggregate ~440 MW flexible-load figures reported by Utility Dive (BGE 188 MW; Pepco 185 MW; Delmarva 34 MW; Potomac Edison 18–33 MW) come from the utilities' **Jan. 2026 EDSSS/VPP pilot proposals**, not these July 1 deferral reports, and should not be conflated with any deferral estimate in them.

## Details

### Statutory architecture

The DRIVE Act findings emphasize that "well-designed TOU pricing of electricity can help mitigate the impacts of electrification on the electric distribution system, reduce GHG emissions during peak hours, and encourage customer adoption of beneficial electrification measures through off-peak cost savings." "Electric distribution system support services" (EDSSS) statutorily include (i) local or system peak demand reduction; (ii) demand response; (iii) avoidance or deferral of a T&D upgrade or capacity expansion; and (iv) facilitating hosting capacity for additional DERs. EDSSS pilots are capped at 2% of each utility's highest coincident peak demand (PUA § 7-1005(f)). Utilities may recover reasonable and prudent costs, including marketing costs, and the PSC may (for good cause) delay compliance deadlines.

### The parallel EDSSS/VPP track and PC 77

On the same July 1, 2025 date, each IOU filed EDSSS (VPP/V2G) pilot proposals. In Order No. 91917 the Commission approved the TOU tariff modifications but **rejected the EDSSS pilots as too narrow** (residential-centric, technology-limited, targeting only a small fraction of the Act's peak-reduction goal), ordering refiling within 90 days and directing expansion to commercial & industrial customers, third-party aggregators, grid export/V2G demonstrations, clearer metrics, a default two-year pilot duration, and LMI equity strategies. As filed, BGE's pilot estimated a $1,545,955 cost yielding a 7.621 MW peak reduction; Potomac Edison's two-year pilot was $3,769,500 (~$0.55/month for a 1,000-kWh residential customer via a proposed "Grid Programs Surcharge").

The refiled pilots (Jan. 20, 2026: BGE ML 326404; PHI ML 326400; Potomac Edison ML 326405) were considered alongside **Public Conference 77** (opened March 2, 2026, ML 327574), which addressed demand-side management, rate design, and DER integration in coordination with the DRIVE Act. A key contested issue: BGE, PHI, and Potomac Edison proposed moving existing EmPOWER Maryland demand-response programs (e.g., BGE's Rider 15 Demand Response Service and Rider 26 Peak Time Rebate) into the DRIVE pilot beginning in 2027. OPC opposed this in its April 1, 2026 comments, asking the Commission to "decline to authorize the transfer of existing EmPOWER demand response programs into the DRIVE pilot," warning that a pilot built on migrated resources "would largely relabel existing activity," and objecting that BGE sought recovery of VPP costs "through a regulatory asset, with a return at the Commission-authorized weighted average cost of capital" — more favorable than EmPOWER's HB 864-restricted, expense-in-year-incurred treatment. The Commission then issued Order No. 92389 (May 4, 2026, ML 329713) "on Demand Management Proposals" and Order No. 92488 (June 29, 2026, ML 331573) "Second Order on DRIVE Act Program Proposals."

### Cost recovery and rate-design specifics

For TOU implementation costs, the Commission authorized regulatory-asset tracking for all utilities but deferred all decisions on rate of return and amortization to future base rate cases. It declined Potomac Edison's proposed "Grid Programs Surcharge" for TOU (combining TOU, EDSSS, and Case No. 9715 storage costs), directing regulatory-asset treatment instead, and noted there is no DRIVE Act requirement (unlike EDSSS) that TOU costs be recovered in the year incurred. OPC's consultant estimated a ~10.3-year breakeven for Delmarva under PHI's proposed budget, flagged that PHI's marketing budget was more than five times BGE's, and recommended capping PHI marketing at $150 per new TOU customer enrolled. The Commission also directed Potomac Edison to file (by its next rate case or within two years) a proposal to end its legacy non-residential **declining block rates** as inconsistent with the DRIVE Act, and to bring the Exelon utilities into that inquiry to the extent they also use declining block rates. All utilities were further directed to bring analyses of their non-residential SOS TOU rates to the Rate Design Work Group within six months.

2. **Read the reports through the July 14, 2026 comment lens.** Review the Notice of Request for Comments (ML 332115) and the stakeholder comments that follow — OPC, Staff, MEA, and the clean-energy coalitions (SEIA/AEU/CHESSA, Tesla, Sunrun, etc.) will summarize and critique the reports' deferral estimates and opt-out positions, which is the fastest route to the substantive conclusions if the raw PDFs are hard to parse.
3. **For the opt-out enrollment question specifically, track the Dec. 31, 2027 PSC report to the General Assembly** — that is where the opt-out transition determination will crystallize. The July 1, 2026 utility reports are inputs to it. Watch Potomac Edison's AMI deployment status: the absence of AMI (and of any AMI timeline) is the binding constraint on any opt-out transition in its territory, and the Commission has already accepted dual-register metering only as a stopgap.
4. **Benchmarks that would change these conclusions.** The "opt-in only" finding flips if the PSC issues an order in Case No. 9761 directing automatic/opt-out enrollment of beneficial-electrification incentive recipients, or if the 2027 report recommends a full opt-out transition. On the EmPOWER-to-DRIVE migration and its cost-recovery treatment, watch Orders 92389 and 92488 and any successor orders — a WACC-based regulatory-asset return on migrated demand response would materially raise the ratepayer cost of the DRIVE program.

## Caveats

- **The three July 1, 2026 reports have now been obtained and summarized (Section 3).** Note that the reports are screening-level: PHI and Potomac Edison identified **no** non-wires solutions for any current constraint, and BGE excluded the constraint-level NWS metric, so **none of the reports provides a firm avoided-capex dollar figure or a TOU-specific deferral MW number.** The ~440 MW aggregate flexible-load figures (BGE 188 MW; Pepco 185 MW; Delmarva 34 MW; Potomac Edison 18–33 MW) come from the utilities' Jan. 2026 EDSSS/VPP **pilot** proposals reported by Utility Dive, **not** from the July 1, 2026 capital-deferral reports, and should not be conflated with any avoided-capex or load-shift figure in those reports.
- **Customer/enrollment counts and rate ratios** are as reported in the July 1, 2025 filings and Order No. 91917; several will have changed by mid-2026 (e.g., following Potomac Edison's April 2026 launch and BGE's May 2026 tariff acceptance).
- **The exact contents of Orders 92158 and 92422** (the common-metrics framework) and Orders 92389/92488 were confirmed by docket title and sequence; their operative text was not retrieved.
- **One documented discrepancy to verify:** Order No. 91917 directed Potomac Edison to adopt a four-hour summer on-peak window of 4–8 p.m. (shifted earlier from 5–9 p.m.), but the filed Potomac Edison tariff language cited states 4–9 p.m. EDT / 3–8 p.m. EST. Confirm the final approved on-peak hours in the accepted compliance tariff (ML 326072/326541, accepted by Letter Order ML 325953) before relying on a specific window.
