## II. COMED'S SUPPLY TIME OF USE PROPOSAL

### A. Overview Of TOUs and How They Benefit Customers

**Q.** What is a time of use ("TOU") rate?

<!-- EPON: goal — stated purpose of a TOU rate: encourage customers to shift usage to off-peak hours; charges can vary by time of day, season, and day type -->

**A.** The purpose of a TOU rate is to encourage customers to shift their electricity usage to off-peak hours when demand is lower. A TOU rate charges a utility customer based on when

[p. 4]

they use energy – that is, if a customer uses a kWh (kilowatt-hour) at a time when demand is high and resources are scarce, that kWh costs more than it does when demand is low and resources plentiful. Charges can vary according to the time of day, season, and day (weekday, weekend, or holiday). When the charges under a TOU rate vary by time of day (like ComEd's proposal), the day is divided into specific periods with different (per kilowatt-hour) charges for each period or "block."

**Q.** What benefits do TOU rates provide to customers?

<!-- EPON: goal, ev-hp, midday-peak — benefits go to customers who shift; price differential must be large enough to induce shifting without deterring sign-up; only EVs and "programmable appliances" named as shifters (no mention of heat pumps); assumes usage can be moved out of peak -->

**A.** TOUs provide benefits to two types of customers: those customers who do shift their usage see bill savings, and all customers see benefits from the improved grid management and the reduced need for new generation. For customers to react to price signals, however, TOU rates must contain differences between the higher-priced "on-peak" and the lower-priced "off-peak" periods that are significant enough to encourage customers to shift usage without deterring customers from signing up for the rate. For example, TOU customers with programmable appliances or electric vehicles ("EVs") would be able to save money on their energy bills by programming their appliances or EV chargers to run during the lowest cost TOU periods. This behavior would also help reduce the distribution grid's peak demand if such usage would have otherwise occurred during that time.

**Q.** What is the difference between a DTOU rate and a STOU rate?

**A.** Distribution TOU rates cover the cost of delivering electricity to a customer's home, while Supply TOU rates cover the cost of the electricity itself, which ComEd must purchase from third-party providers.

[p. 5]

**Q.** What is a "bundled" TOU rate?

<!-- EPON: bundling, stacking, midday-peak — Rate BEST customers are on STOU + DTOU with identical time blocks; savings (and, implicitly, charges) occur in both supply and delivery for the same hours -->

**A.** TOU rates can apply to just the supply portion of a customer's bill (STOU), the delivery portion of a customer's bill (DTOU), or be "bundled," supply and delivery from the electric utility. In ComEd's Rate BEST proposal, customers are on a bundled STOU and DTOU with the same blocks of time applying to the supply and delivery portions of the customer's bill. This bundled TOU offering maximizes benefits, i.e. savings, for customers that shift usage to off-peak hours since the savings occur in both supply and delivery.

### B. ComEd's STOU Proposal

**Q.** At a high level, what is ComEd's STOU rate proposal?

<!-- EPON: bundling, midday-peak, profile — Rate BEST = opt-in residential STOU bundled with the residential DTOU; four periods (Morning 6a–1p, Mid-Day Peak 1p–7p, Evening 7p–9p, Overnight 9p–6a), all days; illustrative summer Mid-Day Peak 4.787 ¢/kWh vs overnight 1.870; non-summer 3.383 vs 2.012 -->

**A.** ComEd's STOU rate, Rate BEST, is an opt-in TOU rate for residential customers that is bundled with ComEd's residential DTOU rate. ComEd proposes a four-period TOU supply rate with summer and non-summer periods. Figure 1 below shows the time periods and illustrative summer and non-summer charges.

**Q.** How will customers benefit if they are on Rate BEST?

<!-- EPON: goal, midday-peak — benefit is conditional on shifting usage from higher-priced to lower-priced periods or "lowering usage when the system peaks are occurring" -->

**A.** Rate BEST provides customers with the opportunity to realize supply and delivery savings, when compared to ComEd's default rates, by shifting usage from higher priced time periods to lower priced ones and by lowering usage when the system peaks are occurring.

Right now ComEd residential supply customers have two options for their energy rates: our default, flat, fixed-price rate ("Rate BES – Basic Electric Service" or "Rate BES") and our residential real-time pricing ("RRTP"), an hourly rate. Under ComEd's default rate offering, electricity usage (in kWh) is charged at published rates (in cents per kWh) that do not vary throughout the course of the day. Unlike our default flat fixed price rate, the kWh charge for Rate BEST will change by time of day, and seasonally. And unlike real-time pricing, the time periods and charges for Rate BEST are published in advance.

**Q.** Which customers would be eligible to take service under Rate BEST?

**A.** As further discussed in ComEd witness Kremer's direct testimony on rehearing (ComEd Ex. 17.0), Rate BEST would be available only to residential customers.

**Q.** When would Rate BEST be effective?

<!-- EPON: goal — effective date rationale: June 2026 billing period, tied to RES concerns over interval data (Rider IMUD, June 2025) and to the June 2026 changes in Supply and Capacity charges -->

**A.** If approved by the Commission, Rate BEST and the other tariff changes necessary to implement ComEd's STOU rate will become effective with the June 2026 monthly billing period. The June 2026 effective date is intended to balance the desire to realize the benefits of a bundled TOU rate and concerns raised by Retail Electric Suppliers ("RESs") regarding barriers for RES entry to TOU rate offerings, namely that interval usage data will not be made available under Rider IMUD – Interval Meter Usage Data until June 2025, and that

[p. 7]

designing and marketing a TOU can be time-intensive for a RES to undertake. A June 2026 effective date for Rate BEST will ensure RESs are provided sufficient time to design and offer TOU products that address customer demands. The June 2026 effective date also aligns the introduction of the STOU with the June 2026 changes in Supply and Capacity charges, which will help customers make informed decisions about the supply rate that best suits their energy needs.

**Q.** Is ComEd's STOU proposal designed to complement the DTOU rate already approved by the Commission?

<!-- EPON: bundling, midday-peak, stacking — Rate BEST uses the same time periods as the DTOU; this is stated as "one reason" Rate BEST is proposed only as a bundled option with the DTOU (no supply-only STOU for non-DTOU customers) -->

**A.** Yes. For example, Rate BEST uses the same time periods as the DTOU rate time periods approved by the Commission in the Final Order. Final Order at 139-40, 147-48. This is one reason ComEd proposes that Rate BEST be available only as a bundled option with ComEd's DTOU rate. In addition, the DTOU rate design just approved by the Commission does not have to change to support bundling with Rate BEST. ComEd witness Kremer expands on the bundled TOU rate design in her direct testimony on rehearing (ComEd Ex. 17.0).

**Q.** How will this complementary TOU design provide benefits to customers?

<!-- EPON: midday-peak, stacking, goal — claim that customers "only have to track the same time blocks" and get benefits on supply and delivery "for shifting load out of peak times" -->

**A.** Because the Rate BEST time periods and the DTOU rate time periods are the same, customers will only have to track the same time blocks but they will receive benefits on their supply and delivery charges for shifting load out of peak times.

[p. 8]

**Q.** How does this rate compare to ComEd's previous STOU pilot, Rate RTOUPP?

<!-- EPON: capacity-mdppec, tmc — BEST differs from the RTOUPP pilot: four periods instead of three, and capacity and certain transmission-related charges broken out and charged on a demand (kW) basis rather than kWh (this kW design was later dropped in the Ex. 18.0 Compromise Proposal) -->

**A.** Rate BEST is structured similarly to ComEd's previous STOU pilot, Rate RTOUPP – Residential Time of Use Pricing Pilot ("Rate RTOUPP" or "RTOUPP").[^2] The primary differences are that Rate BEST has four periods instead of RTOUPP's three (in order to align with the DTOU approved in the January Order), and that capacity and certain transmission-related charges are broken out and charged on a demand (kW) basis instead of RTOUPP's usage (kWh) basis.

[^2]: Rate RTOUPP was approved by the Commission in October 2019. It sunset with the June 2024 monthly billing period, and customers enrolled in Rate RTOUPP were moved to Rate BES.

**Q.** Why does ComEd propose to change how those charges are recovered?

<!-- EPON: capacity-mdppec, tmc — ComEd's stated rationale for kW-based capacity/transmission recovery: rolling demand-based charges into kWh "diluted the price signals and undercompensated" customers who shifted to off-peak (RTOUPP learning) -->

**A.** The change to a demand-based charge for capacity and certain transmission-related charges is to address learnings from the RTOUPP in which we observed that by rolling the demand-based charges into a usage-based charge, ComEd both diluted the price signals and undercompensated those customers that shifted their usage to off-peak. ComEd witness Kremer discusses these charges and this decision in more detail in her direct testimony on rehearing, ComEd Ex. 17.0.

**Q.** How does Rate BEST differ from ComEd's other supply rates?

**A.** As mentioned above, ComEd currently has two supply rates available to residential customers. First, ComEd's flat supply rate, Rate BES, a fixed rate that provides a known price with only a seasonal difference. There are no variations in Rate BES throughout the day. In contrast, Rate BEST provides rates that vary by time of day, as well as by season.

[p. 9]

Second, ComEd's hourly pricing rate, Rate BESH – Basic Electric Service Hourly Pricing ("Rate BESH"), is based on the real-time energy price. Rate BESH is a mechanism for customers to save on their supply costs if those customers adjust their usage based on the real-time energy prices. However, the supply prices change on an hourly basis, and while ComEd provides tools to assist in tracking these prices, some customers may find it difficult to constantly monitor and adjust their usage. In contrast, Rate BEST provides a degree of predictability over Rate BESH because of the established daily periods and charges.

**Q.** How does ComEd's STOU proposal reflect stakeholder feedback?

<!-- EPON: goal — stakeholders' stated rationale for an STOU: more opportunities to save, reduce peak demand, "further the energy transition" -->

**A.** Stakeholders have expressed that an STOU offering, particularly one bundled with the DTOU rate, would present customers with more opportunities to save, potentially reduce peak demand, and further the energy transition. ComEd agrees that a properly designed bundled TOU offering can have these effects. Consistent with the spirit of the January Final Order, ComEd is committed to continue working with interested stakeholders on the contours of its Rate BEST and bundled TOU offering throughout this rehearing proceeding. Those conversations with certain parties have already commenced, and ComEd welcomes additional dialogues with Staff and parties in this rehearing.

## IV. A BUNDLED TOU CREATES MULTIPLE EFFICIENCIES

**Q.** What efficiencies will be gained from the proposed bundled TOU?

**A.** In addition to the benefits customers would realize from the bundled TOU, which I mentioned above, if the Commission were to adopt Rate BEST, two additional efficiencies will be realized – design efficiency and implementation efficiency.

**Q.** What design efficiencies are created by considering, and potentially approving, an STOU in this rehearing?

<!-- EPON: bundling, midday-peak, stacking — identical four periods and seasonality as the DTOU "makes their bundling natural" -->

**A.** Our proposal uses the same four time periods and seasonality approved for ComEd's DTOU rate. This mirroring of design aligns the DTOU and STOU rate to make their bundling natural.

[p. 11]

**Q.** What implementation efficiencies would be achieved by bundling the DTOU and STOU rates now?

**A.** There are two distinct efficiencies achieved through the creation of a bundled TOU rate during this rehearing. First, efficiencies are created related to the IT work needed within ComEd to implement the bundled TOU. Second, customer education and marketing of the bundled TOU rate will be more efficient than marketing a DTOU and STOU separately.

**Q.** What specific IT efficiencies will result from a bundled TOU rate offering?

<!-- EPON: bundling — implementation rationale: DTOU IT work (billing start January 2026) already underway; identical time blocks reduce billing/IT work; STOU/bundle IT work on hold; avoided rework estimated at \$5.7M–\$8M -->

**A.** ComEd intends to implement the DTOU rate beginning with the January 2026 monthly billing period, and the billing and IT implementation work (i.e., development, programming, testing, and training) that is specific only to the DTOU is already underway. Using identical time blocks reduces the volume of billing and IT implementation work across both TOU rates.

IT work necessary to implement the STOU, or work necessary to create the bundled TOU, is on hold pending the final order on rehearing. By proceeding with only the DTOU-specific IT work, and pausing any potentially overlapping work that might change depending on the final order on rehearing, ComEd will reduce the risk of unnecessary work (if no STOU is approved), or rework (if the final STOU approved is different than ComEd's proposal). ComEd estimates these potential wasted costs to be between \$5.7 million and \$8 million, representing work on DTOU that could not be reused if we had to program DTOU now and then had to reprogram it in the near future to address changes to align DTOU with the new bundled STOU/DTOU rate.

[p. 12]

**Q.** What education and customer outreach efficiencies are created by offering the bundled TOU rate?

<!-- EPON: bundling, bill-comparison, goal — one set of marketing materials; outreach to begin Dec 2025/Jan 2026, ramping through Q1–Q2 2026 before June 2026 launch; TOU information to be reflected in the customer-facing online bill comparison tool -->

**A.** A bundled TOU provides consistency in the peak and off-peak periods, making it significantly easier to educate customers and for them to understand how changes in their behaviors impact their total bill. Right now, we anticipate affirmative customer outreach and education on TOU to begin in December 2025/January 2026 and ramp up through Q1 and Q2 leading up to the June 2026 bundled TOU offering. By bundling the TOU rate and rolling out the TOU offerings in 2026, ComEd will be able to use one set of marketing and educational materials, which will better enable ComEd to provide clear and consistent messaging to support informed customer rate decisions. In addition, TOU information will be reflected in our customer-facing online bill comparison tool.

**Q.** Is ComEd open to feedback and suggestions regarding customer education and outreach?

**A.** Yes. ComEd remains committed to Staff's proposed collaboration timeline on customer outreach and educational materials for the DTOU rate as memorialized in the January Order and intends to collaborate with interested stakeholders including Staff, CUB/EDF, and ICEA/RESA on its customer outreach and education plans. Final Order at 167. ComEd also welcomes recommendations and feedback related to the bundled TOU rate.

## V. INTEGRATED DISTRIBUTION COMPANY WAIVER

**Q.** What are the Integrated Distribution Company ("IDC") Rules?

**A.** The IDC Rules are rules utilities must follow if they elect to structure their business as an IDC, rather than a Functionally Separated Utility. At a high level, an IDC is a utility which

[p. 13]

does not actively participate in the retail electric supply market and is prohibited from attempting to obtain or retain electric supply customers. See generally Ill. Admin. Code tit. 83, pt. 452, subpt. B, §§ 452.200 et seq. The IDC Rules prohibit utilities that elected to become an IDC (like ComEd) from promoting advertising or marketing regarding the offering or provision of the retail sale of electricity. However, the Rules allow an IDC to enter into contracts for or promote programs for load curtailment and interruption as long as they are conducted pursuant to a Commission-approved tariff.

**Q.** Does Rate BEST contain many of the same elements and value-drivers as a load curtailment and interruption program?

<!-- EPON: goal, midday-peak — ComEd characterizes Rate BEST as a demand response program in which customers "curtail or delay" usage in response to price signals; used to argue marketing is a Permissible IDC Service -->

**A.** Yes. Fundamentally, both Rate BEST and load curtailment and interruption programs are demand response programs in which customers may curtail or delay their energy usage in response to economic incentives or price signals. For example, like a demand response program, a well-designed TOU will ultimately help reduce peak demand over time. Thus, Rate BEST is conceptually very similar to ComEd's load curtailment and interruption tariffs and should be considered to be a Permissible IDC Service.

**Q.** Would the advertising and marketing of Rate BEST contribute to greater customer education regarding TOU rates?

<!-- EPON: goal, bill-comparison — customer education framing: educate customers on their own consumption patterns, appliance usage characteristics, cost of power, supply and demand; "well-informed customers" exercising choice -->

**A.** Yes. As I explained above, a customer education and information campaign will be needed to bring awareness of this new program to the attention of customers and allow them to decide to enroll. In addition, a major focus of an STOU program is to educate the customer as to the customer's unique consumption patterns, the usage characteristics of various appliances, the cost of power and energy, and the interplay of supply and demand in the

[p. 14]

energy markets. This means Rate BEST will help lay the foundation for a market in which well-informed customers can learn to more effectively exercise choice and to exert control over their energy consumption.

**Q.** Will ComEd's ability to market Rate BEST have an impact on the rate's success?

<!-- EPON: goal, bundling — marketing target: direct marketing primarily to Rate BES (default flat) customers; no marketing to Rate BESH customers or RES customers; success premised on customers "shifting their usage" -->

**A.** Yes. Customer outreach for Rate BEST is important because without appropriate educational materials, customers are unlikely to understand or enroll in the rate, and will miss out on the opportunity to save money by participating in Rate BEST and shifting their usage. ComEd plans to primarily provide direct marketing materials to Rate BES customers and will not market to customers that have elected service under Rate BESH or with a RES.

**Q.** Will ComEd seek a waiver of the IDC Rules if the Commission determines that implementation of Rate BEST requires one?

**A.** Yes. While I am not testifying in any capacity as an attorney in this proceeding, I understand that, as a utility that elected to become an IDC, ComEd is constrained from promoting, advertising, and marketing any supply service. If the Commission determines that Rate BEST is not within the category of Permissible IDC Services (specifically related to curtailment and interruption programs and customer education described above), ComEd will seek a waiver. ComEd obtained a similar waiver when it implemented Rate RTOUPP and Rider RRTP – Residential Real Time Pricing.[^3] See Commonwealth Edison

[^3]: Residential Real Time Pricing is ComEd's implementation of its hourly supply rate, Rate BESH, for residential customers

[p. 15]

Co., Docket No. 08-0411, Order (Oct. 8, 2008); Commonwealth Edison Co., Docket Nos. 18-1725/18-1824 (cons.), Order (Oct. 2, 2019).

## VI. CONCLUSION

**Q.** In sum, what action do you recommend that Commission take on rehearing?

**A.** I recommend that the Commission find that ComEd has met the directive to provide, within 120 days of the Final Order, an STOU and bundled rate strawman. Final Order at 189-190. I also encourage the Commission to consider the evidence provided, including the benefits Rate BEST will provide customers and efficiencies created when bundled with the recently-approved DTOU rate. Finally, if the Commission approves Rate BEST, I recommend that the Commission provide guidance on whether Rate BEST falls within the category of Permissible IDC Services.

**Q.** Will ComEd's DTOU be implemented if Rate BEST is not approved?

<!-- EPON: bundling, goal — DTOU will be implemented by the January 2026 billing period regardless of the Rate BEST outcome; the Order approved the DTOU as a separate offering -->

**A.** Yes. Whether ComEd's proposed Rate BEST and its associated STOU rate design is adopted, as proposed or as modified and agreed to by ComEd, or rejected, is up to the Commission on rehearing. It is ComEd's understanding that the January Order approved ComEd's DTOU as a separate rate offering available independent of the outcome of this STOU strawman proposal. ComEd will comply with the January Order fully, regardless. Thus, even were the Commission to reject Rate BEST, ComEd will, pursuant to the directives of the Commission's Final Order in this proceeding, implement the recently-approved DTOU proposal by the January 2026 monthly billing period.

[p. 16]

**Q.** Does that conclude your direct testimony on rehearing?

**A.** Yes.
