**PUBLIC UTILITIES REGULATORY AUTHORITY**

November 17, 2026

In reply, please refer to:

Docket No. 26-05-10

To: Docket Participants:

Enclosed are interrogatories numbered RRU-221 through RRU-241.

Interrogatories numbered RRU-221 through RRU-228 are directed to the Office of Consumer Counsel (OCC). Interrogatories numbered RRU-229 through RRU-233 are directed to the Conservation Law Foundation (CLF). Interrogatories numbered RRU-234 through RRU-235 are directed to the University of Connecticut and the UConn Health System (UConn). Interrogatories numbered RRU-236 through RRU-241 are directed to The Connecticut Light and Power Company d/b/a Eversource Energy (Company).

The responses to these questions have been deemed necessary by the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (Authority) in connection with the above-referenced docket and will become a part of the record in this proceeding. Please note that persons responsible for responses must be available for cross-examination during a hearing, if held, and must attest to the truth and accuracy of their responses.

Please submit the responses to the Authority’s Executive Secretary no later than December 1, 2026, at 4:00 p.m. The Authority encourages the submission of responses as soon as they are available rather than delaying until all answers are completed. Please list each question and answer on the same sheet of paper using a separate sheet for each interrogatory number, and sequentially number the pages of each response (e.g., p. 1 of 5, etc.). At the top of each page, clearly identify the Docket Number, the interrogatory number (including the prefix), the name of the company and the person responsible for the response, and the date of submission.

Similarly, each Late Filed Exhibit that may be submitted after hearing in this docket shall be sequentially numbered and identified with the Docket Number, Late Filed Exhibit Number, page number, the name of the company and the person responsible for the exhibit, and the date of submission.

If a response to any of the requested information has already been filed in response to another Parties’ interrogatory(ies) in this proceeding, please reference the interrogatory which provides the information. It is the respondent’s responsibility to ensure that any such information is on file with the Executive Secretary of the Authority.

**DOCKET NO. 26-05-10**
**APPLICATION OF THE CONNECTICUT LIGHT AND POWER COMPANY D/B/A EVERSOURCE ENERGY TO AMEND ITS RATE SCHEDULES**

Interrogatories RRU-221 through RRU-228 are directed at the Office of Consumer Counsel (OCC) and refer to Prefiled Testimony submitted by Melissa Whited on behalf of OCC.

## RRU-221

RRU-221 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Whited PFT, p. 25. Confirm that the condition of revenue neutrality should be construed to mean revenue neutral prior to the impact of heat pump adoption. In other words, an average customer switching from Rate 1 to Rate 6 should expect to pay the same annual cost, assuming no change to their load profile.

## RRU-222

RRU-222 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Whited PFT, pp. 29-36. Did OCC conduct analysis showing how household energy costs would change under the proposed rate design? If so, provide the results and any associated workpapers.

## RRU-223

RRU-223 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Whited PFT, p. 36. Is a summer on-peak:off-peak ratio of 4:3:1 necessary to incentivize load shifting? Explain whether this ratio is punitive for non-heat pump load. Why or why not?

## RRU-224

RRU-224 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Whited PFT, pp. 29-36. The residential fixed charge may only recover “fixed costs and operation and maintenance expenses directly related to metering, billing, service connections and the provision of customer service,” except for a residential electric heating service rate class. General Statutes § 16-243(b). Does OCC’s recommended rate structure require revisions in light of this statutory requirement?

## RRU-225

RRU-225 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Whited PFT, p. 31. Is OCC’s proposal to use intraday price variation contingent on the Company’s ability to modify the on-peak period to 4pm to 8pm on weekdays? Would OCC still recommend intraday price variation if the on-peak window for residential rate classes remains 12pm to 8pm on weekdays?

## RRU-226

RRU-226 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Whited PFT, p. 27. Does OCC believe that limiting rate enrollment to only customers who switch from delivered fuel to a heat pump presents a risk of undue discrimination?

## RRU-227

RRU-227 (COINCIDENT PEAK TRANSMISSION RATE) Perkins PFT. Does OCC support UConn’s proposal for an opt-in coincident peak transmission charge for large commercial customers? Explain why or why not.

## RRU-228

RRU-228 (ACOS) Whited PFT, pp. 15-18. Explain how geographic density would be converted into a numeric allocator for purposes of cost allocation.

Interrogatories RRU-229 through RRU-233 are directed at Conservation Law Foundation (CLF) and refer to Prefiled Testimony submitted by Juan Pablo Velez on behalf of CLF.

## RRU-229

RRU-229 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Velez PFT. Explain whether the proposed rate design would apply downward rate pressure for customers who do not adopt heat pump systems.

## RRU-230

RRU-230 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Velez PFT. Has a state utility commission approved a technology-specific rate designed to recover only the incremental cost of serving new load? If so, provide the state, utility, and docket number.

[PDF p. 4]

## RRU-231

RRU-231 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Velez PFT, Ex. JPV-2, p. 3. Provide evidentiary support for the claim that ResStock “systematically overstates electricity consumption for multifamily buildings.”

## RRU-232

RRU-232 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Velez PFT, p. 62. Explain why the analysis uses the AESC regional average value for bundled marginal distribution and transmission costs ($69/kW-year) rather than, for example, the Company’s MCOS study.

## RRU-233

RRU-233 (HEAT PUMP RATE) Velez PFT, pp. 93-94. Estimate the rate impact of automatic enrollment of existing heat pump customers on the residential class.

Interrogatories RRU-234 through RRU-235 are directed at the University of Connecticut and the UConn Health System (UConn) and refer to Prefiled Testimony submitted by Eben Perkins on behalf of UConn.

## RRU-234

RRU-234 (COINCIDENT PEAK TRANSMISSION RATE) Perkins PFT, p. 16. Explain why a distribution rate case is the proper venue to address transmission rate design rather than, for example, the annual Rate Adjustment Mechanism (RAM) proceedings (i.e., for Eversource’s 2026 RAM proceeding, Docket No. 26-01-03).

## RRU-235

RRU-235 (DISTRIBUTION DEMAND CHARGE RATCHET) Perkins PFT, p. 43. What would be a more appropriate ratchet for the distribution demand charges compared to the existing eleven-month ratchet?

Interrogatories RRU-236 through RRU-241 are directed at The Connecticut Light and Power Company (CL&P or Company).

## RRU-236

RRU-236 (COINCIDENT PEAK TRANSMISSION RATE) Perkins PFT. Can the Company’s current metering and billing systems support UConn’s proposed opt-in coincident peak transmission rate structure? Explain why or why not.

## RRU-237

RRU-237 (COINCIDENT PEAK TRANSMISSION RATE) Perkins PFT, p. 29. UConn states that the Company “already manually bills some Rates 55-58 accounts.” Confirm whether this is true, and if so, explain how manual billing is implemented.

## RRU-238

RRU-238 (COINCIDENT PEAK TRANSMISSION RATE) Perkins PFT. Does the Company support UConn’s proposal for an opt-in coincident peak transmission charge? Explain why or why not.

## RRU-239

RRU-239 (TIME-OF-USE RATES) Explain the changes necessary to modify the on-peak window to 4pm to 8pm on weekdays using the Company’s current metering and billing systems.

## RRU-240

RRU-240 (TIME-OF-USE RATES) Can the Company’s current metering and billing systems support seasonally differentiated rates (i.e., separate charges for summer and winter)? If not, explain.

## RRU-241

RRU-241 (TIME-OF-USE RATES) Can the Company’s current metering and billing systems support time-differentiated distribution charges (both volumetric and demand)? If not, explain.
