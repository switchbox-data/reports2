## Summary Recommendations
As part of the EmPOWER Maryland planning process, the Commission is required
to consider multiple factors, including cost effectiveness, non-energy benefits, impacts on
jobs, the environment, greenhouse gas reduction goals, and “the impact on rates of each
ratepayer class.”[^c1] Given the significant, present-day affordability challenges faced by
many Marylanders, the Joint Exelon Utilities respectfully urge that the Commission be
particularly mindful of the “impact on rates”, and how its decisions in setting goals and
approving plans for the EmPOWER 2027-2029 cycle will impact the EmPOWER
surcharge and near-term customer affordability. While the Joint Exelon Utilities continue
to support the EmPOWER Maryland program’s long-term benefits and the State’s
greenhouse gas reduction goals, it is critical that all stakeholders, including the
Commission, utilities, DHCD, and advocacy groups, not lose sight of the fact that
customers pay energy bills on a monthly basis, not on a lifecycle cost-benefits basis.
The Commission’s decisions in setting goals and budgets for the next EmPOWER
program cycle, including decisions on program design such as determining EmPOWER
is cost-beneficial on a total portfolio level and not on a program-by-program basis, are
foundational to what customers see on their utility bills. These Commission decisions
have real, tangible impacts on the amount of the EmPOWER surcharge that customers
will have to pay each month at a time when many struggle to manage their day-to-day
household budgets. Near-term affordability must be top of mind when making decisions
about the costs of the next EmPOWER program cycle.

[^c1]: Md. Code, Section 7-225(d)(3) of the Public Utilities Article.

[PDF p. 3 | printed p. 2]

BGE does not believe EmPOWER program budgets of this size are in alignment
with the State policy of reducing customer bills and affordability as clearly expressed in
the Utility RELIEF Act (Reducing Energy Load Inflation for Everyday Families). The
General Assembly has already provided clear direction in this regard, and the direction is
to lower the EmPOWER surcharge. The Commission’s decisions about goal levels and
program costs for the next EmPOWER program cycle must follow this clear mandate from
the General Assembly. The Utility RELIEF Act, is an omnibus energy bill intended to
provide meaningful, near-term bill relief for customers.[^c2] The Act relies on lowering the
EmPOWER Maryland surcharge as a means of providing near-term bill savings for
customers. The Act reduces the surcharge by: (1) redirecting $100 Million of certain State
special funds to lower the surcharge in 2027; (2) significantly lowering the EmPOWER
energy and greenhouse gas savings goals, with the intent of lowering the surcharge costs
required to meet the goals; and (3) allowing utilities to rely on GHG savings associated
with non-EmPOWER funded solar projects in the State to count towards up to 20 percent
of the utility’s GHG savings goal for the 2027 to 2029 EmPOWER program cycle.[^c3]
The Commission’s role in planning the next EmPOWER cycle for 2027 through
2029 is to determine the GHG savings goals for utilities based on the energy savings
baseline set forth in statute (which the General Assembly reduced significantly in the
Utility RELIEF Act, from 2.5% to 1.75%), and to determine whether plans proposed to
meet those goals are “adequate and cost-effective.”

[^c2]: The Maryland Department of Legislative Services’ fiscal analysis of the Act found that “electric and gas
utility rates decrease”, and specifically, that the Act is expected to have a meaningful effect on rates in the
near term through lowering the EmPOWER surcharge by: (1) directing that $100 Million in certain State
special funds be used to lower the surcharge in 2027; and (2) decreasing GHG reduction targets, which is
expected to “significantly reduce the EmPOWER surcharge for those customers relative to what it otherwise
would have been.” Fiscal and Policy Note, House Bill 1532, at pages 11-12.

[^c3]: The Maryland Department of Legislative Services, Major Issues Review, 2023-2026, at H12 and H19.

On July 10, 2026, the Commission directed utilities to submit EmPOWER program
plans and budgets for the next cycle for its consideration. The order states that due to
“concerns about the on-going cost of the EmPOWER Program,” it is “prudent to consider
a range of outcomes that satisfy goal establishment.” (Order at 9). Thus, the Commission
directed the utilities to prepare and submit plans to achieve GHG savings goals based on
two different methodologies; one proposed by Commission Staff (“Staff”) and Maryland’s
Department of Environment (“MDE”), and another utilizing the first methodology but re-weighting such that 100 percent of the weighting is an estimated useful life (“EUL”) of
seven, which is similar to the methodology proposed by the Joint Exelon Utilities and
supported by Potomac Edison and the Maryland Energy Administration. The second
methodology, which utilizes an EUL of seven, is expected to result in greater surcharge
savings for customers than the methodology put forth by Staff and MDE.

While the Joint Exelon Utilities have complied with the Commission’s directive by
submitting plans for meeting greenhouse gas reduction goals under both methodologies,
the proposal in front of the Commission that is more clearly in line with the intent of the
current statute is the Joint Exelon Utility proposal, which meets the goal developed
utilizing an EUL of seven. Now, the Commission must decide what goal level and what
plans are appropriate for approval.[^c4] These decisions will impact the surcharge amount
that customers will be responsible for paying each month, starting in 2027. In setting the
greenhouse gas savings goals for the next program cycle and in deciding on plans and
budgets for meeting those goals, the Joint Exelon Utilities again urge the Commission to

The General Assembly did not direct the Commission to set greenhouse gas emission

considered. And the method that the General Assembly has chosen to provide near-term,

with the expectation that doing so will also facilitate the lowering of the EmPOWER
Maryland surcharge.

At a minimum, the Joint Exelon Utilities believe that this clear legislative intent
should lead to the Commission adopting the methodology for establishing greenhouse
gas savings goals for the next cycle that utilizes an EUL of seven. This will result in greater

that reducing EmPOWER costs and associated surcharge impacts is an important means
of providing meaningful near-term bill relief for Maryland customers. The Commission
should therefore implement the statute in a manner that effectuates that legislative
determination while continuing to satisfy the State's greenhouse gas reduction objectives.
In addition, the Commission should carefully consider any other authorities it thinks
it has under current law to provide near-term bill relief for customers in making its
greenhouse gas reduction goal decisions, and in considering utility and DHCD plans for
meeting such goals. This includes the Joint Exelon Utility request herein that the

provisions demonstrate that the Commission retains meaningful discretion in establishing
greenhouse gas savings goals and evaluating plans and budgets proposed to achieve
those goals. These provisions also make clear that the General Assembly did not intend
for greenhouse gas reduction objectives to be pursued without regard to customer
affordability or rate impacts. To the contrary, the Utility RELIEF Act reflects the General
Assembly's most recent direction that affordability considerations and surcharge impacts

consider rate impacts and seek to minimize surcharge burdens on customers to the
greatest extent permitted by law. If the Commission believes that it lacks the authority

Exelon Utilities respectfully submit that the Commission should seek legislative changes

manner that best balances customer near-term affordability needs with long-term cost
effectiveness and the State’s greenhouse gas reduction goals.

## 1. Executive Summary: Centering Customer Affordability While Driving Customer Value

### 1.1 Introduction & Summary of Proposal

BGE submits the following EmPOWER program portfolio proposal at a pivotal moment
for both the program and the customers it serves. The 2027-2029 EmPOWER cycle
(Cycle 7) marks the first full three-year cycle under a GHG emissions reduction
framework. At the same time, programs are facing increasing delivery costs associated
with more rigorous evaluation, verification, and measurement (EM&V) requirements that
make every ton of GHG emissions savings more expensive to achieve as lower-cost
measures become less valuable over time. In addition to these pressures, customers are
facing significant affordability challenges across almost every aspect of their lives, from
the rising cost of housing to increased energy capacity prices. In designing both portfolios
presented here, BGE operated with an objective to achieve the Commission’s BGE-specific lifecycle GHG reduction goal while minimizing customer costs. After careful
evaluation of program performance, customer impacts, and cost effectiveness, BGE
recommends that the Commission approve the Low Scenario Portfolio, which allows BGE
to reduce BGE’s portion of the EmPOWER surcharge to a greater extent than the High
Scenario without compromising the quality program offerings that customers have come
to expect or our commitment to reducing GHG emissions.

For nearly two decades, Maryland's EmPOWER program has served as the state's
flagship energy program and one of the most effective tools for addressing energy and
environmental challenges. Since its inception in 2008, EmPOWER has reduced electricity
demand, deferred infrastructure costs, reduced utility bills for participants by an estimated
$861M, and helped with managing demand and load. Equally important, the program has
provided financial incentives that have enabled many Maryland homeowners,
businesses, and institutions to invest in services and technologies that lower operating
costs, improve productivity, reduce GHG emissions, and support compliance with current
building standards. Baltimore Gas and Electric (BGE) has administered EmPOWER
programs since the launch of EmPOWER in 2009. During this time, we have made it our
mission to help customers achieve energy savings through our programming, as
approved by the Maryland Public Service Commission (Commission or PSC), with a focus
on designing and delivering award-winning, globally recognized, innovative programs and
strategies. BGE was among the first utilities to leverage advanced metering infrastructure
(AMI) data to enhance both behavioral and building efficiency programs. BGE’s
PeakRewardsSM Program was one of the first, largest, and most prominent modern
residential direct load control programs in the United States, exceeding 350,000

[PDF p. 12 | printed p. 2]

participating devices as far back as 2011. BGE reinvented Retrocommissioning, creating
what is now recognized nationally as the Building Tune-up Program. During the COVID-
19 pandemic, BGE pioneered the application of Virtual Commissioning techniques to
continue delivering customer value despite significant operational challenges. In total,
nearly $1.7B in EmPOWER rebates and credits have been provided to BGE customers
through our programs. Moreover, BGE has partnered with over 640 Maryland contractors
who have supported EmPOWER programs, driving economic development across the
region. The Utility RELIEF Act, enacted during the 2026 legislative session, reduced
EmPOWER goals for next cycle. BGE remains steadfast in our support of EmPOWER
Maryland and is deeply proud of the grid and environmental benefits the program has
delivered since its inception. BGE employees have dedicated decades-long careers to
helping customers reduce energy costs, improve the comfort and safety of their homes
and businesses, and advance Maryland’s climate goals. BGE recognizes that
EmPOWER has produced significant net benefits and has become a national model for
other energy efficiency and demand-side management programs in the country. Those
achievements are a direct result of BGE’s commitment to the program and our partnership
with the state, the PSC, our customers, trade allies, and community organizations that
have participated in the program for many years.

As BGE developed the proposed portfolios for this upcoming cycle, we found ourselves
in the position of having to balance multiple stakeholder perspectives and competing
policy objectives resulting from the passage of the Utility RELIEF Act. We heard clearly
from Governor Moore, the General Assembly, and customers that near-term affordability
concerns warranted modifications to the program. At the same time, we heard from the
Maryland Energy Efficiency Advocates (MEEA) and other environmental advocates, who
urged the state to maintain the higher statutory savings goals, citing EmPOWER’s long-term customer, economic, and environmental benefits – such longer-term benefits we
wholeheartedly acknowledge. We also heard from our customers whose views on the
program span a broad spectrum, as well as from our trade allies whose businesses
depend on a strong and stable EmPOWER market. These perspectives reflect genuine
concerns on near-term affordability, the economy, progress on climate goals, and long-term energy costs. BGE carefully considered each perspective as we developed this plan.
At this time, when customers are facing higher costs for many essential items and
services like food, housing, medical care and energy, we urge the Commission to
exercise the full extent of its authority to be mindful of how its EmPOWER decisions on
goal setting and plan approval contribute to overall near-term bill affordability for
customers, while continuing to maintain meaningful energy savings and GHG goal
reduction progress. Along these lines, of the two GHG goals that the Commission has
directed the utilities to plan for, BGE believes the Low Scenario proposal before the

[PDF p. 13 | printed p. 3]

Commission represents the best path forward under the Utility RELIEF Act that preserves
the core value of EmPOWER Maryland, while reflecting the policy direction provided by
elected leaders and the diverse stakeholder feedback we received and listened to
throughout this process. Our proposal is intended to balance these tradeoffs with an
emphasis on making the EmPOWER surcharge more affordable. BGE developed its
Cycle 7 portfolio with a focus on affordability while continuing to deliver meaningful
benefits to customers. Under the Low Scenario, the total cost of the Cycle 7 portfolio is
$298M less than the current cycle. When excluding the 2026 imbalances[^f1], an average
residential combined electric and gas customer would have an estimated reduction in the
2027 surcharge of $90.68 compared to the 2026 surcharges.

Adding to the complexity of striving to make EmPOWER more affordable, Cycle 7 also
brings a reduction in the amount of claimable savings available and increased complexity
in how those savings are determined. Lighting measures, which accounted for
approximately half of the Non-Residential subportfolio and represented some of the most
cost-effective measures in Cycle 6, will be worth approximately 70% less on January 1,
2027, than they were worth on December 31, 2026, due solely to EM&V changes. The
decision of the Future Programming Work Group (FPWG) to move from an annual
emissions rate to a marginal emissions rate, combined with HB 864’s requirement to
utilize lifecycle savings instead of annual, has required that we now try to estimate the
GHG emissions reductions of each measure on the scale of hours over many years, as
opposed to utilizing a single savings number. With so many changes and greater
complexity pushing up portfolio costs, BGE worked diligently to make the EmPOWER
surcharge more affordable in both scenarios modeled, though we believe that the
Commission should select the scenario that reduces the surcharge the most to “give
people a little bit of relief at a time when that becomes so necessary.”[^f2]

alignment and the most value to customers. With the temporary reduction in utility goals
for this three-year period, the plans for programming are less reliant on low-impact
measures, high-cost measures, and those with higher evaluation, measurement, and
verification (EM&V) risk, such as advanced power strips or energy efficiency kits. This

Another area of uncertainty in the 2027–2029 planning process is the Demand Response

participation and megawatt (MW) goals and that utilities detail the budgets required to

increasingly dynamic, but the utilities will remain at the forefront of the various efforts and

##### Request for Reconsideration of Non-Residential Lighting Evaluation Methodology
BGE respectfully requests that the Commission order the Independent Evaluator to
reconsider its non-residential lighting savings methodology to ensure alignment with the
Maryland-specific market.

In 2026, the Independent Evaluator (IE) adopted several changes to the non-residential
lighting savings methodology, which will take effect at the start of Cycle 7. These changes
will reduce the amount of savings that can be claimed from efficient LED lighting
measures by approximately 70%. This has turned measures with some of the lowest cost
per ton of GHG saved in the EmPOWER portfolio into measures with some of the highest
cost per ton of GHG saved.

Many of the savings methodology changes stem from the IE’s assumption that the
commercial lighting market in Maryland is already saturated with LEDs and a resulting
belief that potential free ridership should be taken into account. While this approach may
be appropriate in states such as California which have banned the sale of fluorescent
lighting for use in commercial buildings and shifted their baselines for this building type to
LEDs, it does not for Maryland. Unlike in California, fluorescent lighting continues to be
readily available in Maryland, making it still very easy for businesses, non-profits, and
other commercial customers to choose the less efficient, cheaper lighting option.
In Cycle 6, lighting measures accounted for a large majority of non-residential savings.
With the contribution of lighting measures being reduced by 70%, it will not be possible
to incentivize enough lighting measures to achieve the same proportion of non-residential
savings. Therefore, in order to achieve the Commission-directed goals, a greater
proportion of savings, and the commensurate costs, must be shifted to residential
customers. At a high level, without performing the full modeling due to time constraints,
BGE estimates that this change alone has resulted in at least a 10% increase to the total
portfolio budget. BGE disagrees with the IE’s methodology changes for the reasons
expressed above. Additionally, implementing these misguided changes at this time, is
particularly troublesome, given the affordability challenges faced by residential customers
and the recent enactment of the Utility RELIEF Act, with the express intent of reducing
the residential EmPOWER surcharge for customers in the interest of affordability. BGE
does not believe it is in residential or non-residential customers best interests to
implement these methodology changes at any time, and particularly now, to make such
a drastic change in the assumptions around lighting measures that can, and have,
delivered energy efficiency and greenhouse gas emission reduction benefits to Maryland
and customers.

## 2. EmPOWER Legislation Overview and Impacts

### 2.1 Summary of Legislation

After adopting the Climate Solutions Now Act (CSNA) in 2022, which established
statewide decarbonization goals, the Maryland General Assembly adopted HB 864 in
2024 to articulate the EmPOWER program’s role in support of the CSNA. HB 864
reoriented EmPOWER by replacing the historic energy-savings framework with a GHG
reduction framework, expanding eligible program objectives to include beneficial
electrification, establishing GHG reduction targets, and articulating the role for both
behind-the-meter and front-of-the-meter measures, requiring that most reductions come
from behind-the-meter measures.

On May 12, 2026, Governor Wes Moore signed the Utility RELIEF Act into law. This bill
has significant impacts on the EmPOWER program including reducing program goals
from 2.5% to 1.75% of a 2016 baseline, removing gas utilities from the program, allowing
utilities to fulfill up to 20% of their obligation with solar, and instructing the Commission to
release a Request for Information on third-party program implementation. Together, HB
864 and the Utility RELIEF Act transformed EmPOWER from a traditional energy
efficiency program into a GHG reduction program, balancing climate objectives with
customer affordability.

### 2.2 Impact of Legislation on EmPOWER Goals

In legislation, both in HB 864 and the Utility RELIEF Act, EmPOWER’s lifecycle GHG
reduction goals are a translation of annual energy savings goals based on a percentage
of 2016 retail electricity sales. The translation from the clearly defined annual energy
savings to lifecycle GHG reduction requires two critical inputs to develop final goals: a
conversion from energy savings to GHG reduction and a conversion from annual savings
to lifecycle savings.

To convert electric energy savings to GHG reduction, the Utility RELIEF Act specifies the
use of the most recent emissions projections from the Maryland Department of the
Environment (MDE). Future Programming Work Group (FPWG) members developed a
methodology for estimating marginal emissions rates using current data from the U.S.
Department of Energy and PJM. This methodology was accepted by the group and
adopted by MDE as their most recent emissions projection. The new marginal emissions
rates used in developing the electric utility lifecycle GHG reduction goals and this resulting
plan for 2027–2029 vary significantly from the current emissions rates used in the

[PDF pp. 24-26 not transcribed: Sections 2.3 (Future Programming Work Group outcomes: comparability of Cycle 6 and Cycle 7 emissions rates, estimated-useful-life assumptions in the FPWG report and Order No. 92522, DHCD savings projections) and 2.4 (alignment of goals with the MDE plan)]

[PDF p. 27 | printed p. 17]

The baseline (2016 electric sales) and energy savings goal percentage (1.75%) were
both set in the Utility RELIEF Act by the state legislature. Alignment with the MDE plan
comes from the emissions intensity calculations, which HB 864 required come from
MDE’s most recent data and projections. The final portion of the equation, translation of
the goal from annual to lifecycle using an EUL estimate, was left up to the Commission in
the Utility RELIEF Act. Because the same MDE emissions intensities were used when
calculating a goal using EULs of seven and 9.6, both goal-setting methodologies are
equally in alignment with the MDE plan.

## 3. Portfolio Analysis and Program Design

### 3.1 Portfolio Objectives and Themes

When BGE set out to design and model the High and Low Scenarios, BGE kept customer
affordability, customer experience, and compliance with the Utility RELIEF Act at the
forefront. BGE sought to design two implementable portfolios that balance measures and
programs that produce reliable lifecycle GHG savings with innovative new delivery

factors — have collectively reduced many of the low-cost savings opportunities in the
non-residential sector. The external factors influencing this portfolio design are discussed
in more detail in section 3.3, but broadly the significant reductions in commercial lighting
savings paired with a major increase in residential electrification investment has led to the

### 3.2 Stakeholder Outreach and Coordination

#### 3.2.1 Stakeholder Engagement Overview

Stakeholder engagement is a critical component of the EmPOWER cycle planning
process. Without the valuable input we receive from parties with viewpoints other than
our own, we could not develop or implement programs that rise to the standards that BGE
holds itself to. BGE developed a stakeholder strategy as one of the first activities in the
planning process to ensure that we would have enough time to not only engage these
parties near the end once the plan was solidified, but build relationships, have multiple
conversations, and truly listen to their ideas and feedback. BGE’s approach to
engagement was grounded in ensuring that we talked to a wide range of stakeholders,
not only the usual intervenors, but also parties who might feel removed from what can
often be an extremely technical process.

engagement process early at the beginning of the year. We sought input from a broad
range of stakeholders, many of whom we have engaged continually in the past as well as
newer stakeholders. The BGE team sought feedback from stakeholders on current
offerings, potential new offerings, and other opportunities for the BGE EmPOWER
Portfolio in Cycle 7. We endeavored to listen to every perspective and hear candid
feedback. We met with advocacy groups, community organizations, contractors and trade
groups, energy efficiency industry groups, environmental advocates, as well as State and
County Government agencies and departments.

#### 3.3.1 Existing Portfolio Analysis and Key Updates

BGE analyzed the 2025–2026 EmPOWER MD Program Filing Additional Plan
Information Case No. 9705 (ML 312026) in conjunction with actual performance data from
the 2025 EmPOWER program year as a basis for development of the 2027–2029
program plan. The 2025–2026 Additional Program Filing was the first EmPOWER filing
to incorporate a specific GHG reduction goal for each utility. As such, these recent
portfolio designs and results provided a better starting point for planning the 2027–2029
EmPOWER portfolio than other historic cycle plans.

[PDF p. 32 | printed p. 22]

The FPWG and Evaluation Advisory Group (EAG) provided updated GHG emissions
values for the 2027–2029 cycle that differ from those used from 2025–2026. The updated
emissions factors used marginal GHG emissions instead of average annual GHG
emissions. Marginal GHG emissions are calculated using the estimated emissions from
marginal generation that increases or decreases to meet a change in demand during peak
energy usage periods. The marginal generation brought online during peak periods often
has the worst GHG emissions profiles for electrical generation.

To create a baseline of existing programs and measure offerings, BGE applied the
updated marginal GHG emissions factors to the 2025 historical portfolio performance data
to provide an equivalent comparison of current program performance applied to potential
2027–2029 performance. Figure 3 shows the 2025 portfolio performance when translating

the modeling of the plans submitted and the numbers ultimately used by the Independent
Evaluator when the programs are evaluated later in the cycle.

Program evaluation results also influenced portfolio design and individual measure

contributions of non-residential lighting measures on the GHG emissions reductions
targets will be reduced by as much as 70% in Cycle 7 compared to Cycle 6. Much like the
reductions in residential lighting in past filings, non-residential lighting savings are a much
smaller proportion of the 2027–2029 portfolio plan.

#### 3.3.2 BGE Scenario Development

Commission Order No. 92522, published on July 10, 2026, requires utilities to submit two
portfolio plan scenarios reflecting two potential GHG emissions reduction goals. The High
Scenario Plan, which meets the goal resulting from the methodology proposed by
Commission Staff and MDE, was mainly developed in early 2026 by examining the 2024–
2026 EmPOWER filings and applying adjustments to program offerings and GHG
emissions. The Low Scenario Plan, which is based on a goal where the weighted average
EUL is seven, as was proposed by the Joint Exelon Utilities, was developed using historic
program performance and applying an average EUL based on the utilities’ historic mix of
measures implemented or installed by its customers.

The Low Scenario is met by removing some of the less effective program offerings across
the portfolio. Low Scenario budget savings are achieved through a combination of
program sunsetting, measure cuts within continuing programs, incentive and non-incentive reductions, and reorganization of existing programs within the portfolio. It shifts
a significant portion of investment to electrification and home performance for residential
customers and allows non-residential programs to target the highest yield opportunities.
Moreover, this scenario removes measures that have seen stakeholder pushback due to
low cost-effectiveness or evaluation risk, as well as those that do not reflect current
market needs. The Low Scenario offers meaningful surcharge reduction without creating
significant gaps in BGE’s offerings—customers maintain access to efficiency incentives
across nearly all sectors, equipment types, and use cases. Overall, the Low Scenario
represents the streamlined demand-side management portfolio that BGE believes best
balances system benefits with customer cost savings and near-term surcharge relief.
Overall, the plan for the High Scenario largely continues BGE’s existing portfolio but with
the optimization described in the previous section. In the High Scenario, no programs are
entirely cut, except for Smart Energy Academy, with the intention of continuing to provide
as wide a range of efficiency options as possible for customers to participate in
EmPOWER which, in turn, gives BGE the greatest chance of meeting a higher goal.

### 4.2 Efficient Products (Appliance Rebates, Appliance Recycling)

#### 4.2.1 Program Overview

The BGE Efficient Products Program is comprised of two components: the
BGE Residential Appliance Rebate offering and the BGE Residential Appliance
Recycling offering.

The BGE Residential Appliance Rebate component encourages consumers to
purchase higher efficiency appliances by offering incentives for select ENERGY STAR
certified products through multiple sales channels. The offering first launched in 2008 and
offered rebate incentives for ENERGY STAR product measures including refrigerators,
clothes washers, and room air conditioners. During the 2024–2026 cycle, the Appliance
Rebate Program provided incentives through a multitude of delivery channels including
the ENERGY STAR Retail Products Platform (ESRPP); verified instant, post-purchase,
direct-to-consumer kits distributed via non-profit organizations; instant markdowns; and
midstream via distributors. ESRPP incentives are not paid to customers; they are an
upstream incentive paid to retailers to encourage and support energy-efficient
manufacture and retailer stocking habits. BGE requests flexibility to augment delivery
channels and measures (along with their respective incentive caps) offered within the
program during Cycle 7 to react to market adoption and to best serve customers.
Because the incentives are more reflective of the purchasing trends of each measure,
this flexibility will allow BGE to more efficiently manage spending and deliver greater
benefits to our customers.

The BGE Residential Appliance Recycling component offers residential customers
the opportunity to recycle their inefficient operational appliances. BGE was an
EPA Responsible Appliance Disposal (RAD) Partner until EPA sunset the program in
2025. The BGE Appliance Recycling Program ensures that all appliances are recycled

in an environmentally responsible way that continues to meet the former EPA RAD
standards. BGE residential customers can schedule an appliance pickup appointment to
turn in large appliances (refrigerators or freezers) as well as up to two room air
conditioners, compact refrigerators, or dehumidifiers at the same time. BGE maximizes
program accessibility by ensuring sufficient appointment availability and hosting
additional recycling turn-in events throughout the year. Turn-in events allow customers to
recycle a small unit without a large unit pickup appointment. Participating customers then
receive an incentive for each recycled appliance removed from the grid.

The BGE Residential Appliance Recycling Program leverages the retail partnerships
developed by the Appliance Rebate Program. Point of purchase (POP) materials help
advertise the Appliance Recycling offering in participating retail locations. The Appliance

[PDF p. 48 | printed p. 38]

Recycling offering also partners with 26 Home Depot stores in the BGE service territory
to provide the opportunity to schedule recycling pickup appointments at the time of
purchase. This scheduling option offers pickup of the old appliance when the new
appliance is delivered.

During the 2027–2029 program cycle, the BGE Residential Appliance Recycling
offering will continue to maintain the same structure, including small commercial
refrigerator and freezer pickups and a midstream recycling channel through second-hand
appliance retailers. The midstream channel officially launched in 2026 with the goal to
remove inefficient secondhand appliances from these retailers so they do not get resold
and reintroduced to the electric grid.

on number of units recycled.

home solutions, and flexible pathways that accommodate projects completed over
multiple years. At the same time, traditional direct-install offerings have produced
diminishing savings as efficient lighting and other low-cost energy-saving opportunities

### 4.5 Residential New Construction Program

#### 4.5.1 Program Overview

BGE’s Residential New Construction Program encourages the construction of
high-performance homes that exceed applicable energy code requirements and deliver
long-term energy savings, customer affordability benefits, and GHG reductions. By
working directly with builders, developers, architects, engineers, and Home Energy
Rating System (HERS) professionals during the design and construction process, the
program helps incorporate energy-efficient construction practices when they can be
implemented most cost-effectively and with the least disruption to homeowners. The
program's core objective is to improve the efficiency and performance of new homes

[PDF p. 64 | printed p. 54]

before they are occupied, creating benefits that persist throughout the life of the home.
The program utilizes a performance-based framework that rewards verified
improvements in whole-home performance while providing builders with flexibility in
determining how those results are achieved.

The program's continued success is rooted in its stable and trusted market presence.
Over multiple program cycles, BGE has developed long-standing relationships with
builders and trade allies by providing consistent program requirements, predictable
incentives, responsive technical assistance, and reliable program administration. This
continuity enables participating builders to integrate energy-efficient construction
practices into their normal business operations and helps avoid disruptions that can occur
when programs experience significant design changes or interruptions. Strong builder
engagement has remained a defining characteristic of the program and has contributed
to the program's award-winning levels of participation and market impact.
The program's emphasis on whole-house performance and high-quality building envelope
improvements has consistently delivered durable energy savings and GHG reductions,
contributing to performance that exceeded original planning expectations during the
current cycle. A distinguishing feature of the Residential New Construction Program is its
focus on the fundamentals of building science. Participating homes achieve improved
performance through a combination of enhanced construction practices, building
envelope improvements, equipment efficiencies, and quality installation standards. BGE
places particular emphasis on measures such as insulation, air sealing, and thermal
boundary improvements that provide durable benefits regardless of future equipment
replacement decisions. By reducing heating and cooling loads through better
construction, these improvements help lower homeowner energy costs, improve comfort,
and create long-lived GHG reductions that persist for decades. Homes constructed
through the program are also better positioned to accommodate future technology
advancements due to their lower overall energy requirements.

##### Code+ Transition Pathway

BGE proposes to continue the Code+ pathway during the 2027–2029 cycle. While
originally proposed as a temporary offering, program results have demonstrated that
Code+ serves an important and distinct role within the residential new construction
market. The pathway provides an effective mechanism for engaging builders that are
committed to above-code construction but are not yet consistently exceeding the more
stringent construction requirements of ENERGY STAR Version 3.2. Maintaining Code+
will preserve builder participation and prevent backsliding for builders who would
otherwise resort to minimum code construction. Code+ also serves as an additional

[PDF p. 65 | printed p. 55]

channel for promoting high-efficiency electric heat pumps through the program’s additive
measures options.

Importantly, the proposed Code+ pathway is not a continuation of the current offering.
BGE will increase the minimum efficiency requirements of the tier, raising the
performance threshold and ensuring that participating homes deliver greater savings than
those qualifying under the current program design. This enhancement preserves the role
of Code+ as a meaningful path toward ENERGY STAR certification while ensuring
continuous improvement, savings progression, and advancement beyond minimum code
requirements that would not occur in the absence of the program.

##### Residential Code Compliance Initiative

BGE has proposed laying the groundwork for a Residential Code Compliance program
as proposed by ACEEE in the EmPOWER Technical Conference (January 2026). While
there are different flavors of this type of program run throughout the country, the general
purpose is for utilities to support training and education for building codes or support
adoption of new building codes which increase the efficiency of the building stock. The
increased code compliance can be attributed to the utilities by studying the compliance
rates before and after utility training and education campaigns, targeted at code officials,
building inspectors, builders, developers, and home energy raters.

This type of initiative is not a fast launch effort and requires studies to be performed as
well as coordination with state and local organizations. At full scale, this may also lead to
a joint effort across utilities for statewide coverage. BGE’s proposed developments for the
2027-2029 cycle, pending the timing of the adoption of the 2024 IECC in Maryland, are
to complete a code compliance baseline study, develop a strategy for code compliance
training and education with input from state and local organizations, and develop a
pathway to evaluation in coordination with the Company’s evaluators and the EmPOWER
Evaluation Advisory Group (EAG). It is not expected that this initiative will result in GHG
reduction in the 2027-2029 cycle but pending timelines of code adoption and the efforts
in this cycle, could result in low-cost savings in the next EmPOWER cycle.

environment. Rising material costs, workforce constraints, financing challenges, shifting
homebuyer preferences, and evolving building code requirements have increased
pressure on builders while also increasing demand for homes that provide lower operating
costs and improved comfort. Despite these challenges, above-code residential
construction continues to gain acceptance. Builders increasingly recognize that energy
performance can serve as a market differentiator by helping homeowners reduce ongoing
energy expenses while improving comfort and overall home quality. The growing use of
third-party home certification programs and energy rating systems further demonstrates
increased market awareness of whole-home performance.

Experience from the current cycle indicates that builders respond most positively to
program offerings that provide clear performance pathways, straightforward participation
requirements, consistent technical support, and predictable incentive structures. The
program's emphasis on builder flexibility has been particularly important because housing

[PDF p. 67 | printed p. 57]

designs, customer preferences, and equipment selections vary considerably throughout
the market. Maintaining a performance-based approach allows builders to pursue energy
savings while preserving flexibility to respond to evolving consumer demand and local
market conditions.

The Residential New Construction Program has also demonstrated that substantial GHG
benefits can be achieved through improvements in overall home performance. While
equipment and fuel configurations may vary from project to project, investments in
building envelope performance provide persistent savings benefits that remain valuable
regardless of future equipment choices. As a result, BGE expects building envelope
improvements and whole-house efficiency strategies to remain central elements of the
program moving forward.

#### 4.5.5 Eligible Measures/Services

The Residential New Construction Program utilizes a performance-based design that
rewards verified whole-house performance tiers rather than prescribing specific
construction approaches or equipment selections. Participating homes must satisfy
applicable program certification requirements and demonstrate energy performance that
exceeds baseline code requirements.

For the 2027–2029 cycle, eligible measures will include homes that meet or exceed
applicable ENERGY STAR Residential New Construction requirements, with an inclusive
Code+ option based on verified HERS performance. The program will continue to offer
higher-tier incentives for homes that achieve more advanced levels of energy and
environmental performance, including ENERGY STAR NextGen or other equivalent
energy and green building certification standards.

To achieve qualifying performance levels, participating builders must incorporate a range
of eligible measures and construction practices, including:

- Enhanced insulation systems

- Advanced air sealing practices

- Improved thermal boundary construction

- High-performance windows and doors

- Efficient heating, cooling, and water-heating equipment

- Duct design and installation improvements

- HVAC commissioning and quality installation practices

- Other approved measures that contribute to increased whole-home performance

[PDF p. 68 | printed p. 58]

Beginning in the 2027–2029 cycle, BGE will also introduce an all-electric pathway
designed to encourage construction of high-performance all-electric homes. This offering
will complement existing program tiers and provide builders with an additional pathway to
adopt above-code construction practices.

In addition to whole-house performance incentives, the program will continue to offer
targeted bonus incentives for certain high-efficiency measures that support program
objectives and respond to Commission directives and stakeholder feedback. Eligible
additive measures include:

- Air-source heat pumps

- Ground-source heat pumps

- Heat pump water heaters

- Verified HVAC quality installation

- Smart thermostats

These incentives are intended to complement, rather than replace, the program's core
emphasis on verified whole-home performance.

#### 4.5.6 Customer Incentives

The Residential New Construction Program utilizes a tiered incentive structure designed
to align incentive levels with verified improvements in whole-house energy performance.
Incentives increase as participating homes achieve progressively higher efficiency
standards and corresponding customer benefits. This structure rewards measured
outcomes while maintaining flexibility regarding the specific design strategies used to
achieve those outcomes.

See Attachment 2 for measures and incentives.

BGE’s Residential New Construction Program continues to help drive market demand for
ENERGY STAR certified new homes within BGE’s service territory. The marketing and

and other market players. For homebuyers, marketing focuses on increasing awareness

### 4.6 Smart Energy Manager Residential Program

#### 4.6.1 Program Overview

BGE's Smart Energy Manager (SEM) Program uses advanced meter data, behavioral
science, and digital engagement tools to provide customers with personalized energy
insights that help them better understand and manage their energy use. Through Home
Energy Reports, Weekly Usage Reports, High Usage Alerts, and online energy tools,
customers receive timely and actionable information that supports energy-saving
decisions and encourages participation in other EmPOWER Maryland programs.
SEM will continue to evolve to meet the changing needs of BGE's customers. As adoption
of distributed energy resources and electrification technologies increases, customers are
becoming more sophisticated energy consumers and producers. SEM will expand its
ability to deliver personalized insights for customers with technologies such as rooftop
solar and electric vehicles.

Future program enhancements will continue to evaluate how energy information is
presented to customers with increasingly complex energy profiles, including households
that both generate electricity through rooftop solar and consume additional electricity
through electric vehicle charging. As customer adoption of DERs grows, SEM
communications, reports, and analytics will continue to evolve to provide relevant,
actionable guidance tailored to customers' unique energy circumstances.

[PDF p. 70 | printed p. 60]

SEM will also continue to serve as a key customer engagement platform for promoting
energy efficiency, electrification, and GHG reduction opportunities. Throughout the cycle,
BGE will leverage advances in behavioral science, analytics, artificial intelligence, and
customer segmentation to improve communication effectiveness and help customers
make informed energy decisions.

##### Home Energy Reports

Home Energy Reports remain a foundational component of the SEM Program. The
reports provide customers with personalized energy use information, comparisons
to similar homes, energy-saving recommendations, and opportunities to participate in
other EmPOWER Maryland programs.

A key element of the Home Energy Report approach is the use of normative comparisons,
which enable customers to understand how their energy use compares to that of similar
households. Providing this context helps customers identify opportunities to reduce
consumption and reinforces positive energy-saving behaviors. When combined with
personalized recommendations and usage insights, these comparisons have proven to
be an effective and cost-efficient method of encouraging sustained energy savings.
Home Energy Reports will continue to evolve through enhanced personalization, updated
visualizations, and technology-specific insights. As customers increasingly adopt
technologies such as rooftop solar, electric vehicles, and other DERs, BGE will continue
to refine report content, comparative analytics, and customer segmentation approaches
to ensure the information provided remains relevant, meaningful, and actionable. BGE
will also continue to leverage behavioral science, advanced analytics, and artificial
intelligence to improve customer engagement and energy-saving outcomes.

#### 4.6.5 Eligible Measures/Services

Program savings are calculated using a randomized controlled trial methodology.
Customers assigned to the treatment group receive Home Energy Reports, while a
statistically valid control group does not receive the program treatment. By comparing
energy usage between treatment and control groups, the program can isolate and
quantify saving attributable to the Home Energy Report intervention using Commission-approved EM&V practices.

This methodology has consistently demonstrated measurable and verifiable energy
savings and remains an industry-leading approach for evaluating behavioral energy
efficiency programs. Customers may opt out of participation at any time.
In addition to Home Energy Reports, the SEM platform includes complementary customer
engagement tools and communications, such as Weekly Usage Reports, High Usage
Alerts, personalized energy insights, and online energy management resources.

## 5. Non-Residential Portfolio

### 5.1 Portfolio Overview

BGE’s Non-Residential Portfolio, which serves commercial, industrial, non-profit,
educational, multifamily, and other non-residential customers, has seen strong success
in each of the previous program cycles. The portfolio has consistently exceeded its target
while playing a key role in helping Maryland businesses and institutions thrive. Incentives
provided through the non-residential EmPOWER programs allow customers to lower their
utility bills and building maintenance costs. BGE’s proposed portfolio for Cycle 7 builds
on this past success while continuing to evolve programs to better serve the needs of
customers through an increased focus on HVAC measures, the expanded ability to
provide incentives based on GHG savings instead of megawatt-hour (MWh) savings, and
a more cost-effective centralized marketing plan.

BGE is offering the following commercial programs during the current 2024–2026
program cycle:

- Energy Solutions for Business

  - Direct Incentives (formerly Prescriptive)

[PDF p. 73 | printed p. 63]

  - Custom and New Construction

- Instant Discounts (Midstream)

- Small Business Energy Solutions

- Building Tune-up

- Business Energy Manager

The upcoming cycle presents significant headwinds for the non-residential sector,
which has necessitated a major change in how BGE designed this portfolio of programs.
Since EmPOWER’s inception, efficient lighting has been the cornerstone of the
non-residential portfolio. Lighting measures account for approximately half of the current
cycle’s non-residential savings.

In the upcoming cycle, EmPOWER’s Independent Evaluator has dramatically reduced
the amount of savings that can be claimed from efficient LED lighting measures. Much of
this change is an attempt to account for unsubstantiated assumptions about the rate of
LED adoption in the Maryland market, largely based on data from states where, unlike
Maryland, commercial fluorescent lighting has already been banned, as well as over
concerns about potential free ridership. BGE disagrees that this dramatic change of
methodology is appropriate for Maryland. And BGE is particularly concerned about
implementing this change now, as doing so is contrary to the legislative intent to lower
the surcharge to help customers with near-term affordability. Because of this misguided
change, commercial lighting will have its GHG impacts reduced by approximately 70%
for Cycle 7. Effectively, this changes C&I lighting from a measure with one of the lowest
cost per GHG saved to, for some types of lighting measures, one of the highest cost per
GHG reduced.

In Cycle 7, BGE will be shifting the emphasis of the Non-Residential Portfolio from lighting
to HVAC and electrification measures, however those will not be sufficient to make up for
the loss of lighting savings. Therefore, to make the mandated goals, BGE will be shifting
some of the budget that would have been used for lighting to more cost-effective
measures in the residential programs. This will result in a decrease in the EmPOWER
surcharge for non-residential customers, but an increase in the surcharge of residential
customers.

In the following sections, we describe our approach to reshaping the Non-Residential
Portfolio by leveraging lessons learned, stakeholder input, applied research, and
evaluation results. We also note differences in each program between the two portfolio
plans.
