## II. DISTRIBUTION TIME OF USE (DTOU) RATE

### A. Commission Directives and Tariff Structure

**Q.** What direction did the Commission provide for designing a DTOU rate?

<!-- EPON: goal,midday-peak — Commission's directive in the 2023 BE Final Order: on-peak/off-peak differences large enough to shift usage without deterring sign-up; periods should coincide with distribution system peak/off-peak -->

**A.** The Commission's directive in ComEd's first Beneficial Electrification Plan ("BE Plan") proceeding provided that a distribution TOU rate must contain differences between on-peak and off-peak periods that are significant enough to encourage customers to shift usage without deterring customers from signing up for the rate and that the rate's on-peak and off-peak periods should coincide with the distribution system on-peak and off-peak periods. Commonwealth Edison Co., ICC Docket Nos. 22-0432/22-0442 (cons), Final Order (Mar. 23, 2023) ("2023 BE Final Order") at 258.

[p. 4]

**Q.** What are the key components of ComEd's proposed DTOU?

<!-- EPON: fixed-vs-volumetric,midday-peak — DTOU = varying kWh-based DFCs (volumetric, not demand charges); on-/off-peak periods set to match distribution system usage -->

**A.** Under my proposed methodology, ComEd will determine varying kWh-based Distribution Facilities Charges ("DFCs") that correspond with distribution system usage. As I discussed in my direct testimony, the DTOU rate's on-peak and off-peak periods are designed to coincide with distribution system on-peak and off-peak periods, again, aligning the proposed charges with distribution costs. This means that DFCs will be lower during periods of historically lower usage and higher during periods of historically higher usage. See Kremer Dir., ComEd Ex. 3.0. I describe DFCs and the DTOU time periods in more detail later in my testimony.

**Q.** Do parties agree that the DTOU proposal meets the Commission directives in the 2023 BE Final Order?

**A.** Yes. Staff witness Martin agrees that ComEd's proposed DTOU rate complies with the prior Commission directives outlined in the 2023 BE Final Order. Martin Dir., Staff Ex. 1.0 at 5:89. No witness states that ComEd's proposal does not comply.

**Q.** Did any witness raise a concern about the design or structure of the tariffs and the way in which ComEd proposes to effectuate the DTOU rate?

**A.** Only one witness takes issue with ComEd's proposed DTOU tariff design. Specifically, ICEA-RESA witness Wright takes issue with ComEd's decision to modify existing rates rather than establishing a "new separate distribution time-of-use tariff" for several reasons. He expresses concern that a RES will be unable to serve residential customers who have taken a DG Rebate under ComEd's Rider DG REBATE for storage facilities, due to the requirement that such customers commit to taking supply under Rate BESH – Basic

[p. 5]

Electric Service – Hourly Pricing ("Rate BESH") for the lifetime of the facility. Wright Dir., ICEA-RESA Ex. 3.0 at 8:165-176.

**Q.** Can witness Wright's concerns about the tariff structure be addressed without a standalone tariff?

**A.** Yes. With the tariff modifications I discuss below, residential customers on the DTOU rate should be able to benefit from Rider DG REBATE for storage facilities. Indeed, witness Wright agrees that "all of these issues [related to the lack of a standalone tariff] could be resolved by the Commission in theory under the current structure" proposed by ComEd. Wright Dir., ICEA-RESA Ex. 3.0 at 8:165-185.

**Q.** Does the DTOU rate, when paired with a RES time-variant rate, constitute a "time-of-use rate program offered by a utility"?

**A.** Yes. The Act requires that residential customers who take ComEd's DG Rebate for storage "participate in a peak time rebate program, hourly pricing program, or time-of-use rate program … offered by the utility. 220 ILCS 5/16-107.6(c)(2). Legal compliance will be addressed in briefs, but ComEd's DTOU rate, if approved, would as a factual matter be a time-of-use rate offered by ComEd applicable to residential customers who participate in DG Rebate program for storage.

**Q.** Is a tariff change required to allow RESs to serve these residential customers that utilize the DG Rebate for storage?

**A.** Yes. Revisions would be required to Sheet Nos. 248.1 and 248.4 of Rider DG REBATE and are provided as ComEd Exs. 6.01 and 6.02.

[p. 6]

**Q.** Would ComEd's proposed tariff changes "diverge" from RES supply options in the future, as ICEA-RESA witness Wright claims? Wright Dir., ICEA-RESA Ex. 3.0 at 9:176-179.

**A.** No. ComEd expects to continue to offer a DTOU rate that is consistent with its initial proposal and would not intentionally modify the tariffs to create a "divergence." Any changes to Rate BESH and Rate RDS – Retail Delivery Service ("Rate RDS") would have to be filed with the Commission, giving parties the opportunity to review the proposed modifications and the Commission the opportunity to investigate formally if it decided that was appropriate. Additionally, as I explain below, the DTOU rate is designed to complement existing and future time-variant supply options, not conflict or "diverge."

### B. Required Pairing of the DTOU rate with Time-Variant Supply Offerings

**Q.** Do any parties object to ComEd's proposal to require customers who elect supply service through time-variant supply rates to also receive delivery service through DTOU?

<!-- EPON: bundling,stacking — ICEA-RESA objects to mandatory pairing of DTOU with any time-variant supply rate; ComEd defends it -->

**A.** Yes. ICEA-RESA witness Wright objects to the proposed requirement that residential customers on time-variant rates must take the DTOU rate, in part because he believes all ARES customers should have access to fixed, or set, DFCs. Wright Dir., ICEA-RESA Ex. 3.0 at 5.

**Q.** What are DFCs?

<!-- EPON: fixed-vs-volumetric — DFCs are a flat per-kWh charge for all residential customers today, with no time/day distinction; revenue requirement per delivery class set via ECOSS -->

**A.** As I described in my direct testimony, ComEd's DFCs are listed beginning in the Delivery Service Charge Informational Sheet No. 64 et. seq., supplement to Rate MRPP in ComEd's Schedule of Rates. The DFCs are designed to recover delivery service costs related to

[p. 7]

purchase and maintenance of equipment like poles, conduit, wire and cable, transformers, and other facilities required to maintain a reliable distribution system. The DFCs vary by delivery class and are set to recover the revenue requirement allocated to each delivery class for such distribution services. The assigned revenue requirement is determined in ComEd's embedded cost of service study ("ECOSS"). For all residential customers, DFCs are a per kWh charge which are multiplied by the total kWh usage during the month without any distinction based on day or time when the kWhs are delivered. See Kremer Dir., ComEd Ex. 3.0.

**Q.** Do residential customers get to choose their DFCs?

<!-- EPON: bundling,lockout — a customer's DFC (flat vs TOU) is dictated by supply election; only flat supply (Rate BES or RES flat) keeps fixed DFCs; time-variant supply forces DTOU -->

**A.** No. As I described above, ComEd assigns DFCs in all scenarios for residential customers based upon their delivery service class; the pairing of the DTOU rate to time-variant supply rates is no different. DFCs are tied to a customer's supply election, and the customer makes that decision when they choose their supply rate. Under ComEd's proposal, nearly all residential customers can retain access to fixed DFCs by electing either ComEd's flat supply rate (Rate BES) or a RES flat-supply rate. Witness Wright's implication that residential customers would otherwise be able to choose their DFCs is inaccurate.

**Q.** Has CUB-EDF witness McCann provided any evidence that the required pairing of the DTOU rate with time-variant supply offerings will be disruptive to customers?

<!-- EPON: bundling,stacking — CUB-EDF suggested pairing DTOU with a ComEd-offered TOU supply rate (which did not exist then); ComEd says DTOU designed for customers responding to supply price signals -->

**A.** No. CUB-EDF witness McCann claims that moving time variant rate customers onto a new rate schedule could be "disruptive," but he provides no evidence to support that claim. He then suggests that the DTOU rate be paired with a ComEd-offered TOU supply rate which does not exist, and which ComEd is not neither [sic] proposing nor required to offer.

[p. 8]

McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 10:177-180. As discussed in my direct testimony, and as I further discuss below, the DTOU rate is intended to maximize customer savings with minimal risk for those customers responding to supply price signals. ComEd's proposal does that.

**Q.** Will customers, on average, save when the DTOU rate is paired with existing time-variant supply offerings?

<!-- EPON: bill-comparison,profile — savings claim: vast majority of RRTP (hourly pricing) customers save on DTOU even without changing behavior; ~$900,000 net annual savings across four delivery classes (March 2024 switching report) -->

**A.** Yes. As noted in my direct testimony, the vast majority of customers on ComEd's Residential Real-Time Pricing ("RRTP") will have savings on the DTOU rate even if they do not change their behavior. Based upon the average savings calculated and using the number of RRTP customers for each delivery class in the March 2024 switching report, the net savings for RRTP customers for the four delivery classes for an annual period is approximately \$900,000. Kremer Dir., ComEd Ex. 3.0 at 27:510 – 31:544.

**Q.** How does the DTOU rate interact with a time-varying supply rate?

<!-- EPON: stacking,midday-peak,bundling — DTOU "complements" time-variant supply; distribution costs "not volatile", <5 cents/kWh between flat DFC and Mid-Day Peak DFC; DTOU "reinforces" actions of risk-tolerant customers (price signal stacks on supply signal) -->

**A.** The DTOU rate, as proposed, is intended to complement a customer's choice to participate in a time-variant supply rate. While I agree with ICEA-RESA witness Wright's testimony that customers have varying levels of tolerance for risk, his assertion that the DTOU rate needs to be evaluated separately by customers based upon their tolerance for risk does not follow. Whereas the supply market can see volatile price swings in each hour, the costs related to the distribution system are not volatile; they are known in advance, are entirely predictable, and at most only reflect a difference of less than 5 cents per kWh between the flat rate and the Mid-Day Peak period on the DTOU rate. A customer enrolling in a time-variant supply rate has already determined that they are willing to accept the level of risk

[p. 9]

associated with that product; the DTOU rate will simply reinforce the actions these risk tolerant customers are rewarded for performing.

**Q.** Do CUB-EDF witness McCann's and ICEA-RESA witness Wright's recommendations also create issues for BES customers taking service under Rider POGNM?

**A.** Yes. CUB-EDF witness McCann's and ICEA-RESA witness Wright's recommendations that the DTOU be optional would create problems for customers on BES taking service under Rider POGNM who carry over credits for excess generation. To the extent these customers generate more than they consume in a monthly billing period, they receive a kWh credit that rolls over into subsequent months; if these customers were paying/receiving a single flat rate for their supply and multiple rates for their delivery, it would introduce an additional level of complexity to tracking and administering credits for excess generation.

**Q.** Do any parties propose modifications to the DTOU rate time periods proposed by ComEd?

<!-- EPON: midday-peak — Staff proposes Overnight start at 9 pm (vs ComEd 11 pm); CUB-EDF proposes RTOUPP-style periods and a summer-only "Super Peak" -->

Staff witness Martin proposes beginning the Overnight period at 9:00 p.m., rather than beginning at 11:00 p.m. as proposed by ComEd, and CUB-EDF Witness McCann proposes two different alternatives to align the DTOU rate time periods with the periods developed for the now concluded Rate RTOUPP – Residential Time of Use Pricing Pilot ("Rate RTOUPP" or "RTOUPP"). **A.** Yes. The Overnight period is designed to encourage customers to begin EV charging as late as possible as other local loads decrease and customers go to bed. [p. 11]

period. The impact on local transformers is one of the key reasons for choosing 11:00 p.m. as the start time for the Overnight period. If there are multiple (_e.g._, between three and five) EVs charging on a single transformer at Level 2, transformer overloading could accelerate, requiring additional investment in transformer upgrades. While this level of EVs may be a decade away, the DTOU rate could be in place a decade from now and as customers enroll and change behavior based on the time periods, it is best to design the DTOU rate time periods with this greater level of adoption in mind.

**Q.** Is it appropriate to modify the DTOU rate time periods to align with those developed for Rate RTOUPP as recommended by CUB-EDF witness McCann? McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0, at 13:232-15:256.

**A.** No. CUB-EDF witness McCann's recommendation to align these distribution rates with a pilot program that has concluded is not logical. If and when ComEd were to introduce a new supply TOU rate, any existing TOU distribution rates could be considered in the alignment of supply rates.

**Q.** Is it appropriate to modify the DTOU rate structure to align with those developed for Rate RTOUPP as recommended by CUB-EDF witness McCann? _Id._

<!-- EPON: midday-peak,profile — DTOU has four periods (Overnight, Morning, Mid-Day Peak, Evening) vs RTOUPP's three (Peak, Off-Peak, Super-Peak); Morning and Evening priced the same initially but structure allows four distinct rates as solar/EV profiles change -->

**A.** No. Only three periods were designated in Rate RTOUPP: Peak, Off-Peak, and Super-Peak, and four time periods are designated for the DTOU rate: Overnight, Morning, Mid-Day Peak, and Evening. Although ComEd proposed that the Morning and Evening periods reflect the same rate in its original submittal, the intention with DTOU was to provide a

[p. 14]

pricing structure that is flexible enough to provide for four distinct rates in order to account for changing profiles in the future due to factors such as solar or EV charging habits.

**Q.** Is ComEd open to changing the names of its four DTOU rate time periods, as suggested by CUB-EDF witness McCann? _Id._

**A.** Yes. ComEd is open to considering alternative names for its four proposed DTOU rate time periods to support customer understanding.

**Q.** Is it appropriate to introduce seasonality via Summer/Non-Summer DTOU rates, as recommended by CUB-EDF witness McCann? McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 24:407-408.

<!-- EPON: midday-peak,fixed-vs-volumetric,profile — ComEd rejects summer/non-summer DTOU: delivery cost causation is kW-based demand, "not seasonal"; Mid-Day Peak DTOU is year-round, no seasonal differentiation -->

**A.** No. CUB-EDF witness McCann argues that summer and non-summer rates would reflect seasonal differences in cost causation and references "two distinctive load patterns or profiles between" Summer and Non-Summer periods. McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 16:262-17:271 (emphasis added). While seasonality would be a potentially logical recommendation for a supply product, the logic does not necessarily hold true for a distribution rate. Using kWh-based load patterns for the purposes of determining the appropriate allocation of costs related to the distribution system is inappropriate and unrelated to costs. For a delivery rate, the appropriate touchstone is the kilowatt-based demand on the system. That is how ComEd has structured the proposed DTOU rate. Supply costs, which are driven by the cost of the kWh load in the wholesale supply market, are higher in summer months and lower in non-summer months, causing ComEd's supply expenses to be higher in the summer months and lower in non-summer months; therefore, a customer supply rate that is higher in the summer would align with the costs

[p. 15]

incurred by ComEd. The costs that ComEd incurs to build and maintain the distribution system, however, are not seasonal.

Finally, even were it otherwise valid (which it is not), the overall design which CUB-EDF witness McCann recommends is technically a rate structure with three periods each day and would need to be restructured to have four periods.

**Q.** Can you provide an example of what you mean when you say that the costs ComEd incurs to build and maintain the distribution system are not seasonal?

**A.** Yes. Take a transformer installation for example. I am not a distribution engineer, but as I understand it, the total cost for ComEd to install a typical small overhead transformer can range from \$5,000 to \$10,000, depending upon the size of the transformer, the location where it will be placed, etc., but the price would not fluctuate based upon the time of year it is purchased, or the Summer / Non-Summer period in which the peaker associated with this transformer peaks. Furthermore, this exemplar transformer, if deemed necessary to maintain the grid, would be purchased by ComEd and amortized over a period of decades, rather than billed to customers in the month the cost is incurred. To the extent that a distribution rate is designed to recover significantly less in non-summer months and more in summer months, as proposed by CUB-EDF witness McCann, it would not align with when costs occur and/or when ComEd recognizes the costs.

**Q.** Are there other problems with CUB-EDF witness McCann's proposal?

<!-- EPON: midday-peak,stacking,ev-hp — CUB-EDF summer Mid-Day Peak DFC of ~24 cents/kWh (~4.5x the 5.5 cent flat DFC) rejected as deterring time-variant supply enrollment; ComEd's own DTOU Mid-Day Peak shifts revenue to net-metered solar customers (Rider POGNM) borne by non-solar customers -->

**A.** Yes. First, CUB-EDF witness McCann's proposal for a summer Mid-Day Peak period rate of 24 cents per kWh, which is nearly 4.5 times the flat rate of 5.5 cents per kWh, risks deterring customers from enrolling in a time-variant supply rate which is paired with the

[p. 16]

DTOU rate. Second, CUB-EDF witness McCann's proposal could unfairly shift revenues to net metering customers with solar taking service under Rider POGNM. That cost shift would be borne by non-solar customers. To be sure, revenue shifting to solar from non-solar customers is unavoidable under any DTOU rate that creates higher prices during the Mid-Day Peak period coinciding with high levels of daytime solar generation, but CUB-EDF witness McCann's proposal to include a seasonal dimension amplifies that effect and burden on non-solar customers.

### D. Marketing/Customer Outreach

**Q.** Has ComEd developed a "clear plan for onboarding customers" into the DTOU rate program, as suggested by Staff witness Martin? Martin Dir., Staff Ex. 1.0 at 15:290-293.

<!-- EPON: bundling,lockout — onboarding plan: DTOU mandatory for residential time-variant supply customers (ComEd/ICEA-RESA/CUB-EDF dispute: required vs optional vs partial); Jan 1, 2026 implementation -->

**A.** Yes. ComEd is planning marketing materials with language that explains the DTOU rate and ComEd will send those materials to residential customers on a time variant supply rate ahead of the January 1, 2026 implementation date. I explain these notifications in more detail below. Final plans for onboarding customers cannot be fully developed until the Commission provides its ruling on some of the contested issues, including concerning alternative DTOU proposals, in this proceeding, which is not expected until January 2025. The most important issues are whether the DTOU rate: (a) will be the required distribution rate for residential customers on a time variant supply rate, as proposed by ComEd; (b) will be completely optional for all residential customers, as proposed by CUB-EDF; or (c) will be required for some, as proposed by ICEA-RESA.

[p. 17]

**Q.** What is ComEd's plan for providing notifications to RRTP customers being switched to the DTOU rate?

<!-- EPON: bundling,lockout — RRTP customers automatically switched to DTOU effective Jan 2026 billing; opt-out only by electing a flat supply rate -->

**A.** ComEd's notifications to customers on RRTP will inform those customers that based upon their enrollment in RRTP, they are being switched to ComEd's DTOU rate, effective with their January 2026 monthly billing period. This notification would include educational materials describing the DTOU rate, including that the DTOU rate is paired with their time variant supply rate and that they must elect a flat supply rate to opt out of the DTOU rate. For customers newly enrolling in a time-variant ComEd supply rate after January 1, 2026, ComEd will provide notification to the customer that both their supply and distribution rates are being switched.

**Q.** What is ComEd's plan for providing notifications to RES customers being switched to the DTOU rate?

**A.** ComEd's notifications to customers that are enrolled on a RES time variant rate prior to January 1, 2026 would be subject to the RES notifying ComEd that their customer is on a time variant supply rate. ComEd's notification will then inform those customers that based upon their election of a time variant supply rate, they are being switched to ComEd's DTOU rate, effective with their January 2026 monthly billing period. This notification would include educational materials describing the DTOU rate but also material ComEd is developing to accompany any marketing materials related to time-variant supply rates, whether they are offered by ComEd or a RES.

For customers who are newly enrolling in a time-variant ComEd supply rate after January 1, 2026, ComEd will provide notification to the customer that both their supply

[p. 18]

and distribution rates are being switched. For customers enrolled or newly enrolling after January 1, 2026 in a RES time-variant supply rate, the RES will be required to notify ComEd of such customer's enrollment and, upon notification by the RES, ComEd will inform the customer that, based upon their election of a RES time-variant rate, they are being switched to the ComEd DTOU rate. See ComEd Exs. 3.01 and 3.02 (providing revisions to Rate RESS - Retail Electric Supplier Service ("Rate RESS") requiring the RES to notify ComEd of any customers taking or terminating service under a time-variant rate). ComEd further anticipates developing language regarding the DTOU rate to accompany any marketing materials related to time-variant supply rates, whether they are offered by ComEd or a RES.

However, ComEd is open to additional feedback and guidance on how it can make sure customers understand what will happen to their electricity bills, including how they can access additional resources and support as they choose between rate offerings. As requested by Staff witness Martin and ICEA-RESA witness Wright, ComEd agrees to work with its marketing team, Elevate Energy, the Commission's Office of Retail Market Development, and RES parties including ICEA and RESA, to develop the specific content of the educational materials and messaging, and to provide these materials by August 31, 2025. Martin Dir., Staff Ex. 1.0 at 15:290; Wright Dir., ICEA-RESA Ex. 3.0 at 7:13.

As discussed in my direct testimony, ComEd has proposed to expand the description in the bill line item supporting the new DFC charge and to add a new line item to the bill for all commercial customers (with the exception of those in the Watt-Hour Delivery Class) showing off-peak demand and the resulting charge of zero.

**Q.** Does the DTOU rate have a term requirement?

<!-- EPON: lockout,bundling — no DTOU term requirement (tied to supply election); RES supply contract may impose minimum term; Hourly Pricing customers may leave at any time -->

A customer on a RES time-variant supply rate which would be paired with a TOU distribution rate would be subject to the terms of their supply agreement with their RES, which may require a minimum term.

**Q.** Can you clarify what happens if a customer begins receiving credits under Rider EVEN but does not remain on a time-variant supply rate that is paired with a TOU distribution rate for 24 months, as requested by Staff witness Martin? Martin Dir., Staff Ex. 1.0 at 13:260-261.

<!-- EPON: bill-comparison,goal — Low Income Discount (Docket 24-0163) is compatible with DTOU enrollment -->

## III. RIDER EVEN – ELECTRIC VEHICLE ELECTIVE NOTIFICATION

**Q.** What was the Commission's directive on including an incentive for EV owners as part of ComEd's DTOU proposal?

<!-- EPON: ev-hp,goal — Commission directive (2023 BE Final Order at 258): DTOU must include an EV-owner incentive but not be exclusive to EV owners -->

**A.** In the 2023 BE Final Order, the Commission stated that ComEd's DTOU rate proposal should include an incentive for EV owners (e.g., monthly/annual bill credits) to go on the rate, though the rate should not be available exclusively to EV owners. 2023 BE Final Order at 258.

**Q.** Accordingly, what is the purpose of the \$2 per month credit provided under Rider EVEN for a period of 24 months?

<!-- EPON: ev-hp — Rider EVEN: $2/month credit for 24 months, incentive to enroll in time-variant supply paired with DTOU -->

**A.** The \$2 per month credit proposed by ComEd is "an incentive for EV owners (i.e., monthly/annual bill credits) to go on [the distribution TOU rate] … ." 2023 BE Final Order at 258. It is intended to provide a fair and reasonable incentive to enroll in a time-variant supply rate that is paired with a TOU distribution rate.

[p. 21]

**Q.** Is the Rider EVEN \$2 per month credit intended to offset the purchase price of an EV, as proposed by by [sic] CUB-EDF witness McCann? McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 22:371.

**A.** No. The Rider EVEN monthly credit is not intended to offset the purchase price of an EV, nor to provide compensation based upon "the system value", as proposed by CUB-EDF witness McCann. McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 22:371. In addition, CUB-EDF witness McCann's criticism of the credit is misplaced in this proceeding. Instead, compensation for any value to the distribution system attributable to the customer's EV contemplated by witness McCann should be addressed in other proceedings, such as the ongoing Commission investigation into the Value of Distributed Energy Resources ("DER").

**Q.** Even if the Commission were to determine in this proceeding that Rider EVEN should also compensate participants for some value provided by their EV, would CUB-EDF witness McCann's valuation be appropriate?

**A.** No. CUB-EDF witness McCann states that a "more efficient" incentive would be based on system savings created by shifting load, and refers to a report which speculates that RTOUPP "customers may be charging more at home and, implicitly, less during Super Peak hours because of their enrollment in the TOU rate." McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 22:360-362 (emphasis added). He builds on this speculative analysis by comparing the cost of a new combustion turbine as a benchmark for compensation. _Id._ at 22:357-358, 22:361-362. The assertion that an EV, which has no generation capability, should be compensated based upon the value of a combustion turbine in the PJM capacity market

[p. 22]

which can be called upon to provide generation is not appropriate. A combustion turbine operating in the PJM capacity market can be called upon to perform and faces monetary penalties if they do not; they must post collateral to ensure such performance and can risk bankruptcy if they fail to perform.

<!-- EPON: ev-hp,midday-peak,goal — Rider EVEN only obligation is to stay on rate; DTOU price signal: lower delivery cost vs flat if load shifts out of Mid-Day Peak, higher if not -->

Under Rider EVEN, the only obligation of an EV owner signing up for the DTOU rate is to remain on the rate, and even that participation is completely voluntary – the EV owner can leave the DTOU rate at any time without penalty and will simply forego any remaining monthly credits. The basic design of the DTOU rate provides incentive for EV owners to shift load away from the Mid-Day Peak period by decreasing their delivery costs, relative to the flat rate, for shifting the load; if they do not shift load they will see increased delivery costs relative to the flat distribution rate. CUB-EDF witness McCann recommends preemptively rewarding EV drivers based upon the assumption they will shift load but does not actually require the customer to shift load. Clear price signals should not be blunted through the provision of exorbitant additional compensation simply for signing up for the DTOU rate and accompanying time variant supply.

**Q.** Have you estimated the cost of CUB-EDF witness McCann's proposal?

**A.** Yes. Based on the illustrative example below, I conclude CUB-EDF witness McCann's proposed compensation level of \$9 per month "for an indefinite period" would cost \$2.16 million per year. McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 22:368 (emphasis added). This is in comparison to ComEd's proposal equating to \$480,000 per year for two years, which I describe below. Even when limited to two years at \$463/year, CUB-EDF witness McCann's proposal would still cost \$18,520,000, equating to 18 times more than ComEd's

[p. 23]

proposal (\$960,000 over two years). Table 5, below, shows the comparison between the two proposals assuming 20,000 participants were receiving credits under Rider EVEN.

**Q.** Who would be responsible for paying the costs in the example you provided?

**A.** The costs related to incentives provided under Rider EVEN will be recovered, based upon a customer's delivery class, from all ComEd customers, including low income customers less likely to purchase EVs, especially in early stages of market adoption. As CUB-EDF witness McCann notes, EV purchasers already receive substantial state rebates and federal tax credits, as well as existing ComEd rebates. McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 21:348-352. There is not a need to expand Rider EVEN to further incentivize EV adoption on the backs of all other customers.

**Q.** Does CUB-EDF witness McCann present any other recommendations for the Rider EVEN incentive?

**A.** Yes. CUB-EDF witness McCann states that, in comparison to ComEd's proposed credit of \$2/month, the "much stronger and more obvious" incentive would be to make ComEd's Level 2 EV charger rebate available to TOU customers. McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 22:355-356. But, he appears to fail to realize that ComEd's proposal already does that.

**Q.** Will ComEd's Level 2 EV charger rebate be available to TOU customers, as recommended by CUB-EDF witness McCann, under ComEd's original proposal? McCann Dir., CUB-EDF Ex. 1.0 at 22:355-356.

<!-- EPON: ev-hp,bundling — Level 2 EV charger rebate requires 3 years on eligible program: Hourly Rate BESH, a RES time-variant rate, or Residential Optimization Pilot; first two are paired with DTOU -->

**A.** Yes. Under ComEd's proposal, all DTOU customers will have access to the Level 2 EV charger rebate. ComEd's Electric Vehicle Charger and Installation Rebate is available to

[p. 26]

customers who meet certain qualifications, including participation in one of three eligible programs for a three year period.[^2] These programs include:

1. ComEd's Hourly Rate BESH;
2. A time-variant supply rate offered by a RES; or
3. The Residential Optimization Pilot.

Both Option 1 (ComEd's Hourly Rate), and Option 2 (a time variant rate offered by a RES), would be paired with the distribution TOU rate, thus qualifying the customer for a Level 2 EV charger rebate. These requirements are based upon the Beneficial Electrification Plan currently in effect but may be subject to change based upon subsequent BE Plans.
