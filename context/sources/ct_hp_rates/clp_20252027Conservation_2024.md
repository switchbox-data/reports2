# SECTION SIX: BENEFIT-COST SCREENING

## 6.1 OVERVIEW

For the 2025-2027, the Companies used identical benefit-cost (“B/C”) methodologies for program and measure screening. The B/C screening tools contain consistent methodologies and the same sources for program-induced avoided costs and benefits. The electric and fossil fuel avoided costs are based on a regional avoided energy supply cost study completed in 2024 for New England[^101] (“2024 AESC”). The transmission and distribution (electric) avoided costs are based on studies conducted by the Companies

Each Company screened the 2025-2027 Plan on an annual basis for the 2025, 2026, and 2027 program years (5 sets of Company B/C tables x 3 years). In addition, the Companies provide a combined statewide B/C table for each program year. These statewide combined B/C tables include all benefits and costs from the electric and natural gas programs rolled up into three annual portfolio tables. The Companies use the Connecticut PSD to verify savings assumptions, including the results of program evaluations.[^102] The PSD provides engineering estimates, savings algorithms, and measure life estimates used by the Companies within their programs. The PSD also reflects the results of evaluations by providing realization rates to “true-up” savings based on third-party independent evaluations.

The Companies evaluate all electric and natural gas conservation measures in the 2025-2027 Plan within an integrated supply-and-demand planning framework to ensure that the programs are cost-effective and yield positive net benefits to customers. Use of common cost-effectiveness testing methodologies and savings assumptions allows DEEP, PURA, the EEB, and others to compare the benefits, costs, and B/C ratios on a program and measure basis. This chapter provides details on the B/C tests the Companies used, including: (1) use of avoided costs from the 2024 AESC, (2) types of B/C tests to be used in the 2025-2027 Plan, and (3) benefits used within each of the B/C tests and their source.

DEEP’s Final Determination made several recommendations to align Connecticut’s energy efficiency cost-effectiveness tests with the state’s energy policy goals and priorities. Cost-effectiveness testing plays a key role in

- **Recommendation 1.** Create a new Connecticut Efficiency Test (“CTET”) that applies the principles of the Modified Utility Cost Test (“MUCT”) to all programs and continues the supplemental use of the Total Resource Cost (“TRC”) test for income-eligible programs. The Companies will replace the current MUCT with the CTET.
- **Recommendation 2.** Modify the primary CTET to capture avoided GHG emissions.[^103]
- **Recommendation 3.** Modify the CTET to capture the utility system benefit of reduced arrearages, collection costs, debt write-offs, and/or administrative costs.

The new CTET will help increase the programs’ benefits and therefore the B/C ratio for energy efficiency programs.

## 6.3 AVOIDED ENERGY SUPPLY COST STUDY

The Companies updated most of the avoided costs used in the Companies’ B/C testing for the 2025-2027 Plan based on the completed 2024 AESC study[^104] and estimated avoided costs using the provided “User Interface” as part of the 2024 AESC study that allowed for the creation of avoided cost tables for specific states and scenarios. The New England energy efficiency program administrators sponsored the 2024 AESC. In addition, other non-utility parties (e.g., regulators and consultants) formed the Avoided Cost Study Group to oversee the development of the 2024 AESC. Previous iterations of an avoided cost study were conducted on a biennial basis. However, beginning in 2015, the AESC moved to a three-year cycle which coincides with the current three-year planning cycle in Connecticut. The development of the 2025-2027 Plan coincided with the release of the 2024 AESC.

- The **Utility Cost Test (“UCT”)** includes the value of utility-specific benefits and program costs associated with those benefits. For example, the UCT includes energy avoided costs from electric and natural gas conservation measures/programs and all program costs associated with acquiring those benefits. The UCT does not include customer out-of-pocket costs, or costs or benefits associated with oil or propane savings. Nor does the UCT include NEIs or the non-embedded value of GHG emission reductions.
- The **CTET** includes benefits of the avoided costs of electricity, natural gas, oil, propane, and non-embedded gas emissions as well as low-income non energy impact (“NEI”) costs associated with “arrearages, debt write-off costs, or administrative costs” and all program costs associated with acquiring those benefits. The CTET does not include customer out-of-pocket costs.
- The **TRC** includes all energy and non-energy benefits, such as water savings, non-embedded emissions, environmental attributes, and non-energy impacts. On February 19, 2020, DEEP issued their Approval with Conditions for the 2020 Plan Update, including Compliance Order No. 2 directing the Companies to include NEIs into the HES-Income Eligible program.[^105] In addition, the TRC includes all costs associated with acquiring these savings. This includes program costs and customer out-of-pocket costs.
