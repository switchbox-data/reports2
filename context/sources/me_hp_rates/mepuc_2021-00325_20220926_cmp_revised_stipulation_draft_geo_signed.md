STATE OF MAINE
PUBLIC UTILITIES COMMISSION

Docket No. 2021-00325

September __, 2022

MAINE PUBLIC UTILITIES COMMISSION
Investigation into Transmission and Distribution Utility Rate Design to Promote State Policies

REVISED STIPULATION ON CMP ISSUES

The undersigned, being parties to this proceeding, agree as follows:

1. On October 22, 2021, in the above-captioned proceeding, the Maine Public Utilities Commission ("Commission") issued a Notice of Investigation, which opened an investigation to consider transmission and distribution ("T&D") utility rate design issues as required by recently-enacted legislation and to support related State policies regarding climate change and electrification. As stated in the Notice of Investigation, the specific purpose of the Commission's investigation in this proceeding is to consider rates for energy storage technologies, residential electric vehicles (EV), and heat pumps. The Notice on Investigation [sic] required that to the extent not already filed, Maine's T&D utilities (including both investor-owned and consumer-owned utilities) should submit proposals for rates for:
   1. energy storage-related end uses;
   2. residential electric vehicle ("EV") charging; and
   3. heat pumps, including for space and water heating end uses.

   Central Maine Power Company ("CMP" or the "Company") submitted its response to the Notice of Investigation on December 1, 2021, including proposed revisions to certain of the Company's tariffs.

2. An initial case conference in this matter was held on Tuesday, January 4, 2022. The interventions of the following parties were granted without objection: the Governor's Energy Office ("GEO"), the Office of the Public Advocate ("OPA"), Competitive Energy Services ("CES"), Natural Resources Council of Maine, Conservation Law Foundation ("CLF"), Acadia Center, Efficiency Maine Trust ("EMT"), Northeast Clean Energy Council, Maine Renewable Energy Association, Biddeford, Saco and Old Orchard Beach Transit Committee ("BSOOB")

<!-- PDF page 2 -->

and Greater Portland Transit District ("METRO"). Industrial Energy Consumer Group ("IECG"), Casco Bay Island Transit District ("Casco Bay Lines"), and ChargePoint Inc. ("ChargePoint") were subsequently granted intervention. By Procedural Order dated January 4, 2022, the following initial schedule was adopted:

- January 11, 2022 – Data Requests on the utilities' December 1, 2021 filings;
- January 25, 2022 – Responses to Data Requests
- February 4, 2022 – Technical Conference
- February 17, 2022 – Responses to Oral Data Responses

3. On January 27, 2022, in response to intervenor comments filed in this proceeding, updated its proposed tariff submittals for Rate B-DCFC and Rate B-CPT.

4. On April 7, 2022, the Hearing Examiners issued a Procedural Order (Straw Proposal), through which the Commission Staff set forth a Straw Proposal to informs [sic] the parties of Staff's thinking about the rate design issues that have been at the heart of this case. In addition, the Straw Proposal continued the process for putting into place rates for three specific end uses, EV charging, heat pumps and energy storage. In several places, the Straw Proposal identified areas where information was required from one or more transmission and distribution utilities. The Staff requests comments and input from all parties regarding the Straw Proposal by April 21, 2022, and set a workshop/technical conference for April 29, 2022. CMP, Versant Power, GEO, OPA, CES, CLF, Acadia Center, EMT, BSOOB and METRO, IECG, Casco Bay Lines, and ChargePoint submitted comments on the Straw Proposal on April 21, 2022.

5. On April 26, 2022, the Hearing Examiners issued a Procedural Order (Converting Workshop into Settlement Conference), through which the workshop/technical conference previously scheduled for April 29, 2022 was converted to a settlement conference. A subsequent settlement conference was held on May 13, 2022. Based on discussions at those settlement conferences, the Stipulating Parties (CMP, the OPA, Efficiency Maine Trust,

<!-- PDF page 3 -->

Governor's Energy Office, Competitive Energy Services, LLC, Conservation Law Foundation, Acadia Center, Greater Portland Transit District, Biddeford, Saco and Old Orchard Beach Transit Committee, Casco Bay Island Transit District, ChargePoint Inc., and Natural Resources Council of Maine and IECG) have agreed to the terms set forth in this Revised Stipulation.

6. To reach conclusion for certain CMP rates that may support beneficial electrification investigated in this proceeding, and including electric vehicle rates considered in Docket 2021-00198, the certain of the Stipulating Parties [sic] entered into a Stipulation dated August 22, 2022 ("Prior Stipulation"), as filed by CMP with the Commission on August 23, 2022, which provides: :

   a. CMP shall remove the technology-specific eligibility restrictions to Rate A-LM to allow any residential or non-residential customer with monthly demands less than 20 kW to elect to take the rate as a separately metered service from their whole house service. The existing time of use ("TOU") periods will remain unchanged under the revised Rate A-LM. Transmission costs will be collected through on-peak and shoulder kWh charges and Low Income and Efficiency Maine Trust assessments as well as stranded costs would continue to be collected through all-hours kWh charges. This revised Rate A-LM will be effective October 1, 2022.

   b. CMP and EMT shall work in a collaborative manner to produce and deliver to eligible and likely eligible customers educational materials that promote awareness of Central Maine Power rates that facilitate beneficial electrification, and to periodically share with Stipulating Parties information regarding the efficacy of these educational efforts.

   c. CMP shall modify the pricing of its existing Residential Service – Optional Time-of-Use Rate (Rate A-TOU-OPTS) to reflect marginal costs for the service charge as informed by CMP's marginal cost study in Docket No. 2018-00194. The existing TOU periods will remain unchanged under the revised Rate A-TOU-OPTS. The kWh charges will be set equal across all hours. Transmission costs and Low Income and Efficiency Maine Trust assessments would continue to be collected through all-hours kWh charges. This revised Rate A-TOU-OPTS will be effective October 1, 2022. The parties agree that this optional rate is intended only as an interim whole house rate to encourage adoption of beneficial electrification technologies. The parties agree that a long-term rate for this purpose should be designed to more accurately reflect the potential contribution to peak demand of consumption by customers on a rate offered for this purpose. Therefore, this rate shall sunset effective October 31, 2024, which the parties currently believe is sufficient time for development, approval and implementation of a permanent replacement whole house rate that encourages adoption of beneficial electrification technologies.

   d. The Company shall implement a phased expansion of the B-DCFC Rate. In the first phase, effective October 1, 2022, the B-DCFC rate will eliminate the vintage eligibility restriction such that all electric vehicle charging stations, not just those that became operational on or after February 25, 2020, are eligible to take service under the rate.

<!-- PDF page 4 -->

In the second phase, effective July 1, 2023, the B-DCFC rate will be renamed B-CPT (Coincident Peak Transmission) and the tariff language will be updated to reflect the language included in the Company's January 27, 2022 B-CPT tariff filing in this proceeding. The methodology for calculating distribution rates for the B-CPT Rate shall be the same methodology used for the B-DCFC Rate. Under the B-CPT Rate, the end-use restrictions of the B-DCFC Rate will be removed such that all MGS, IGS, and LGS customers taking service at secondary or primary voltage levels will be eligible to take service under this optional, whole-facility rate. The B-CPT Rate shall not have a termination date.

e. The Company acknowledges the time-of-use periods it uses to assess distribution demand charges to MGS, IGS, LGS, B-DCFC, and B-CPT customers and to provide time-variant rates to residential and small commercial customers are out of date and require reexamination. For CMP's next distribution rate case, the Company will conduct an updated marginal cost of service study that evaluates its distribution time-of-use periods, including seasonality, for these rate classes and its long-run marginal costs for distribution service as required under 35-A M.R.S. §3153-A. The Company will propose to update the time-of-use periods consistent with the results of the marginal cost of service study.

f. Tariffs for revised Rate A-LM, Rate A-TOU-OPTS, and Phase I of Rate B-DCFC were filed by the Company along with the Prior Stipulation. The Company also included a draft tariff for Rate B-CPT to illustrate the language of the tariff to be effective July 1, 2023. The actual Rate B-CPT prices are yet unknown as they may change from the current Rate B-DCFC prices over the course of normal price change business (e.g., January 1st transmission price changes and July 1st annual compliance filing price changes).

g. Device Level Metering for Billing and Settlement Purposes

      i. ChargePoint raised the possibility of using embedded metering in networked devices to convey usage information for billing and settlement purposes.

      ii. The Parties agree that this issue merits further consideration including, but not limited to, evaluation of data, interoperability of hardware, communications devices, information systems, software, data protocols utilized by different devices, cybersecurity, measurement and verification access, and the customer experience. These factors, among others, may influence the design of such a system(s) as well as the cost for utility communications, data management and data integration issues. The parties acknowledge that EMT has considered embedded metering functionality in past and current programs to improve the management of EV charging. A Residential Distributed Energy Resource (DER) Load Management Innovation Pilot tested load shaping interventions in a variety of networked DERs in 2019-2020. EMT has leveraged findings from this residential DER initiative in subsequent programs for managed residential charging, including a pilot for EV smart charging hardware and a pilot for onboard technology solutions. Beginning in FY 2023, a Load Shifting Initiative approved in EMT's most recent Triennial Plan will provide incentives to promote load management using EV smart chargers on a statewide level. The Parties are also aware of pilots in a few jurisdictions utilizing embedded metering in networked EV chargers and EV devices that

<!-- PDF page 5 -->

      may provide useful information. The Parties are also aware that there are load management programs for EVs and other DERs that do not use embedded metering, including, among other programs, Avangrid's OptimizEV EV managed charging pilot in Ithaca, NY.

      iii. The Parties agree that EMT will collaborate with CMP and stakeholders to better understand possible uses of embedded metering, including other telematics data, for utility billing purposes to encourage electrification of transportation and home heating.

h. Continued Stakeholder Discussion on Advanced Rate Designs

      i. Stipulating Parties agree to continue discussions on ongoing rate design improvements to rates for new advanced technologies for transportation, space heating and cooling, and energy storage and to leverage the best use of advanced metering infrastructure data to enable rate design.

      ii. Stipulating Parties request that, to the extent an appropriate proceeding is not already underway or forthcoming, the Commission open a proceeding or proceedings to consider more advanced rate designs for proposal to the Commission consistent with cost-causation and Maine's public policy goals for decarbonization prior to the expiration of time-limited rates set forth in this stipulation. This proceeding should include a retrospective examination of how existing rates are working to advance beneficial electrification so as to help reduce greenhouse emissions to meet the greenhouse gas emissions reduction levels set forth in M.R.S. Title 38, section 576-A.

      iii. Specifically regarding the Transit Agencies, Stipulating Parties request that, at the conclusion of three years, the Commission open a proceeding to review the experience of public transit agencies regarding electrification of their fleets and to explore whether additional rate design changes are needed to support the further electrification of their fleets.

7. On August 25, 2022, IECG informed the Hearing Examiners and parties to this proceeding via email that it was in opposition to the Prior Stipulation and sought an opportunity to file comments. On August 26, 2022, the Hearing Examiners issued a Procedural Order (Comments on CMP Stipulation), which provided that IECG and any other parties that have not signed the Prior Stipulation were invited to file written comments describing their objections to the Prior Stipulation, with these comments due on or before September 7, 2022. The Procedural Order provided that reply comments could be filed by any party that supports the Prior Stipulation on or before September 14, 2022. IECG submitted comments in opposition to

<!-- PDF page 6 -->

the Prior Stipulation on September 7, 2022 and CMP submitted reply comments on September 14, 2022.

8. In IECG's written comments on the prior Stipulation, IECG stated that the Prior Stipulation was inadequate with respect to heat pump rates. Specifically, IECG urged that the Commission order CMP and Versant Power to each file by October 15, 2022 whole house heat pump rates that allocate both transmission and distribution costs to summer residential electricity consumption rather than winter consumption. In CMP's September 14, 2022 reply comments, the Company requested that the Commission not consider the Prior Stipulation until such time as CMP, IECG and other parties were given an opportunity to pursue implementation of the IECG proposal.

9. Based on the results of discussions among the Stipulating Parties, the Stipulating Parties agree that in addition to the rate design changes prescribed above, CMP will continue to collaborate with the Stipulating Parties to develop an additional new pilot residential heat pump rate. Any proposed tariff for this rate will be filed by CMP no later than October 7, 2022. The proposed pilot residential heat pump rate may be approved by the Director of Technical Analysis. The proceeding shall remain open to allow resolution of the proposed pilot additional residential heat pump rate. The pilot rate will sunset automatically with the adoption of a replacement residential beneficial electrification rate or rates by the Commission.

10. The execution of this Revised Stipulation by any Stipulating Party shall not constitute precedent as to any matter of law or fact nor, except as expressly provided herein, shall it foreclose any of the Stipulating Parties from making any contention or exercising any right, including rights of appeal, in any other Commission proceeding or investigation, or any other trial or action.

11. The Stipulating Parties intend that this Revised Stipulation be considered by the Commission for adoption as an integrated solution to the issues addressed herein which arose in the above-captioned proceeding and as otherwise presented in this Revised Stipulation. The

13. The Stipulating Parties to this Revised Stipulation agree that the record in support of this Stipulation includes all filings in Docket Nos. 2021-00198 and 20021-00325 [sic] and all responses to data requests relating to those dockets.

14.
