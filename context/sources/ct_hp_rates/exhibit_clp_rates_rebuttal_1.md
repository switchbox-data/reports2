## {15} II. PROPOSED ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT RIDER

{16} **Q. Please summarize the Company’s proposal regarding the introduction of an
{17} Economic Development Rider.**

{18} A. The fundamental components of the EDR include a provision for determining customer
{19} eligibility and qualification, a schedule of discounts and term of service and discount,
{20} as well as reference to a Service Agreement. The EDR as proposed is a form of rider
{21} which can be utilized for establishing a schedule of discounted rates and charges for
{22} customers with incremental new or expanded service requirements. The EDR is
{23} intended to be applied to rates applicable to serving the incremental load of qualifying
{24} large commercial and industrial customers. A schedule of discounted rates would be
{25} determined on a customer-specific basis, pursuant to a service agreement entered into [PDF p. 8 | printed p. 6]
{1} under the rider. Consistent with established regulatory principles, the EDR is intended
{2} first and foremost to benefit ratepayers through improved system economics and
{3} affordability, while also supporting broader economic development objectives.

{4} The EDR shall be available to new and existing large commercial and industrial
{5} customers that demonstrate incremental load growth. A New Customer is a customer
{6} establishing electric service within the service territory that has not taken service within
{7} the prior twelve months. An Existing Customer is a customer that expands operations
{8} and demonstrates measurable incremental load above a defined baseline.

{9} As proposed, eligibility is limited to customers taking service on the Company’s large
{10} general service rates — Rate 57 for manufacturing customers and Rate 58 for non-
{11} manufacturing customers — with demand generally exceeding one megawatt (“MW”).
{12} The proposed EDR is limited to incremental (i.e., new or expanded) load only, and
{13} discounts shall not be available relative to existing load. The EDR is not available to
{14} any other service class or for load retention purposes. Of note, there are a limited
{15} number of Rate 39 customers, and only three of whom receive distribution level
{16} service. No new customers are authorized to participate in Rate 39. Rate 39 is a
{17} somewhat dynamic rate that allows incremental load, above an interruptible contract
{18} demand level, to be served under a firm service rate. This incremental load may be
{19} eligible for enrollment under the EDR. This demonstrates the need for case by case
{20} analysis of eligibility for the EDR, including but not limited to an examination of (1)
{21} the differentiation and extent of firm service load, (2) specific electric service analysis [PDF p. 9 | printed p. 7]
{1} and review to ensure physical requirements and determination of any upgrades,
{2} equipment, etc. are identified and the economics and customer contributions, if any,
{3} relative to the level of discount and costs of any construction work are established up
{4} front. Further, in order to qualify, the Company proposes that a customer must as a
{5} minimum demonstrate that its project: (i) involves new or expanded operations
{6} resulting in incremental load[^2] that supports system efficiency; (ii) provides economic
{7} value to customers within its service territory, including job creation or capital
{8} investment; and (iii) is designed, further, to support regulatory policy objectives, and
{9} to provide value during the term of project participation in the EDR. Customers looking
{10} to qualify will also be required to provide the Company with information describing
{11} why electric load is increasing, anticipated investment, and changes in employment
{12} levels or jobs expected to be created, as well as a narrative describing what impact the
{13} EDR had on the customer’s expansion or decision to add new load.

{14} The Company also proposes a declining, time-limited discount applied to incremental
{15} load over a five-year term, consistent with the declining structure proposed in the
{16} Company’s prior economic development rate.
