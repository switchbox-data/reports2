# ComEd's Delivery Time-of-Day (TOD) Program

**FACT SHEET — March 2026**

Commonwealth Edison (ComEd) customers now have another choice that can help lower their bills: Delivery Time-of-Day (TOD) Pricing. CUB has advocated for this optional program for years, because it can help customers save money while making our power system more resilient and sustainable.

### What is Delivery Time-of-Day Pricing?

As the name indicates, this program impacts the delivery side of the bill – what you pay ComEd to maintain its wires and deliver electricity to your home – plus a profit for the utility. Specifically, under Delivery TOD Pricing you'll pay a different "Distribution Facility Charge."

> **[Image: sample ComEd bill excerpt, "DELIVERY – ComEd", total \$60.43, with an asterisk and arrow pointing at the Distribution Facility Charge line.]** Lines shown: Customer Charge \$15.26; Standard Metering Charge \$3.81; Distribution Facility Charge, 651 kWh X 0.06228 = \$40.54 (arrow points here); IL Electricity Distribution Charge, 651 kWh X 0.00126 = \$0.82. **This shows the single-family "Standard" rate of 6.228¢ is the rate as billed (after adjustment factors).**

Most ComEd customers pay a fixed per-kilowatt-hour (kWh) distribution charge that doesn't change over the course of a day. But Delivery TOD customers pay a distribution charge that varies by a set schedule you know in advance.

The TOD schedule divides the day into four periods – with three out of the four periods (18 hours) charging a lower rate than the standard distribution charge. This schedule applies every day, 365 days a year.

- **Morning (6 a.m. – 1 p.m.): LOWER** than standard ComEd distribution rate.
- **Mid-Day Peak (1 p.m. – 7 p.m.): HIGHER** than standard ComEd distribution rate.
- **Evening (7 p.m. – 9 p.m.): LOWER** than standard ComEd distribution rate.
- **Overnight (9 p.m. – 6 a.m.): LOWEST** rate.

The key to saving money in the TOD program is to avoid heavy electricity usage during the one period – the "Mid-Day Peak" – in which the utility charges a significantly higher rate than the standard distribution charge.

### How does Delivery Time-of-Day Pricing work?

Exact prices for the Time-of-Day rates differ depending on whether you live in a single-family home or a home in a multi-family building (3 or more units), and whether or not your home is heated with electricity.

### How can I save money in the Delivery (TOD) program?

Time-of-Day prices are lower than ComEd's standard rate for 18 out of 24 hours. You save money by minimizing use of your biggest energy-burning appliances during that six-hour Mid-Day Peak period (1 p.m. – 7 p.m.). The biggest energy-using appliances may vary from house to house, but the [U.S. Department of Energy has a calculator](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/estimating-appliance-and-home-electronic-energy-use) to help you identify the ones in your home. (Generally, air conditioning, electric space heating, and charging an electric vehicle are big energy hogs.)

### What customers are the best candidates for ComEd's Delivery Time-of-Day program?

The best candidates for the Time-of-Day program are households that…

- Can set the thermostat a few degrees higher between 1 p.m. and 7 p.m. in the summer.
- Are able to shift use of major appliances (dishwasher, clothes dryer, and washing machine, etc.) to outside the peak hours.
- Are electric heat customers who can set the thermostat a few degrees cooler between 1 p.m. and 7 p.m. during the colder months.
- Are electric vehicle (EV) drivers who can charge overnight, when rates are least expensive. (If you register your EV when enrolling in Time-of-Day, you earn a \$2 a month credit, per EV. The per month credit is limited to two EVs and 24 months.)

**Note:** Most customers who are already saving on [Hourly Pricing](https://hourlypricing.comed.com/), a ComEd program that charges a supply price that changes hourly, can also save on Delivery Time-of-Day Pricing. Both programs benefit households that can shift their usage to lower-priced times. Read more about Hourly Pricing below.

### Are any customers NOT good candidates for TOD?

**Warning:** Certain solar customers may not be good candidates. Specifically, "legacy" net metering customers who installed their solar systems before 2025 may lose access to a portion of their net metering credits if they participate in the Delivery Time-of-Day program but aren't on a time-variant supply rate, such as Hourly Pricing. To summarize: CUB does not recommend the specific arrangement of combining legacy net metering, Delivery Time-of-Day Pricing, and a fixed supply rate.

### How can I learn more about the Time-of-Day program and if it's right for me?

ComEd has a tool that allows you to examine whether you would've paid more or less had you been on the Time-of-Day rate over the past year. Also, read ComEd's Frequently Asked Questions. [Links in the source PDF: the tool is ComEd's authenticated sign-in page; the FAQ is <https://www.comed.com/ways-to-save/for-your-home/manage-my-energy/delivery-time-of-day-pricing/todp-FAQs>.]

### Other than savings, what are the benefits of Delivery Time-of-Day Pricing?

Time-of-Day is called a dynamic pricing plan – such optional programs are designed to encourage people to use electricity during "off-peak" times, when energy usage and demand are low. That has a number of benefits:

- Reducing electricity usage during peak times reduces demand during those times, and that can lower market supply prices for everyone.
- Lower demand during peak times reduces the stress on the power grid, improving reliability and extending the life of costly-to-replace infrastructure.
- The power plants that only run during peak hours tend to be the dirtiest, so reducing your contribution to peak load reduces pollution.

### Does the Delivery Time-of-Day program impact the supply part of electric bills?

No. If you choose Delivery Time of Day Pricing, your supply options don't change. They are:

- The fixed supply rate through ComEd.
- Hourly Pricing, a ComEd program that charges you a supply rate that can change hourly. The least expensive hours for Hourly Pricing, on average, tend to match up with the least-expensive periods for Delivery Time-of-Day pricing. Thus, if you are a household that can shift your electricity usage to off-peak, lower-priced hours, you have the potential to reduce your electric bill through a combination of Hourly Pricing supply rates and Delivery Time-of-Day rates.
- An alternative electricity supplier. (Be careful of bad deals. Read CUB's fact sheet.) [link in source PDF: <https://www.citizensutilityboard.org/electriccompetitioncomed/>]

### How do I sign up for Delivery Time-of-Day Pricing?

You can enroll in Time-of-Day Pricing at ComEd's website. For more information on the program, read ComEd's Frequently Asked Questions.
