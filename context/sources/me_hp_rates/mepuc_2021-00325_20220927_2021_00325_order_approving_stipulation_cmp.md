Investigation into Transmission and Distribution Utility Rate Design to Promote State Policies

ORDER APPROVING STIPULATION (CMP)

BARTLETT, Chair; DAVIS and SCULLY, Commissioners

## I. SUMMARY

In this Order, the Commission approves a stipulation between Central Maine Power Company (CMP) and the Office of the Public Advocate (OPA), the Governor's Energy Office (GEO), Competitive Energy Services (CES), ChargePoint, Inc., the Conservation Law Foundation (CLF), the Natural Resources Council of Maine (NRCM), the Acadia Center, Greater Portland Transit District and the Biddeford, Saco and Old Orchard Beach Transit Committee (together referred to as the Transit Agencies), Casco Bay Island Transit District (Casco Bay Lines), Industrial Energy Consumers Group (IECG) and Efficiency Maine Trust (EMT). The stipulation was filed on September 23, 2022.

## II. BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY

During its 2021 session, the Legislature enacted several laws relevant to utility rate designs to promote State policies. One directs the Commission to review alternative rate structures to support electric vehicle (EV) charging stations for nonresidential applications, including, but not limited to, for light duty vehicles, medium duty vehicles, heavy duty vehicles and transit and other fleet vehicles. An Act to Facilitate Maine's Climate Goals by Encouraging Use of Electric Vehicles. P.L. 2021, c. 402. (Chapter 402). Another, An Act To Advance Energy Storage in Maine. P.L. 2021, c. 298 (LD 528) (Chapter 298), directs the Commission, in Section 7, to investigate and, where appropriate, implement T&D utility rate designs that account for variation in the cost components of electricity as the load or demand on the electricity system fluctuates in conjunction with energy storage.

On July 27, 2021, the Commission issued a Notice of Inquiry seeking responses to certain questions raised in Chapter 298 as well as other rate design matters. _Me. Pub. Utils. Comm'n, Comm'n Initiated Inquiry into Rate Design Issues Associated with 2021 Legislation_, Docket No. 2021-00198, Notice of Inquiry (July 27, 2021). Comments from various parties discussed principles of rate design, rates to promote the use of distributed generation, load reduction and the reduction of greenhouse gases and rates used in connection with desired public policy outcomes. Most commenters indicated that rates designed to promote energy storage should be optional.

<!-- PDF page 2 -->

On October 22, 2021, the Commission issued a Notice of Investigation (Notice) commencing this docket. This investigation was opened to consider transmission and distribution (T&D) utility rate design issues as required, in part, by the recently-enacted legislation and to support related State policies regarding climate change and beneficial electrification. The investigation considered rates for energy storage technologies, electric vehicles (EV), and heat pumps. The Notice required that to the extent not already filed, Maine's T&D utilities (including both investor-owned and consumer-owned utilities) submit proposals for rates for:

1. energy storage-related end uses;
2. residential EV charging; and
3. heat pumps, including for space and water heating end uses.

CMP submitted its response to the Notice of Investigation on December 1, 2021, including proposed revisions to certain of the Company's tariffs.

Also on December 1, 2021, Fox Islands Electric Cooperative (Fox Islands), and Versant Power (Versant) filed comments and proposed tariffs in response to the Notice. On December 2, 2021, CES filed for intervention, and on December 3, 2021, EMEC filed its proposed tariffs. The Transit Agencies submitted comments on December 22, 2021.

In a December 3, 2022 [sic] Procedural Order, the Examiners made CMP and Versant parties to the case, and scheduled an Initial Case Conference.

At the Initial Case Conference, held on January 4, 2022, the interventions of the following parties were granted without objection: the GEO, the OPA, CES, NRCM, CLF, Acadia Center, EMT, Northeast Clean Energy Council, Maine Renewable Energy Association, and the Transit Agencies. IECG, Casco Bay Lines, and ChargePoint were subsequently granted intervention.

A Scheduling Order was issued on January 4, 2022, pursuant to which data requests were submitted to CMP, Fox Islands, and Versant.

On January 11, 2022, Madison Electric Works (MEW) submitted a late-filed response to the Commission's Notice of Investigation. CMP, Fox Islands, and Versant responded to the data requests by January 25, 2022. Additionally on January 25, 2022, Versant submitted a revised proposed tariff, and on January 27, 2022, CMP submitted its updated proposed tariff.

On February 28, 2022, the Examiners issued a Procedural Order rescheduling a technical conference and requesting additional information from CMP to which CMP responded on March 3, 2022. A Technical Conference was held March 4, 2022. Versant updated the record by filing its 2015 Cost of Service Study.

On March 18, 2022, the Examiners released a Straw Proposal and requested comments. As of April 25, 2022, the Maine Transit Association (the MTA), the Transit Agencies, the OPA, CES, the CLF, ChargePoint, CMP, Acadia, Versant, EMT, and the GEO had submitted comments on the Straw Proposal.

## III. DESCRIPTION OF THE STIPULATION

### A. New Rates.

The stipulation proposes several modifications to existing rates as follows:

#### 1. Changes to Rate A-LM.

CMP agrees to modify Rate A-LM by removing the restrictions on which end uses devices may qualify and by allowing customers to use the rate as a separately-metered service, while using the existing TOU periods.

#### 2. Changes to Rate A-TOU-OPTS.

CMP agrees to modify the pricing under Rate A-TOU-OPTS in accordance with the marginal cost study the Company filed in Docket No. 2018-00194. The existing TOU periods will remain unchanged. The parties specifically agree that this optional rate

<!-- PDF page 4 -->

would be an interim whole house rate for the purpose of encouraging the adoption and use of beneficial electrification technologies. The parties also agree that a long-term rate for this purpose should be designed to better reflect the contribution to peak demand. The parties agree that the rate should sunset on October 31, 2024, which they believe is sufficient time to allow for the development of a better rate.

#### 3. Changes to Rate B-DCFC.

CMP agrees to phase in an expansion of existing rate B-DCFC. First, all EV charging stations, not just those that became operational on or after February 25, 2020, would immediately become eligible for the rate. Next, effective July 1, 2023, the rate would be renamed Rate B-CPT (Coincident Peak Transmission) and end-use restrictions will be removed, allowing all MGS, IGS and LGS customers to use this optional whole facility rate.

#### 4. Updated Cost of Service Study.

CMP agrees to conduct a marginal cost of service study (MCOSS) and to propose to update its out-of-date TOU periods in its current distribution rate case.[^1]

#### 5. Residential Heat Pump Pilot

CMP and the parties agree to collaborate to develop a pilot that will consist of a new residential heat pump rate. CMP agrees to file a proposed tariff for this pilot rate on or before October 7, 2022.

### B. Other Provisions.

In addition to the new rates, the stipulation contains the following provisions.

#### 1. Embedded Metering.

The parties agree that EMT will work with CMP and stakeholders to learn about possible uses of embedded metering including for utility billing purposes.

#### 2. Continued Stakeholder Discussions.

The parties agree to hold continued discussions about further improvements to CMP's rate design that would provide better incentives for the growth of beneficial electrification. The parties contemplate the Commission opening a proceeding (or proceedings) to consider more advanced rates consistent with cost causation and the goals of fighting climate change. The parties propose that the proceeding(s) aim to lead to new rates before the expiration of the time-limited rates in the stipulation. The parties specifically request that the Commission open a proceeding at the end of three years to examine the experience of the transit agencies and the electrification of their fleets.

## IV. LEGAL STANDARD

To approve a stipulation, the Commission must consider the following four criteria:

1. whether the parties joining the stipulation represent a sufficiently broad spectrum of interests to ensure no appearance or reality of disenfranchisement;
2. whether the process that led to the stipulation was fair to all parties;
3. whether the stipulated result is both reasonable and not contrary to legislative mandate; and
4. whether the overall stipulated result is in the public interest.

MPUC Rules, ch. 110, § 8(D)(7).

## V. DECISION

As detailed below, the Commission finds that all of the criteria for approval have been satisfied.

### A. The Parties to the Stipulation Represent a Sufficiently Broad Spectrum of Interests.

As indicated, nearly all parties have signed the stipulation, and it is not opposed. Generally, when only the utility and the OPA are signatories, the Commission has found a sufficiently broad spectrum of interests, given that these entities often represent opposing views. _See Public Utilities Commission, Investigation of Stranded Cost Recovery, Transmission and Distribution Utility Revenue Requirements and Rate Design of Bangor Hydro-Electric Company (Phase II)_, Docket No. 97-596, Order at 6 (Feb. 29, 2000) and _Maine Public Utilities Commission, Investigation of Retail Electric Transmission Services and Jurisdictional Issues_, Docket No. 99-185, Order Approving Stipulation (Maine Public Service Company) at 3 (Aug. 11, 2000). Here, there are many more parties to the agreement than just the OPA, including environmental, governmental, and business entities. The Commission is thus satisfied that the stipulation represents a broad spectrum of interests.

### B. The Process Was Fair.

No party has argued that the process was not fair. All parties were provided and in fact had the opportunity to participate in the settlement discussions that led to the agreement. The Commission finds that the process was fair and open, and that the second criterion for approval is satisfied.

<!-- PDF page 6 -->

### C. The Stipulated Result Is Reasonable, Not Contrary to Legislative Mandate, and in the Public Interest.

The Commission finds that the stipulation is in keeping with the scope of recent legislation in that is furthers [sic] the goals of addressing climate change by creating rate designs that should provide incentives to customers to adopt the use of EVs, electric heating and electric storage. The Commission finds that nothing in the stipulation is contrary to any legislative mandate and that the overall result is reasonable and in the public interest. Thus, the final two criteria are met.

## VI. CONCLUSION

Accordingly, the Commission

**O R D E R S**

1. That the stipulation filed September 23, 2022, is hereby approved. A copy of the stipulation and its attachments is attached to this order and incorporated herein by reference;
2. That CMP shall file compliance tariffs in furtherance of this order;
3. That CMP shall file, on or before October 7, 2022, a proposed tariff for a residential heat pump rate pilot; and
4. That CMP shall annually file a report with the Commission describing the participation rates by customers for each new tariff, and a calculation or an estimate of the likely savings by customers that results from adopting the new rates.

## NOTICE OF RIGHTS TO REVIEW OR APPEAL

5 M.R.S. § 9061 requires the Public Utilities Commission to give each party at the conclusion of an adjudicatory proceeding written notice of the party's rights to seek review of or to appeal the Commission's decision. The methods of review or appeal of Commission decisions at the conclusion of an adjudicatory proceeding are as follows:

1. Reconsideration of the Commission's Order may be requested under Section 11(D) of the Commission's Rules of Practice and Procedure (65-407 C.M.R. ch. 110) within 20 days of the date of the Order by filing a petition with the Commission stating the grounds upon which reconsideration is sought. Any petition not granted within 20 days from the date of filing is denied.

2. Appeal of a final decision of the Commission may be taken to the Law Court by filing, within 21 days of the date of the Order, a Notice of Appeal with the Administrative Director of the Commission, pursuant to 35-A M.R.S. § 1320(1)-(4) and the Maine Rules of Appellate Procedure.

3. Additional court review of constitutional issues or issues involving the justness or reasonableness of rates may be had by the filing of an appeal with the Law Court, pursuant to 35-A M.R.S. § 1320(5).

Pursuant to 5 M.R.S. § 8058 and 35-A M.R.S. § 1320(6), review of Commission Rules is subject to the jurisdiction of the Superior Court.
