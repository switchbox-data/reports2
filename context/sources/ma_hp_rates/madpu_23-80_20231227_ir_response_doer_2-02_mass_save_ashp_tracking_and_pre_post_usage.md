**Request No. DOER 2-02:**

Refer to the Company's response to DPU 2-03, 2-05, and Exh. Unitil-JDT Rate Design Model Electric Final 8-17-23 PUBLIC.

a. Explain how the Company tracks customers who have received an incentive for an air source heat pump (ASHP) through the Mass Save program, including whether the ASHP is a full- or partial-displacement of a customer's original heating source.

b. Please provide average monthly consumption data, including at least 12 months, or as much is available, pre- and post-installation for these customers by rate class.

c. Compare the actual heating load requirements from the response to part (b) of this question to the detailed calculations contained in Exh. Unitil-JDT Rate Design Model Electric Final 8-17-23 PUBLIC.

d. Please describe the Company's strategy to revise, or otherwise update, the proposed HP-RES and HP-RES LI tariffs as the Company gathers more data on heating loads from participating customers over time.

**Response:**

a. The Company (both the gas and electric divisions) maintains a common set of alpha-numeric identifiers for all measures offered as part of the MassSave® suite of programs. These Measure IDs are referenced in the statewide Technical Reference Manual ("TRM"), in each of the Program Administrators' benefit-cost models, and by the statewide rebate fulfillment vendor serving the Residential Retail core initiative, as well as other core initiatives in which heat pumps are offered, including the Income Eligible Coordinated Delivery core initiative, and the Commercial and Industrial Existing Building core initiatives. Each type of full and partial displacement heat pump (distinguished by heating fuel being displaced) is represented by a unique Measure ID. When rebates are issued to customers by the rebate fulfillment or other vendor, they collect data on the specific type of heat pump measure being rebated, including Measure ID and associated equipment tonnage. The vendor also provides demographic details on participating customers, including utility account number(s), names and service address. This data is either provided to the Company by the vendor or directly uploaded to the Company's energy efficiency tracking system, eTrack+. A list of all heat pump measures offered by the Company for both electric and gas Mass Save energy efficiency portfolios is provided in DOER 2-02 Attachment 1.

b. Please see DOER 2-02 Attachment 2.

c. Please see DOER 2-02 Attachment 3 which summarizes the data provided in DOER 2-02 Attachment 2 and calculates the 'Annualized Increase in Heating Season Monthly Average Electric Usage' and the 'Weighted Annualized Increase in Heating Season Monthly Average Electric Usage.' The annual increase in average usage is 3,471 with a standard deviation of 2,932 and the weighted annual increase average is 3,962. The estimate of additional kWh used in the development of the heat pump rate was 5,927 which is 0.84 standard deviations from the non-weighted average and 0.67 standard deviations from the weighted average. As such, the estimate of additional kWh used in the development of the heat pump rate is in alignment with this data set.

d. As indicated in the Testimony of Mr. Taylor, Exhibit Unitil-JDT-1 at 24, an estimate of the additional kWh a residential customer would use after the installation of a heat pump was derived by converting the average heating gas usage of a gas heating customer on the Company's Rate R-3 to an equivalent kWh usage based on an assumed efficiency of heat pumps. This was then used to develop a rate that would recover the same level of total fixed costs in order to make the new rate revenue neutral. The Company will compare this calculated usage to the actual usage of customers on the HP-HES [sic; possibly intended "HP-RES"] and HP-RES LI tariffs and evaluate whether additional adjustments to those tariff rates are warranted to ensure revenue neutrality. Such changes can be proposed to the Department as part of the Company's next base rate.
