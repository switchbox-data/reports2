**CHESAPEAKE CLIMATE ACTION NETWORK'S MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO CERTAIN DISCOVERY REQUESTS**

The Chesapeake Climate Action Network ("CCAN") respectfully requests that the Maryland Public Service Commission direct Baltimore Gas & Electric ("BGE") to provide responses to CCAN Data Request ("DR") Nos. 1-12 through 1-21. BGE's objection is impermissibly vague, and the information CCAN requests is relevant to this historic test year rate case.

### **BACKGROUND**

CCAN served DR Set No. 1 on BGE on August 21, 2026 (**Attachment A**), and on August 26, 2026, BGE objected to an unspecified portion of DR Nos. 1-12 through 1-21 "to the extent" they are not relevant. BGE's objection is reproduced here in full:

> BGE objects to CCAN DR 1-12 thru 1-21 to the extent that these questions seek information that is not relevant to this proceeding. In Case No. 9888, BGE is seeking a increase [sic] in electric revenue requirements based on an adjusted historic test year. Issues concerning electric system planning are under consideration in Case No. 9665.

Counsel for CCAN and BGE conferred on August 31, 2026, but could not come to agreement. Because BGE's objection is impermissibly vague, and CCAN only requests information relevant to this proceeding, CCAN requests that the Commission compel BGE to respond to CCAN DR Nos. 1-12 through 1-21.

### **STANDARD OF REVIEW**

Parties must raise objections to discovery requests with sufficient particularity, "so that counsel may meet and confer _once_ to try to resolve the objections."[^1] "No benefit is achieved by allowing piecemeal objections to producing requested discovery, as this adds unnecessary expense to the parties and unjustified burden on the court."[^2]

[^1]: _Hall v. Sullivan_, 231 F.R.D. 468, 473 (D. Md. 2005) (emphasis added).

[^2]: _Id._

[PDF p. 3 | printed p. 2]

The proper scope of discovery is broad and allows for discovery regarding "_any_ matter that is not privileged . . . if the matter sought is relevant to the subject matter involved in the action."[^3] In the context of discovery, relevancy "has been construed broadly to encompass any matter that bears on, or that reasonably could lead to other matter that could bear on, any issue that is or may be in the case."[^4] Parties may not avoid disclosing certain information merely because it relates to a potential argument that they disagree with.[^5]

### **ARGUMENT**

### **I. BGE's objection to CCAN's request for information is impermissibly vague.**

BGE objects to CCAN DR Nos. 1-12 through 1-21 only "to the extent that" they do not seek relevant information, yet BGE does not specify which of the requests, or portions thereof, to which its objection applies. Because BGE failed to state its objection with sufficient particularity, CCAN must defend the entire swath of data requests without knowing which are potentially objectionable and/or why BGE contends they are not relevant to this proceeding. This situation prejudices CCAN and is not what the relevant caselaw contemplates.

In addition, BGE's apparent practice of lodging boilerplate objections, if countenanced, will delay and impede the discovery process. As demonstrated by Staff's recently filed motion to compel, BGE appears to be circumventing its obligation to provide objections within 3 days by declining to provide information on impermissibly vague grounds.[^6] If allowed to continue, BGE's overly broad and vague objections will prevent parties from properly narrowing the scope of discovery disputes before seeking relief from the Commission, thereby adding "unnecessary expense to the parties and unjustified burden" on the Commission.[^7]

In short, the Commission should reject BGE's objection as impermissibly vague and order a full response to CCAN DR Nos. 1-12 through 1-21.

### **II. CCAN seeks information relevant to this historic test year proceeding.**

CCAN DR Nos. 1-12 through 1-21 seek information that is directly relevant to the proper allocation of costs to different categories of customers, an issue of central importance in every rate case. As BGE Witness Manuel explains, cost allocation includes assigning costs to different customer classes "according to demand on the distribution system, customer-based requirements,

usage, and/or revenues."[^8] "When allocating distribution costs . . . it is important to understand the concepts of Non-Coincident Peak demand and Coincident Peak demand."[^9]

In DR Nos. 1-12 through 1-21, CCAN seeks information regarding demand on the distribution system, the number and types of customers being served by distribution assets, and these customers' contribution to peak loads. CCAN's central request is for information relating to BGE's substation-transformers and feeders, including (but not limited to):

- Each asset's voltage, as well as summer and winter normal ratings (DR Nos. 1-12, 15)
- The total number of customers served by each asset broken down by type, including by major customer class and heat pump users (DR Nos. 1-12, 15)
- Each asset's current and forecasted coincident summer and winter peak loading (DR Nos. 1-13, 16)
- Whether each asset has been flagged by BGE as having an N-0 thermal constraint or an N-1 contingency overload, as well as the relevant status and type of remediation (DR Nos. 1-14, 17)
- BGE's forecast for how heat pump customers may contribute to each asset's summer and winter peaks (DR No. 1-18)

All the above information is directly relevant to determining whether BGE is properly allocating distribution costs to customers based on their contribution to distribution system constraints. Specifically, the above information will illuminate (1) the extent of excess capacity on BGE's distribution system; (2) the demand that different customer types place on that excess capacity; and (3) whether costs are being allocated fairly based on that demand.

As noted above, BGE has failed to explain why CCAN's request is not relevant to this proceeding. The objection must fail on its face, however, because the information CCAN seeks pertains to a central component of ratemaking in every historic test year case. Therefore, it is relevant and should be provided by BGE. While BGE may disagree with the need to evaluate its cost allocation decisions, that is no basis to withhold relevant information.[^10] Instead, BGE is free to challenge any arguments that CCAN may raise through rebuttal testimony, cross-examination, and briefing.

### **III. CCAN's request will not pose undue burden or hardship and will provide relevant information to all parties.**

In its responses to discovery from the Office of People's Counsel ("OPC") and Staff, BGE has already disclosed information relating to the capacity of its substation-transformers and feeders. Not only has BGE acknowledged this information as relevant, but also has shown that the information is in its possession and that it can be quickly disclosed to the parties without undue burden or hardship. For example, in response to several data requests from OPC and Staff, BGE provided detailed information on the capacity of various feeders and substations including data on reliability,[^11] load forecasting,[^12] customer counts and peak loads,[^13] as well as BGE's methodology for identifying poorly performing feeders.[^14]

As illustrated by these disclosures, the data CCAN seeks is of interest to multiple parties. By providing data on distribution system constraints with full transparency, BGE can assist all parties in assessing the reasonableness of the utility's spending and rate design methodologies, as well as reduce the need for piecemeal disclosures regarding individual distribution assets.

### **IV. Conclusion**

For the reasons stated above, the information sought by CCAN is relevant to this proceeding, and not subject to objection—let alone the impermissibly vague objections raised by BGE. Accordingly, CCAN respectfully requests that the Commission order BGE respond to CCAN data request numbers 1-12 to 1-21, in full.
