**Re:** PC44 Rate Design Work Group Leader's Report and Recommendations on Full-Scale Time of Use Rate Offerings

Dear Mr. Johnston,

In January of 2017, the Maryland Public Service Commission (the "Commission") convened a broad group of stakeholders to explore and pilot cost-based time-varying or time of use rates ("TOU") in Maryland's Public Conference 44 ("PC 44") grid modernization proceeding. The stakeholders convened by the Commission came to a consensus on all major elements of a cost-based TOU rate pilot for the three participating investor-owned utility service territories. The Commission ordered the PC44 TOU Pilot (the "Pilot") based on these recommendations which ran from June 2019 through May 31, 2021 with approximately 3,800 customers of Baltimore Gas and Electric Company ("BGE"), Potomac Electric Power Company ("Pepco"), and Delmarva Power & Light Company ("Delmarva") (collectively, the "Joint Utilities"). The Pilot evaluated customers' willingness and ability to respond to an on- to off-peak ratio between 4:1 and 6:1 with distribution, transmission, and energy components of the rates varying between utilities.

The Brattle Group, retained by the Joint Utilities to assist in the pilot design and analysis, presented the Pilot results to the Commission on January 20, 2022. The Pilot results indicated that customers, both overall and low- and moderate-income ("LMI") customers specifically, responded to the rate by shifting usage off-peak thus reducing peak grid demands from those customers by 9.3 percent to 13.7 percent in the summer months and by 4.9 percent to 5.4 percent for winter months. There was also a small conservation effect in the summer months[^1] and on average customers in all test groups saved money compared to standard flat residential rates. Customer savings over the two year pilot were between 2.3 percent and 9.7 percent of their bills across the three Joint Utilities.[^2] As a result of this successful pilot, the Commission directed the workgroup to provide recommendations for a full scale TOU offering to make these rate offerings available on an opt-in basis for Maryland customers.

In considering the appropriate path for a full rollout of TOU rates in Maryland, the workgroup[^3] notes that TOU rates have two primary impacts: (i) the TOU rates enable changes to customer load profiles as customers shift usage to the non-peak periods; and (ii) the TOU rates result in lower distribution system peaks. The Pilot was able to evaluate customer changes in usage and bill impacts but did not have sufficient time or scale to determine cost reductions from the system peak reductions — while cost can be calculated by extrapolation for distribution system margin cost of service, that analysis was not undertaken. That said, cost reductions, for each utility could be achieved through long-term, significant peak reductions as a result of the impact on capacity prices embedded in supply costs, and overall avoidance or deferral of investment in transmission and distribution capacity-related costs. These potential cost reductions, however, are largely dependent on the number and behavior of customers who choose to participate.

The workgroup recommends moving forward with a full scale opt-in roll-out of TOU rates using one of the two rate designs outlined below. The workgroup expects that distribution system, transmission, and supply savings may not be measurable for many years but, based on the pilot results, the workgroup submits that savings, or deferred investments, could occur compared to the status quo of standard flat residential rates if customer selection of TOU rates and customers behavior (load-shifting or conservation) is significant and sustainable. The workgroup recommends adoption based on the recognition that (i) TOU rates could play a significant role in the load flexibility needed in coming years as the grid is modernized; (ii) customers have more choices in end-use technologies; and (iii) Maryland is undertaking economy-wide greenhouse gas reduction efforts.

Looking forward, the workgroup also notes that as more renewables are brought onto PJM's and Maryland's grid, and customer behavior changes with adoption of electric vehicles and electric-heat pump technologies, these TOU rates may well require periodic revisions. Although the conceptual rate design remains unchanged, as the underlying peaks and prices of the rate elements evolve it may be necessary to send price signals to customers that reflect current grid system costs as those change throughout the day and seasonally as a result of changing resource mix and customer load patterns.

In designing the pilot rate, the workgroup used cost-based principles to identify the on- and off-peak periods and fine-tuned the existing distribution, transmission, and supply rate elements to develop the on- and off-peak periods and costs based on each of the Joint Utilities' respective costs. In addition, the working group also considered the willingness of customers to adopt the rates and the customer experience in designing the rates. For this reason, the recommended rates deviate from those that would result from pure cost-based principles in several ways, as discussed more below.

In revisiting the Pilot design, the workgroup has developed a number of consensus recommendations for adjustments to the pilot rates for the purpose of implementing a full-scale opt-in rate. The only element of the design which did not reach consensus is the distribution rate, therefore two separate cost-based approaches are provided for Commission consideration.

## Participation

During the Pilot, the Commission set limitations on Pilot participation that were designed to secure sufficient participation for a statistically robust treatment and control group analysis while limiting potential cost shifting to non-pilot participants. In recommending full-scale opt-in roll out of a new TOU offering, these limitations are no longer necessary. The following Pilot limitations are discussed below: net metering, other pilot programs, retail supply customers, and SOS customers, with specific recommendations for each. Additionally, during the pilot customers were required to have 12 months of usage to be eligible for the rates. As a permanent rate offering this restriction would be removed.

### Net Metering

Pilot participants who engaged in net metering were capped at 10% of the pilot population to ensure that this specific customer type was not over-represented in the sample. With a full scale roll out, this is no longer a concern. The workgroup recommends that all customers, regardless of participation in net metering, should be allowed to select the TOU rate. While no system changes are required to accommodate net metering, the Joint Utilities would need to make system changes in order to allow for the TOU rate customers to participate in aggregated net metering. The workgroup recommends not opening the rate to aggregated net metering customers at this time and revisiting this group for potential inclusion at the first quadrennial review.[^4]

### Other Pilot Programs

In order to maintain the statistical validity of TOU Pilot results, the eligibility for participation was restricted to customers who were not participating in another utility pilot program, including, but not limited to, Community Solar. In a full-scale offering, this is no longer a concern and this eligibility criterion will be removed.

### Retail Supply Customers

The Pilot design, offering both a distribution and a supply TOU rate, did not allow customers to choose the TOU distribution rate while simultaneously enrolling with a retail supplier. Primarily, this option was not permitted due to the separate retail supply time varying rate pilots that were approved by the Commission. Further, for utility pilot purposes, allowing this option would have compromised pilot analysis by allowing for a nearly infinite variety of on- to off-peak ratios based on varying retail supply offerings. The workgroup recommends that all retail supply customers should be allowed to select the TOU distribution rate while maintaining or choosing a retail supply option.

### Standard Offer Service Customers

The pilot was initially designed as a combined distribution and supply (SOS) rate. In transitioning to a full roll out, the utilities have determined that they are unable to offer the distribution TOU rate paired with any of their other SOS offerings (flat or legacy TOU SOS rates). In order to provide this option, the Joint Utilities would require a significant overhaul of their tariffs and billing systems. In transitioning to a full roll out, the workgroup recommends continuing to offer a combination of both the distribution and supply TOU rates, adding the ability for customers to select another retail supplier, as discussed above. The workgroup recommends that the Commission revisit the potential for pairing the TOU Distribution rate with a flat SOS rate at the first quadrennial review.

## Rate Adjustments

During the pilot period the TOU rate was updated periodically based on utility distribution rate cases and the results of seasonal SOS auctions. In transitioning to a more permanent TOU offering, the workgroup proposes the following adjustments to the TOU rate:

- Distribution rates will be updated during distribution rate cases, including updates to the underlying data; i.e., updates to the class cost of service study and the TOU period billing determinants.
- Supply rates will be updated along with the residential SOS rates and through transmission rate update processes.

## Recommended Rate Design

The workgroup considered the three primary design elements: (1) on- and off-peak periods; (2) supply rates; and (3) distribution rates. Ultimately the workgroup agreed on the first two elements as outlined below with minor adjustments from the Pilot design. The distribution rate element is non-consensus and the two cost-based proposals are provided below.

### Peak Periods

The workgroup agreed, based on analysis of system elements that adjustments to the on- and off-peak periods are appropriate. BGE's summer peak hours are shifted later by one hour and an additional winter evening peak is added for all service territories. The on-peak periods continue to apply only to non-holiday weekdays.

**Summer:**

| Utility            | On-peak hours                    |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| BGE                | 3 PM – 8 PM (Hours ending 16–20) |
| Pepco and Delmarva | 2 PM – 7 PM (Hours ending 15–19) |

**Non-Summer (Joint Utilities):** 6 AM – 9 AM and 5 PM – 9 PM (Hours ending 7–9 and 18–21)

### Supply Rate

The workgroup agreed that the SOS rate will continue to be calculated as it was in the Pilot. Starting from the auction-based SOS rates, the Joint Utilities will add transmission and estimated capacity costs into the peak periods to create the on- to off-peak differential. The SOS rates will be set seasonally, consistent with existing utility TOU rates. When there is adequate enrollment, OPC and Staff recommend separately bidding the TOU rate schedule in the supply procurement process; the workgroup did not determine a threshold or timeframe for when to consider pursuing separate bidding.

### Distribution Rate Options

In considering the appropriate methodology for developing the on- and off- peak distribution rates the workgroup settled on two potential options but did not reach a consensus between the two options.

- **Option One (Staff and Joint Utilities):** Continue the pilot offering which was designed from existing distribution rates and shifted all primary distribution system costs into the on-peak period.
- **Option Two (OPC):** On- and off-peak ratios based on an average-and-peak approach which results in approximately 60% of primary distribution being placed in the on-peak period and 40% distributed across all hours. The average-and-peak percentages differ across the three utilities based on each utility's average load factor for the residential class.

An objective in developing the distribution rate was to ensure that summer or winter rates did not make it impossible for customers to achieve savings in both winter and summer seasons and also provide for distribution company revenue neutrality. Ideally, the rates should be designed to allow an average load profile customer to achieve approximately the same bill if they do not engage in load shifting. In considering party proposals the workgroup determined that allocating distribution costs seasonally, as initially proposed by OPC, generally resulted in much higher rates during the summer than during the winter. The results of allocating TOU costs without tempering seasonal impacts would result in customers on TOU rates being unable to achieve any bill savings during the summer months relative to the standard residential rate — even if they shifted 10% of their load to off-peak hours.

All parties supported eliminating the "seasonality" issue and made consensus modifications to the proposed designs. For the PHI Utilities, which have seasonally-differentiated residential distribution rates, all distribution rate proposals have been designed to be seasonally revenue neutral relative to flat distribution rates. As outlined in the bill impacts chart below, each of the rate designs starts from approximate seasonal revenue neutrality in all three service territories. The Joint Utilities and Staff recommend using the pilot design, placing all primary distribution costs into the on peak period. Staff argues that its higher ratio provides a stronger price signal, more consistent with the pilot design and results, and offers a larger potential savings benefits for customers. The Joint Utilities contend that the primary distribution system is designed to meet peak demands, so apportioning all primary costs to the peak period is a reasonable cost-based approach and avoids adding another layer of complexity (i.e., peak and average allocation) into the calculation. Further, the Joint Utilities are concerned that OPC's recommendation will lead to a low price signal for third party supplier customers who only receive the distribution portion of the TOU rate.[^8]

OPC's rationale for applying the average-and-peak method is that it recognizes that the distribution system is not purely built to meet the peak demand, but also serves load in all hours. The methodology therefore assigns a portion of primary distribution costs to the off-peak periods in recognition that the distribution system must serve load in those hours as well. The portion of costs allocated to the off-peak periods is determined based on class average load as a percentage of class peak demand (i.e., class load factor). OPC also noted that particularly steep on- to off-peak price ratios may impede customer enrollment. OPC cited the low pilot enrollment levels for DPL coupled with its 6:1 price ratio in the Pilot in support of its concerns.[^9]

The workgroup was unable to reach consensus on either of these two designs and therefore requires a determination from the Commission. The workgroup recommends that the Commission adopt the same methodology for each service territory.

## Metrics

By design, a pilot allows for extensive data collection and analysis to determine the impact of any particular actions. This pilot study set up both a treatment group and control group. Unfortunately, in a system wide rollout of a new TOU rate this type of tracking and analysis is expensive and cumbersome; the workgroup also believes it is largely unnecessary as the pilot provided proof of effectiveness of these rates in producing peak reductions.

In order to track whether a permanent TOU rate is delivering the desired results in terms of both load shaping and potentially minimizing distribution system investments Staff and OPC requested a comprehensive reporting regimen moving forward. The proposal provides two types of reporting — annual and quadrennial. The annual reporting is meant to simply monitor the ongoing impact of the rate offering in readily measured criteria and metrics. Meanwhile, the quadrennial report (once every four years) would provide an opportunity to analyze and consider the rate's design elements and whether those should be updated based on current system data.

The Joint Utilities do not agree that the level of detail requested for annual reporting is necessary given that a robust pilot was conducted that provided proof of effectiveness of the rates. In discussion with the Joint Utilities the parties agreed that the following information could be provided with additional effort.

### Annual Reporting

**A. Load Curve Data**

1. Aggregated and average 8760 load curve for R and RTOUP/RD
2. Aggregated and average 8760 load curve for weekday/non-holidays by season for R and RTOUP/RD

**B. Peak Demand Comparison**

1. Total class and average customer peak load contribution (PLC) for PJM capacity
2. Total class and average customer network transmission PLC
3. Monthly class non-coincident peak (NCP) demand (including date and time) for R and RTOUP/RD
4. Monthly distribution system coincident peak (CP) demand for R and RTOUP/RD

**C. Additional Reporting**

1. Monthly customer count data for R and RTOUP/RD
2. Monthly kWh sales data for R and RTOUP/RD (R sales in total; RTOUP/RD sales by peak and off-peak by month)
3. Cumulative bill savings — calculated at the RTOUP/RD class level — for each month relative to what bills would have been on R rates
4. Annual total Company cost for education and outreach
5. Summary of education and outreach activities
6. Copies of education and outreach materials for activities geared towards recruitment
7. Results from any surveys or focus groups conducted regarding customer understanding and satisfaction with RTOUP/RD
8. Net Energy Metering — excess generation in total for R and by TOU period for RTOU/RD

### Quadrennial Reporting

1. Discussion of annual reporting results around change in peak consumption from load curves, PLC/TPLC, and NCP/CP
2. Discussion of any modifications that should be made to RTOUP/RD, including education and outreach activities
3. Discussion of changes to other TOU classes or additional TOU rate offerings
4. Assessment if RTOUP/RD offering is still beneficial
5. Discussion whether ongoing annual and quadrennial reporting is required or what level of reporting is still necessary

The workgroup recommends that the Commission order the Joint Utilities to report to Staff and OPC on the elements outlined above. The workgroup was unable to reach consensus on the feasibility and value of reporting on estimated reductions in energy, capacity, and transmission costs. The workgroup requests additional time to fully develop this issue for Commission consideration. While the parties are unlikely to reach consensus, they are not yet prepared to offer all the information necessary for a Commission decision. In addition, the parties suggest that the Commission consider whether additional reporting metrics should be added to the annual reporting at each quadrennial review.

## Recruitment and Outreach

The broad success of the recommended TOU rate will depend on the Joint Utilities' ability to effectively communicate the rate and its benefits to customers. The benefit of a TOU rate comes in load shifting — peak reduction and potentially increased off-peak consumption at scale. In order to be effective the rate must have a meaningful impact on load. As such, the workgroup discussed recruitment on a spectrum of low, medium and high as outlined below:

#### Table 2: Marketing and Outreach Options

| Level  | Examples of Collateral                                                                                               | Estimated Cost per Utility[^10] |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Low    | Website banners; bill inserts and messaging; social media; OPower modules                                            | `$50`–`$75`K                    |
| Medium | All of the above plus: targeted direct mail and email using data analytics                                           | `$100`–`$200`K                  |
| High   | All of the above plus: broader direct mail and email using data analytics, digital advertising and paid social media | `$200`–`$350`K                  |

Ultimately the success and impact of the rate will depend on adoption of the rate and degree to which customers shift their usage. Both OPC and Staff agree that recruitment approaches should differ by utility based on the differing pilot acceptance rates. For example, DPL effectively did a full-scale rollout during the pilot in order to recruit the minimum sample size; therefore a high level of recruitment tactics in that territory may not result in additional enrollments. Meanwhile, the majority of customers in BGE's territory were not contacted during the pilot. Therefore, a utility-specific approach to recruitment is more likely to provide the greatest value to ratepayers.

The workgroup recommends a staged approach which begins with the low, least expensive, levels of recruitment and begins adding additional recruitment outreach based on enrollment levels and trends. The workgroup would reconvene after 1 year of recruitment to determine whether to increase marketing efforts. However, the Joint Utilities are prepared to provide any of the three levels of recruitment that the Commission directs. Finally, the workgroup would welcome input if the Commission wishes to further explore participation goals tied to recruitment plans, as well as timeframes to revisit recruitment thresholds or provide any other guidance.

## Net Metering Compensation

During the Pilot period, net metering customers' production and consumption were tracked separately during the on- and off-peak periods. The net calculation each month determined an on-peak credit or charge and an off-peak credit or charge where a customer would either carry forward a credit or pay a charge in each month. At the end of the net metering year (April) the customer's net generation was compensated based on the existing flat "supply-only" level that is paid to all NEM customers. This approach was based on balancing the interest of both pilot and non-pilot participants.

For a full scale roll out, there is a disagreement between OPC and Staff on one hand and the Joint Utilities on the other about the requirements of Maryland Public Utilities Code (PUA) §7-306 which governs net metering. The specific portion under debate is PUA §7-306(f), which states:

> (f) (1) The electric company shall calculate net energy metering in accordance with this subsection.
>
> (2) Net energy produced or consumed on a regular basis shall be measured in accordance with standard metering practices.
>
> (3) If electricity supplied by the grid exceeds electricity generated by the eligible customer–generator during a month, the eligible customer–generator shall be billed for the net energy supplied in accordance with subsection (e) of this section.
>
> (4) If electricity generated by the eligible customer–generator exceeds the electricity supplied by the grid, the eligible customer–generator shall be billed only customer charges for that month in accordance with subsection (e) of this section.

The Joint Utilities propose to net and carry generation separately in the on- and off-peak periods. Customers will be charged monthly for any net consumption in each period individually. Credits are carried forward in each period individually and any net balances carried forward and payable in the annual April true-up will be compensated at the average residential rate in effect in the previous twelve month period. The Joint Utilities' interpretation of the statute relies on language in PUA §7-306(f)(2) which requires calculating net energy metering "in accordance with standard metering practices." The Joint Utilities believe that separately netting on- and off-peak periods is a standard metering practice for time-varying rates. This interpretation allows the Utilities to charge consumption in each period individually, regardless of whether a customer carries net generation in the other period.

Staff and OPC have raised concerns that the Joint Utilities' approach does not satisfy the requirements of PUA §7-306(f)(4) which prohibits charges (other than the fixed customer charge) in a month where a customer has net generation. Staff and OPC note that under the Utilities' proposal a customer could have generation that exceeds consumption for a month but still pay volumetric rates.[^11] Staff and OPC support crediting on-peak generation against on-peak consumption and off-peak generation and off-peak consumption. However, if total generation in the month, including net metering credits, exceeds total consumption then Staff and OPC believe the PUA is explicit that the customer cannot be charged more than the customer charge. Staff notes that a conditional, if-then function, which compares total generation and carried forward credits to total consumption, would allow net metering crediting by peak period while ensuring the customer is only billed the customer charge when net generation exceeds consumption.

The workgroup agreed that separately crediting the on- and off-peak periods follows the principles of cost causation. However, the workgroup was unable to reach consensus on interpretation of PUA §7-306 and this issue requires a legal determination from the Commission on the appropriate way to charge customers when net generation exceeds consumption. The workgroup does recommend that the yearly net generation (trued-up in April) should be compensated at the average residential rate in effect in the prior 12 month period.

## Issues to Address Moving Forward

Over the course of workgroup discussions several additional issues have been raised that would be appropriate for future resolution in this workgroup.

First, Staff has advocated for a broader look at existing utility tariff offerings including legacy TOU rates after approximately one year of offering this new TOU rate. The goal of that process would be to consider the future of the legacy rates that currently have low customer enrollment or minimal price signals. OPC concurs that a broader look at existing utility tariff offerings is warranted, particularly to address the issue of seasonality (or lack thereof) in standard residential rates. In addition, OPC suggests that this process include workgroup efforts to develop rates that would promote beneficial electrification.

Second, Staff and OPC would like this group to determine when it would be appropriate for this new TOU rate to be considered as a separate cost of service class in the Joint Utilities' class cost of service studies in future rate cases. The workgroup engaged in minimal discussions but were unable to determine the appropriate time to undertake such analysis.
