## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### Program Participation and Impact Highlights

#### Program Impacts

2018 was an excellent year for BGE's EmPOWER Maryland programs with the entire portfolio achieving over 738,000 MWh annualized electric energy savings or over 2.3% of 2016 baseline sales. Natural gas savings were over 5.6 million therms. Avoided generation from these savings over the estimated life of the energy efficiency measures and services installed during 2018 is over 5.3 million MWh. This represents a reduction of nearly 4 million pounds of carbon dioxide gas emissions according to the EPA Greenhouse Gas Equivalents Calculator, over 2.2 million tons of coal not burned or over 846,000 vehicles not driven for a year. In addition to the energy savings realized and associated environmental benefits customers generated by choosing more efficient alternatives in the way they use electricity and natural gas, BGE customers received incentives and bill credits of over $101 million. This was over 71% of BGE's total EmPOWER Maryland spend for the year. Estimated savings on customer bills over the life of measures installed during 2018 are nearly $572 million. Total economic benefits returned to BGE customers is summarized below;

2018 also marks the tenth year of BGE's EmPOWER programs and a decade of EmPOWER Maryland achievements in reducing electricity usage, natural gas consumption and greenhouse gas emissions. The numbers are impressive. Since 2009, over 438,000 unique participants have taken advantage of BGE energy efficiency programs. In addition, at the end of 2018, BGE's PeakRewards program had over 317,000 unique participants with over 318,000 participants in the A/C program and nearly 23,000 participants in the Water Heater Program. BGE customers have purchased over 316,000 appliances and recycled over 77,000. Over 290,000 customers have invited BGE into their homes to perform a Quick Home Energy Checkup ("QHEC") and have allowed BGE to install almost 4,000,000 energy saving measures during those visits. BGE's customers have constructed nearly 21,000 new homes to more efficient standards incentivized by EmPOWER Maryland. They have replaced over 23,000 inefficient HVAC units with new units to cool and heat their homes while reducing energy consumption and have installed over 48,000 state of the art smart thermostats offered by multiple energy efficiency programs and PeakRewards to ensure that their HVAC units deliver comfort with the best use of energy. The above participant numbers do not include BGE's lighting program because BGE cannot capture the customer's information, but BGE has incentivized customers to purchase over 33,000,000 energy efficient CFL and LED bulbs and fixtures.

[PDF p. 6 | printed p. 5]

BGE's commercial customers were quick to recognize the value of energy efficiency to their business. Nearly 17,000 small business owners and over 22,000 larger businesses have participated in BGE's commercial programs. Ten Combined Heat and Power ("CHP") projects have been completed with more in the works. A rather recent commercial program, the Instant Discount – Mid-stream program, provides incentives at the distributor level to encourage the purchase of more efficient lighting and other products and has already resulted in the purchase of over 2,000,000 such items.

Because of their enthusiastic support for the EmPOWER Maryland programs, BGE's customers realize real dollar savings on their bills. Those savings approximate over $4.6 billion over the expected lives of the measures they have installed. Additionally, their decisions to opt for energy efficient products and services to reduce energy usage has generated over $570 million in incentives. BGE customers who have taken advantage of BGE's Smart Energy Rewards peak time rebate program have received nearly $58 million in bill credits and those customers who have elected to participate in BGE's PeakRewards program have received nearly $250 million in bill credits plus the value of the thermostats and switches installed in their homes of over $158 million. In total, that adds up to nearly $5.7 billion.

The residential New Construction Program had the greatest number of new homes constructed since the initiation of the program. In addition, the incorporation of a smart thermostat option for participants has been well received with a significant number of homes taking advantage of the opportunity to add a smart thermostat.

BGE successfully launched the new residential Smart Thermostat Program in the first quarter of 2018, offering customers a variety of in-store and on-line options to get a smart thermostat rebate. Over 13,800 ENERGY STAR certified smart thermostats were incentivized with an up to $100 rebate. Smart thermostats continue to be the leading instant rebated product sold on BGE Marketplace representing 67% of all smart thermostat rebates. BGE successfully executed three Smart Energy Rewards ("SER") Program Energy Savings Days ("ESDs") on July 27, 2018, August 7, 2018 and August 29, 2018, which demonstrated the ability of BGE customers to reduce their energy use when called upon. Those customers participating in various BGE energy efficiency programs received incentives amounted to ~$950,000 during 2018. Of note was BGE's QHEC program where these customers accounted for over 9% of all QHEC participants. The Residential Lighting Program provided nearly 500,000 energy efficient LED bulbs to local food banks for distribution to their clients as part of BGE's outreach to limited income residents. Additionally, within BGE's Smart Energy Manager ("SEM") Program, BGE partnered with the Maryland Department of Housing and Community Development ("DHCD") to identify eligible customers who potentially qualified for the Weatherization Assistance Program ("WAP") and Limited Income Energy Efficiency Programs ("LIEEP") but had not applied. **Demand Reduction Capability:**

As noted when BGE provided the Q3 2018 EmPOWER Maryland results, the summary tables reflect a declining capability of BGE's Demand Response (DR) for the PeakRewards and Smart Energy Rewards Programs. With the implementation of Advanced Metering Infrastructure ("AMI"), BGE can more accurately measure the load-reducing capabilities of its DR programs. Prior to sufficient AMI data, BGE and PJM used R&D impact tables developed through pilot programs. As home use technologies improve and conservation efforts continue to climb, those impact tables quickly became dated. BGE is getting to the point of having sufficient historical AMI data to re-estimate the impacts that our DR programs have on our customers' electric consumption. BGE's recent AMI-enabled impact estimations for our PeakRewards Program are about one-third lower than the former estimates developed in the 2008-2011 Smart Energy Pilots. We attribute the reductions to the great successes of conservation in Maryland. Successful conservation reduces the base from which DR can reduce. Over the last 5 years alone, BGE's residential customers have realized a 10% reduction in electric energy use, and their peak demand measured by the peak load contribution ("PLC") has declined 17%. These magnitudes of conservation result in less opportunity to achieve peak-load reductions in DR programs.

_Residential Lighting Program_

Participant: A household that purchases discounted screw-in LED bulbs or fixtures from a participating retailer (brick and mortar or on-line) or a household that receives bulbs from a participating food bank through BGE's outreach to donate LED bulbs for limited income customers. This number is derived from the number of bulbs sold or distributed using an estimated bulbs per household factor of 24 bulbs per household which is consistent with the DOE2 estimate used in BGE's initial EMPOWER Maryland filing.

Measure: LEDs or fixtures of various wattages

_Residential Appliance Rebate Program_

Participant: A residential customer or a landlord of a residential customer who purchases a qualified appliance and submits a rebate application form. Note – if a customer purchases an appliance and submits a rebate application, that customer is counted as one participant. If that rebate application is for multiple appliances, that customer is still counted as a single participant. However, if later that same customer purchases another appliance and submits a new rebate application, they will be counted as another participant.

Measures: Qualified appliances under the program - clothes washers, electric clothes dryers, hybrid heat pump water heaters, pool pumps, refrigerators and dehumidifiers.

_Residential Appliance Recycling Program_

Participant: A residential customer who requests removal of a qualified refrigerator, freezer or room A/C from home (the latter if already removing a refrigerator or freezer). Note – if a customer recycles an appliance and receives an incentive, that customer is counted as one participant. If that incentive is for multiple appliances, that customer is still counted as a single participant. However, if later that same customer recycles another appliance and receives an additional rebate, they will be counted as another participant.

Measure: Qualified appliances in working condition - refrigerator and/or freezer, dehumidifier and room air conditioner. Limit of three large units (refrigerator and/or freezer) and four small units (room air conditioners and/or dehumidifiers) per BGE residential household.

**Home Optimization and Retrofit Programs:**

_Residential Quick Home Energy Check-Up (QHEC) Program_

Participant: A residential customer who has a walk-through of their home by a QHEC professional and accepts installation of at least 1 energy savings measure as recommended by the QHEC professional.

Measure: LED light bulbs, faucet aerators, efficient-flow fixed or handheld showerheads, ShowerStart™ showerhead adapter, water heater pipe insulation (installed on hot and cold pipes for six feet from water heater), smart power strips.

_Residential Home Performance with ENERGY STAR® (HPwES) Program_

Participant: A residential customer who receives a home energy audit by a Home Performance Contractor is counted under the number of completed audits in the mini-table. A residential customer who installs energy efficiency improvements recommended by the above contractor is counted under completed projects when the job is finished.

Measure: Audits: LED bulb installations, faucet aerators, efficient-flow showerheads – fixed mount or hand held, water heater pipe insulation, smart power strips and ShowerStart™ showerhead adapter.

Completed Jobs: Measures eligible for rebates include air sealing, insulation measures and duct sealing, HVAC and air conditioning equipment rebates, smart thermostats.

_Residential HVAC Program_

Participant: A residential customer who purchases new HVAC equipment, completes duct sealing or has a diagnostic tune-up performed.

Measure: New furnace, heat pump, central or room A/C, duct sealing of system, diagnostic tune-up of HVAC equipment, proper sizing, check-up, and install of HVAC equipment.

_Smart Thermostats_

Participant: A residential customer or landlord of a residential customer who purchases a smart thermostat for their residence or a residential customer who participates in the Thermostat Optimization service

Measure: A qualifying ENERGY STAR® certified thermostat. A current list of qualified Smart Thermostats is available on the ENERGY STAR® website at: [https://www.energystar.gov/productfinder/product/certified-connected-thermostats/results](https://www.energystar.gov/productfinder/product/certified-connected-thermostats/results)
