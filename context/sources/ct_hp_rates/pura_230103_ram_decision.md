## II. LEGAL STANDARD

Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19b provides a procedural framework for reconciling the energy adjustment clauses and transmission rate adjustment clauses for EDCs. The Authority must continually monitor and oversee the application of the adjustment clauses, and at least annually undertake a proceeding to determine if charges or credits made to the adjustment clauses reflect actual prices or costs and are computed in accordance with the applicable clause. Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19b(h). The Authority must recompute charges or credits and direct an EDC to take corrective action when the Authority finds such charges or credits do not reflect actual prices or costs or are not computed in accordance with the applicable clause. _Id._ The energy adjustment and transmission adjustment clauses allow the EDCs to recover their prudently incurred costs in accordance with Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19e. Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19b(c) and (d).

[PDF p. 6 | printed p. 4]

In relevant part, Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19e(a) provides that the Authority shall examine the operations and internal workings of public service companies and the establishment of the level and structure of rates in accordance with the following principles:

> (4) that the level and structure of rates be sufficient, but no more than sufficient, to allow public service companies to cover their operating costs including, but not limited to, appropriate staffing levels, and capital costs, to attract needed capital and to maintain their financial integrity, and yet provide appropriate protection to the relevant public interests, both existing and foreseeable . . . ; and

> (5) that the level and structure of rates charged customers shall reflect prudent and efficient management of the franchise operation.

Only those expenses that are reasonable and necessary to provide service to the public may be included as an allowable expense. Further, in assessing a utility’s management and operations, the Authority applies the prudence standard, which is the standard of care a reasonable person would exercise under the same circumstances confronting the management of the utility at the time of the decision to take such actions. _See,_ _e.g.,_ Decision, Feb. 27, 2003, Docket No. 99-09-12-RE02, _Application of The Connecticut Light and Power Company and The United Illuminating Company for Approval of Their Millstone Nuclear Generation Assets Divestiture Plan - Disposition of Proceeds_; Decision, Jan. 28, 2008, Docket No. 07-07-01, _Application of the Connecticut Light and Power Company to Amend its Rate Schedules_, p. 16; Decision, Aug. 6, 2008, Docket No. 08-02-06, _DPUC Investigation into The Connecticut Light and Power Company’s Billing Issues_, p. 10.

A finding of imprudent management may result in the disallowance of cost recovery by the utility. Specifically, the costs or additional costs arising from an imprudent decision or action are not recoverable by the utility. _See,_ _e.g.,_ Decision, Aug. 6, 2008, Docket No. 08-02-06, _DPUC Investigation into the Connecticut Light and Power Company’s Billing Issues_, p. 11 (if “[a]ctions by regulated public utilities which are found to constitute imprudent management . . . result in increased costs [, such costs] are generally disallowed.”); Decision, July 21, 1988, Docket No. 87-11-01, _Public Hearing to Investigate Whether Charges or Credits Made Under the Purchased Power, Fossil Fuel, Purchased Gas Adjustment and/or Generation Utilization Adjustment Clauses Are Accurate for the Preceding Three Months_, p. 14 (same).

[PDF p. 7 | printed p. 5]

Importantly, the utility carries the burden of proof at any hearing involving a rate. Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-22. This burden requirement implemented by the General Assembly in rate-related proceedings is significant because it attempts to remedy a critical challenge in setting rates — asymmetric access to information. The utility retains the majority of the relevant and critical information necessary for the Authority to make findings of fact and associated determinations on rates. Therefore, the Authority and other parties are at an information disadvantage compared to the utility and must rely on the utility’s application materials, responses to interrogatories, and witness testimony. The clarified burden under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-22 addresses this information imbalance by imposing an affirmative obligation on the utility to present sufficient evidence to support the proposed rate amendment.

In administrative proceedings, the appropriate standard of proof is the preponderance of the evidence. _Jones v. Connecticut Med. Examining Bd._, 309 Conn. 727, 734-735 (2013); _Connecticut Light & Power Co. v. Connecticut Dept. of Pub. Util. Control_, Docket No. CV094019951S, 2010 WL 797137, at *10 (Conn. Super. Ct. Feb. 5, 2010); _Goldstar Med. Services, Inc. v. Dep’t of Soc. Services_, 288 Conn. 790, 821 (2008). Consequently, to carry its statutory burden, the utility must provide (or ensure the record contains) a preponderance of evidence that the requested rates are “sufficient, but no more than sufficient” and “reflect prudent and efficient management.” _See Tianti v. William Raveis Real Estate, Inc._, 231 Conn. 690, 702, (1995) (finding that the preponderance of evidence standard is met when a fact is “more probable than not.”). Notably, this burden requires the utility to provide more than mere declarations of fact. _Connecticut Nat. Gas Corp. v. Pub. Utilities Comm’n_, 29 Conn. Supp. 379, 394 (1971) (“[t]here is no sacrosanctity about the testimony of any company officer regardless of his position which gives such testimony any godlike fiat that must be accepted out of hand by the PUC.”). More to the point, “[b]ald statements need to be covered with some evidential hair . . . .” _Id._ Further, “[a]n administrative agency is not required to believe any witness, even an expert.” _Goldstar_, 288 Conn. at 830 (citations omitted). It is the Authority’s province to “make determinations of credibility, crediting some, all, or none of a given witness’ testimony.” _Id._

#### 2. Federally Mandated Congestion Charges

Federally Mandated Congestion Charges must be separately identified on customer bills.[^4] Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-245d. For cost recovery purposes, the Authority divides these costs into two categories: 1) energy-related costs associated with serving Eversource’s SS and LRS customers, which are only recovered from these customers, and are referred to as the BFMCC; and (2) system-reliability-related costs, referred to as the NBFMCC, which are recovered by Eversource from all of its distribution customers, including those served by licensed electric suppliers. _See_ Decision, Dec. 19, 2003, Docket No. 03-07-01, _Application of The Connecticut Light and Power Company to Establish the Transitional Standard Offer_, pp. 7-8, 10, and 14.

##### a. 2022 BFMCC Revenue and Expenses

The Authority finds that the Company over-collected 2022 BFMCC revenue in the amount of $8,562,783. RAM Application, Ex. E, p. 1 as modified by the April 19, 2023 Interim Decision, Section IV. H., Calculation of Carrying Charges, p. 18.-.

The BFMCC recovers expenses associated with the Locational Marginal Price (LMP) differential, which represents the difference between the wholesale energy price of the Connecticut Load Zone and the ISO New England Inc. (ISO-NE) Internal Hub. Paruta & Shelnitz PFT, p. 16. The LMP is the cost to serve an increment of load at any particular physical point on the electric grid. _Id._ The LMP is comprised of the following three components: (1) energy; (2) congestion; and (3) line losses. _Id._

On customer bills, the BFMCC appears as a component of the GSC, and customers see only their kilowatt-hour (kWh) usage multiplied by the GSC rate, which is inclusive of the BFMCC. _Id._, pp. 16-17. The Company separates the BFMCC revenues from the GSC retail revenue amounts for the purposes of its RAM filing. _Id._, p. 17. The carve-out amount is calculated by multiplying the billed kWh by the rate set for the BFMCC on January 1 or July 1. _Id._

Eversource carried forward from 2022 a total BFMCC over-recovery of $8,232,762 exclusive of carrying charges. Paruta & Shelnitz PFT, pp. 17-18. According to Eversource, the primary driver for the over-recovery was approximately $10 million of higher congestion and line loss credits, as compared to the prior year. _Id._, p. 18.

### D. RAM COMPONENT COST ALLOCATION METHODOLOGIES

In this proceeding, the Authority began reviewing the Company’s existing RAM Component cost allocation factors and methodologies through interrogatories and cross-examination during the June 6, 2023 hearing. The Authority pursued this line of inquiry for two primary reasons: (1) to conduct a comprehensive review of the Company’s cost allocation methodologies for their adherence to cost causation principles and other rate setting principles; and (2) to identify potential opportunities for improvement and standardization of cost allocation and rate design methodologies between the two EDCs. Additional discovery is needed on these topics to determine what, if any, modifications may be necessary and the attendant impacts of such modifications. The Authority plans to conduct additional discovery regarding the EDCs’ RAM Component cost allocation and rate design practices in a reopener to Docket No. 22-09-08 or another proceeding and to issue Orders regarding any potential modifications in the Companies’ future annual RAM proceedings (e.g., 24-01-03 and 24-01-04).

Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 16-19 and 16-19a direct the Authority to ensure that “rates conform to the principles and guidelines set forth in section 16-19e, [and are not] unreasonably discriminatory or more or less than just, reasonable and adequate.” Moreover, Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19e(a) provides that the level and structure of rates must “provide appropriate protection to the relevant public interests, both existing and foreseeable.” In a separate context, Conn. Gen. Stat. § 16-19e(b) also asserts that rates should not be “unduly burdensome or disruptive to any group or class of customers.” In furtherance of this statutory framework, the Authority typically employs the principle of cost causation, which allocates costs to customers based on the costs they cause a utility to incur. However, strict adherence to such principles may not always further the statutory mandate to protect the relevant public interests or to ensure that rates are not unduly burdensome for any group of customers. The Authority will adhere to Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 16-19, 16-19a, and 16-19e and weigh the above principles and considerations in its review of the EDCs’ RAM Component cost allocation and rate design methodologies.

The following paragraphs summarize the Authority’s initial observations regarding Eversource’s cost allocation methodologies for the NBFMCC, SBC, and TAC charges based on evidence in the instant proceeding. Additional discovery will be conducted on these three RAM Components, and on the GSC and BFMCC, as described above. Though changes to all RAM Components will be considered, modifications or standardization in cost allocation and rate design methodologies may not be necessary in all cases.

#### 1. NBFMCC Cost Allocation

Eversource allocates the NBFMCC currently using a twelve coincident peak (12CP) allocation methodology, which uses historical load research. Hr’g Tr. June 6, 2023, 42:18-43:18. Specifically, Eversource takes the net cumulative NBFMCC costs incurred by the Company and allocates the revenue required to recover these costs to customer classes based on the customer classes’ 12CP. So, for example, if a total NBFMCC cost of $3 million was to be allocated across two customer classes, one with a 12CP of 10 megawatts (MW) and another with a 12CP of 20 MW, then the first customer class would be allocated $1 million and the other would be allocated $2 million. The NBFMCC revenue requirement for each customer class is then divided by an estimate of the customer class’s kWh sales, which results in the $/kWh NBFMCC rate to be charged to each customer class.

In an effort to gain additional feedback from Eversource, the Authority asked the Company to file a summary that unbundles the NBFMCC and assigns the various cost categories a specific allocation methodology. Hr’g Tr. June 6, 2023, 46:21-51:12; _see also_ Late Filed Ex. 1. #### 2. SBC Cost Allocation

Eversource pro-rates the SBC costs across rate classes using the prior year actual SBC costs that are tracked under each individual rate class, which ties the costs incurred for the SBC programs to be recouped by the same rate class of customers who generated the costs initially. Motion No. 7, Ex. 5, pp. 2-3; Hr’g Tr. June 6, 2023, 39:4-11. Specifically, Eversource takes the net cumulative SBC costs incurred by each customer class and allocates the revenue required to recover these costs to the same customer class. The SBC revenue requirement for each customer class is then divided by an estimate of their kWh sales, which results in the $/kWh SBC rate to be charged to each customer class.

Eversource indicated they believe that the current methodology they are using equitably allocates the costs across rate classes appropriately. Hr’g Tr., June 6, 2023, 39:12-20. Eversource alluded to the fact that the current methodology lumps the cost items together, which can cause a loss of the granularity and specificity of each of the types of costs within the SBC, and indicated that a potential new cost item could merit a more specific allocator. Hr’g Tr. June 6, 2023, 41:15-42:9.

In contrast, UI allocates the SBC revenue requirement based on the total annual kWh energy usage from the applicable test year. Specifically, UI takes the net cumulative SBC costs incurred by the Company and allocates the revenue required to recover these costs to customer classes based on the customer classes’ total annual kWh. _See_ Proposed Final Decision, July 14, 2023, Docket No. 23-01-04. As an initial assessment, the Authority is inclined to implement some version of UI’s SBC revenue allocation methodology for both EDCs as an energy-based allocator is both an appropriate mechanism for allocating costs for the overall SBC rate and has been consistently relied upon to allocate public policy costs since it generally reflects the proportional contribution of each rate class to the overall revenue requirement. However, as noted above, the Authority will continue to conduct discovery on this topic in a future proceeding(s) and, thus, will reserve its final determination for that proceeding(s).

#### 3. RDM Cost Allocation

The RDM revenue requirement is currently allocated based upon projected kWh energy usage that allocates the cost across the various rate classes based upon such usage. Motion No. 7, Ex. 7. Specifically, Eversource takes the cumulative base distribution revenue requirement over- or under- recovery incurred by the Company in the prior rate period (RDM revenue requirement) and allocates the revenue to customer classes based on the customer classes’ total annual kWh. So, for example, if a total RDM revenue of $3 million was to be allocated across two customer classes, one with annual kWh sales of 1 GWh and another with annual kWh sales of 2 GWh, then the first customer class would be allocated $1 million and the other would be allocated $2 million. The RDM revenue requirement for each customer class is then divided by an estimate of their kWh sales, which results in the $/kWh RDM rate to be charged to each customer class; in this example, the RDM rates for both customer classes would be $0.001/kWh.

Eversource indicates that the use of annual energy sales to determine a single RDM rate for all kWh sales is easy for customers to understand and is simple and appropriate for relatively minor adjustments with no major disadvantages. Interrog. Resp. CAE-66. The Company adds that decoupling mechanisms that track charges and credits based on actual versus authorized distribution revenue by rate class can present unintended consequences. _Id._ Certain classes may fall short of their authorized revenue and other classes may exceed their authorized revenue, which would lead to certain classes seeing decoupling charges and others seeing decoupling credits in the same year. _Id._ Therefore, the Company does not support a methodology that tracks charges and credits within rate classes as the results could be impacted by, for example, customer migration between rate classes, thereby causing an inequitable result among rate classes. _Id._

As an initial assessment, the Authority is inclined to implement changes to the RDM cost allocation and rate design methodologies to better reflect the underlying cost causation principles relied upon to allocate base distribution costs amongst customers classes and design rate through distribution rate cases. Indeed, the RDM is simply a reconciling mechanism for such base distribution costs and rates; as such, utilizing cost allocation and rate design principles different than those used to allocate base distribution rates undermines the effectiveness of those principles without a logical foundation for doing so. Moreover, the Company’s assertion that customers can easily understand how the RDM is calculated is irrelevant; indeed, if customer understanding of rate setting was a prerequisite, the rate setting process would be significantly less complicated.[^18] Lastly, such changes will not impact the effectiveness of the RDM in conveying the benefits of beneficial electrification (e.g., transportation electrification, fuel switching, etc.), which will result in increased kWh sales and a net RDM credit to customers, as the only requirement

for the RDM to be able to convey such benefits to customers is that it accurately reflects the over-collection of distribution revenues by the Company.[^19]

The Authority will explicitly explore utilizing the proportional class revenue allocation determined in the Company’s immediate past rate case proceeding to apportion RDM revenues instead of energy sales in a future proceeding(s). The Authority will also explicitly explore options to allocate the RDM revenue requirement apportioned to a specific customer class between demand and kWh charges, as applicable, based on the proportion of base distribution revenue allocated to such charges for that customer class. Such an approach would result in a reduced share of the RDM revenue requirement paid for by higher load factor customers. The Authority does not, however, plan to explore an approach that would maintain any over- or under-collection of base distribution revenues within a given customer class as such an approach may result in large over- or under-collections that would cause customer rate shock. As noted above, the Authority will continue to conduct discovery on this topic in a future proceeding(s) and, thus, will reserve its final determination for that proceeding(s).

#### 4. Cost Allocation Test Year

The Authority reviewed the EDCs’ current methodologies for cost allocation across rate classes and found that, in all cases, Eversource and UI used different test years for the same RAM Components. As an example, UI utilizes load research data from 2021 to apply the demand allocators for the NBFMCC and TAC, whereas Eversource utilizes data from 2020. Docket No. 23-01-04, Motion No. 4, Att. 1, Ex. L, p.1; Motion No. 7, Ex. 5, p. 4. Additionally, for the RDM, UI uses the previous calendar year of actual kWh sales to allocate the RDM across rate classes, and Eversource uses forecasted kWh sales. Docket No. 23-01-04, Motion No. 4, Att. 1, Ex. I, p. 1; Motion No. 7, Ex. 7. Such variation in test year practices adds unnecessary complexity to an inherently vast and complex set of information and practices that encompasses the RAM proceedings. To aid the review and analysis of the Authority, Participants, and interested stakeholders, the Authority finds it necessary to standardize the test years being utilized by the Companies.

As such, the Authority directs the Company in their annual March 1 RAM Application filings, to use the prior year of available kWh sales data and load research data when allocating revenue requirements to individual rate classes. If prior year data is unavailable and the Company uses data and load research from another year, the Company shall identify the alternate year used and include a narrative explaining why the prior year data is not available for the specific RAM Component.
