**AVAILABLE** for the entire electrical requirements of single-family residences, residential outbuildings, individual apartments and general service use (i.e. common areas) in apartment buildings, where residential use constitutes over 50 percent of the metered energy. The over 50% residential use requirement does not apply to agricultural/farm customers.

In compliance with PA 98-149 amending Subsection 16-19ff (a) of the Connecticut General Statutes, residential use in recreational campgrounds may receive service under this rate. In compliance with Public Act 02-17, amending Subsection 16-19ff (a) of the Connecticut General Statutes, use at a marina that qualifies for residential type use may receive service under this rate. A marina must have PURA-approved policies and procedures prior to billing any submetered customer. The marina owner (i.e. the “Submetering Customer”) must comply with The Regulations of Connecticut State Agencies listed below. A marina must have PURA-approved policies and procedures prior to billing any submetered customer.

To ensure the quality of service and equitable treatment of the metered campsites and boat slips (i.e. “Submetered Party”), campgrounds and marinas are required to follow Conn. Agencies Regs. Section 16-11-236 Calculating Submetered Party’s Cost, Section 16-11-237 Installation and Maintenance of Submeters, and Section 16-11-238 Meter Test and Record Retention. ## COMPETITIVE TRANSITION ASSESSMENT COST ADJUSTMENT (PDF p. 379)

**PURPOSE:**

The Competitive Transition Assessment (“CTA”) Cost Adjustment shall adjust the CTA charge, annually, on a fully reconciling basis, to recover or refund a projection of current year CTA expenses or credits calculated in accordance with the current methodology approved by the Public Utility Regulatory Authority (“PURA”) for such recovery or refund, and recover or refund the difference between the Company’s CTA revenue or revenue credits and the Company’s CTA costs or credits from the prior period, with carrying charges calculated in accordance with this tariff, as approved by PURA.

**APPLICABILITY:**

This rider applies to all Customers taking Delivery Service under the Company’s rates.

**DETERMINATION AND IMPLEMENTATION OF CTA ADJUSTMENT:**

For each annual period, every Customer shall be credited or charged a CTA Cost Adjustment, which shall be applied on either a per kilowatt-hour (kWh), kilowatt (kW), or kilovolt-Ampere (kVA) basis.

The CTA Adjustment shall be determined by dividing (1) the current period’s CTA revenue requirement; plus (2) a true-up of any under or over recoveries, including carrying charges, from the prior year’s CTA Adjustment charges or credits, by the kWh sales anticipated for the 12-month period over which the CTA Adjustment will be applied. The CTA Adjustment shall be determined and approved by PURA at the time of an annual Reconciling Adjustment Mechanism (“RAM”) proceeding.

**CARRYING CHARGES:**

Carrying charges will accrue on the monthly balance of the over/under-recovery at the prime rate as published by the Wall Street Journal.

**COMPETITIVE TRANSITION ASSESSMENT CHARGE:**

The Company will submit an annual CTA Adjustment filing which shall be subject to full review by the PURA. The filing shall be submitted once data for a given annual period is available. The CTA Adjustment will be applied as a separate line item on Customer bills for the 12 months beginning approximately 120 days following the annual period subject to reconciliation, or such other period as determined by PURA.

The amount of the charge will show as a line item under the Local Delivery Detail category on Customer bills.

## REVENUE DECOUPLING MECHANISM (RDM) RIDER (PDF p. 404)

**PURPOSE:**

This Rider provides a mechanism (“RDM Adjustment”) to reconcile annual authorized distribution revenue with annual actual distribution revenue received during an initial period and subsequent annual periods, with carrying charges calculated in accordance with this tariff, as determined and approved by the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (“PURA”). This reconciliation will be collected or credited over the subsequent 12-month Reconciling Adjustment Mechanism (“RAM”) period.

**APPLICABILITY:**

This rider applies to all Customers taking Delivery Service under the Company’s rates.

**DETERMINATION AND IMPLEMENTATION OF RDM ADJUSTMENT:**

For each annual period, every Customer shall be credited or charged an RDM Adjustment, which shall be applied on a per kilowatt-hour (kWh) basis. The RDM Adjustment will reconcile the difference between the annual distribution revenue approved by PURA (“revenue target”) and the Company’s actual distribution revenue for a given 12-month period with carrying charges. Such difference shall be determined and approved by PURA at the time of an annual Reconciling Adjustment Mechanism (“RDM”) proceeding.

The RDM Adjustment shall be determined by dividing the difference in the approved distribution revenue target and actual distribution revenue for the preceding annual period with carrying charges, by the kWh sales anticipated for the 12-month period over which the RDM Adjustment will be applied. The total annual revenue target will be the annual distribution revenues as approved by PURA in Docket No. 26-05-10 or a subsequent regulatory proceeding, plus or minus any true-up from the previous annual period. Actual distribution revenue shall not include revenue specifically identified and approved for exclusion by PURA.

**CARRYING CHARGES:**

Carrying charges will accrue on the monthly balance of the over/under-recovery at the prime rate as published by the Wall Street Journal.

**REVENUE DECOUPLING MECHANISM:**

The Company will submit an annual RDM Adjustment filing which shall be subject to full review by PURA. The filing shall be submitted once data for a given annual period is available. The RDM Adjustment will be applied as a separate line item on Customer bills for the 12 months beginning approximately 120 days following the annual period subject to reconciliation, or such other period as determined by PURA.

The amount of the charge will show as a line item under the Local Delivery Detail category on Customer bills.

## STORM COST RECOVERY ADJUSTMENT FACTOR (SCRAF) (PDF pp. 426–429)

**1.01 Purpose**

The purpose of this tariff is to provide a mechanism to adjust rates to distribution service customers to recover catastrophic storm and pre-staging event costs deemed prudent by PURA and in excess of the amount recovered in base distribution rates or refund to customers any amount recovered in base distribution rates that is in excess of the amount necessary to fund prudently incurred catastrophic storm and pre-staging event costs on an annual basis.

**1.02 Applicability**

The Storm Cost Recovery Adjustment Factor (“SCRAF”) shall be a per kilowatt-hour (“kWh”) factor applicable to all electricity, measured in kWhs, delivered by the Company to its customers under its Distribution Service tariffs. For billing purposes, customers billed on either a kilowatt (“kW”) or kilovolt-Ampere (“kVA”) demand basis will be converted from a per kWh basis. The SCRAF will be presented as a separate line item in the Local Delivery section on customers’ bills.

**1.03 Effective Date**

There are two time periods for applicability of the SCRAF: 1) for Catastrophic Storm and Pre-Staging Events which occurred during and prior to February 2025, and 2) for Catastrophic Storm and Pre-Staging Events which occurred after February 2025. For Catastrophic Storm and Pre-Staging Events which occurred during and prior to February 2025, any amounts not securitized will be recovered through the SCRAF over a six year amortization period starting July 1, 2027. Carrying charges will apply from the date of cost incurrence on the net balance of the Catastrophic Storm Reserve Fund and Pre-Staging Events Reserve Fund at the Company’s applicable Weighted Average Cost of Capital for these storms from the date of cost incurrence through the date at which costs are fully recovered.

For Catastrophic Storm and Pre-Staging Events which occurred after February 2025, the date on which the annual SCRAF becomes effective shall coincide with the date of the second rate change implemented under the annual Rate Adjustment Mechanism (RAM) process. This date is typically September 1st annually, but may be adjusted in the event PURA deems other concerns necessitate a different date. Carrying costs will apply from the date of cost incurrence on the net balance of the Catastrophic Storm Reserve Fund and Pre-Staging Events Reserve Fund at the Prime Rate as published by the Wall Street Journal. The Company shall submit SCRAF filings as outlined in Section 1.06 of this tariff.

**1.04 Catastrophic Storm and Pre-Staging Event Costs**

Catastrophic storm costs are defined as the costs associated with storms where the total incremental costs incurred are greater than $5.4 million, an amount determined in the Company’s most recent distribution rate case in Docket No. 26-05-10. Pre-staging event costs are defined as the costs associated with events in which the Company has notified PURA of its intent to pre-stage resources for an expected weather event, but where that [PDF p. 427 | printed p. 427] event doesn’t materialize into a catastrophic storm (i.e., total incremental costs do not exceed $5.4 million). Catastrophic storm and pre-staging event costs may include, but are not limited to, the following: 1) direct labor costs, 2) payroll-related overheads, 3) material, 4) vehicles, 5) outside contractors and vendors, 6) food and lodging, and 7) employee expenses and other.

**F FACTORADJUSTMENT:**

The F Factor Adjustment is intended to provide predictable and adequate funding for capital investments. There is a limit to the amount of capital improvements that may be included in the annual F Factor Adjustment, imposing an annual capital expenditure constraint up to the amount forecasted in Docket No. 26-05-10. Beginning with the annual PBMRP adjustment effective July 1, 2028, the Company’s actual capital additions for the Prior Year are allowed for inclusion in the calculation of the F Factor to the extent that the actual capital additions do not exceed the Forecasted Budget (referred to as the F Factor allowance). To the extent that the cumulative actual capital costs in-service through the Prior Year, in aggregate, exceeds the Forecasted Budget through the Prior Year, then the F Factor allowance is capped at the cumulative Forecasted Budget. The F Factor allowance will be calculated to determine the revenue [PDF p. 434 | printed p. 434] requirement (inclusive of depreciation expense, property tax expense, return on rate base, and income tax) on actual plant additions (including plant additions, cost of removal, retirements, and accumulated depreciation) up to the Forecasted Budget amount, and to reflect the revenue requirement for the Prior Year and through the Rate Year on in service capital additions placed in service in the Prior Year, provided that those in service capital additions are not reflected in base rates or in another rate mechanism.

**EXOGENOUS COSTS:**

Exogenous Events are positive or negative cost changes that (1) are beyond the Company’s control, (2) arise from a change in tax laws or accounting change, or regulatory, judicial or legislative changes, (3) are unique to the gas distribution industry or are not reflected in the calculation of GDPPI, and (4) meet a threshold of significance of 0.5% of allowed total base distribution revenue requirement, as approved in this rate case. The Company would include any such request for exogenous event cost recovery in its annual PBR adjustment filing. The following are examples that would qualify for cost recovery as an Exogenous Event (whether positive or negative): (1) State Initiated Cost Change; (2) Federally Initiated Cost Change; (3) Regulatory Cost Reassignment; or (4) Externally Imposed Accounting Rule Change.

Exogenous event costs can be ongoing or discreet and non-recurring. The Company proposes to reflect ongoing exogenous event costs as a change in base distribution rates and that a non-recurring exogenous cost would be collected through a separate reconciling factor or as an amortized expense.

The significance threshold for Exogenous Costs is set at 0.5% for each individual Exogenous Event occurring on or after July 1, 2027, adjusted annually thereafter based on changes in the composite I Factor and PFP X factor $(I_t - X)$ used in calculating the PBRM. The significance threshold is noncumulative and is subject to a finding by PURA that the exogenous costs arise from a single exogenous event (i.e., exogenous costs from separate exogenous events cannot be aggregated into a single total for purposes of determining whether the threshold of significance is met). Exogenous Event cost recovery requires that the Company present supporting documentation and rationale to PURA for a determination as to the appropriateness of the proposed recovery or refund.

Once approved for recovery by PURA, the amount of the cost change occurring in the Prior Year and deferred for recovery or refund, shall be recovered, or returned to customers either as an adjustment to base rates (if permanent) or as an adjustment to a separate, reconciling rate, as applicable.

**EARNINGS SHARING**

Earnings Sharing provides an important protection for Customers in the event that expenses increase at a rate much different than the revenue increases generated by the PBMRP adjustment. In the event that the Company’s actual Distribution Return on Equity (ROE) for any calendar period ending December 31st of the years 2027 through 2030, unless extended by PURA, exceeds the Earnings Sharing Threshold, the difference between actual earnings and [PDF p. 435 | printed p. 435] earnings calculated at the authorized Return on Equity shall be shared with customers if the Company’s actual ROE exceeds the authorized ROE. Such earnings above the Earnings Sharing Threshold will be shared 50 percent to the Company and 50 percent to Customers.

Earnings Sharing, when applicable, shall result in a single, uniform per kWh credit to all distribution service customers taking service under retail tariffs. The Earnings Sharing credit will be included with base distribution rates for each class. Any Earnings Sharing credit or charge shall be in effect for a period of one year and shall be subject to a full adjudicatory hearing before PURA.
