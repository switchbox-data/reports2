### B. Purpose, Scope, and Identification of Exhibits

**Q.** What is the purpose of your surrebuttal testimony in this proceeding?

**A.** The purpose of my surrebuttal testimony is to respond to issues raised by parties in rebuttal testimony regarding Ameren Illinois' proposed Beneficial Electrification (BE) Portfolio and its compliance with Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M) of the Public Utilities Act. In response to stakeholder concerns, I recommend that the Commission approve the Company's updated portfolio, including the addition of the ChargeReady program, which expands charging infrastructure support for residential and non-residential customers. ## III. SECTION 16-105.17(F)(2)(M)(I) – MAKE-READY PROGRAM

### A. Compliance

**Q.** What comments do other parties make about the inclusion of a customer-side make-ready program in the proposed BE Portfolio?

**A.** Generally, parties recommend that Ameren Illinois include a customer-side make-ready offering in its proposed plan. Illinois Environmental Protection Agency (IEPA) witness Lakhchaura recommended that the Company dedicate at least $20 million annually for commercial make-ready programs and $5 million annually for residential make-ready programs.[^1] Similarly, both Joint Non-Government Organizations (JNGO) witnesses Patel and Urbaszewski argue that the Company's current line extension policy does not constitute a plan for make-ready investments.[^2] Therefore, both witnesses recommend that the Company include make-ready programs in the portfolio at the same investment levels as BE Plan 2. Finally, Staff witness Hamidu also recommends that the Company include a customer-side make-ready offering in its portfolio but states that a formal Benefit Cost Analysis (BCA) is not required for it because a customer-side make-ready program should be classified as a Category 2 investment for which such analysis is not necessary. This is because Category 2 investments are considered to be "required" investments.[^3]

## V. SECTION 16-105.17(F)(2)(M)(III) – COMMERCIAL TARIFFS FOR EVS

**Q.** Do other parties comment on the ChargeSmart offering and its compliance with Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii)?

**A.** Yes. Mr. Hamidu concludes that Ameren Illinois complies with Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii) through its ChargeSmart program. Mr. Hamidu notes that while JNGO argues the program does not fully satisfy the statute because certain charging options continue to include demand charges, Ameren Illinois explained that the ChargeSmart program includes rate design elements previously approved by the Commission that provide alternatives to traditional demand-based rate structures. Mr. Hamidu recommends that the Commission find that the ChargeSmart program complies with the requirements of Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii).[^17]

[^17]: Staff Ex. 17.0 at 27-28.

**Q.** Do any other parties comment on the ChargeSmart offering?

**A.** Yes. Mr. Patel contends that Ameren Illinois' ChargeSmart offering does not fully satisfy the statutory requirement to provide commercial tariffs that utilize alternatives to traditional demand-based rate structures. He states that Ameren Illinois does not currently offer a demand-charger alternative under the DS-2 tariff and relies instead on a demand charge limiter available only to certain public charging customers under DS-3 and DS-4. Mr. Patel further notes that the demand charge limiter is being phased out and will expire unless Ameren Illinois takes action to extend it. He recommends extending demand charge mitigation mechanisms to all commercial EV charging applications, including DS-2 customers, and eliminating the proposed phase out.[^18]

[p. 16, lines 288–306] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

[^18]: JNGO Ex. 15.0 at 7.

**Q.** How do you respond to Mr. Patel's claim that "the Company currently does not offer a commercial tariff with an alternative to demand charges under its DS-2 offering; instead it only offers a demand charge limiter for public charging (under DS-3 or DS-4)?"[^19]

[^19]: JNGO Ex. 15.0 at 7:135-38.

**A.** I do not agree with Mr. Patel's claim. I believe that Mr. Patel either misunderstands the purpose of the rate limiter, or the structure of the Company's DS-2 rates. The purpose of the demand rate limiter is to take the Company's demand-based rates and convert them to a kilowatt-hour (KWH) based rate. This potentially benefits charging customers to the extent they have poor load factors because such customers would have high demand but low total KWH. Such a structure would not make sense for DS-2 because DS-2 customers already pay a per KWH based rate. They do not need an alternative to demand-based rates, because they do not pay demand-based rates.

**Q.** How do you respond to Mr. Patel's statement that the Rider EVCP rate limiter will be phased out after 2032?

**A.** The Company does plan to file an updated tariff seeking to extend the rate limiter. But given that the current Commission-approved rate limiter will already be in place beyond the end of the Grid Plan period, I do not believe this as an issue to be resolved in the current Grid Plan proceeding.

[p. 17, lines 307–328]

**Q.** Does the Company offer alternatives to traditional demand-based rates to charging customers other than public charging?

**A.** Yes, while it is true that the rate limiter as approved by the Commission only applies to public charging customers, Rider EVCP (ChargeSmart) also states that "A Customer that has installed or installs a new EV Charging Station shall be billed a Distribution Delivery Charge based only on the maximum Demand in kW occurring during the On-Peak period of the Billing Period, as applicable. Maximum Demand during Off-Peak periods will not be billed." This means that DS-3 and DS-4 charging customers enrolled in ChargeSmart can charge an EV while paying no demand charge at all, so long as that charging occurs in off-peak hours, rather than paying traditional demand charges in those periods. This both incentivizes an efficient use of the grid and EV adoption and applies to any DS-3 or DS-4 charging customer, not just public charging.

**Q.** Do you believe this satisfies Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii)?

**A.** Yes. I disagree with Mr. Patel's assertion that the Company does not provide alternatives to traditional demand-based rate structures. As discussed above, DS-2 customers already take service under a volumetric, kWh-based rate and therefore do not face traditional demand charges in the first place. In addition, ChargeSmart provides DS-3 and DS-4 charging customers with alternatives to traditional demand-based rate structures through provisions such as the elimination of off-peak demand charges and the public charging rate limiter. These features encourage EV charging during periods that are beneficial to the grid and facilitate charging for public, fleet, and other commercial EV applications. Accordingly, the Company agrees with Mr. Hamidu that the ChargeSmart program includes rate design elements that satisfy the requirements of Section 16-105.17(f)(2)(M)(iii) and recommends that the Commission approve the proposal as filed.

## VI. FLEET ASSESSMENT

### A. Applicability and Program Value

**Q.** What comments do witnesses make regarding the inclusion of fleet assessments?

**A.** JNGO and Staff both recommend the inclusion of a Fleet Assessment program in the 2029-2031 BE Portfolio. Mr. Urbaszewski reiterates his position that fleet assessments provide value because they inform fleet owners whether there is "sufficient grid and make-ready capacity to charge their vehicles in a timely manner while also managing their charging schedule to balance electricity costs with their operating requirements."[^21] JNGO further recommends the inclusion of the program at an annual budget of $850,000 plus inflation.[^22] While Mr. Hamidu does not provide a specific budget recommendation, he does recommend that the Company propose the program in surrebuttal at a budget in line with historical spending.[^23] Both witnesses indicate that fleet assessments provide value to customers by helping inform decisions related to EV adoption and the supporting charging infrastructure.

[p. 19, lines 349–370] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

[^21]: JNGO Ex. 16 at 5:85-87.

[^22]: JNGO Ex. 16.0 at 6.

[^23]: Staff Ex. 17.0 at 22.

**Q.** What is a Fleet Assessment program?

**A.** A Fleet Assessment program is an advisory and planning tool intended to help non-residential customers evaluate opportunities to transition vehicle fleets from internal combustion engine vehicles to electric vehicles. Fleet assessments generally include: (1) an evaluation of the customer's existing fleet operations and vehicle usage patterns; (2) identification of potential EV replacement vehicles; (3) analysis of charging requirements; (4) a recommendation to electrify or not; (5) financial analyses such as total cost of ownership, payback period, and other fleet electrification economics; (6) evaluation of potential emissions reductions; and (7)

recommendations regarding available grants, rebates, and fleet electrification strategies (if available). The assessment is designed to provide customers with information to support decisions regarding future vehicle purchases and fleet electrification planning.

**Q.** Do you agree with the recommendation and comments from witnesses regarding the inclusion of a Fleet Assessment program?

**A.** I do not agree with the recommendation to add a Fleet Assessment program to the portfolio. I maintain the position expressed in my revised rebuttal testimony that Ameren Illinois is not required by CRGA to include fleet assessments in the portfolio and that the Company believes responsibility for this type of activity rests with the Illinois EPA.

Fleet assessments are primarily consultative and educational in nature. Their principal purpose is to assist customers in evaluating vehicle replacement decisions, fleet electrification strategies, grant applications, emissions impacts, and operational considerations. A typical fleet assessment may include an evaluation of a customer's existing fleet, identification of potential EV replacement vehicles, analysis of charging requirements, total cost of ownership comparisons, emissions reduction estimates, and recommendations regarding available funding opportunities. While assessments may also identify charging infrastructure needs, they do not directly deploy charging infrastructure or reduce the cost of EV adoptions.

[p. 20, lines 371–391] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

These planning and vehicle adoption functions align more closely with the transportation electrification planning, vehicle incentive, and grant support activities administered by the IEPA. By contrast, the Company's proposed BE Portfolio is focused on supporting charging infrastructure deployment and customer access to charging. I agree with Staff and JNGO that this issue should be further addressed in briefs.

**Q.** Do you agree with Mr. Urbaszewski's statement that fleet assessments are needed to inform customers whether there is sufficient grid and make-ready capacity to support fleet electrification?

**A.** I do not. While grid capacity and charging infrastructure considerations may be a follow-on after the fleet has decided to electrify, fleet assessments are broader planning studies that evaluate customer specific vehicle suitability, fleet operations, charging requirements, financial impacts, emissions reductions, and available funding opportunities associated with fleet electrification. As a result, the disagreement on this topic is not with the importance of providing customers information regarding electric service and charging infrastructure needs, but rather with the assertion that a separate Fleet Assessment program is necessary to provide that information. To the extent customers require information regarding electric service availability,

[p. 21, lines 392–413] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

infrastructure requirements, distribution system upgrades, charging load impacts, or applicable electric rates, Ameren Illinois already provides that information through existing customer-facing planning, interconnection, and new service processes. Through these established processes, Company personnel evaluate system capabilities, identify necessary distribution system upgrades, provide customers with information regarding costs and timelines associated with serving new load, and discuss available rate and service options. In addition, customers have access to the Company's public facing hosting capacity map and other planning tools that provide information regarding available system capacity.

Accordingly, the grid capacity, make-ready, and load accommodation information referenced by Mr. Urbaszewski is already available through existing Company processes and does not require a separate Fleet Assessment program within the BE Portfolio. To the extent fleet assessments are intended to help customers evaluate vehicle replacement decisions, electrification strategies, emissions impacts, financing opportunities, and available incentives, those functions are not encompassed within the Company's responsibilities related to supporting infrastructure development and charging access.

**Q.** Do you believe that there is a more effective avenue for increasing EV adoption?

**A.** Fleet assessments require a meaningful upfront administrative investment, while the timing and extent to which participating fleets ultimately move forward with electrification can be uncertain. Although fleet assessments may provide educational value and help organizations evaluate electrification opportunities, directing resources toward the ChargeReady program provides a more direct pathway to supporting charging infrastructure deployment and EV adoption. In my view, this approach is more closely aligned with the intended outcomes of the proposed portfolio and represents a more efficient use of ratepayer resources.

[p. 22, lines 414–433] _(paragraph continues from previous page)_

**Q.** What comments do other parties make about the impact of the program?

**A.** Mr. Urbaszewski cites the BE Plan 1 Evaluation and states that the evaluation surveyed six of the forty-nine customers who had received a fleet assessment. "Of these six, they found that one had already purchased at least one EV, and two were still considering purchases."[^24]

[^24]: JNGO Ex 16.0 at 6:102-103.

**Q.** Is there any further insight from the BE Plan 1 Evaluation that informs the impact of the program?

**A.** A limitation of the BE Plan 1 Evaluation's fleet assessment analysis is that it did not account for participation in other BE programs. As a result, EV adoption outcomes attributed to fleet assessments may be overstated because the analysis could not distinguish the influence of the assessment itself from the influence of financial incentives and other program offerings that directly reduced upfront costs and supported vehicle purchases. There were 55 assessments completed from 2023 to 2025. To my knowledge, only one vehicle converted out of the 2,552 vehicles included in the assessments. While the program has seen increased participation throughout 2025, actual conversions have been limited compared to the number of vehicles included in the assessments. As stated by Opinion Dynamics in the BE Plan 1 Evaluation, "Although the fleet assessment is a crucial factor in helping businesses move forward with the EV transition, it does not address all their concerns. Participants report that barriers to transition include concerns about vehicle range and upfront costs.”[^25]

[p. 23, lines 434–456]

[^25]: Docket No. 22-043, BE Plan 1 Evaluation, p. 12.

**Q.** Based on the BE Plan 1 Evaluation and current implementation experience, do you believe the Fleet Assessment program is effective in addressing the primary barrier to the purchase of an electric vehicle, which is the upfront costs?

**A.** I do not. While fleet assessments can provide meaningful customer education by helping organizations understand fleet electrification opportunities, charging needs, and potential implementation strategies, they do not directly address one of the primary barriers to EV adoption—upfront costs. As identified in the Company's evaluation, the initial cost of vehicles and associated infrastructure remains a significant factor influencing customers' decisions to electrify their fleets. Because fleet assessments do not provide direct financial assistance, there is uncertainty regarding whether participating customers will ultimately proceed with fleet conversion. By comparison, the IEPA's vehicle incentives, for example, directly reduce the upfront cost of EVs, which provides more direct support for the key financial barriers that customers face and creates a clearer pathway from customer interest to project implementation. The Company believes that other programs and incentives are more likely to facilitate EV adoption and charging infrastructure deployment than a Fleet Assessment program. Accordingly, the approach proposed in my surrebuttal testimony represents a more targeted and efficient use of ratepayer resources while continuing to complement the broader customer education efforts led by the IEPA.

**Q.** Are you able to address the IEPA's request for additional information for the submetering pilot?

**A.** Yes. Ameren Illinois conducted an EV Submetering Pilot from Q4 2024 through Q3 2025 to evaluate whether vehicle telematics and networked charger data could provide sufficiently accurate and complete EV charging measurements to support future programs, rates, or billing applications. The pilot identified concerns regarding data accuracy and completeness. Where utility-grade submeter data could be compared to charger-reported data, differences ranged from 10% to 26%, with an average variance of approximately 16%. In residential applications, an average of 26% of expected data intervals were missing.

Based on these findings, Ameren Illinois concluded that the technologies evaluated in the pilot do not currently provide the level of accuracy and reliability necessary to support billing, rate design, or other programs that rely on precise EV energy measurements. Accordingly, Ameren Illinois did not propose broader deployment of submetering in its current Grid Plan. Ameren Illinois will continue to monitor developments in submetering technologies and evaluate future opportunities as data quality and reliability improve.
