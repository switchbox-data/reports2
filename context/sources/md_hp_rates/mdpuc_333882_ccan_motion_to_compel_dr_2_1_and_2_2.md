**CHESAPEAKE CLIMATE ACTION NETWORK'S MOTION TO COMPEL RESPONSES TO CERTAIN DISCOVERY REQUESTS**

The Chesapeake Climate Action Network ("CCAN") respectfully requests that the Maryland Public Service Commission direct Baltimore Gas & Electric ("BGE") to provide responses to CCAN Data Request ("DR") Nos. 2-1 and 2-2. BGE's objections are impermissibly vague and otherwise frivolous. The information CCAN requests is relevant to this historic test year rate case and the Commission should order BGE to produce responses to CCAN's second set of data requests.

**BACKGROUND**

CCAN served DR Set No. 2 on BGE on August 26, 2026 (**Attachment A**), and on August 31, 2026, BGE objected to an unspecified portion of DR Nos. 2-1 and 2-2 "to the extent" they are not relevant and stated the requests were unduly burdensome. BGE's objection is reproduced here in full:

> BGE objects to CCAN DR 2-1 and 2-2 to the extent that these questions seek information that is not relevant to this proceeding. In Case No. 9888, BGE is seeking a [sic] increase in electric revenue requirements based on an adjusted historic test year. Issues concerning electric system planning are under consideration in Case No. 9665. Additionally, responding to these questions would be unduly burdensome as BGE has more than 1,500 feeders and nearly 350 substations, with multiple transformers at each substation.

Counsel for CCAN and BGE conferred over email on September 1, 2026, but could not come to agreement. Because BGE's objections are impermissibly vague, and CCAN only requests information relevant to this proceeding, CCAN requests that the Commission compel BGE to respond to CCAN DR Nos. 2-1 and 2-2.

**STANDARD OF REVIEW**

Parties must raise objections to discovery requests with sufficient particularity, "so that counsel may meet and confer _once_ to try to resolve the objections."[^1] "No benefit is achieved by

[^1]: _Hall v. Sullivan_, 231 F.R.D. 468, 473 (D. Md. 2005) (emphasis added).

[PDF p. 3 | printed p. 2]

allowing piecemeal objections to producing requested discovery, as this adds unnecessary expense to the parties and unjustified burden on the court."[^2]

Maryland courts have held that trial courts "are vested with a reasonable, sound discretion in applying the discovery rules, which discretion will not be disturbed in the absence of a showing of its abuse."[^3] The proper scope of discovery is broad and allows for discovery regarding "_any_ matter that is not privileged . . . if the matter sought is relevant to the subject matter involved in the action."[^4] In the context of discovery, relevancy "has been construed broadly to encompass any matter that bears on, or that reasonably could lead to other matter that could bear on, any issue that is or may be in the case."[^5] Parties may not avoid disclosing certain information merely because it relates to a potential argument that they disagree with.[^6]

**ARGUMENT**

**I. BGE's objection to CCAN's request for information is impermissibly vague.**

Like its blanket objection to CCAN DR Nos. 1-12 to 1-21, BGE objects to CCAN DR Nos. 2-1 and 2-2 "to the extent that" they do not seek relevant information. Once again, BGE fails to specify which aspects of the requests to which its objection applies. Because BGE failed to state its objection with sufficient particularity, CCAN must defend the entirety of the data requests without knowing which are potentially objectionable and/or why BGE contends they are not relevant to this proceeding. This situation prejudices CCAN and is not what the relevant caselaw contemplates.

In addition, BGE's apparent practice of lodging boilerplate objections, if countenanced, will delay and impede the discovery process. As demonstrated by Staff's recently filed motion to compel—and CCAN's first motion to compel—BGE appears to be circumventing its obligation to provide objections within 3 days by declining to provide information on impermissibly vague grounds.[^7] If allowed to continue, BGE's overly broad and vague objections will prevent parties from properly narrowing the scope of discovery disputes before seeking relief from the Commission, thereby adding "unnecessary expense to the parties and unjustified burden" on the Commission.[^8]

In short, the Commission should reject BGE's objection as impermissibly vague and order a full response to CCAN DR Nos. 2-1 and 2-2.

**II. CCAN seeks information relevant to this historic test year proceeding.**

CCAN DR Nos. 2-1 and 2-2 seek information that is directly relevant to the proper allocation of costs to different categories of customers, an issue of central importance in every rate case. As BGE Witness Manuel explains, cost allocation includes assigning costs to different customer classes "according to demand on the distribution system, customer-based requirements, usage, and/or revenues."[^9] "When allocating distribution costs . . . it is important to understand the concepts of Non-Coincident Peak demand and Coincident Peak demand."[^10]

In DR Nos. 2-1 and 2-2, CCAN seeks information regarding:

- Each feeder's actual hourly metered loading or demand, in the same unit used for the feeder's rating (DR No. 2-1); and
- Each substation transformer's actual hourly metered loading or demand, in the same unit used for the transformer's rating (DR No. 2-2).

Both requests seek "historical values [that] should reflect actual metered data specific to that transformer, not weather-normalized, estimated, or 'representative' values."[^11] The above information is directly relevant to determining whether BGE is properly allocating distribution costs to customers based on their contribution to distribution system constraints. Specifically, the above information will illuminate (1) the extent of excess capacity on BGE's distribution system; (2) the demand that different customer types place on that excess capacity; and (3) whether costs are being allocated fairly based on that demand.

As noted above, BGE has failed to explain why CCAN's request is not relevant to this proceeding. BGE inserts its opinion that "[i]ssues concerning electric system planning are under consideration in Case No. 9665." That incorrectly characterizes the nature of CCAN's data requests, which seek historic test year information relative to BGE's proposals for cost allocation <u>in this rate case</u>. That similar issues—in BGE's estimation—are being addressed in another case does not speak to the relevance of the information <u>here</u>. The objection must fail on its face because the information CCAN seeks pertains to a central component of ratemaking in every historic test year case. Therefore, it is relevant and should be provided by BGE. While BGE may disagree with the need to evaluate its cost allocation decisions, that is no basis to withhold relevant information.[^12] Instead, BGE is free to challenge any arguments that CCAN may raise through rebuttal testimony, cross-examination, and briefing.

**III. CCAN's request will not pose undue burden or hardship and will provide relevant information to all parties.**

In response to this second set of requests, BGE provides a bare representation that "responding to these questions would be unduly burdensome as BGE has more than 1,500 feeders and nearly 350 substations, with multiple transformers at each substation." First, this is insufficient on its face because BGE fails to explain <u>why</u> presenting the limited information about these assets is burdensome. For example, BGE has not provided any explanation as to how this data is originally measured, what it takes from a technical standpoint to collect the data once it has been measured, and whether said collection has not already been done.

Further, BGE again fails to clarify why responding to CCAN's data requests would be any more burdensome than how it has already responded to other parties. In its responses to discovery from the Office of People's Counsel ("OPC") and Staff, BGE has already disclosed information relating to the capacity of its substation-transformers and feeders. Not only has BGE acknowledged this information as relevant but also has shown that the information is in its possession and that it can be quickly disclosed to the parties without undue burden or hardship. For example, in response to several data requests from OPC and Staff, BGE provided detailed information on the capacity of various feeders and substations including data on reliability,[^13] load forecasting,[^14] customer counts and peak loads,[^15] as well as BGE's methodology for identifying poorly performing feeders.[^16]

As illustrated by these disclosures, the data CCAN seeks is of interest to multiple parties, is not duplicative, and is **fully** within BGE's ability to provide without undue burden. By providing data on distribution system constraints with full transparency, BGE can assist all parties in assessing the reasonableness of the utility's spending and rate design methodologies, as well as reduce the need for piecemeal disclosures regarding individual distribution assets.
